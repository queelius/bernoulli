# Editorial completion criteria

1. **Immediate orientation:** abstract and opening explain the protocol context,
   the exact disclosure, its necessary public-view premises, and its significance.
   A reader can identify the contribution without reconstructing the whole protocol.
2. **Logical structure:** the singleton result leads; the aggregate extension,
   model, proof, computation, prior art, and limitations have clear roles.
   Notation is introduced before use. Paragraphs advance the argument rather
   than repeat exclusions. A short Comment need not imitate a full empirical paper.
3. **Checkable validity:** exact source locations support visibility, insertion,
   finalization, count, and confidentiality claims. Honest construction,
   randomness, conditioning, frozen reuse, and limits of the formal comparison
   are explicit. No target weakening, unsupported operational prevalence, or
   implicit complete repair is introduced.
4. **Transparent methods:** a reader can reconstruct the finite checks, their
   scope, parameters, counts, expected outcomes, and reproduction command.
   Proofs, finite implementation checks, and empirical observations stay distinct.
5. **Reviewer usability:** a small worked example or visual explains the attack;
   tables are interpretable independently; code and source premises can be found
   without access to the parent workspace. The draft is concise and professionally
   critical of the specification, not the authors.
6. **Independent iteration:** at least three independent initial reports and a
   subsequent adversarial review of the actual revision. Each material objection
   receives an evidence-based accept/reject/defer decision; required defects are
   resolved or explicitly block completion. Cosmetic preferences need not converge.
7. **Format and integrity:** canonical and venue texts remain scientifically
   consistent; citations and declarations are accurate; final PDFs compile and
   are visually inspected. Archives and documentation reflect the final text.
   Tests/coverage cover code changes, and clean-extraction checks cover the delivered
   artifacts. Preserve earlier negative findings and unrelated work.

Editorial guidance: Mensh and Kording, *Ten simple rules for structuring papers*,
PLOS Computational Biology (2017), DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619: use one central
contribution, orient readers before details, organize results by their logical
support, and revise through reader feedback. These are writing recommendations,
not empirical guarantees of acceptance. The MDPI Comment policy requires a focused,
appropriate critique of a specific article; Entropy permits free-format initial
submission. Primary links and access limits are in `sources.json`.
