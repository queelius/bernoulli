Dear Editors of Entropy,

Please consider the attached proposed Comment, “Comment on Qin et al.
Privacy-Preserving On-Chain Attestation for Cross-Domain Data Flows via GBFPlus.
Entropy 2026, 28, 947.” It concerns the adjacent-domain confidentiality goal
of [the original article](https://doi.org/10.3390/e28090947).

The Comment identifies an honest execution admitted by the published
specification: a time window finalizes after one successful insertion.
Public occupancy then identifies its complete share set. XORing those stored
words reveals the adjacent-domain identifier exactly, without a data identifier,
query token, or regulator secret. The note proves this identity and a limited
aggregate extension, provides a finite reproduction, and distinguishes the
result from the article's identifier-privacy game.

The contribution is this concrete protocol counterexample. The note attributes
the underlying sharing algebra and existing padding/masking techniques to
prior work. It does not claim a primitive break, deployed-system exploit,
full-path reconstruction, or complete repair. The source premises and a small
standalone supplementary artifact make the claim directly checkable.
