**Prioritized finding M1 — minor, qualify the masking observation.** In frozen `inputs/body.tex:226–234`, the antecedent of “this statistic” is the all-cell XOR, which is a full word containing the unmasked prefix. The explicit conditions in lines 229–230 require masks independent of the identifiers and one another, but do not state independence from the prefix. Those conditions alone establish a uniform masked suffix, not necessarily concealment of the identifier by the full word.

Concrete counterexample: take one insertion, `lambda=4`, `ell=2`, and draw `M` uniformly from `{0,1,2,3}`, independently of the identifier `N`. Set `P=M`, and mask the target as `P || (N XOR M)`. For each fixed `N`, the suffix is uniform. However, an observer XORs the two halves of the full occupied-cell XOR and gets `N` with certainty. For `N=0`, the possible full words are `{0,5,10,15}`; for `N=1`, they are `{1,4,11,14}`. The two supports are disjoint. Ordinary independently sampled sharing words do not change this fact. This is an objection to the sufficiency of the sentence's explicit conditions, not to the intended usual construction in which a fresh mask is sampled independently of existing state. If “fresh” already carries that stronger meaning, the defect is ambiguity rather than a false intended lemma.

Evidence: `scripts/independent_checks.py` constructs all four identifier cases without the bundled experiment functions; exact views are in `evidence/independent-checks.json`. The artifact's masking test checks only suffix marginal uniformity, which is true even in this counterexample and therefore does not settle the full-word wording.

Smallest remedy: name the suffix statistic and state the relevant independence. The coordinator proposed: “Fresh uniform secret masks on the adjacent identifiers, independent of the identifiers, prefixes, and one another, make the recovered suffix uniform.” This is correct for the singleton and certified disjoint cases. More formally, take the mask vector independent of the joint identifier/prefix vector, with mutually independent uniform components; the XOR of at least one such mask is uniform, even conditional on all identifiers and prefixes. Retain the existing complete-view and reuse disclaimer. If desired, append “in these certified cases” to make the scope explicit. The proposed clarification that the padding word is independent of the remaining cells is also correct: conditioning on those cells, XOR with that word is uniform. Neither observation proves a complete repair.

**Mathematical and protocol audit.** I checked the insertion pseudocode directly against the source PDF, including a rendered view of page 11 (`evidence/algorithm-11.png`). Algorithm 4 line 3 places the adjacent identifier directly in the target suffix. Lines 7–15 select the first vacancy as residual position, fill remaining vacancies, and reuse old occupied words. Lines 18–22 implement atomic full-support failure and increment the successful count only on success. Section 5.2 states that timeout invokes finalization; the transaction following equation (5) contains the finalized filter and successful count. Section 6.2 explicitly gives occupancy bits and occupied payload storage. These are sufficient premises for the counterexample; no statistical approximation in the source is needed.

The induction behind equation (1) is sound: after success every selected position is occupied, and future updates touch only empty cells, so all prior row equations remain valid. The occupied set is exactly the union of successful supports. A first insertion necessarily succeeds for a nonempty distinct support within the array; for `c=1`, that union is the sole support. Hence the full XOR equals `P || N` for every free-word realization, including zeros. Suffix extraction is linear. Uniformity of individual shares is separately valid for `kappa>=2`, conditional on the fixed support, prefix, and identifier, because at least one independent uniform free word remains in the residual. The `kappa=1` exception is correctly excluded from the uniform-marginal sentence, while recovery itself still works.

For the aggregate, equality between union cardinality and the sum of support cardinalities forces every occupied cell's multiplicity to be one. Thus XORing all occupied words adds every row exactly once. `c>=1`, `c*kappa<=m`, and the honest-history premise matter and are stated. Overlap does not refute the proposition because the scan abstains. Even parity cancellation or a stronger attack in some overlapping case would not invalidate this sufficient condition. The statement that different individual histories can share one aggregate correctly limits the inference. The two-choice singleton comparison succeeds with probability one without granting the publisher's identity or assuming identical signatures/digests. The main probability claim is an exact identity, not an estimate from the seeded runs.

Boundary cases checked by reasoning and/or executable cases include `kappa=1`, `kappa=m`, `c=0` abstention, zero targets, zero free/residual words, full supports, all support orderings in the finite domain below, and partial overlaps with one remaining vacancy. Widths are positive integers with `0<ell<lambda`; the minimum nontrivial width is `lambda=2, ell=1`. The stated scan cost is consistent with scanning a resident logical array: `m` occupancy inspections, `kappa` full-word XORs, one word accumulator and an index/counter. It excludes transfer/parsing/authentication as stated, and is not a CPython timing claim. Repeated reads of one frozen tuple add no new randomness. A minimum count strictly above `m/kappa` indeed excludes the certificate; a minimum merely above one does not generally do so.

It exhausts every ordered support, every two-bit target, and every free-word assignment for every two-attempt history with `m=1..4` and `k=1..m`.
