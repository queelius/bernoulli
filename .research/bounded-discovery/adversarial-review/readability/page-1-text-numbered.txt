015                                                    Abstract
016           GBFPlus supports authorized tracing of cross-domain data transfers through public ledger
017       attestations. Its published description treats the adjacent-domain identifier as protected metadata.
018       We show that an allowed one-insertion attestation reveals that identifier exactly. Public occupancy
019       identifies all shares of the inserted word; their exclusive-or (XOR) reconstructs the word, including
020       its unmasked identifier suffix. This holds for every construction realization and requires neither
021       a query token nor a candidate data identifier. A public occupancy test also identifies some larger
022       batches in which the same operation reveals an aggregate XOR, rather than individual identifiers.
023       We prove these statements and reproduce them with finite exhaustive checks and stored share
024       arrays. The disclosure concerns adjacent-domain metadata, distinct from the source paper’s
025       data-identifier privacy game and signer anonymity. The stated metadata-confidentiality claim
026       therefore needs to account for allowed partial batches and the complete public payload view.
027 
028 Keywords: garbled Bloom filter; metadata confidentiality; public occupancy; cross-domain attesta-
029 tion; XOR sharing

032 1    The confidentiality claim and public view
033 Qin et al.’s GBFPlus protocol records cross-domain data transfers in public ledger attestations while
034 supporting authorized tracing [1]. The source article lists the adjacent domain among the protected
035 metadata (Table 1 and Section 4.2); Section 6.2 specifically describes recovering its identifier only
036 after the consistency-prefix and assigned-domain checks. We identify an allowed execution in which
037 an ordinary ledger observer recovers that identifier without either check: a time window closes
038 after one successful insertion, and the publicly occupied cells contain exactly that insertion’s shares.
039 XORing their payloads reconstructs the inserted word, whose suffix identifies the adjacent domain.
040      Three features of the published specification suffice for this disclosure. First, Algorithm 4 encodes
041 an unmasked adjacent-domain identifier in the shared word. Second, Section 5.2 publishes the
042 finalized filter and its successful insertion count in the transaction following source Equation (5);
043 Sections 5.2 and 6.2 make occupied cells distinguishable from empty cells. Third, Section 5.2 finalizes
044 a filter when its time window ends, without specifying a minimum insertion count. Thus an honest
