                                                    Abstract
          A compact approximate relation can preserve a structural constraint while changing the
      dependence between query operands. Per-tuple false-positive and false-negative rates therefore
      do not suffice to specify query error. We separate exact truth, construction randomness, and
      frozen source identity, and audit the resulting operator and invariant-preservation formulas. For
      a monotone query with independent one-sided source errors, the sharp worst error over exact
      truth profiles is 1 − (1 − α)s0 for false positives and 1 − (1 − β)s1 for false negatives, where s0 , s1
      are one-sided sensitivities. These are elementary consequences of established certificate theory.
      Marginal-only bounds and a conditional shared-rate extension state what remains sound when
      independence is unavailable. For normalized binary-witness queries of source occurrence at most
      two, the required sensitivities have a linear-time graph formula. A stored-filter experiment uses
      these contracts to calibrate symmetric relation queries, comparing canonical keys with ordered
      keys followed by symmetric repair. We distinguish exact model guarantees from empirical
      hash behavior and from existing probabilistic lineage and inference methods. The resulting
      framework supports a narrow, reproducible query-calibration study; it does not establish a
      universal probability calculus or cryptographic security.

1     Introduction
Approximate membership structures trade space for errors [6]. Using them to represent relations
introduces a further question: what error guarantee applies to a query that combines their answers?
A tuple false-positive rate of α becomes α2 for a conjunction of two independent absent sources,
but remains α if both operands read the same source. Canonicalizing a symmetric pair deliberately
creates such reuse. It can preserve the relation’s symmetry and its tuple marginals, while changing
both query error and the difficulty of exact inference.
    This paper develops the relational part of the Bernoulli-set model [27] around that distinction.
Exact relations are fixed; randomness belongs to a constructed approximate representation. Re-
peated probes read frozen outcomes. We treat selection, projection, and join, then examine which
invariant-preserving constructions require shared sources. The main query question is deliberately
limited: for positive monotone lineage, what per-source error budget guarantees a desired query
error for every exact truth profile with the relevant answer? We assume no prior distribution over
exact databases.
    Our contribution is a scoped synthesis and calibration study:

    1. We give explicit source semantics and operator formulas, distinguishing independence of tuples
       from independence of sources. This identifies the assumptions needed by familiar product
       formulas and clarifies when canonical constraint preservation changes them.

    2. We derive sharp one-sided truth-uniform contracts from monotone boundary certificates,
       together with marginal-only and shared-rate bounds. For binary witnesses of source occurrence
       at most two, elementary graph structure makes the homogeneous contract inexpensive to
       compute.




                                                    2
    3. We connect the contract to a concrete partitioned Bloom representation of a symmetric
       relation, including a construction check based only on occupied bits, and compare it with
       ordered-tuple storage followed by symmetric repair.

The certificate identities, graph techniques, and probability machinery are not claimed as new
general theory. The study tests whether combining them gives a useful specification for approximate
relational representations.

Position relative to probabilistic databases. Boolean lineage and provenance semirings
already record how tuples contribute to an answer [16]. Weighted evaluation, tractability boundaries,
materialized views, and correlated lineage are well developed [10, 22, 17, 23]. Once a truth profile
and a construction law are fixed, our query probability is an instance of probabilistic inference over
that lineage. The origin of uncertainty in a representation does not evade this prior art. Integrity
constraints and conditioning also have substantial prior treatment [12, 18]; constraint preservation
is not a territory unique to approximate data structures.

Probability bounds and program logics. Dissociation provides sound bounds by replacing
shared occurrences with appropriately parameterized copies [13]; subsequent work develops relational
evaluation, weighted model counting, and anytime refinement [14, 8, 29]. Union-bound program
logics account for failure probabilities without asserting independence [2], while separation logics
reason explicitly about independence and conditional probability [19]. Recent imprecise probabilistic
programming also supports structured uncertain inference [20]. Our marginal-only bound is a
certificate application of the union bound, not a replacement for these calculi.

Sensitivity and approximate membership. For monotone Boolean functions, certificate com-
plexity and sensitivity coincide [5]; pointwise noise bounds in terms of sensitivity are established [15].
The exact one-sided extremal formulas below follow directly from maximal false and minimal true
inputs. A targeted literature search did not locate the identical query-calibration formulation, but
this does not establish originality. Bloom filters supply the implemented membership mechanism [6].
Adaptivity is a separate concern [3]: our guarantees do not allow a query to be selected from
observed errors and then treated as an independent fixed probe.
    The companion manuscripts develop set-level composition, maps, and information-theoretic
storage analysis [24, 26, 25]. Here storage claims are limited to explicit constructions and measured
payloads. A tuple-rate identity is not by itself a total-memory comparison. Sections 2 and 3 specify
the semantics; sections 4 and 5 audit operators and structural constraints; section 6 develops the
contracts; and section 7 reports their empirical use and limitations.

R ⊆ U . A random approximate relation is a random variable with values in 2U , equivalently a
family of membership functions R±     θ : U → {0, 1} indexed by a construction seed θ. The seed is

    Unless stated otherwise, probabilities refer to construction randomness with the exact relation
and query held fixed. The notation Rτε denotes homogeneous per-tuple rates ε on nonmembers
and τ = 1 − ω on members. These are pointwise construction probabilities, not the probability

conditional rates on an empty class are undefined. For a fixed realization, its fraction of errors over
a finite candidate set is a deterministic quantity, varying across constructions.

A concrete implementation must justify the assumptions it uses; section 7 separates an ideal hash
model from measurements of an implemented filter.

Shared storage versus shared outcomes. The standard perfect-hash-plus-fingerprint construc-
tion, also discussed in the companion filter manuscript [28], illustrates this distinction [11]. Place
member keys injectively with g, store their M -bit fingerprints V [g(x)] = H(x), and accept a key
when H(x) = V [g(x)]. Assume H is an ideal random function independent of g, and construction
inspects H only on members. Conditional on g and the stored fingerprints, distinct absent keys
have independent uniform fingerprints. Each match has probability 2−M , even if the keys share
a slot. Since this probability is constant, independence survives averaging. Thus shared physical
storage need not imply dependent answers; repeated canonical keys do share an outcome. This
idealized realization does not prove that a particular finite hash implementation meets the same
law.

3.2   Conditioning and repeated queries
All later fixed-query guarantees are unconditional over construction, or conditional on explicitly
declared construction information. A query chosen after observing answers can select a known
false positive. Thus a pointwise construction FPR does not imply the same FPR conditional

                                                  5
on a transcript, or a bound on every query in an adaptive workload; adaptive filters address a
separate problem [3]. For a fixed finite workload, a union bound on per-query errors remains
valid without independence between queries. Conditioning on a relation-wide property can also
destroy the independent-source law; it must be modeled explicitly, as in conditioning probabilistic
databases [18].
    Subset and equality tests remain ordinary Boolean lineages over the joint source space. For
example, two independent approximations agree on one true tuple with probability τ1 τ2 + ω1 ω2 ;
the product over tuples additionally requires tuple independence. The companion set composition
manuscript [24] supplies the corresponding set-level calculations. An approximation compared with
itself agrees with probability one, regardless of its marginal error rates.


4     Error propagation through relational operators
The relational operators of section 2 (selection, projection, and join) build new relations from old
ones. When the operands are random approximate relations rather than exact relations, each
operator induces a derived approximate relation whose membership test is a Boolean function of the
operand membership tests. Two questions then arise for every operator. First, what are the derived
error rates? Second, does the derived relation still satisfy element-wise independence (assumption 2),
the axiom on which the binomial error-count machinery of section 3.1 rests? The answers depend
on which random sources the outputs use. Selection restricts the existing tests. Projection of an
independent-tuple input preserves independence, although its rates can vary by fiber. A join can
reuse an input source in several outputs and thereby correlate them. Canonical representations also
share sources; projection of such a representation need not preserve tuple independence.
    Throughout, R and S are exact relations of objective interest, approximated by Bernoulli
relations RτεRR and SετSS over their respective tuple universes, with τR = 1 − ωR the true-positive
rate and ωR the false-negative rate (likewise for S). Except in the explicitly identified source-
sharing extension below, the two approximations are independent of each other and each satisfies
assumption 2: distinct tuple trials are mutually independent, with common rates within each exact
membership class. This does not include a self-join using the same construction as both operands.
Any predicate appearing in an operator (the selection predicate θ, the join condition, the equality
of projected attribute values) is an exact predicate on known tuple values: it carries no randomness.
The only randomness in any derived relation is inherited from the operand membership tests.

4.1   Selection
Selection σθ keeps exactly those tuples of a relation that satisfy a predicate θ on their values.
Because θ is exact, the approximate selection is nothing more than the operand relation read on a
smaller universe.

4.2   Projection
Projection ΠY onto an attribute subset Y drops the remaining columns. Several distinct tuples of
R can agree on their Y -components and so collapse onto a single projected tuple. The pre-image
of a projected tuple is its fiber.

Definition 4.1 (Fiber). For a projected tuple s in the target universe of ΠY , the fiber of s is the
set of operand tuples that project onto it,

                                     ϕ(s) = { t ∈ U | t|Y = s } ,                                  (1)

and its size is the fan-in k(s) = |ϕ(s)|. We use the image of the candidate universe as the target
universe, so every fiber considered is nonempty. A target tuple with an empty fiber is rejected
deterministically.

of its fiber is accepted by R± : projection is a logical o r over the fiber. Because the fiber tuples
are distinct elements of U, their R± -memberships are independent (assumption 2), and the o r
composes their rates multiplicatively on the complementary event.

5     Preservation of relational invariants
A relation may be required to satisfy reflexivity, symmetry, single-valuedness, or transitivity in every
realization. Per-tuple error rates alone do not ensure these global properties. We first compute
preservation probabilities under the independent-tuple model, then distinguish three ways to enforce
constraints: sharing a canonical source, adding reported tuples, and deleting reported tuples. The
constructions have different rate and dependence consequences; they do not give a classification of
all possible repairs.
    Throughout the preservation calculations, the universes are finite, R is fixed and satisfies the
named property, and Rτε satisfies assumption 2, with τ = 1 − ω. Probabilities concern the random
construction, and each constructed tuple bit is reused. The canonical construction below deliberately
replaces tuple independence by independence between equivalence classes.

5.1   Reflexivity
A relation on A is reflexive when it contains every diagonal pair.

their common acceptance probability lies strictly between zero and one. Independence remains
between distinct unordered pairs. A later operator must therefore use these shared source identities.

5.3    Single-valuedness

5.4    Transitivity
Transitivity requires every reported two-edge path to have a reported shortcut. The premises are
the reported pairs, not merely the true pairs of the exact relation. Triple constraints overlap, so
their probabilities cannot in general be multiplied. A disjoint subcollection nevertheless gives a
simple upper bound.
Theorem 5.5 (A sufficient regime for vanishing transitivity). Suppose the fixed rates satisfy 0 <
ε, τ < 1. Put p− = min{ε, τ }, p+ = max{ε, τ }, and n = |A|. Then

which tends to zero as n → ∞.
Proof. Choose ⌊n/3⌋ vertex-disjoint ordered triples (a, b, c). On each triple, reporting (a, b) and

this event has probability at least p2− (1 − p+ ) > 0. Events on the selected triples use disjoint trials

    This sufficient condition does not cover all boundary-rate cases. For example, an empty exact
relation with ε = 0 remains empty and transitive surely. There is no universal ordering of the four
invariants by preservation probability. Nor does the bound imply a complexity lower bound for
repairing transitivity.

5.5    Canonical representation and specific repair operators
Fix an equivalence on a tuple universe U. Call a constraint class-induced when its satisfying relations
are exactly the unions of equivalence classes, called orbits here. Every orbit O of an admissible
exact relation is homogeneous: either O ⊆ R or O ∩ R = ∅. Symmetry has orbits {⟨a , b⟩, ⟨b , a⟩}
of size two off the diagonal and one on it. For invariance under coordinate permutations, repeated
coordinate values can likewise make actual orbit sizes smaller than the maximum.
    Reflexivity instead forces a specified collection of tuples to be present; it is not, by itself, a
class-induced constraint. Single-valuedness imposes a cardinality cap on each row, not equality of
all bits in an orbit. We treat these constructions separately.

5.6   The approximate partial function
For product key and value types, write X = X1 × · · · × Xn and Y = Y1 × · · · × Ym . A partial function
is a single-valued relation f ⊆ X × Y; it is total when every key has a value. Its domain is ΠX (f) and
its image is ΠY (f). A partial function may be total; the term does not require an undefined key.

Definition 5.1 (Approximate partial function). A random approximate partial function of f is a
random relation f ± ⊆ X × Y whose every realization is single-valued, together with specified error
guarantees relative to f. For a defined key x, its three disjoint outcomes are returning f(x), returning
a different value, and returning no value. For an undefined key, the outcomes are returning a value
and returning no value. These probabilities may be specified pointwise or averaged under an explicit
key distribution.

    Domain false-positive and false-negative rates alone do not determine value accuracy: returning
a wrong value at every defined key can preserve the domain exactly. A raw tuplewise approximation
of a function graph need not meet this definition because it can report multiple values. The
deletion construction above enforces the required type and gives its correct-value probability; map
constructions and composition are the subject of the companion paper [26].

6.3   Why preserving tuple rates does not preserve inference cost
Canonical symmetry can turn read-once lineage into shared-source lineage. For a simple cubic graph
G, let the exact relation D(v, a, b, c) list each vertex’s three neighbors. Consider the fixed positive
query
                        ∃v, a, b, c : D(v, a, b, c) ∧ R(v, a) ∧ R(v, b) ∧ R(v, c).
Suppose all queried R tuples are exact nonmembers with FPR p. Independent directed sources yield
query FPR 1 − (1 − p3 )|V | . After symmetry identifies opposite orientations, each undirected edge is
one independent bit used twice. Each tuple still has FPR p and each witness still has probability
p3 . Yet the query is false exactly when the zero edges form an edge cover. At p = 1/2 its FPR is

contract and a fixed-profile exact probability are different objectives.


7     Stored-filter calibration and verification
The previous results are probability statements under declared models. We test their use in a
concrete representation of a symmetric relation, then distinguish what the experiment establishes
from what remains assumed.

7.1   Construction and model guarantee
The implementation is a frozen partitioned Bloom filter: k byte-aligned partitions each have m
bits, with one key hash per partition. A build inserts n distinct representatives. Membership uses
only the serialized bit array and a 40-byte header containing shape and salt metadata; it neither
stores nor consults the exact member set at query time. The canonical/ordered mode is supplied
by the query context, outside this header. Canonical mode stores unordered tuple keys. Ordered
mode stores both orientations and answers R(u, v) by OR-ing their probes. Both modes preserve
symmetry deterministically and have no false negatives.

7.2   Fixed query workload and baselines
Each query is a 12-cycle of binary witnesses, with s0 = 8. We use 128 disjoint bundles for each of

bundles are fixed before construction.

7.3   Mathematical checks and inference comparisons
The accompanying exact checker enumerates all 197 monotone functions on one through four vari-
ables and verifies 1,970 homogeneous and heterogeneous contract identities using rational arithmetic.
It also checks the graph formula on 305 small labeled graphs of maximum degree two. Additional
checks cover 256 source-covariance cases, weighted paths and cycles, degenerate rates, and the cubic
edge-cover example. These finite checks support the implementations; the proofs establish the
general results.
    A separate synthetic-lineage benchmark compares occurrence-product estimates, conservative
dependence bounds, exhaustive joint probabilities, the standard path/cycle recurrence, and the
symmetric dissociation bounds of Gatterbauer and Suciu [13]. For normalized positive DNF, dis-
sociation replaces each repeated source with independent copies; copy probabilities pi provide the
upper bound and 1 − (1 − pi )1/di the symmetric lower bound, where di is its occurrence count. This
is a published baseline, not an invented independence correction; optimized scaled dissociation [29]
is not implemented.

8    Scope of an encrypted-search application
Approximate relations can model the functionality of a private search index: tuples may encode
document–term incidence, co-occurrence, or candidate matches, and a positive query combines
approximate probes. The source map must still reflect repeated trapdoors, common stored filters,
and canonical keys. Subject to a justified construction law, the preceding bounds describe false
matches and missed answers. They can guide allocation of an error budget before a separate security
analysis.
    This functionality model does not specify confidentiality. Two structures with identical FP and
FN rates can expose different sizes, access patterns, query-equality patterns, or stored representa-
tions. A secret salt alone does not establish an encrypted-search security theorem. Such a theorem
needs a concrete construction, adversary, experiment, and declared leakage, including what repeated
and adaptive queries reveal. In particular, the probability of a false positive on an exact nonmember
is not the posterior probability that a returned match is false. No cryptographic security claim
follows from the Bernoulli channel parameters or from the experiments in this paper.


9    Conclusion
An approximate relation needs both an error law and a source map. Relational operators can reuse
sources, and canonical constraint preservation can create such reuse intentionally. The independent-
tuple model remains useful for specific calculations, but its assumptions do not automatically survive
projection, joins, canonicalization, or conditioning.
    For monotone queries, boundary certificates give sharp one-sided contracts under independent
sources and sound bounds under weaker assumptions. Binary witnesses of source occurrence at
most two admit inexpensive contract calculation. A conditional shared-rate model covers a useful
kind of construction-induced dependence. The stored-filter experiment illustrates the consequence:
sizing at a tuple FPR of 0.05 produced query FPR about 0.33 on a valid false profile, while query
calibration was compatible with the 0.05 ideal-model target. Canonical storage used fewer bytes
than ordered storage with symmetric repair at that target in the measured instance.

    The established lineage, certificate, counting, and membership-structure results used here are
essential prior art. The current evidence supports a carefully scoped account of their interaction,
not a claim of a new universal calculus. A stronger publication contribution would require a useful
contract compiler with broader measured benefit, a nontrivial tractable class under additional
semantic constraints, or a construction-specific guarantee beyond the ideal hash model. The present
artifacts make those questions testable without confusing marginal accuracy, query accuracy, and
structural validity.
