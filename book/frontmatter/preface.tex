\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}
\label{ch:preface}

This book is about one object. A query returns a bit, and the bit is
observed through a binary channel: a true negative is reported as positive
with probability $\fprate$ (the false-positive rate) and a true positive is
reported as negative with probability $\fnrate$ (the false-negative rate).
A Bloom filter answering a membership query is such a channel, with
$\fnrate = 0$. So is one round of the Miller--Rabin test, a survey respondent
answering under randomized response, a noisy classifier, the readout of a
qubit, and a keyword lookup in a Bloom-filter-based encrypted index. The book
takes the channel as its primitive and follows it outward: to Boolean
combinations of noisy bits, to approximate sets, maps, relations and algebraic
types, to constructions that meet the space bounds, and finally to the places
where the model stops describing the system in front of it.

\section*{What kind of contribution this is}

The contribution is a unification, not a collection of new theorems. Nearly
every individual result in these pages has a named predecessor, and several
are decades old. The per-element channel is randomized response\index{randomized response} on an
indicator \parencite{warner-1965}. The $\log_2(1/\fprate)$ bits-per-element
bound for approximate membership is due to \textcite{carter-et-al-1978}, and
the case with false negatives was settled to leading order by
\textcite{pagh-rodler-2001}. Forward propagation of error probabilities
through logic gates goes back at least to \textcite{parker-mccluskey-1975},
and the noisy-parity identity is Lemma~1 of \textcite{gallager-1962}. The
perfect hash filter, a minimal perfect hash function plus short fingerprints,
is described by \textcite{dietzfelbinger-pagh-2008}. Program values that carry
a correctness probability through a computation are the subject of Decaf
\parencite{boston-et-al-2015} and its relatives, and a probabilistic relational
algebra was given by \textcite{fuhr-roelleke-1997}. Each chapter ends with a
section titled \emph{Notes and Lineage} that says who established each main
result, what the chapter adds (usually a presentation, a unification, or a
correction), and what it deliberately leaves out. The intent is that no reader
should have to guess whether a result is being claimed.

What the unified treatment does add, I think, is twofold. First, a single
vocabulary (the rate pair, model order, the Kronecker factorization of
independent queries) for objects that live in communities which rarely cite
one another: data structures, databases, survey statistics, diagnostic
testing, complexity theory, cryptography, and quantum measurement. Second, an
honest map of where that vocabulary fails. The most important entry on the map
is in \Cref{ch:boolean-algebra}: a rate pair does not propagate through a
Boolean formula. The false-positive rate of an AND depends on which negative
input assignment occurred, so a gate that receives only a summary pair can be
wrong by a factor of two on a three-input formula
(\Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample}). What does propagate exactly is the error
conditional on each latent input assignment; prior-averaged rates and
worst-case bounds are the honest summaries. Shared inputs, shared intermediate
values, adaptive queries and construction-time conditioning each break the
independence the model needs, and Part~V is devoted to that boundary.

\section*{Relation to the source manuscripts}

The book grew out of a set of manuscripts on Bernoulli sets, their
composition, classification measures, entropy bounds, the Bernoulli hash
function, the perfect hash filter, approximate maps, approximate relations,
algebraic types, and quantum readout
\parencite{towell-bernoulli-sets,towell-bernoulli-composition,towell-bernoulli-classification-measures,towell-bernoulli-entropy,towell-bernoulli-hash-function,towell-bernoulli-phf,towell-bernoulli-maps,towell-bernoulli-relations,towell-bernoulli-data-type,towell-bernoulli-quantum-readout}.
Writing the book, and auditing it with exhaustive finite enumeration and exact
rational arithmetic, turned up errors in several of them: a claim that the rate
pair carries through an arbitrary Boolean tree; a finite order-statistic law
for the Bernoulli hash function that fails already at two keys and two slots;
an adaptive acceptance threshold presented as saving space when it trades away
recall; a closure theorem for composed approximate maps that fails when two
inputs share an intermediate value; overbroad closure and independence claims
for approximate relations; a scope statement for quantum readout that confused
a diagonal measurement with a diagonal state; and a space lower bound with
false negatives described as open when it is not. Earlier drafts of this book
had their own errors, among them a perfect-hash-addressed fingerprint table
presented as meeting the space floor, which no minimal perfect hash permits.
Where a chapter departs from a source manuscript, the chapter is the corrected
version and supersedes it; \Cref{app:principles} tabulates the corrections.

\section*{Intended reader}

The reader I have in mind is a researcher or graduate student who already
works with one of these objects (a filter, a sketch, a diagnostic test, a
randomized algorithm, a probabilistic database, an encrypted index, a readout
model) and wants to see it in relation to the others. The book assumes
undergraduate probability, basic linear algebra, and comfort with proofs.
Information theory beyond entropy and channel capacity is developed where it is
used. The book is not a survey of filter engineering and reports no
performance benchmarks; for the current state of practical filters and
retrieval structures see \textcite{graf-lemire-2022} and
\textcite{dietzfelbinger-et-al-2026-ribbon}.

\section*{How to read it}

Part~I (\Cref{ch:noisy-bit,ch:binary-channels,ch:boolean-algebra,ch:axioms-kronecker})
builds the atom: the noisy bit, binary channels and model order, Boolean
algebra under noise with the latent-assignment caveat, and the two
independence axioms with the Kronecker factorization they imply. Everything
later depends on it, \Cref{ch:boolean-algebra,ch:axioms-kronecker} most of all.
Part~II (\Cref{ch:approximate-sets,ch:set-composition,ch:classification-measures,ch:information-bounds})
moves from bits to sets: the Bloom filter read as a channel, set operations
and the model order they raise, classification measures as random variables,
and the space bounds. Part~III
(\Cref{ch:approximate-functions,ch:sketches-estimators,ch:randomized-algorithms,ch:approximate-relations,ch:algebra-types})
generalizes to maps and $n$-ary channels, sketches, randomized algorithms,
error contracts for approximate relations, and algebraic types. Part~IV
(\Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets,ch:perfect-hash-filters}) treats constructions
that approach the bounds, read as seed search and as perfect hashing with
fingerprints. Part~V
(\Cref{ch:not-bernoulli,ch:cipher-maps-trapdoor,ch:encrypted-search,ch:operator-representation})
is the boundary: where the model fails, cipher maps and trapdoor computing,
encrypted search, and the operator representation that connects the
framework to quantum measurement.

Readers with narrower aims can take shorter paths. For data structures:
Part~I, \Cref{ch:approximate-sets,ch:information-bounds,ch:approximate-functions},
and Part~IV. For databases: Part~I, \Cref{ch:set-composition,ch:approximate-relations}.
For security: Part~I, \Cref{ch:approximate-sets}, and
\Cref{ch:not-bernoulli,ch:cipher-maps-trapdoor,ch:encrypted-search}. For
quantum readout: \Cref{ch:binary-channels,ch:boolean-algebra,ch:axioms-kronecker},
\Cref{ch:not-bernoulli}, and \Cref{ch:operator-representation}.
\Cref{app:notation} collects the notation, \Cref{app:principles} restates the
framework's principles together with their scope, and \Cref{app:companion}
indexes the companion code.

\section*{The companion package and notebooks}

The Python package \texttt{bernoulli-types} (import name \texttt{bernoulli}),
maintained in the \texttt{bernoulli-py/} directory of the source repository,
is the book's executable companion. It implements the atom, exact error
propagation for Boolean formulas over independent noisy bits, approximate sets,
maps, relations and sketches, and the classification measures. Fifteen
notebooks reproduce the numerical claims of the chapters they accompany; the
chapters without a notebook (\Cref{ch:axioms-kronecker,ch:algebra-types,ch:cipher-maps-trapdoor,ch:operator-representation})
are theoretical. The package is research software at an alpha stage, not a
production library.

\section*{Names}

``Bernoulli'' is overloaded. A Bernoulli filter in target tracking\index{Bernoulli filter (target tracking)}
\parencite{ristic-et-al-2013}, a Bernoulli factory in simulation\index{Bernoulli factory}
\parencite{keane-obrien-1994}, and the Bernoulli map of dynamical
systems\index{Bernoulli map} are unrelated to the objects here. In this book the name refers only to the
Bernoulli-distributed error indicators that the framework attaches to each
query.
