\section{What This Chapter Is For}
\label{sec:what-this-chapter-is-for}

The fifteen chapters that came before this one have repeatedly made a
particular promise. When the prose ran up against a phenomenon that
did not fit the Bernoulli axioms cleanly, the discussion was held over
to ``a later chapter on what the framework does not cover.'' This is
that chapter. The job of \S16.1 is to list those held-over questions,
route each one to the section that takes it up, and state the posture
under which the chapter proceeds.

The decision to defer rather than hedge was deliberate. Each earlier
chapter could have qualified its results with footnotes about the
cases where Axiom 1 or Axiom 2 fails, but the qualifications would
have distracted from the affirmative content. Better to write the
positive theorems clearly in chapters 1 through 15 and collect the
negative space here.

\subsection*{The deferrals being closed}

Five specific deferrals seeded earlier in the book are addressed in
this chapter.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Chapter 1 \S1.4 (the sampling diagnostic) ended with an open
    question: \emph{what if the error rates vary across samples
    rather than being a fixed property of the channel?} The chapter
    deliberately stopped short of the time-varying case because the
    iid baseline had not yet been established. The time-varying case
    is taken up in \Cref{sec:varying-rates}.

  \item Chapter 1 exercise 10, refined in the chapter 3 notebook on
    Miller-Rabin, asked: \emph{what happens to the $k$-round bound
    when the witnesses are not independent?} The notebook acknowledged
    that the analysis assumed independent witnesses and noted that
    real composites can have correlation structure in their strong-liar
    sets. That correlation case is the subject of
    \Cref{sec:correlated-noise}.

  \item Chapter 5 \S5.4 (one-sided sets) introduced the perfect-filter
    line and observed in passing that \emph{the input distribution
    might adapt to the filter's behavior over time}, for example a
    spam classifier whose adversary updates strategies based on which
    messages got blocked. That adaptive-input case is handled in
    \Cref{sec:adversarial-inputs}.

  \item Chapter 10 \S10.5 (sketch composition) raised the case of an
    adversarial data stream: an attacker who chooses the next item
    conditional on the sketch's prior state. The sketch composition
    theorem of that chapter assumed iid input; the adversarial case
    is taken up in \Cref{sec:adversarial-inputs}.

  \item Chapter 13 exercise 11 (boundary algebra) asked when
    structures fail to fit the algebra of approximate types. Some
    boundary cases there were genuinely adversarial; others were
    structural mismatches in which Axiom 1 or Axiom 2 fails despite
    the input being natural. These boundary cases are sorted between
    \Cref{sec:adversarial-inputs} and
    \Cref{sec:where-framework-helps}.
\end{itemize}

The reader who tracked the open questions through the prior chapters
should find each one closed below.

\subsection*{Three failure modes}

The deferrals fall into three failure modes, each named explicitly in
this chapter and addressed in its own section.

\textbf{Varying rates over time (\Cref{sec:varying-rates}).} The
error rate $\fprate$ (or $\fnrate$) is not a stable property of the
channel. It drifts with query index, perhaps because the underlying
data structure changes (a Bloom filter being filled past capacity),
perhaps because the environment changes (a sensor's noise grows with
temperature), perhaps because the calibration data was not
representative of deployment data (classifier drift). This violates
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom 2): the per-block rates are no
longer stable across queries. When Axiom 2 fails, the channel matrix
$Q$ becomes a sequence $(Q_t)$ rather than a single $Q$, and the iid
concentration tools of chapter 11 must be replaced with their
martingale counterparts.

\textbf{Correlated noise across queries (\Cref{sec:correlated-noise}).}
Two queries to the same approximate structure share error sources.
The same query repeated is fully deterministic; two queries to
overlapping bit positions of a Bloom filter are positively correlated;
sequential draws from an online learning system have a temporal
dependence structure. This violates \Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom 1):
the error events at distinct inputs are no longer mutually independent.
When Axiom 1 fails, the factored joint distribution behind every
multi-query formula in the book becomes an inequality, and the
composition theorem of chapter 3 \S3.3 picks up correction terms.

\textbf{Adversarial inputs (\Cref{sec:adversarial-inputs}).} The
query stream is not iid; the queries are chosen by an adversary, or
by an adaptive learner, to expose the worst-case behavior. Both
axioms can break here. The adversary's queries are not independent
(each is conditional on the responses to earlier queries, breaking
Axiom 1's independence across distinct inputs), and the worst-case
query distribution sits at the tail of whatever per-block rate
distribution the channel admits (breaking the Axiom 2 stability that
licensed a single $\fprate$ value to begin with). The right tools
here are worst-case bounds, cryptographic hash families, and
oblivious-computing primitives, not concentration inequalities.

\subsection*{Posture}

The chapter is written under a deliberate honesty discipline. Where
the Bernoulli framework genuinely does not apply, the chapter says
so, names the violated axiom, and points to the analytical tool that
replaces the broken machinery. The framework is not stretched to
cover cases where its axioms manifestly fail.

\Cref{sec:where-framework-helps} pushes back against pure
relinquishment by catalogueing the cases where the framework's
geometric and structural intuitions remain useful even when the
strict axioms fail. The perfect-filter line of chapter 6 \S6.4 still
classifies systems by their rate-plane position when the rates are
time-varying. The channel-matrix view of chapter 9 \S9.2 still
applies query by query when the matrix is not stable across queries.
The PPV/NPV machinery of chapter 7 \S7.4 still holds at any single
query even when the prevalence drifts. These are partial successes,
not full applications, and they are catalogued honestly rather than
oversold.
