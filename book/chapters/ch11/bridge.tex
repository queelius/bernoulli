\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch11-bridge}

The four prior sections of this chapter trace one figure from four
angles. \Cref{sec:miller-rabin-formal} closes the chapter~3 \S3.4
deferral by restating the Miller-Rabin witness predicate as a formal
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance with $\fnrate = 0$ and
$\fprate \le 1/4$ per round (\Cref{thm:miller-rabin-formal}), which
makes the per-round bound a theorem with citations to Rabin (1980)
rather than an unmotivated assertion. \Cref{sec:las-vegas-vs-monte-carlo}
separates that contract from its sibling: Las Vegas algorithms
(\Cref{def:las-vegas}) trade unbounded worst-case runtime for a
deterministic output, while Monte Carlo algorithms
(\Cref{def:monte-carlo}) trade a bounded error rate for a fixed
runtime, and the Monte Carlo regime is exactly the regime in which
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ analysis applies. \Cref{sec:rp-bpp} carves up
the Monte Carlo regime into the classical complexity classes RP, coRP,
ZPP, and BPP, and reads each one as a location on the chapter~6 rate
plane: RP (\Cref{def:rp}) sits on the FNR-zero axis of the perfect
filter line of \Cref{def:perfect-filter-line}, coRP on the FPR-zero
axis, ZPP at the origin, BPP in the bounded interior. The one-sided
versus two-sided distinction of \Cref{sec:one-sided-two-sided} is the
same distinction that separates RP from BPP. \Cref{sec:sample-complexity}
closes the arc by quantifying how many independent rounds drive the
chapter~4 Axiom~1 composition bound below a target $\delta$: the
multiplicative Chernoff bound (\Cref{thm:chernoff}) gives the tight
$k = \Theta(\log(1/\delta))$ count that makes randomized algorithms
practical at all, and the same machinery sharpens the variance-based
confidence interval of the chapter~7 binomial estimator
(\Cref{sec:binomial-estimator}) from polynomial to exponential decay.
Five sections, one $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$: a per-round bound, a
runtime contract, a location on the rate plane, a sample budget.

The chapter~11 picture extends in two directions before Part~III
closes. \Cref{ch:approximate-relations} lifts the analysis from
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ and the approximate functions of chapter~9 to
$\Bernoulli{R}$ for binary relations $R \subseteq A \times B$. A
random approximate relation is what is needed once the question stops
being ``does $x$ belong to $S$'' and starts being ``do $x$ and $y$
stand in some relation,'' which is the natural setting for join
queries, equality predicates over pairs, and multi-table membership
tests. The randomized-algorithm vocabulary of the present chapter
carries over without modification: a Monte Carlo relation-test
algorithm is a $\Bernoulli{R}$ instance with a known per-pair error
rate, and amplification by repetition is the chapter~3 composition
theorem specialized to the corresponding two-state channel.

\Cref{ch:algebra-types} closes Part~III by giving the type-driven
algebra under which the chapter~10 sketches, the present chapter's
randomized algorithms, and the chapter~12 relations are all instances
of a small set of constructors over $\Bernoulli{T}$. Sketches over
product types $\Bernoulli{T \times U}$, sketches over sum types
$\Bernoulli{T + U}$, sketches over list types $\Bernoulli{L\,T}$, and
Monte Carlo decision procedures as $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ all
compose by structure once the type constructors are fixed. The
present chapter is the algorithmic instance of that algebra; the next
two chapters supply the relational instance and the algebraic
vocabulary that unifies them.
