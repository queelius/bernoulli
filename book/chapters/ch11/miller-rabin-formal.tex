\section{Miller-Rabin Formal}
\label{sec:miller-rabin-formal}

Chapter~3 \S3.4 (\Cref{sec:miller-rabin}) walked through the Miller-Rabin
primality test\index{Miller--Rabin test} as a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance, stating the per-round
bound $\fprate \le 1/4$ as a fact and applying it. This section closes that
deferral, restating the walkthrough as one formal theorem with the per-round
bound credited to Rabin and the $k$-round bound derived from the composition
theorem.

Fix an odd $n > 3$, write $n - 1 = 2^r d$ with $d$ odd, and let $W_n(a)$ be the
single-round witness test on $a \in \{2, \ldots, n-2\}$, returning $\top$
(\textsc{probably-prime}) or $\bot$ (\textsc{composite}), with the predicate
``$n$ is prime.''

\begin{theorem}[Miller-Rabin as a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$]
\label{thm:miller-rabin-formal}
For $a$ drawn uniformly from $\{2, \ldots, n-2\}$, $W_n(a)$ is a
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance (\Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}) with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mr-formal-fnr}
  \fnrate = 0
\end{equation}
for every $n$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mr-formal-fpr}
  \fprate \le \tfrac14
\end{equation}
for every odd composite $n > 3$. If $k$ independent witnesses are drawn with
replacement and the test declares \textsc{probably-prime} only when every round
does, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mr-formal-k-round}
  \fnrate^{(k)} = 0
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \fprate^{(k)} \le \left(\tfrac14\right)^{\!k}
\end{equation}
for every odd composite $n > 3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Zero false-negative rate.} If $n$ is prime, Fermat's little theorem gives
$a^{n-1} \equiv 1$, and in the field $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ the only square
roots of $1$ are $\pm 1$, so tracing $a^d, a^{2d}, \ldots, a^{2^r d} = 1$
backwards reaches $\pm 1$ and the witness test passes. Every witness yields
$\top$, so $\fnrate = 0$.

\emph{Per-round bound.} For odd composite $n$, let $L_n = \{a \in \{2, \ldots,
n-2\} : W_n(a) = \top\}$ be the strong-liar set. Rabin's theorem
\parencite{rabin-1980}, sharpening the conditional analysis of
\textcite{miller-1976}, gives $|L_n| \le (n-3)/4$ via the Chinese remainder
structure of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. With $a$ uniform over the pool of size
$n - 3$, $\fprate = |L_n|/(n-3) \le 1/4$. (A base outside $L_n$ is a
\emph{witness}\index{witness (Miller--Rabin)} to compositeness.)

\emph{The $k$-round bound.} The bases $a_1, \ldots, a_k$ are independent uniform
draws, so the events $\{a_i \in L_n\}$ are independent; this is independence
across stages\index{independence!across stages}
(\Cref{rem:composition-independence}), supplied by the sampling, and it holds
whether or not a base repeats. The $k$-round test is the $k$-fold AND of
$W_n(a_1), \ldots, W_n(a_k)$, an FNR-zero chain with $\fnrate_i = 0$ and
$\fprate_i \le 1/4$, and every round sees the same latent truth\index{latent assignment} (the fixed $n$
is prime or composite), the regime in which the corner products of chapter~3
are exact. By the composition theorem\index{composition theorem} (\Cref{thm:composition}) the
false-negative rate is $\fnrate^{(k)} = 1 - \prod_i(1 - \fnrate_i) = 0$, and the
dual all-error direction gives the false-positive rate as the product (a
composite survives only if every round is fooled),
\[
  \fprate^{(k)} = \prod_{i=1}^{k} \fprate_i \le \left(\tfrac14\right)^{\!k},
\]
uniform over odd composite $n > 3$.
\end{proof}

The division of labor is the one chapter~3 promised: number theory supplies the
strong-liar bound, the framework supplies the rest. The two error directions
take the two faces of the composition theorem, $1 - \prod(1 - \omega_i)$ for the
monotone false-negative direction and $\prod \varepsilon_i$ for the all-error
false-positive direction, exactly as \S3.3 distinguishes them. Choosing $k$ to
drive $\fprate^{(k)}$ below a target $\delta$ is the sample-complexity question
of \Cref{sec:sample-complexity}.
