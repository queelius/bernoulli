\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch11}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
The witness test and its conditional deterministic form are
\textcite{miller-1976}; the unconditional strong-liar bound $\fprate \le 1/4$
behind \Cref{thm:miller-rabin-formal} is \textcite{rabin-1980}, and
\textcite{agrawal-kayal-saxena-2004} later placed primality in P. The classes RP,
coRP, ZPP, and BPP, their one-sided and two-sided error conditions, and ZPP $=$
RP $\cap$ coRP are \textcite{gill-1977}. The exponential tail bounds are
\textcite{chernoff-1952} and \textcite{hoeffding-1963}; the forms used here, and
the majority-vote amplification of BPP, follow \textcite[Ch.~4]{mitzenmacher-upfal},
with \textcite{motwani-raghavan} the standard treatment of Las Vegas and Monte
Carlo algorithms and \textcite[Ch.~31]{cormen-clrs} for the number theory. The
contraction algorithm is \textcite{karger-1993}. Amplitude amplification is
\textcite{grover-1996} for search and \textcite{brassard-2002} in general.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
Nothing in the chapter is a new theorem. It restates the classical taxonomy in
the rate coordinates of the rest of the book (RP and coRP on the two axes of the
perfect filter line, BPP in the interior), with two clarifications that matter
for the framework: the rates of a randomized algorithm are per input, so the
class definitions supply worst-case pairs rather than constant rates, and the
independence that amplification uses is independence across runs on one input,
not Axiom~1 across elements. The comparison of OR-amplification with amplitude
amplification is an exposition of the $1/p$ versus $1/\sqrt{p}$ scaling.

\paragraph{Left out.}
Derandomization, the relations among BPP, the polynomial hierarchy, and BQP,
pseudorandom generators, and the number theory of the strong-liar bound, which
is cited rather than proved.
