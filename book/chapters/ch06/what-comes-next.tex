\section{What Comes Next}
\label{sec:ch6-what-comes-next}

The chapter fixed the algebra of set operations: closed-form block rates for
union, intersection, complement, and difference (\Cref{sec:union-of-sets},
\Cref{sec:other-set-ops}), with the caveat that a composed set is higher
order and its summary pair does not compose further
(\Cref{rem:composing-composed}); the classification of which compositions
leave the perfect filter line (\Cref{sec:cancellation}); and the operations
under which each axis of the line is closed (\Cref{sec:perfect-filter-line}). Two questions remain for
the rest of Part~II: how good an approximate set is against a workload, and
how small it can be made. \Cref{ch:classification-measures} turns the per-block
rates into the workload-dependent measures practitioners report, positive and
negative predictive value, $F_1$, recall and precision, feeding the
composition rates directly into them (a union of FNR-zero filters is
FNR-zero by \Cref{prop:fnr-zero-closure}, so its PPV follows with no extra
work), and it retires the interval-estimator deferral of
\Cref{sec:sampling-rates} with the Wilson, Clopper-Pearson, and Jeffreys
constructions. \Cref{ch:information-bounds} gives the rates a space-cost
analog, the classical floor of $-\log_2 \fprate$ bits per element for an
FNR-zero set and its two-sided form, against which the closure results bound
what a composition can cost; \Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} then constructs FNR-zero structures near
the floor, the perfect hash filter and the Bernoulli Hash Function, the
latter meeting it in space at an exponential construction cost.
