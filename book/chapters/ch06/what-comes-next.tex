\section{What Comes Next}
\label{sec:ch6-what-comes-next}

The chapter has fixed the algebra. \Cref{sec:union-of-sets} and
\Cref{sec:other-set-ops} gave closed-form rates for union, intersection,
complement, and difference of approximate sets. \Cref{sec:cancellation}
classified the compositions that escape the perfect filter line.
\Cref{sec:perfect-filter-line} collected the closure results into a
single subalgebra statement and named the two halves of the line.
What we have not yet done is connect those rates to the two questions
that motivate the rest of Part~II: how good is an approximate set when
measured against a workload, and how small can one be made? The next
two chapters take up each question in turn.

\subsection*{Chapter 7: rates feed into classification measures}

\Cref{ch:classification-measures} takes the per-block rates
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$ this chapter computed and turns them into the
workload-dependent quality measures practitioners actually report:
positive predictive value, negative predictive value, $F_1$, and the
recall-precision pair. Each is a function of $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ and
the prevalence of in-set elements in the query stream. The composition
formulas of this chapter feed those derivations directly. A union of
two FNR-zero Bloom filters at output FPR $\fprate_{\cup}$, by
\Cref{prop:fnr-zero-closure}, is itself FNR-zero; chapter~7's PPV
expression in $\fprate_{\cup}$ and the workload prevalence then gives
the PPV for the composed structure with no additional work.

Chapter~7 also retires the deferral \Cref{sec:sampling-rates} flagged in
Part~I. The Wald confidence interval for a binomial rate was a working
placeholder, and the chapter introduces the Wilson, Clopper-Pearson, and
Jeffreys alternatives that handle the boundary at $\fprate = 0$ or
$\fprate = 1$ properly. The estimator vocabulary chapter~1 left
incomplete is finished there.

\subsection*{Chapter 8: rates have a space-cost analog}

\Cref{ch:information-bounds} bounds how compact an approximate set can
be. Every operation of this chapter has a space-cost analog: the entropy
of an FNR-zero set with false-positive rate $\fprate$ is
$-\log_2 \fprate$ bits per stored element, the entropy of a composed
structure is the entropy of its rate point, and the chapter~6 closure
results on the FNR-zero axis then bound the bits per element a
composition can possibly cost. The floor governs compression along the
axis; \Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} achieves it constructively with
the Bernoulli Hash Function, closing the standard Bloom filter's
$\approx 44\%$ overhead. The composition theorem of chapter~3 returns
in chapter~8 dressed as the bit-budget arithmetic for chained
approximate sets, and the chapter~6 formulas this section closes are the
operations the bit budget applies to.

\subsection*{Closing}

The bibliographic notes that follow name the antecedents of the
set-operation framework: the Bernoulli composition manuscript that
collected the closed-form rates, Mitzenmacher and Upfal for the survey
context of Bloom-filter union and intersection, and the chapter~3
treatment of De~Morgan under noise that the complement-swap result
specializes. Chapter~7 then opens the second half of Part~II.
