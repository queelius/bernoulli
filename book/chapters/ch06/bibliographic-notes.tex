\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch6}

\paragraph{The composition framework.}
The rate-propagation formulas of \Cref{sec:union-of-sets,sec:other-set-ops}
are the set-level instances of the framework of
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-composition}, which proves the propagation laws for
the full algebra, union, intersection, complement, difference, and symmetric
difference, under independence of the constituent $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
outputs, and catalogues which compound operations preserve one-sidedness. That
manuscript carries the partition-weighted forms used here for the
off-axis rates; this chapter derives each rule from the predicate algebra of
\Cref{sec:predicate-algebra} and the gate rules of \Cref{sec:or-not} and
connects it to its Boolean-algebra ancestor.

\paragraph{Bloom filter union and intersection.}
That two Bloom filters of equal length and shared hash family admit a bitwise
OR and AND of their arrays is folklore, but what the result \emph{computes},
and at what rate, is treated in \textcite[Ch.~5]{mitzenmacher-upfal} and
surveyed in \textcite{broder-mitzenmacher-2004}. The union case is clean: the
bitwise OR is exactly a filter for $A \cup B$ with $\fnrate = 0$ preserved.
The intersection case is subtle: the bitwise AND does not in general produce a
filter for $A \cap B$, admitting ghost members absent from both inputs.
\Cref{sec:other-set-ops} sidesteps the trap by treating intersection at the
predicate level, which gives the correct rate without committing to a
representation.

\paragraph{De~Morgan under noise.}
The complement-swap of \Cref{prop:complement-swap}, that complementing an
approximate set exchanges its two rates, is the set-level shadow of the NOT
rule of \Cref{sec:or-not}. Classical De~Morgan, $\complement(A \cup B) =
\complement A \cap \complement B$, still holds at the set level, but the two
sides traverse the rate plane by different paths: one touches the FNR-zero
axis when both inputs are FNR-zero, the other the FPR-zero axis after the
swap. ``Different paths, same destination'' is what makes the
perfect-filter-line classification of \Cref{sec:perfect-filter-line}
informative about which compound operations stay one-sided and which are
forced into the interior.

\paragraph{Perfect-filter lineage.}
The constructive side of building a set whose rates sit exactly on the
FPR-zero axis has roots in the perfect-hashing literature opened by
\textcite{carter-wegman-1979} and is taken up in
\Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters}, which builds on
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-hash-function} for the space-optimal construction.
The Bloom filter of \textcite{bloomfilter} occupies the opposite axis and has
been the canonical one-sided approximate set since 1970; its behavior under
union and intersection is what this chapter's closure analysis formalizes.
Chapter~6 sits between the predicate algebra of chapter~5 and the constructive
machinery of chapter~15: the algebra of operations, divorced from any
representation.
