\section{Intersection, Complement, Difference}
\label{sec:other-set-ops}

The union of \Cref{sec:union-of-sets} lifts the OR gate to sets; the
remaining Boolean operations lift the same way. Each composed predicate is a
Boolean combination of the operand predicates\index{AND gate}, and its rates follow from the
gate analysis of \Cref{ch:boolean-algebra} and cross-set independence. The
recurring structure of \Cref{prop:union-fpr-fnr} persists: one rate is a
clean product over a homogeneous truth-region, the other is a
partition-weighted sum over regions where the operands play different roles.

\subsection*{Intersection}

\begin{definition}[Intersection of approximate sets]
\label{def:intersection-of-sets}
For $\ASet{A}, \ASet{B}$ over $\Universe$, the \emph{intersection}
$\ASet{A} \cap \ASet{B}$ has predicate $(\ASet{A} \cap \ASet{B})(x) =
\ASet{A}(x) \land \ASet{B}(x)$, approximating $A \cap B$\index{intersection}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Intersection rate formulas]
\label{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr}
Under the cross-set independence of \Cref{prop:union-fpr-fnr}, the
intersection has false-negative rate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intersection-fnr}
  \fnrate_\cap \;=\; 1 - (1 - \fnrate_A)(1 - \fnrate_B),
\end{equation}
and, for a query distribution drawing $x$ from the negatives of $A \cap B$
in the regions $A \setminus B$, $B \setminus A$,
$\Universe \setminus (A \cup B)$ with weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$,
false-positive rate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intersection-fpr}
  \fprate_\cap
  \;=\; w_1 (1 - \fnrate_A)\fprate_B
        + w_2 \fprate_A (1 - \fnrate_B)
        + w_3 \fprate_A \fprate_B.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x \in A \cap B$, the intersection reports $\bot$ (a false negative) iff
at least one operand misses; the complementary joint event factors by
independence, giving \eqref{eq:intersection-fnr}, the De~Morgan
dual\index{De Morgan duality} of the union FPR \eqref{eq:union-fpr}. A false positive needs both operands to
report $\top$ at an $x \notin A \cap B$, and the per-region probability
depends on the operands' truth there: on $A \setminus B$ the first correctly
accepts a member while the second false-positives, $(1 - \fnrate_A)\fprate_B$;
on $B \setminus A$ symmetrically $\fprate_A(1 - \fnrate_B)$; on
$\Universe \setminus (A \cup B)$ both false-positive, $\fprate_A \fprate_B$.
Total probability over the three regions gives \eqref{eq:intersection-fpr}.
\end{proof}

The FPR is the dual of the union FNR \eqref{eq:union-fnr}, with the same
three-region structure: the intersection of two approximate sets is a
higher-order Bernoulli set, and \eqref{eq:intersection-fpr} is its marginal
FPR. The bare product $\fprate_A \fprate_B$ is only the third term, the
both-outside region. It is exact when the two operands approximate the
\emph{same} objective set ($A = B$, so $A \setminus B = B \setminus A =
\varnothing$ and $w_1 = w_2 = 0$), and it is the limit when the universe
dwarfs both sets ($w_3 \to 1$). Outside those regimes the first two terms
dominate, and they are \emph{linear} in a single operand rate, not
quadratic: a query landing in $A \setminus B$ false-positives at rate
$\fprate_B$ alone, because $\ASet{A}$ accepts the genuine member. The widely
quoted ``intersection multiplies false-positive rates'' holds for filters of
one set: intersecting $k$ independent FNR-zero filters of a common $A$ drives
the FPR to $\prod_i \fprate_i \to 0$, since on the dominant outside region a
negative survives only if every filter false-positives.

\subsection*{Complement}

\begin{definition}[Complement of an approximate set]
\label{def:complement-of-set}
The \emph{complement}\index{complement} $\complement \ASet{A}$ has predicate
$(\complement \ASet{A})(x) = \lnot \ASet{A}(x)$, approximating
$\complement A = \Universe \setminus A$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Complement swaps the rates]
\label{prop:complement-swap}
If $\ASet{A}$ has rates $(\fprate_A, \fnrate_A)$, then $\complement \ASet{A}$
has rates
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complement-swap}
  \fprate_\complement = \fnrate_A, \qquad \fnrate_\complement = \fprate_A.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A false positive of $\complement \ASet{A}$ is the event $x \in A$ (a negative
of $\complement A$) with $\ASet{A}(x) = \bot$, a false negative of $\ASet{A}$
at rate $\fnrate_A$. A false negative is $x \notin A$ with $\ASet{A}(x) =
\top$, a false positive of $\ASet{A}$ at rate $\fprate_A$. The rates swap.
\end{proof}

This is the set-level reading of NOT-under-noise\index{NOT gate} from \Cref{sec:or-not}.
Complement is an involution exchanging the two coordinate axes of the rate
plane; unlike intersection and difference, it needs no partition, because NOT
acts pointwise and reclassifies every element by the same rule.

\subsection*{Difference}

\begin{definition}[Difference of approximate sets]
\label{def:difference-of-sets}
The \emph{difference}\index{difference (set)} $\ASet{A} \setminus \ASet{B} = \ASet{A} \cap
\complement \ASet{B}$ has predicate $\ASet{A}(x) \land \lnot \ASet{B}(x)$,
approximating $A \setminus B = A \cap \complement B$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Difference rate formulas]
\label{prop:difference-fpr-fnr}
Under the same independence, the difference has false-negative rate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:difference-fnr}
  \fnrate_\setminus \;=\; 1 - (1 - \fnrate_A)(1 - \fprate_B),
\end{equation}
and, over the negatives of $A \setminus B$ in the regions $A \cap B$,
$\Universe \setminus (A \cup B)$, $B \setminus A$ with weights
$w_1, w_2, w_3$, false-positive rate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:difference-fpr}
  \fprate_\setminus
  \;=\; w_1 (1 - \fnrate_A)\fnrate_B
        + w_2 \fprate_A (1 - \fprate_B)
        + w_3 \fprate_A \fnrate_B.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\ASet{A} \setminus \ASet{B} = \ASet{A} \cap \complement \ASet{B}$ and
apply \Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr} with $\complement \ASet{B}$ carrying
rates $(\fnrate_B, \fprate_B)$ by \Cref{prop:complement-swap}. The FNR is
$1 - (1 - \fnrate_A)(1 - \fprate_B)$, which is \eqref{eq:difference-fnr}. The
FPR substitutes the swapped second-operand rates into the three-region sum,
with the regions relabeled for the objective set $A \cap \complement B$: on
$A \cap B$, $\ASet{A}$ accepts and $\complement \ASet{B}$ false-positives
(rate $\fnrate_B$); on $\Universe \setminus (A \cup B)$, $\ASet{A}$
false-positives and $\complement \ASet{B}$ accepts (rate $1 - \fprate_B$); on
$B \setminus A$, $\ASet{A}$ false-positives and $\complement \ASet{B}$
false-positives (rate $\fnrate_B$). This is \eqref{eq:difference-fpr}.
\end{proof}

Both operands' \emph{both} rates appear in the difference. The consequence is
sharp and is the reason the difference matters for the perfect filter line:
even an FNR-zero $\ASet{A}$ inherits false negatives at rate $\fprate_B$,
because a member of $A \setminus B$ that $\ASet{B}$ false-positives is wrongly
excluded; and even FNR-zero operands leave a positive difference FPR through
the $w_2 \fprate_A(1 - \fprate_B)$ term, because a point outside both sets
that $\ASet{A}$ false-positives passes the difference (the complemented
$\ASet{B}$ correctly accepts it). The difference of two one-sided sets is
therefore two-sided, a fact the worked example makes concrete and
\Cref{sec:cancellation} reads as an escape from the line.

\subsection*{Worked example}

Take two FNR-zero Bloom filters with $\fprate_A = 0.01$, $\fnrate_A = 0$ and
$\fprate_B = 0.02$, $\fnrate_B = 0$, in a universe large enough that the
region outside both sets dominates each partition ($w_3 \to 1$ for the
intersection, $w_2 \to 1$ for the difference).

\emph{Intersection.} The FNR is $1 - (1 - 0)(1 - 0) = 0$: the intersection of
two FNR-zero sets stays FNR-zero. The FPR is
\eqref{eq:intersection-fpr}, dominated by the outside-both term,
$\fprate_\cap \approx \fprate_A \fprate_B = 0.01 \times 0.02 = 0.0002$, an
order of magnitude below either operand. (Were the two sets distinct enough
that a query could land in $A \setminus B$ or $B \setminus A$, the linear
terms $w_1 \fprate_B + w_2 \fprate_A$ would lift the rate well above the
product.) Intersection of same-set filters drives the FPR down; union
inflates it; the two are exact duals.

\emph{Complement.} $\complement \ASet{A}$ has rates $(\fnrate_A, \fprate_A) =
(0, 0.01)$: the FNR-zero false-positive machine becomes an FPR-zero
false-negative machine, moved from the horizontal axis to the vertical one.

\emph{Difference.} Here the products mislead. Naively pairing $\fprate_A$
with $\fnrate_B = 0$ would suggest $\fprate_\setminus = 0$; but that is only
the $w_3$ term of \eqref{eq:difference-fpr}. The dominant $w_2$ term survives,
\[
  \fprate_\setminus \approx \fprate_A (1 - \fprate_B)
                    = 0.01 \times 0.98 = 0.0098,
  \qquad
  \fnrate_\setminus = 1 - (1 - 0)(1 - 0.02) = 0.02.
\]
The difference of two FNR-zero Bloom filters is \emph{two-sided}, at
approximately $(0.0098, 0.02)$, not the FPR-zero point $(0, 0.02)$ the bare
product would give. Both failure modes are real: $\ASet{B}$'s false positives
become wrongful exclusions (the FNR $= \fprate_B$), and $\ASet{A}$'s false
positives on points outside both sets survive the difference (the FPR
$\approx \fprate_A$). This two-sidedness is the cancellation phenomenon
\Cref{sec:cancellation} scrutinizes.

\subsection*{A caveat on bit-AND of Bloom filters}

\Cref{def:intersection-of-sets} is a predicate operation: query both filters
and AND the verdicts. For two Bloom filters of equal length built with the
same hash family, the bitwise AND of the two arrays answers every query
exactly as the predicate AND does, since all $k$ probed bits are set in the
AND array iff they are set in both arrays. It is nevertheless not a Bloom
filter for $A \cap B$: a bit set in one array by an element of $A \setminus
B$ and in the other by an element of $B \setminus A$ survives the AND, so the
AND array contains, and is generally fuller than, the array that inserting
$A \cap B$ directly would produce, and its false-positive rate is
correspondingly higher. The shared hash family also violates the cross-set
independence that \Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr} assumes
(\Cref{rem:cross-set-independence}), so the proposition describes
independently hashed operands, not the bitwise construction; the chapter
notebook measures the discrepancy.
