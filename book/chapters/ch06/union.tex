\section{Union of Approximate Sets}
\label{sec:union-of-sets}

Chapter~5 framed an approximate set as a noisy membership predicate with
two per-block rates $(\fprate, \fnrate)$. The first compositional step asks
what the union of two such sets is and what rates it inherits. The answer is
the composition theorem of \Cref{thm:composition} applied to the OR gate of
\Cref{sec:or-not}, lifted from the per-bit level to the per-set level.

\begin{definition}[Union of approximate sets]
\label{def:union-of-sets}
For approximate sets $\ASet{A}, \ASet{B} : \Universe \to
\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ over a common universe, their \emph{union}
$\ASet{A} \cup \ASet{B}$ is the approximate set whose predicate is the
Boolean OR of the operand predicates, $(\ASet{A} \cup \ASet{B})(x) =
\ASet{A}(x) \lor \ASet{B}(x)$, approximating the objective set $A \cup B$.
\end{definition}

To evaluate the union at $x$, query both operands and OR the verdicts;
the rates follow from the operand rates and the OR-gate analysis of
\Cref{ch:boolean-algebra}.

\begin{proposition}[Union rate formulas]
\label{prop:union-fpr-fnr}
Let $\ASet{A}, \ASet{B}$ approximate $A, B \subseteq \Universe$ with rates
$(\fprate_A, \fnrate_A)$, $(\fprate_B, \fnrate_B)$, and assume the operand
error events at each query point are mutually independent. Then the union
has false-positive rate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:union-fpr}
  \fprate_\cup \;=\; 1 - (1 - \fprate_A)(1 - \fprate_B),
\end{equation}
and, for a query distribution drawing $x$ from the regions $A \setminus B$,
$B \setminus A$, $A \cap B$ of $A \cup B$ with weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$,
false-negative rate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:union-fnr}
  \fnrate_\cup
  \;=\; w_1 \fnrate_A (1 - \fprate_B)
        + w_2 \fnrate_B (1 - \fprate_A)
        + w_3 \fnrate_A \fnrate_B.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $x \notin A \cup B$, the union reports $\top$ iff at least one operand
false-positives; the complementary event factors by independence, giving
$\fprate_\cup = 1 - (1 - \fprate_A)(1 - \fprate_B)$, the same for every
negative of $A \cup B$. A false negative needs both operands to report
$\bot$, and the per-region probability depends on the operands' truth
there: on $A \setminus B$ the first must miss a member while the second
correctly rejects, $\fnrate_A(1 - \fprate_B)$; on $B \setminus A$
symmetrically $\fnrate_B(1 - \fprate_A)$; on $A \cap B$ both must miss,
$\fnrate_A \fnrate_B$. The law of total probability over the three regions
gives \eqref{eq:union-fnr}.
\end{proof}

The asymmetry between the two rates is structural and recurs for every
operation in this chapter. The union FPR is a closed-form polynomial in the
operand FPRs alone, requiring no prior, because every negative of $A \cup B$
lies in the single region $\Universe \setminus (A \cup B)$. The union FNR
mixes three per-region rates, because the positives of $A \cup B$ split into
three blocks where the operands play different roles; the composed set is
higher-order, and \eqref{eq:union-fnr} reports its marginal FNR over a
query distribution. When the operands are FNR-zero ($\fnrate_A = \fnrate_B =
0$) every term vanishes and the union stays FNR-zero, the first closure fact
of \Cref{sec:perfect-filter-line}.

\paragraph{Two realizations on Bloom filters.}
\Cref{def:union-of-sets} is a predicate-level operation, \emph{predicate-OR}:
keep both filters, query each, OR the outputs; its FPR is
\eqref{eq:union-fpr}. When both operands are Bloom filters with the same
parameters and hash family, a tempting shortcut is \emph{bit-OR-Bloom}: OR
the two bit arrays into one bitmap and query it once. This is a different
operation. Bit-OR-Bloom is exactly a Bloom filter holding the union of the
inserted items, so its rate is the chapter-5 formula at the combined count,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bit-or-bloom-fpr}
  \fprate_{\text{bit-OR}} \;\approx\; \bigl(1 - e^{-k(n_A + n_B)/m}\bigr)^k,
\end{equation}
materially larger than \eqref{eq:union-fpr}: at $m = 5000$, $k = 7$,
$n_A = n_B = 500$ the operand rate is $\fprate \approx 0.0082$, predicate-OR
gives $\approx 0.0163$, and bit-OR-Bloom gives $\approx 0.137$. Both
preserve FNR-zero, but they ask different questions. Predicate-OR requires
one filter to have \emph{all} $k$ bits set; bit-OR-Bloom requires only that
at each queried position \emph{some} filter's bit is set, with the
responsible filter free to vary by position, so it is the more permissive
operation and carries the larger FPR. Predicate-OR is the union of
\Cref{def:union-of-sets}; bit-OR-Bloom is a representation choice that trades
a higher FPR for one bitmap and one lookup, and it further requires a shared
hash family.

For two FNR-zero Bloom filters at $\fprate_A = 0.01$, $\fprate_B = 0.02$ the
predicate-OR union has $\fprate_\cup = 1 - (1 - 0.01)(1 - 0.02) = 0.0298$ and
$\fnrate_\cup = 0$. The second-order term $\fprate_A \fprate_B = 0.0002$ is
small, so for small rates the union FPR is nearly additive,
$\fprate_\cup \approx \fprate_A + \fprate_B$, bracketed by
$\max(\fprate_A, \fprate_B) \le \fprate_\cup \le \fprate_A + \fprate_B$.

\eqref{eq:union-fpr} is the composition theorem of \Cref{thm:composition}
applied to the OR gate with $\eta_i = \fprate_i$, the lift to sets that
\Cref{sec:bridge-to-sets} previewed: the same Boolean operation, the same
algebra, with $\fprate_i$ reread from a per-bit crossover to a per-block set
rate. The $n$-ary union is the same theorem folded, $\fprate_\cup = 1 -
\prod_i (1 - \fprate_i)$, and since OR is associative and commutative with
identity $\ASet{\EmptySet}$, the union is a monoidal fold over approximate
sets.

\begin{remark}[Cross-set independence]
\label{rem:cross-set-independence}
\Cref{prop:union-fpr-fnr} assumes the operand error events are independent
at each point, and the conclusion is exactly as good as that assumption. Two
Bloom filters built on the \emph{same} hash family and bit array over the
same stored set are not independent but identical, $\ASet{A}(x) =
\ASet{B}(x)$ everywhere, so their predicate-OR is a single filter with FPR
$\fprate$, not the $1 - (1 - \fprate)^2 = 2\fprate - \fprate^2$ the formula
predicts; \eqref{eq:union-fpr} over-states the rate. Independence is what the
product of marginals encodes, and constructions that share randomness across
operands fall outside it, the cross-query analog of the within-query
dependence \Cref{rem:bloom-independence} flagged for a single filter. This is
why \Cref{def:union-of-sets} keeps the operands as separate objects.
\end{remark}
