\section{The Perfect Filter Line}
\label{sec:perfect-filter-line}

\Cref{sec:one-sided-two-sided} named the perfect filter line as the union of
the two coordinate axes of the rate plane. The composition formulas of this
chapter, with the cancellation classification of \Cref{sec:cancellation}, let
us read the line as an algebraic object: a distinguished subset of $[0,1]^2$
that some operations preserve, some map to itself, and some exit.

\begin{definition}[The perfect filter line]
\label{def:perfect-filter-line}
The \emph{perfect filter line} is
$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\fnrate} \cup \mathcal{L}_{\fprate}$, where the
\emph{FNR-zero axis} is $\mathcal{L}_{\fnrate} = \{(\fprate, 0) : 0 < \fprate
\le 1\}$ and the \emph{FPR-zero axis} is $\mathcal{L}_{\fprate} = \{(0,
\fnrate) : 0 < \fnrate \le 1\}$. An approximate set \emph{lies on the line}
when its rate point $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ belongs to $\mathcal{L}$.
\end{definition}

The axes meet only at the origin $(0,0)$, the exact set, excluded by the
strict inequalities: a set on the line achieves certainty in exactly one
direction of report, and the origin achieves it in both at the cost of the
space approximate sets exist to save. We draw $\fprate$ horizontal and
$\fnrate$ vertical, so $\mathcal{L}_{\fnrate}$ is the horizontal arm and
$\mathcal{L}_{\fprate}$ the vertical one; \Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets}
builds on the horizontal arm and \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} on the
vertical.

\begin{proposition}[FNR-zero axis closure]
\label{prop:fnr-zero-closure}
Let $\ASet{A}, \ASet{B}$ be FNR-zero, so $\fnrate_A = \fnrate_B = 0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\ASet{A} \cup \ASet{B}$ is FNR-zero, with $\fprate_\cup = 1 -
    (1 - \fprate_A)(1 - \fprate_B)$;
  \item $\ASet{A} \cap \ASet{B}$ is FNR-zero, with $\fprate_\cap$ the
    three-region rate \eqref{eq:intersection-fpr}, equal to $\fprate_A
    \fprate_B$ when $A = B$ or in the large-universe limit;
  \item $\complement \ASet{A}$ is FPR-zero, at $(0, \fprate_A)$;
  \item $\ASet{A} \setminus \ASet{B}$ is \emph{two-sided}, with
    $\fnrate_\setminus = \fprate_B$ and $\fprate_\setminus = w_2 \fprate_A
    (1 - \fprate_B) > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
So $\mathcal{L}_{\fnrate}$ is closed under union and intersection, mapped to
$\mathcal{L}_{\fprate}$ by complement, and \emph{not} closed under difference.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Parts (1) and (2) set $\fnrate_A = \fnrate_B = 0$ in
\Cref{prop:union-fpr-fnr} and \Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr}: the union FNR
\eqref{eq:union-fnr} and intersection FNR \eqref{eq:intersection-fnr} both
vanish, while the FPR formulas are unaffected by the operand FNRs. Part (3) is
\Cref{prop:complement-swap} at $\fnrate_A = 0$. For part (4), the difference
is $\ASet{A} \cap \complement \ASet{B}$; with $\ASet{B}$ FNR-zero its
complement is FPR-zero at $(0, \fprate_B)$, so the intersection is mixed-axis
and \Cref{prop:mixed-intersection-escape} applies, giving $\fnrate_\setminus =
\fprate_B$ and a strictly positive $\fprate_\setminus = w_2 \fprate_A(1 -
\fprate_B)$ on the region outside both sets.
\end{proof}

Part (4) is the difference's signature: two FNR-zero operands compose to a
two-sided set, because the intermediate complement sends $\ASet{B}$ to the
opposite axis and the intersection then mixes axes. The difference stays
one-sided only across axes, when $\ASet{A}$ and $\ASet{B}$ start on
\emph{opposite} arms so the complement aligns them; same-axis operands always
produce a two-sided difference.

\begin{proposition}[FPR-zero axis closure]
\label{prop:fpr-zero-closure}
Dually, if $\ASet{A}, \ASet{B}$ are FPR-zero then $\ASet{A} \cup \ASet{B}$ and
$\ASet{A} \cap \ASet{B}$ are FPR-zero, $\complement \ASet{A}$ is FNR-zero, and
$\ASet{A} \setminus \ASet{B}$ is two-sided.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By complement-swap from \Cref{prop:fnr-zero-closure}: an FPR-zero set is the
complement of an FNR-zero set, and the rate-plane algebra is symmetric under
coordinate exchange, so each conclusion transposes.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The shape of the line.}
$\mathcal{L}$ is non-convex: the midpoint of a segment from $(\fprate, 0)$ to
$(0, \fnrate)$ is $(\fprate/2, \fnrate/2)$, interior to the square and on
neither axis. The two arms meet only at the excised origin, so $\mathcal{L}$
is a wedge of two open segments rather than a connected curve, and no convex
mixing of one operand from each arm preserves it, the geometric content of
\Cref{prop:mixed-intersection-escape}. This is the precise sense in which the
line is a \emph{subalgebra} of the rate plane: closed under same-axis union
and intersection, swapped by complement, and exited by mixed-axis
intersection and same-axis difference. Same-axis chains compose freely;
cross-axis chains leave the line, and the space guarantees of
\Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} and \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} no longer
apply to the result.

\begin{remark}[The two-pass skeleton]
\label{rem:two-pass}
A natural design pairs a cheap FNR-zero filter $\ASet{A}_-$ at $(\fprate_-,
0)$ with a precise FPR-zero perfect filter $\ASet{A}_+$ at $(0, \fnrate_+)$
for the same $A$, accepting only when both accept. By
\Cref{prop:mixed-intersection-escape} this predicate-intersection is
mixed-axis and hence two-sided, with FNR $\fnrate_+$ and a residual FPR
$w_2 \fprate_-(1 - \fnrate_+)$ on inputs outside the perfect filter's
certainty region. The cheap filter discards most non-members and the precise
filter certifies the rest, which is the algorithmic skeleton of the perfect
hash filter; \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} is what turns the skeleton into a
genuinely one-sided FPR-zero construction rather than a two-sided
approximation.
\end{remark}

\Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} builds the Bernoulli Hash Function on
$\mathcal{L}_{\fnrate}$ at the entropy floor, closing the standard Bloom
filter's $\log_2 e \approx 1.44$ overhead, and by
\Cref{prop:fnr-zero-closure} its unions and intersections stay on the axis at
predictable rates. \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} builds the perfect hash
filter on $\mathcal{L}_{\fprate}$; mixed-axis composition with a Bloom filter
leaves the line and forfeits the FPR-zero certainty, so the designer must
respect the closure constraints to keep either chapter's guarantees.
