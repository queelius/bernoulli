\section{\texorpdfstring{$\Bernoulli{T}$}{Bernoulli T} for Composite \texorpdfstring{$T$}{T}}
\label{sec:bernoulli-of-composite}

§13.1 stated that $\Bernoulli{-}$ preserves type structure: applying
it through any of the four constructors produces a $\Bernoulli{-}$
instance assembled from the factor lifts. This section makes the
four rules concrete, one constructor at a time. The product rule is
the structural backbone, and the other three follow its pattern.

\subsection*{Product types: the Kronecker isomorphism}

A noisy pair is a pair of noisy values. If $\tilde{t}$ is a
$\Bernoulli{T}$ instance with channel matrix $Q_T$, and $\tilde{u}$
is a $\Bernoulli{U}$ instance with channel matrix $Q_U$, the
component-wise pair $(\tilde{t}, \tilde{u})$ is a noisy value over
$T \times U$ whose joint distribution at each true input
$(t, u) \in T \times U$ is the product of the marginal distributions.
The joint channel matrix is the Kronecker product of the two
component matrices: a $(K_T K_U) \times (L_T L_U)$ matrix whose
$((j_T, j_U), (\ell_T, \ell_U))$-entry is $(Q_T)_{j_T, \ell_T}
\cdot (Q_U)_{j_U, \ell_U}$.

This is the chapter~4 and chapter~9 Kronecker factorization theorem
read at the type level. Chapter~4's \Cref{thm:kronecker} treated the
joint distribution of $m$ Boolean queries; chapter~9's
\Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} extended the same result to n-ary
channels. Both said the same structural thing: independent components
give a joint channel matrix that factors as a Kronecker product. The
$m$ in chapter~4's statement and the second factor in chapter~9's
statement both index into a product of types, even when the original
chapters did not say so. We now name that product.

\begin{theorem}[Product-type isomorphism]
\label{thm:bernoulli-product}
For algebraic types $T$ and $U$ with independent noise channels,
\[
  \Bernoulli{T \times U} \;\cong\; \Bernoulli{T} \otimes \Bernoulli{U},
\]
where the right-hand side is the Kronecker product of the two
component channel matrices. The joint channel matrix has
$K_T K_U$ rows (one per input block in the product) and $L_T L_U$
columns (one per output label in the product), and factors entry-wise
as $(Q_T \otimes Q_U)_{(j_T, j_U), (\ell_T, \ell_U)} =
(Q_T)_{j_T, \ell_T} \cdot (Q_U)_{j_U, \ell_U}$.
\end{theorem}

The statement is the chapter~9 \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} specialized
from a Kronecker power $Q^{\otimes m}$ (which assumed all $m$ queries
share the same channel) to a heterogeneous Kronecker product
$Q_T \otimes Q_U$ (where the two factors have different channels and
even different dimensions). The proof is the same step-for-step
calculation: Axiom~1 factors the joint distribution into a product
across components, Axiom~2 keeps each component's row stable, and
\Cref{thm:kronecker} reads off the matrix structure. The new content
here is the interpretation: the formula is an isomorphism of types,
not just a property of joint channels.

A parameter-count consequence falls out for free. Per the corollaries
of chapter~4 and chapter~9, the joint channel of $\Bernoulli{T \times
U}$ would, without the structural assumption, require $K_T K_U
(L_T L_U - 1)$ free parameters. Under independence, it requires only
$K_T (L_T - 1) + K_U (L_U - 1)$: the parameters of $Q_T$ plus the
parameters of $Q_U$. Parameter cost is additive in the components,
not multiplicative.

\subsection*{Sum types: block-diagonal channels}

A sum $T + U$ has values that are either a $T$ tagged $\mathrm{Left}$
or a $U$ tagged $\mathrm{Right}$. A noisy sum-typed value reports a
tag plus a payload, and in the cleanest case the noise on the two
summands is independent: errors within the $T$ summand are governed
by $Q_T$, errors within the $U$ summand by $Q_U$, and the tag is
either preserved (the common case) or flipped.

When the tag is preserved, the joint channel matrix on $T + U$ is
block-diagonal: rows indexed by $T$-blocks map to columns indexed by
$T$-outputs via $Q_T$, rows indexed by $U$-blocks map to $U$-outputs
via $Q_U$, and the off-diagonal blocks are zero. We write
\[
  \Bernoulli{T + U} \;\cong\; \Bernoulli{T} \oplus \Bernoulli{U},
\]
where $\oplus$ is the direct sum of channels: a block-diagonal
matrix with $Q_T$ in the upper-left and $Q_U$ in the lower-right.
The sum-of-types case is parameter-additive too: $K_T (L_T - 1) +
K_U (L_U - 1)$ free parameters, the same count as the product case
because in both cases the noise is structured by the per-component
matrices and nothing else. The difference is the input/output
dimensions: $K_T + K_U$ rows and $L_T + L_U$ columns for the sum,
versus the product's multiplicative dimensions.

When the tag itself is noisy (a tag flip occurs with probability
$\delta$), the channel matrix gains off-diagonal mass linking
$T$-blocks to $U$-outputs and vice versa. The structural form is no
longer pure block-diagonal but a sum of the two pure block-diagonal
channels weighted by tag-preservation and tag-flip probabilities.
We work with the tag-preserving case throughout, returning to tag
errors in chapter~16's catalogue of boundary cases.

\subsection*{List types: per-position lifts}

A list $L T$ of length $n$ is the same as a fixed-length product
$T^n = T \times T \times \cdots \times T$. For lists of known length,
the product rule applies directly:
\[
  \Bernoulli{T^n} \;\cong\; \Bernoulli{T}^{\otimes n}
                  \;\cong\; \Bernoulli{T} \otimes \cdots \otimes
                            \Bernoulli{T} \quad (n \text{ factors}).
\]
This is precisely the chapter~4 setting: $m = n$ independent queries
on the same channel produce the joint matrix $Q_T^{\otimes n}$, the
Kronecker power of the per-position channel. The parameter count is
$K_T (L_T - 1)$, independent of $n$: adding list positions does not
add parameters, only multiplies the joint matrix shape.

Variable-length lists introduce a second source of noise: the length
itself is an attribute that may be uncertain. A HyperLogLog sketch
estimates the cardinality of an input set without remembering its
elements; the cardinality is the length of the underlying list (with
duplicates collapsed), and HyperLogLog is a $\Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}}$
on that length. A CountMin sketch stores a noisy count of each
distinct key, also a $\Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}}$ per key. For a
variable-length list with noisy length and noisy per-position
contents, the joint structure is
\[
  \Bernoulli{L T} \;\cong\; \Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}} \otimes
                            \Bernoulli{T}^{\otimes n},
\]
read as a product of an $\mathbb{N}$-valued length channel and an
$n$-fold per-position channel, where $n$ is itself the random
realized length. The full noisy list is the joint outcome.

\subsection*{Function types: per-input lifts}

A function $f : T \to U$ approximated noisily produces a
$\Bernoulli{U}$ instance on each input $t \in T$, with the noise
characteristics potentially differing input by input. The full
specification is one $\Bernoulli{U}$ per element of $T$:
\[
  \Bernoulli{T \to U} \;\cong\; T \to \Bernoulli{U}
                       \;\cong\; \prod_{t \in T} \Bernoulli{U}.
\]
This is exactly the chapter~9 framework. The n-ary channel
$(X, Y, Q)$ of chapter~9 has input domain $X$, output domain $Y$, and
channel matrix $Q$ specifying a $\Bernoulli{Y}$ distribution for each
input block of $X$. Rephrased at the type level: the channel is a
function from input blocks to noisy-output distributions, i.e., a
function $X \to \Bernoulli{Y}$, which is the same as $\Bernoulli{X
\to Y}$ in the function-type sense.

Two sub-cases are worth naming. When the codomain is $\BoolType$, a
$\Bernoulli{T \to \BoolType}$ is a noisy predicate, the chapter~5
approximate-set abstraction. When the codomain is a tag plus a
payload, $\Bernoulli{T \to U + V}$, the function returns a noisy
tagged value, combining the function rule with the sum rule. The
chapter~12 approximate relation is the case
$\Bernoulli{T \times U \to \BoolType}$: a noisy predicate on a product
type.

Parameter count grows with $|T|$: each of the $|T|$ inputs contributes
one row of $Q$ and hence $L_U - 1$ free parameters, for a total of
$|T| (L_U - 1)$. Function types are richer than the other three
constructors precisely because they make the parameter count grow
with the cardinality of the domain.

\subsection*{Summary: four composition rules}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Type constructor & Lift of $\Bernoulli{-}$ & Channel-matrix form \\
\hline
$T \times U$ & $\Bernoulli{T} \otimes \Bernoulli{U}$ & Kronecker product \\
$T + U$ & $\Bernoulli{T} \oplus \Bernoulli{U}$ & block-diagonal \\
$L T$ & $\Bernoulli{T}^{\otimes n}$ (and maybe $\Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}}$ length) & Kronecker power \\
$T \to U$ & $T \to \Bernoulli{U}$ & one $\Bernoulli{U}$ per input \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each row reads as a recipe for composing $\Bernoulli{-}$ instances by
type structure. Given a composite type $T$ built from the four
constructors, the recipe says how to assemble $\Bernoulli{T}$ from
$\Bernoulli{-}$ instances on the components. The recipes are not
optional: they are forced by Axioms~1 and~2 applied at the relevant
type-structural level. They are the four faces of one underlying
isomorphism, which §13.3 names.

\subsection*{Worked example: a labeled classifier}

A 3-class classifier reports a class label plus a count of times the
input has been seen. The output type is
$\{\text{cat}, \text{dog}, \text{bird}\} \times \mathbb{N}$, a
product of a 3-element enumeration and the natural numbers. The
classifier is noisy in two ways: the class label may be wrong
(a confusion matrix among the three classes) and the count may be
noisy (a CountMin-style sketch with additive overcounting).

The class component is a $\Bernoulli{\{\text{cat}, \text{dog},
\text{bird}\}}$, governed by a $3 \times 3$ confusion matrix
$Q_{\text{class}}$. The count component is a
$\Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}}$, governed by a per-count channel
$Q_{\text{count}}$. Under independence of the two error sources, the
joint $\Bernoulli{}$ instance on the output type factors:
\[
  \Bernoulli{\{\text{cat}, \text{dog}, \text{bird}\} \times \mathbb{N}}
  \;\cong\;
  \Bernoulli{\{\text{cat}, \text{dog}, \text{bird}\}} \otimes
  \Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}}.
\]
The joint channel matrix is the Kronecker product
$Q_{\text{class}} \otimes Q_{\text{count}}$, and the parameter count
is $3 \cdot 2 + K_{\mathbb{N}} (L_{\mathbb{N}} - 1)$, the sum of the
two components' counts. To compose two such classifiers (say, to ask
whether the same image gets the same label and the same count from
two independent runs of the system), one applies the join-rate
calculation of chapter~12 to the label component and the chapter~10
sketch-composition rules to the count component, and the two
calculations carry no cross-interaction. The type structure tells
you where to apply which rule.

The classifier example illustrates the payoff. Composing approximate
structures used to require deriving rules per data structure. Under
the type-driven view, derivation reduces to applying four standard
recipes, one per constructor, to the components of the composite
type. §13.3 packages this observation as the chapter's headline
result.
