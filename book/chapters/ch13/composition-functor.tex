\section{Structure Preservation and Its Limits}
\label{sec:composition-functor}

\Cref{sec:bernoulli-of-composite} gave the lift on each constructor. This section
collects the rules, states what they give, and marks where they stop.

\subsection*{The rules as one map}

Under the independence hypotheses of the table in
\Cref{sec:bernoulli-of-composite}, the lift $\Bernoulli{-}$ takes a type-level
operation to a channel-level one. The product becomes a Kronecker product
(\Cref{thm:bernoulli-product}), the list a Kronecker power, the function a
per-input Kronecker product. A map that takes operations to corresponding
operations is \emph{structure-preserving}; the categorical name is
\emph{functor}, and the Kronecker product\index{Kronecker product} is the tensor of the monoidal category
of finite stochastic matrices. In summary:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\hline
Type level & Channel level & Reading \\
\hline
$T \times U$ & $\Bernoulli{T} \otimes \Bernoulli{U}$ & Kronecker product \\
$L T$ & $L\, \Bernoulli{T}$, or a mixture over lengths & per-position lift \\
$T \to U$ & $T \to \Bernoulli{U}$ & per-input lift \\
$T + U$ & does \emph{not} factor & tag coupling \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Within their hypotheses the first three rows are isomorphisms: the type and the
channel specify the same $\Bernoulli{-}$ value up to relabeling. The fourth row
is the exception, the sum, where the tag error couples the components and no
factorization recovers the channel (\Cref{prop:sum-nonfactor}); the mixture in the
list row arises when the length itself is noisy. That exception is where the framework's
atom sits: the optional type's tag error\index{sum type} is the membership error of an
approximate set (\S13.2).

\begin{remark}[The probability monad]
\label{rem:monad}
Noise-introducing functions $f : T \to \Bernoulli{U}$ are the Kleisli arrows of
the probability monad\index{monad!probability} of \textcite{giry-1982}. Two arrows
$f : T \to \Bernoulli{U}$ and $g : U \to \Bernoulli{V}$ compose into $T \to
\Bernoulli{V}$ by feeding $f$'s output through $g$ and averaging over the
intermediate value (the bind), and a pure value embeds as the point distribution
on it (the unit); for finite types the composite's matrix is the matrix
product of the serial-composition remark in \Cref{sec:n-ary-kronecker}. This is the standard semantics of probabilistic
programs \parencite{kozen-1981,ramsey-pfeffer-2002}, and \textcite{wadler-1992}
gives the programming-language reading of monads. Kleisli composition describes
one evaluation with fresh randomness at every stage. It does not describe a
stored approximate function queried at several inputs, whose randomness was drawn
once at construction: two inputs that meet at the same intermediate value share
the stored stage's realized error (\Cref{rem:channel-composition}), a dependence
the per-evaluation semantics does not represent.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{What the table gives, and what it does not}

The table states how the joint error law of a composite \emph{value} factors when
its components' noise is independent, and how many parameters that law needs.
That is useful bookkeeping: a structure built from the three factoring
constructors with independent noise has a joint law fixed by its components'
channels, and the sum marks where a tag error couples components even then.

It is not a composition calculus for \emph{computations} on approximate values,
for three reasons the earlier chapters make concrete. First, an operation applied
to noisy components produces an output whose error depends on the latent input,
not only on the output's truth value: the AND of two noisy bits already has three
distinct negative-case rates, so a two-rate pair does not propagate exactly
through a program\index{counterexample!two-rate composition} (\Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample}). Exact propagation is per
latent assignment (\Cref{prop:latent-propagation}), and a single pair survives
only as a prior average\index{prior-averaged rate} or a worst-case
bound\index{composition!worst-case rate bounds}
(\Cref{prop:worst-case-rate-bounds}). Second, programs that use a value twice,
and stages that are stored rather than re-randomized, break the independence the
table assumes (\Cref{rem:channel-composition}). Third, some structures do not meet
the function row's hypothesis at all: a CountMin sketch\index{Count-Min sketch} read as $\Bernoulli{T \to
\mathbb{N}}$ answers different items from shared counters, so its per-item errors
are dependent and the per-input Kronecker product does not describe them
(\Cref{sec:sketches-as-bernoulli}).

With those limits stated, the earlier structures fit the vocabulary as follows.
An approximate set (\Cref{ch:set-composition}) is $\Bernoulli{T \to \BoolType}$,
its Axiom~1 being the function row's hypothesis, and the chapter-6 operation
rates are per-latent-assignment calculations of the kind
\Cref{prop:latent-propagation} makes general. An approximate relation
(\Cref{ch:approximate-relations}) is $\Bernoulli{T \times U \to \BoolType}$, a
predicate on a product domain, whose query errors chapter~12 bounds query by
query rather than reading them off the table. Sketches
(\Cref{ch:sketches-estimators}) are noisy values of aggregate types whose
per-query marginals fit the table and whose joint laws do not. The n-ary channel
of \Cref{ch:approximate-functions} is the building block $X \to Y$ underneath
them all.

\subsection*{One object, several readings}

There is one underlying object across the book. The atom is the noisy bit
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$, and the other structures are $\Bernoulli{T}$ values for
algebraic types built from $\BoolType$ and the constructors. The type algebra
organizes where independence makes their error laws factor and where it does
not; the error of computations over them is analyzed per latent assignment, or
bounded. The remaining chapters use this organization rather than extend it:
chapter~14 analyzes a seed-search construction near the entropy floor,
chapter~15 the perfect hash filters, chapter~16 catalogues where the
independence assumptions break, and chapters~17 and~18 turn the same rates to
cryptographic use.
