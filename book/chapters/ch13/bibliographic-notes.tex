\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch13}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
Values that are correct with a tracked probability, composed through programs,
have a direct predecessor in approximate programming. Decaf
\parencite{boston-et-al-2015} gives each value a type qualifier
\texttt{@Approx(p)}, the probability that it is correct, and infers the
qualifier of an expression as the product $p_a\, p_b\, p_{\mathrm{op}}$ of its
operands' and operator's probabilities; Rely
\parencite{carbin-misailovic-rinard-2013} verifies such reliability bounds for
programs on unreliable hardware, Chisel \parencite{misailovic-et-al-2014}
optimizes against them, EnerJ \parencite{sampson-et-al-2011} separates approximate
from precise data by type, and $\mathtt{Uncertain}\langle T\rangle$
\parencite{bornholt-mytkowicz-mckinley-2014} makes a distribution over $T$ a
first-class value. The probability monad behind \Cref{rem:monad} is
\textcite{giry-1982}; its use as a semantics of probabilistic programs is
\textcite{kozen-1981} and \textcite{ramsey-pfeffer-2002}, with
\textcite{wadler-1992} for monads in programming and \textcite{maclane-categories}
for the categorical machinery. The four-constructor view of algebraic data types
is standard \parencite{hudak-1989}.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
The constructor table follows the data-type manuscript
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-data-type}. Relative to Decaf's single correctness
probability, the lift tracks a confusion matrix per type, so the false-positive
and false-negative directions stay separate, and the chapter records which
constructors make a composite value's error law factor (products, lists, and
functions under independence) and which do not (sums, through the tag error,
identified with approximate-set membership error). It also corrects earlier
statements of the table as a composition calculus: the table describes values,
not computations, and two-rate pairs do not compose exactly through programs.

\paragraph{Left out.}
A type system. A sound two-rate (confusion-matrix) qualifier discipline over sum,
product, and function types, with inference and with the per-latent-assignment
and dependence issues above handled soundly, would extend Decaf's
single-probability qualifiers; it is an open research direction, not a result of
this book. Recursive types beyond lists, higher-order functions, and dependence
tracking across shared subterms are also left out.
