\section{HyperLogLog}
\label{sec:hyperloglog}

The complementary streaming question is cardinality: estimate the number $n$
of distinct items among $x_1, \ldots, x_N$, ignoring multiplicity. Exact
counting needs $\Theta(n)$ bits in the worst case; HyperLogLog\index{HyperLogLog}
returns an estimate with relative error of order $1/\sqrt{m}$ using $m$ small registers,
at space \emph{doubly} logarithmic in $n$, so a few kilobytes estimate
cardinalities into the billions.

\subsection*{The leading-zero trick}

Let $h : \Universe \to [0,1]$ be a uniform hash, read as a binary fraction
$0.b_1 b_2 \cdots$, and let $\rho(x)$ be the position of its first $1$ bit
(leading zeros plus one). For uniform $h(a)$ the bits are fair coins, so
$\Prob{\rho \ge k} = 2^{-(k-1)}$, and the maximum of $\rho$ over $n$ distinct
items grows as $\log_2 n$ with $O(1)$ spread. Tracking only the running maximum
of $\rho(h(a))$ and reporting $2^{\max}$ therefore estimates $n$ from a single
small integer (the probabilistic counting\index{probabilistic counting} of
\textcite{flajolet-martin-1985}), but the multiplicative standard deviation is a factor of roughly
two: an order-of-magnitude check, useless quantitatively. The fix is averaging.

\subsection*{The construction}

HyperLogLog averages without a second hash by \emph{stochastic averaging}: the
first $\log_2 m$ bits of each item's hash route it to one of $m$ buckets, and
the remaining bits feed that bucket's leading-zero count. The $m$ registers act
as independent observations of the single-register estimator on streams of
expected length $n/m$.

\begin{definition}[HyperLogLog sketch]
\label{def:hyperloglog}
Fix $m = 2^p$ registers ($p \ge 4$) and a hash $h : \Universe \to \{0,1\}^{32}$.
The sketch is a register array $M[0..m-1]$, initialized to zero, with
\textsc{Update}$(a)$ splitting $h(a)$ into a $p$-bit bucket index $j$ and a
remainder $w$ and setting $M[j] \gets \max(M[j], \rho(w))$, and
\textsc{Estimate}$()$ returning
\[
  \hat n = \alpha_m \cdot \frac{m^2}{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} 2^{-M[j]}},
\]
with $\alpha_{16} = 0.673$, $\alpha_{32} = 0.697$, $\alpha_{64} = 0.709$, and
$\alpha_m = 0.7213/(1 + 1.079/m)$ for $m \ge 128$.
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{HyperLogLog update and estimate}
  \label{alg:hyperloglog-update-estimate}
  \SetKwProg{func}{procedure}{}{}
  \SetKwProg{fn}{function}{}{}
  \func{\textsc{Update}($a$)}{
    $x \gets h(a)$; $j \gets$ first $p$ bits of $x$; $w \gets$ remaining bits\;
    $M[j] \gets \max(M[j], \rho(w))$\;
  }
  \BlankLine
  \fn{\textsc{Estimate}()}{
    $Z \gets \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} 2^{-M[j]}$\;
    \Return $\alpha_m \cdot m^2 / Z$\;
  }
\end{algorithm}

The sum $Z = \sum_j 2^{-M[j]}$ is the harmonic mean of the per-bucket estimates
$2^{M[j]}$ (up to a factor $m$), which suppresses an outlier register that drew
an unlucky run of leading zeros; the $m^2$ rescales $n/m$ per bucket to $n$
total, and $\alpha_m$ removes the constant bias of the harmonic mean and the
discrete $\rho$, an integral \textcite{flajolet-et-al-2007} evaluate. For a
32-bit hash with $p = 8$ ($m = 256$), each $M[j] \in \{0, \ldots, 24\}$ fits in
$5$ bits, so the sketch is $256 \cdot 5 = 1280$ bits ($160$ bytes) regardless of
stream length.

\begin{proposition}[HyperLogLog standard error]
\label{prop:hyperloglog-error}
For $m \ge 128$ registers and $n$ large relative to $m$, the relative standard
error of the estimate is $\sigma(\hat n)/n \approx 1.04/\sqrt{m}$; at $m = 2048$
this is about $2.3\%$.
\end{proposition}

The $1.04$ is the asymptotic per-register factor of
\textcite{flajolet-et-al-2007}, from an exact generating-function calculation we
do not reproduce. The $1/\sqrt{m}$ dependence is the central-limit scaling of
$m$ near-independent observations, and doubling the accuracy quadruples the
register count. When $n$ is small relative to $m$ (many registers still zero)
the algorithm switches to a linear-counting correction $\hat n = m \ln(m/V)$
with $V$ the count of zero registers; a saturation correction handles the
high-cardinality end.

\subsection*{The $\Bernoulli{T}$ type and merging}

Read through \Cref{ch:approximate-functions}, HyperLogLog is a $\Bernoulli{T}$
value with $T = \mathbb{N}$, as CountMin is, but its channel $Q$
(\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}) is \emph{not} triangular: it errs both
ways\index{two-sided error}. The
limiting distribution of $\log\hat n - \log n$ is approximately normal with
standard deviation $1.04/\sqrt{m}$ \parencite{flajolet-et-al-2007}, so each row of
$Q$ concentrates on a geometric interval around $n$, symmetric on a
multiplicative rather than additive scale, the qualitative contrast with
CountMin's one-sided additive error. Two sketches on the same hash and register
count merge by entry-wise maximum, $(M^{(1)} \sqcup M^{(2)})[j] =
\max(M^{(1)}[j], M^{(2)}[j])$, which is exactly the sketch of the union stream
because each bucket stores a maximum and the max over a union is the max of the
per-stream maxima. As with CountMin, sharing the hash is what makes the merge
exact; it is a property of the max-register construction, not a channel
composition.

\subsection*{Worked example}

Take $m = 256$ and a stream of $n = 10000$ distinct items. The relative standard
error from \Cref{prop:hyperloglog-error} is $1.04/\sqrt{256} = 0.065$, so a
single sketch typically lands within about $\pm 650$ of $10000$, with each
register holding the maximum $\rho$ of the $\approx 10000/256 \approx 39$ items
in its bucket. The estimate is nearly unbiased in this regime but high-variance
on one seed: averaging
$100$ independent seeds returns an empirical mean of $9970$ with relative
standard deviation $0.063$, both matching the proposition. The headline figure
is a standard error, not a deterministic bound; the value of the sketch is that
the spread shrinks as $1/\sqrt{m}$. \Cref{sec:minhash-lsh} turns to similarity:
estimating the Jaccard overlap of two sets from compact signatures.
