\section{Sketches as Bernoulli of \texorpdfstring{$T$}{T}}
\label{sec:sketches-as-bernoulli}

\subsection*{What is a sketch?}

A \emph{sketch} is a function from a stream or multiset of items, drawn
from some universe $\Universe$, to a compact summary, together with a
query routine that reconstructs an answer to a fixed family of
questions about the input from the summary alone. The reconstruction
is approximate: the query returns an estimate $\hat\theta$ of some
quantity $\theta(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ of the stream, and the error
$\hat\theta - \theta$ is bounded by a known analytic expression in the
summary size and the input. What makes a sketch a sketch, and not
merely a compressed representation, is that the size of the summary
grows sublinearly (often poly-logarithmically) in the stream length
$n$, the per-item update cost is constant or near-constant, and the
error bound is a clean function of the design parameters. The reader
already saw the preview at \Cref{sec:bridge-to-sketches}, which framed
the three sketches of this chapter as the canonical instances of the
trade-off for frequency, cardinality, and similarity queries
respectively; this section makes that framing the operating definition
for the rest of Part III.

\subsection*{Three canonical sketches, three types}

The three sketches built in this chapter differ chiefly in what they
estimate, which is to say in their output type.

\paragraph{CountMin estimates per-item frequency.}
Given a stream $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ and a query item $a \in
\Universe$, CountMin returns an estimate $\hat f_a$ of the true count
$f_a = |\{i : x_i = a\}|$. The output type is $T = \mathbb{N}$, and
the error is \emph{one-sided}: the construction guarantees $\hat f_a
\ge f_a$ deterministically, with the over-estimate bounded in
probability by a quantity that scales with the stream length and the
sketch width. This one-sided structure is the direct analogue of the
Bloom filter's one-sided membership report
(\Cref{ch:approximate-sets}): the construction sets counters
monotonically, no operation ever decreases a counter, and the worst
case is that some other item's hashes coincide with $a$'s and inflate
the reported count. False under-estimation, the analogue of a Bloom
false negative, is impossible by construction.

\paragraph{HyperLogLog estimates cardinality.}
Given a stream that may contain duplicates, HyperLogLog returns an
estimate $\hat n$ of the number of distinct items $n = |\{x_1, \ldots,
x_n\}|$. The output type is again $T = \mathbb{N}$, but the error
structure is qualitatively different: the estimate is approximate in
both directions, with a relative error of order $1/\sqrt m$ in the
register count $m$. The headline result is that $m = 2048$ registers
of six bits apiece (a kilobyte and a half) suffice to estimate the
cardinality of a stream of billions of distinct items to about two and
a half percent relative accuracy. The space cost is logarithmic in
the cardinality being estimated rather than linear in it, which is the
qualitative win that makes HyperLogLog the standard sketch for
distinct counts in production analytics systems.

\paragraph{MinHash estimates Jaccard similarity.}
Given two sets $A, B \subseteq \Universe$, MinHash returns an estimate
$\hat J(A, B)$ of the Jaccard similarity $J(A, B) = |A \cap B| / |A
\cup B|$. The output type is $T = [0, 1]$ (the unit interval),
reflecting the fact that the quantity being estimated is a probability
rather than a count. The error is symmetric: $\hat J - J$ is centered
at zero with variance $J(1 - J)/k$ in the signature length $k$, and
the deviations on either side of the true value have the same
distribution. The sketch is built from the simple observation that
the minimum of a random hash function over a set is equally likely to
come from any element, so the probability that two sets share their
minimum is exactly the fraction of elements they share. The full
construction is the obvious generalization: run the trick $k$ times
with independent hashes, count the matches, divide by $k$.

\subsection*{Why the chapter 9 framework unifies them}

The three sketches above estimate three different things over three
different domains, and the conventional treatment introduces each one
as its own structure with its own analysis. The chapter 9 framework
gives all three the same shape. Each sketch is a function
\[
  s \;:\; \Universe^* \longrightarrow T,
\]
where $\Universe^*$ is the space of finite streams over the universe
$\Universe$ and $T$ is the output type. The query algorithm is the
projection from a stream to its sketch value, and the error structure
is summarized by a row-stochastic channel matrix $Q$ in the sense of
\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}: rows of $Q$ are indexed by the true value
$\theta$ of the queried quantity, columns by the reported value
$\hat\theta$, and the entry $Q_{\theta, \hat\theta}$ is the
conditional probability that the sketch reports $\hat\theta$ when the
input has true value $\theta$. The matrix $Q$ is typically very
large or even continuous (HyperLogLog's $\theta$ ranges over the
positive integers; MinHash's ranges over the unit interval), but in
every case it has structure: CountMin's $Q$ is upper triangular
(one-sided), HyperLogLog's concentrates within a multiplicative band
around the diagonal (relative error), and MinHash's is approximately
binomial along each row (signature counts).

The structural payoff is composition. The independence properties
that drive the per-sketch analyses (pairwise-independent hashes for
CountMin, independent hash registers for HyperLogLog, independent
permutation samples for MinHash) all amount to the same statement in
the channel-matrix language: the joint behaviour of multiple sketch
queries, or of a single sketch built by aggregating multiple streams,
factors as a Kronecker product of the per-query or per-substream
matrices. \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} is the chapter 9 result that
makes this factorization a theorem rather than a per-sketch lemma.
Merging two CountMin sketches, merging two HyperLogLog sketches,
querying a MinHash signature at $k$ positions are all instances of
the same Kronecker structure, with the per-instance content amounting
to filling in the correct entries of the elementary matrices.

\subsection*{Signpost}

The next three sections build the three sketches from scratch, each
one following the same template: problem setup, construction with
pseudocode, error-bound theorem, identification of the
$\Bernoulli{T}$ type, and merge or composition rule.
\Cref{sec:countmin} builds CountMin; \Cref{sec:hyperloglog} builds
HyperLogLog; \Cref{sec:minhash-lsh} builds MinHash and its
