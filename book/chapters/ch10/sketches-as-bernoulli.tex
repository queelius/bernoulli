\section{Sketches as Bernoulli of \texorpdfstring{$T$}{T}}
\label{sec:sketches-as-bernoulli}

A \emph{sketch} maps a stream or multiset over a universe $\Universe$ to a
compact summary, with a query routine that reconstructs an estimate
$\hat\theta$ of some quantity $\theta$ of the stream from the summary alone.
What makes it a sketch, rather than a compressed representation, is that the
summary grows sublinearly (often poly-logarithmically) in the stream length,
the per-item update is near-constant, and the error $\hat\theta - \theta$ is
bounded by a clean function of the design parameters
(\Cref{sec:bridge-to-sketches}). This chapter builds three, distinguished by
what they estimate, which is to say by their output type.

\emph{CountMin} (\Cref{sec:countmin}) estimates per-item frequency $f_a$, with
$T = \mathbb{N}$ and \emph{one-sided} error: the construction guarantees
$\hat f_a \ge f_a$ deterministically, the multiset analogue of the Bloom
filter's one-sided membership report (\Cref{ch:approximate-sets}).
\emph{HyperLogLog} (\Cref{sec:hyperloglog}) estimates distinct-item cardinality
$n$, again with $T = \mathbb{N}$ but with two-sided relative error of order
$1/\sqrt{m}$ in the register count $m$. \emph{MinHash} (\Cref{sec:minhash-lsh})
estimates Jaccard similarity $J(A,B)$, with $T = [0,1]$ and symmetric error of
variance $J(1-J)/k$ in the signature length $k$.

The chapter-9 framework gives all three the same shape. Each is a function
$s : \Universe^* \to T$ whose error is summarized by a row-stochastic channel
$Q$ (\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}) with rows indexed by the true value $\theta$,
columns by the report $\hat\theta$: CountMin's $Q$ is upper triangular
(one-sided), HyperLogLog's concentrates in a multiplicative band (relative
error), MinHash's is binomial along each row. The structural payoff is
composition: the per-sketch independence assumptions all reduce to the same
channel statement, and merging two sketches or querying one at $k$ positions is
the Kronecker factorization of \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} rather than a
per-sketch lemma. Each of the next three sections follows one template,
construction, error bound, $\Bernoulli{T}$ type, merge; \Cref{sec:ch10-bridge}
hands the pattern to the type algebra of Part~III's close.
