\section{MinHash and LSH}
\label{sec:minhash-lsh}

The third primitive estimates the \emph{Jaccard similarity} $J(A, B) = |A \cap
B|/|A \cup B| \in [0,1]$ of two sets. Exact computation needs space linear in
the set sizes, a deal-breaker across a corpus of millions of documents over a
vocabulary of millions of shingles. MinHash\index{MinHash} (\textcite{broder-1997}) reduces
each set to a signature of $k$ integers and estimates $J$ from two signatures in
$O(k)$ time.

\subsection*{The collision identity}

Fix a hash $h : \Universe \to [0,1]$ that is injective on $A \cup B$ and
min-wise independent there (every element of any subset is equally likely to
attain its minimum, as for a uniformly random permutation;
\textcite{broder-et-al-2000}), and write $\min h(S) = \min_{a \in S} h(a)$.

\begin{proposition}[MinHash collision probability]
\label{prop:minhash-collision}
Under the hypotheses above,
\[
  \Prob{\min h(A) = \min h(B)} = J(A, B).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The minimum of $h$ over $A \cup B$ is attained at a unique $a^*$. The minima of
$A$ and $B$ coincide iff $a^* \in A \cap B$ (otherwise the set not containing
$a^*$ has a strictly larger minimum). By min-wise independence every element of
$A \cup B$ is equally likely to be $a^*$, so $\Prob{a^* \in A \cap B} = |A \cap B|/|A \cup
B| = J(A, B)$.
\end{proof}

The minimum hash over a union is a uniform sample from the union, and the chance
it falls in a subset is the subset's proportion. One hash converts the
similarity question into a Bernoulli coin of bias $J$; estimating $J$ is
repetition.

\subsection*{The construction and its error}

\begin{definition}[MinHash sketch]
\label{def:minhash}
Let $h_1, \ldots, h_k$ be independent hashes of the kind above. The
\emph{signature} of a set $A \subseteq \Universe$ is
\[
  \sigma(A) = \bigl(\sigma_1(A), \ldots, \sigma_k(A)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \sigma_i(A) = \min_{a \in A} h_i(a),
\]
and the \emph{estimator} of $J(A, B)$ is
\[
  \hat J = \frac{1}{k}\,\bigl|\{i : \sigma_i(A) = \sigma_i(B)\}\bigr|,
\]
the fraction of agreeing components.
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{MinHash signature and Jaccard estimate}
  \label{alg:minhash-signature-estimate}
  \SetKwProg{fn}{function}{}{}
  \fn{\textsc{Signature}($A$)}{
    \For{$i \gets 1$ \KwTo $k$}{$\sigma_i \gets \min_{a \in A} h_i(a)$\;}
    \Return $(\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_k)$\;
  }
  \BlankLine
  \fn{\textsc{Estimate}($\sigma(A), \sigma(B)$)}{
    $c \gets 0$\;
    \For{$i \gets 1$ \KwTo $k$}{\lIf{$\sigma_i(A) = \sigma_i(B)$}{$c \gets c + 1$}}
    \Return $c/k$\;
  }
\end{algorithm}

The signature costs $O(k)$ space and updates in $O(1)$ per inserted item via
$\sigma_i \gets \min(\sigma_i, h_i(a))$ (deletion is unsupported, the price of a
min-summary).

\begin{proposition}[MinHash error bound]
\label{prop:minhash-error}
The estimator satisfies $\Expect{\hat J} = J$, $\mathrm{Var}[\hat J] = J(1 -
J)/k$, and, by Hoeffding's inequality, $\Prob{|\hat J - J| \ge \epsilon} \le 2
\exp(-2 k \epsilon^2)$ for every $\epsilon > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each $i$ let $Y_i$ indicate the event $\sigma_i(A) = \sigma_i(B)$. Each
$Y_i$ is $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(J)$ by \Cref{prop:minhash-collision}, and the $Y_i$
are independent across $i$ since the $h_i$ are. Then $\hat J = \frac1k \sum Y_i$ has mean $J$ and
variance $J(1-J)/k$, and Hoeffding's bound for a mean of $k$ variables in
$[0,1]$ gives the tail.
\end{proof}

The error is two-sided\index{two-sided error} and symmetric, like HyperLogLog's
(\Cref{prop:hyperloglog-error}) and unlike CountMin's. Inverting the tail for a
target $\delta$ gives accuracy $\epsilon = \sqrt{\ln(2/\delta)/(2k)}$, so the
signature length grows linearly in $\ln(1/\delta)$ and quadratically in
$1/\epsilon$.

\subsection*{The $\Bernoulli{T}$ type}

Read through \Cref{ch:approximate-functions}, MinHash is a $\Bernoulli{T}$
value with $T = [0,1]$. Since $k\hat J$ is a sum of $k$ independent
$\mathrm{Bernoulli}(J)$ variables, each row of the channel $Q$
(\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}) is a $\mathrm{Binomial}(k, J)$ distribution on
$\{0, 1/k, \ldots, 1\}$,
\[
  Q_{J,\, j/k} = \binom{k}{j} J^j (1 - J)^{k-j},
\]
symmetric under $\hat J \mapsto 1 - \hat J$, $J \mapsto 1 - J$, the contrast with
CountMin's triangular and HyperLogLog's log-normal rows. The
\Cref{prop:minhash-error} concentration is the channel-matrix view of the
Binomial's concentration around its mean. The independence here is across the
$k$ hash functions for one pair of sets; estimates for different pairs that
share a set share its signature and are dependent.

\subsection*{LSH for approximate nearest neighbour}

Estimating $J$ for every pair in a corpus of $N$ documents costs $O(kN^2)$.
Locality-sensitive hashing\index{locality-sensitive hashing}
(\textcite{indyk-motwani-1998}) uses signatures as
bucket keys so that similar pairs collide. Partition the length-$k$ signature
into $b$ bands of $r$ rows ($k = br$); two sets are \emph{candidates} if they
agree on all $r$ rows of at least one band. A band agrees with probability
$J^r$, so the candidate probability is the S-curve
\[
  p(J) = 1 - (1 - J^r)^b,
\]
with a sharp transition near $J^* \approx (1/b)^{1/r}$: larger $r$ steepens and
selects, larger $b$ raises and admits. Tuning $(b, r)$ to a target $J^*$ and
building the $b$ band tables once turns the $O(N^2)$ comparison into a
sub-linear bucket lookup, with an exact pass filtering the small candidate set.

\subsection*{Worked example}

Take $|A| = |B| = 100$, $|A \cap B| = 30$, so $|A \cup B| = 170$ and $J = 30/170
\approx 0.176$. With $k = 200$,
\[
  \mathrm{Var}[\hat J] = \frac{0.176 \cdot 0.824}{200} \approx 7.26 \times
  10^{-4},
\]
a standard error of $0.027$. The Hoeffding bound at $\epsilon = 0.05$ is
$2e^{-2 \cdot 200 \cdot 0.0025}$, that is $2e^{-1} \approx 0.74$, loose here because
Hoeffding ignores the $J(1-J)$ factor that shrinks the variance for small $J$;
the exact binomial standard error $0.027$ is the relevant figure. A single
sketch returns, say, $\hat J = 0.185$, within one standard error of $0.176$.
Halving the error to $0.013$ quadruples the signature to $k = 800$, the same
$1/\sqrt{k}$ scaling as HyperLogLog. \Cref{sec:ch10-bridge} recaps the common
pattern and hands the three sketches to the type algebra of Part~III's close.
