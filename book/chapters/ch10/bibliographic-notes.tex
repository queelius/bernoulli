\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch10}

\paragraph{The Count-Min sketch.}
The construction of \Cref{sec:countmin} and the additive error bound of
\Cref{thm:countmin-bound} are due to
\textcite{cormode-muthukrishnan-2005}. Their paper introduces the
$d \times w$ counter table with $d$ pairwise-independent row hashes,
the update rule that increments one counter per row, the query rule
that returns the minimum across rows, and the analysis that gives the
$\hat f_a \le f_a + \epsilon F$ guarantee with probability at least
$1 - \delta$ when $w = \lceil e / \epsilon \rceil$ and
$d = \lceil \log(1 / \delta) \rceil$. The proof in their Theorem 1 is
the Markov-plus-union-bound argument reproduced in \Cref{sec:countmin};
the chapter adds nothing to the proof itself. What the chapter adds is
the reading of CountMin as a $\Bernoulli{\mathbb{N}}$ instance:
the channel matrix $Q$ has rows indexed by true counts and columns by
estimated counts, the upper-triangular shape encodes the one-sided
$\hat f_a \ge f_a$ guarantee, and the merge operation (entry-wise
addition of two tables with matched parameters) is a composition under
the chapter~9 framework rather than a CountMin-specific recipe.
Cormode and Muthukrishnan also catalog applications (heavy hitters,
quantile estimation, inner-product estimation) that the chapter does
not cover; their paper is the right entry point for readers who want
the full operational repertoire.

\paragraph{The HyperLogLog cardinality estimator.}
The HyperLogLog construction of \Cref{sec:hyperloglog} and the
$1.04 / \sqrt{m}$ standard relative error of
\Cref{prop:hyperloglog-error} are due to
\textcite{flajolet-et-al-2007}, building on the earlier LogLog and
probabilistic-counting work of Flajolet and Martin. Their Theorem 1 is
an exact generating-function calculation that gives both the bias
correction $\alpha_m$ and the asymptotic standard deviation factor; the
chapter quotes the constants but does not reproduce the derivation. The
limiting log-normal shape of the estimation error, which underwrites
the symmetric-on-a-log-scale rows of the $Q$ matrix in
\Cref{sec:hyperloglog}, is also their result, as are the small-$m$
corrections and the tabulated $\alpha_m$ values that production
HyperLogLog implementations use. The merge operation (entry-wise
maximum on the register array) is the natural composition under the
underlying max-of-leading-zeros structure and is treated as a
chapter~9 composition rather than as a HyperLogLog-specific operation;
the equivalence between the merged sketch and a HyperLogLog of the
union stream is left as an exercise at the end of the chapter.

\paragraph{MinHash and Jaccard similarity.}
The MinHash sketch of \Cref{sec:minhash-lsh} is due to
\textcite{broder-1997}, whose original setting was near-duplicate
detection across a large web corpus. The key probabilistic identity,
$\Pr[\min h(A) = \min h(B)] = J(A, B)$ for a uniform hash $h$, is from
that paper; the $k$-hash signature, the estimator
$\hat J = (1/k) |\{i : \sigma_i(A) = \sigma_i(B)\}|$, and the Hoeffding
concentration bound of \Cref{prop:minhash-error} are the standard
treatment that follows from the identity. The reading of MinHash as a
$\Bernoulli{[0, 1]}$ instance, with rows of $Q$ indexed by true Jaccard
similarity and columns by the empirical fraction of agreeing signature
positions, is again the chapter~9 framework rather than the original
formulation, but the underlying Binomial-in-$k$ structure of $\hat J$ is
already implicit in Broder's analysis.

\paragraph{Locality-Sensitive Hashing.}
The LSH construction of \Cref{sec:minhash-lsh} is due to
\textcite{indyk-motwani-1998}, who developed it for approximate nearest
neighbor search in high dimensions and proved that the curse of
dimensionality can be partially evaded by hashing to buckets in which
nearby points collide with high probability and far points collide with
low probability. The MinHash-plus-band structure of the chapter is the
specialization of their general LSH framework to the Jaccard metric:
MinHash signatures provide the locality-sensitive hash family, the
band-and-row parameter sweep controls the precision/recall trade-off,
and the bucketing turns an $O(N^2)$ pairwise comparison into an
$O(N)$-amortized indexed lookup. Indyk and Motwani's paper covers
LSH families for Hamming distance and $\ell_p$ norms in addition to
the Jaccard case the chapter develops; their framework is the right
entry point for readers who want the general theory rather than its
MinHash specialization.
