\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch10-bridge}

Three sketches, one skeleton, differing in output type and channel shape.
CountMin (\Cref{sec:countmin}) is the frequency sketch: $T = \mathbb{N}$,
one-sided, an upper-triangular $Q$ with tail $\Prob{\hat f_a \ge f_a + \epsilon
F} \le \delta$ (\Cref{thm:countmin-bound}). HyperLogLog (\Cref{sec:hyperloglog})
is the cardinality sketch: $T = \mathbb{N}$, two-sided and log-normal, with
relative error $1.04/\sqrt{m}$ (\Cref{prop:hyperloglog-error}) setting the band
width. MinHash (\Cref{sec:minhash-lsh}) is the similarity sketch: $T = [0,1]$,
binomial rows, Hoeffding-controlled at signature length $k$
(\Cref{prop:minhash-error}). Each comes with a construction, a row-by-row error
bound on $Q$, and a merge rule.

The merge rules share a structure this chapter notes but does not axiomatize.
Merging CountMin tables is entry-wise \emph{addition}, HyperLogLog registers
entry-wise \emph{maximum}, MinHash signatures (forming the union signature)
entry-wise \emph{minimum}; each is associative, commutative, and has an identity
(the zero table, the zero register bank, the all-$\infty$ signature), and each
respects the \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} channel structure. The merge rule is
dictated by the algebra of the underlying $\Bernoulli{T}$ type, which
\Cref{ch:algebra-types} treats systematically, showing the three sketches to be
instances of the product, sum, and list constructors rather than ad hoc
designs. Before that, \Cref{ch:randomized-algorithms} turns from streaming
summaries to randomized algorithms, where a one-sided Monte Carlo procedure is a
$\Bernoulli{\{\text{accept}, \text{reject}\}}$ instance and amplification by
repetition is composition of one channel with itself, and
\Cref{ch:approximate-relations} lifts the framework from functions to relations
for join and multi-attribute queries. The three threads converge in
\Cref{ch:algebra-types}, the close of Part~III.
