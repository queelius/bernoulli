\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch15}

\paragraph{The BPHF construction.}
The construction of \Cref{def:bphf} and the space accounting of
\Cref{prop:bphf-space} follow \textcite{towell-bernoulli-phf}, ``The
perfect hash filter,'' which introduced the BPHF as the FPR-zero
counterpart of the BHF. The manuscript works in greater generality: the
rank-bearing construction of \Cref{sec:bphf-construction} extends to
richer per-element values (relevance scores at arbitrary precision,
sorted positions in any application ordering, vector-valued metadata
under a chosen encoding), and the ranked search of \Cref{sec:ranked-search}
is one of several deployment patterns it catalogues. A reader wanting the
broader framing, with the BPHF as an instance of an optimal
perfect-hash-addressed Bernoulli map, should treat the manuscript as the
next reading.

\paragraph{The perfect-hashing literature.}
The perfect hash $\phi$ inherits its construction and bounds from the
literature surveyed in \S14.7. The two foundational entries are
\textcite{carter-wegman-1979}, ``Universal Classes of Hash Functions,''
which established the pairwise independence the rank-comparison check
rests on, and \textcite{belazzougui-2009}, ``Hash, Displace, and
Compress'' (the BDZ scheme), which achieves $O(n)$ expected construction
and $O(n \log \log \log n / \log n)$ output bits at under $3$ bits per
element for $n$ in the millions. The BPHF inherits both unchanged,
because the perfect-hash construction depends on $A$ alone and not on
what is stored at each slot, and the choice among the displacement-based
families (with Fredman-Komlos-Szemeredi, Hagerup-Tholey, MWHC, and BBHash
the historical line BDZ caps) is an implementation detail that does not
affect the space or time bounds.

\paragraph{The ranked-search literature.}
The application of \Cref{sec:ranked-search} sits in a long line of work on
probabilistic information retrieval, the standard treatment being
\textcite{manning-raghavan-schutze}, \emph{Introduction to Information
Retrieval}. The line runs from the Robertson-Sp\"arck Jones framework of
the 1970s through TF-IDF weighting and BM25 to modern
neural-embedding scoring. The chapter is agnostic about which scoring
function $s$ supplies the ranks; the BPHF accommodates any whose output
reduces to a per-document rank at build time. Its contribution is not the
scoring function but the post-scoring indexing structure for top-$k$
serving.

\paragraph{The \emph{maph} C++ library.}
The production-grade implementation is the \emph{maph} C++ library,
distributed with the \textcite{towell-bernoulli-phf} manuscript: a
header-only C++17 realization of the BPHF build and query paths over a
configurable displacement-based perfect-hash backend. It is the reference
against which the \Cref{prop:bphf-space} bound can be checked empirically;
the asymptotic constants the chapter reports for the perfect-hash overhead
match its measured values to within the variation across backend.
