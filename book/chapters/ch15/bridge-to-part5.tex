\section{Bridge to Part V}
\label{sec:bridge-to-part5}

The chapter has assembled the constructive answer to the FPR-zero
arm of the perfect-filter line. The BPHF of \Cref{def:bphf} reaches
$\fprate = 0$ in the witness query model, is exact at full resolution
with a true perfect hash, and trades a controlled $\fnrate \approx
1 - m/n$ for a smaller table through a $k$-perfect hash of $m$ slots.
It spends $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil + o(1)$ bits per element from
\Cref{prop:bphf-space}, and applies cleanly to ranked retrieval
through the construction \Cref{sec:ranked-search} worked out. The chapter's bridge has two threads to tie before Part~V opens:
the place of chapters~14 and~15 in Part~IV's larger architecture,
and the new questions Part~V picks up that the present part does not
address.

\subsection*{Closing Part IV}

Part~IV opened with the chapter~8 entropy floor, which set a lower
bound on the bits per element any one-sided approximate set must
spend. Chapter~14 introduced the BHF as the construction that
reaches the floor on the FNR-zero arm of the perfect-filter line,
with the $\log_2 e \approx 1.44$ multiplicative overhead the
standard Bloom filter pays disappearing into a constant-bit additive
overhead. The present chapter introduces the BPHF as the construction
that reaches the analogous bound on the FPR-zero arm, with the bit
budget shifted from $\lceil -\log_2 \fprate \rceil$ to $\lceil \log_2
n \rceil$ and the controlled error rate shifted from $\fprate$ to
$\fnrate$. The two constructions together cover the perfect-filter
line at the asymptotic bit-budget optimum on each side; the part
closes with both arms supplied with their constructive realization.

The architecture has a clean shape in retrospect. Chapters~5 and~6
named the perfect-filter line (\Cref{def:perfect-filter-line},
building on \Cref{def:one-sided}). Chapter~8 derived the entropy
floor (\Cref{thm:entropy-floor}) as the bit-budget lower bound any
structure on the line must respect. Chapters~14 and~15 constructed
the two optimum points on the line, each at one extreme error axis,
each reaching the floor up to integer rounding plus a sublinear
correction. The space-accuracy duality of the chapter~6 geometry
becomes a deployment-time decision once the two chapters have
supplied the operating points: applications where false negatives
are unacceptable take the FNR-zero arm and use the BHF; applications
where false positives are unacceptable take the FPR-zero arm and use
the BPHF.

\subsection*{Part V preview}

Part~V picks up two threads. The first is diagnostic: chapter~16
catalogues the structural ways a system can fail to be Bernoulli
(varying rates, correlated errors, adversarial inputs that break the
fixed-rate assumption the chapter~5 axioms rest on), giving the
practitioner a checklist for when the framework applies cleanly and
when it requires modification. The second is generative: chapters~17
and~18 develop \emph{cipher maps}, the algebraic framework that
recasts the BHF/BPHF perfect-hash-and-fingerprint architecture as
the building block for encrypted search, with the perfect hash
replaced by a trapdoor function whose forward direction is public
and reverse direction is private. The FPR and FNR guarantees of the
present part carry over into the cryptographic setting; the
architectural template is the same.

\subsection*{What \texorpdfstring{\S15.6}{section 15.6} supplies}

The construction the chapter has developed has antecedents in the
perfect-hashing literature, in the universal-hashing literature on
which the rank-comparison check rests, and in the ranked-retrieval
literature that the application of \Cref{sec:ranked-search} draws
on. The chapter's prose has cited these sources sparingly to keep
the argument moving at its own pace; the bibliographic notes of
\Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch15} attach the construction to its published
antecedents and name the implementations (most prominently the
\emph{maph} C++ library) that are the production-grade realizations
of the design the chapter has presented. The next section is the
place to consult for the chapter's external dependencies and for
pointers to the literature the practitioner should know about when
deploying a BPHF in production.
