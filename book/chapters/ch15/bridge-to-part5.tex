\section{Bridge to Part V}
\label{sec:bridge-to-part5}

The chapter assembled the constructive answer to the FPR-zero arm of the
perfect-filter line. The BPHF of \Cref{def:bphf} reaches $\fprate = 0$ in
the witness query model, is exact at full resolution, and trades a
controlled $\fnrate \approx 1 - m/n$ for a smaller table through a
$k$-perfect hash, at $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil + o(1)$ bits per element
(\Cref{prop:bphf-space}), applying cleanly to ranked retrieval through
the construction of \Cref{sec:ranked-search}.

\subsection*{Closing Part IV}

Part~IV has a clean shape in retrospect. Chapters~5 and~6 named the
perfect-filter line (\Cref{def:perfect-filter-line}, building on
\Cref{def:one-sided}); chapter~8 derived the entropy floor
(\Cref{thm:entropy-floor}) as the bit-budget lower bound any structure on
the line must respect; chapters~14 and~15 constructed the two operating
points, each at one extreme error axis, each reaching its bound up to
integer rounding and a sublinear correction. The BHF reaches the floor on
the FNR-zero arm, dissolving the standard Bloom filter's $\log_2 e
\approx 1.44$ overhead into a constant additive one
(\Cref{thm:bhf-optimality}); the BPHF reaches the analogous bound on the
FPR-zero arm, with the budget shifted from $\lceil -\log_2 \fprate
\rceil$ to $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$ and the controlled error from
$\fprate$ to $\fnrate$. The two are the endpoints $(\fprate, 0)$ and
$(0, \fnrate)$ of the line; a structure that tolerates both errors can
interpolate between them, trading one axis against the other, but the
endpoints are where the bit budget is minimized for a one-sided
guarantee. The space-accuracy duality of the chapter~6 geometry becomes
a deployment decision once the operating points are in hand:
false-negative-intolerant applications take the BHF, false-positive-
intolerant ones the BPHF.

\subsection*{Part V preview}

Part~V picks up two threads. The first is diagnostic: chapter~16
catalogues the structural ways a system fails to be Bernoulli, varying
rates, correlated errors, and adversarial inputs that break the
fixed-rate axioms, giving a checklist for when the framework applies
cleanly and when it needs modification. The witness model's dependence on
an unforgeable rank (\Cref{rem:witness-is-query-model}) is one entry in
that catalogue and the hinge to the second thread, which is generative:
chapters~17 and~18 develop \emph{cipher maps}, recasting the
perfect-hash-and-fingerprint architecture as the building block for
encrypted search, with the perfect hash replaced by a trapdoor whose
forward direction is public and reverse private. The FPR and FNR
guarantees of the present part carry into the cryptographic setting on
the same architectural template. \Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch15} attaches the
construction to its antecedents, including the \emph{maph} C++ library
that is its production-grade realization.
