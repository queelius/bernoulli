\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch14-bridge}

The chapter answered the optimality question chapter~8 left open. The
Bernoulli Hash Function (\Cref{def:bhf}) reaches the entropy floor of
\Cref{thm:entropy-floor} up to integer rounding and a sublinear
correction (\Cref{thm:bhf-optimality}), meets the FPR target under a
standard uniformity assumption (\Cref{prop:bhf-fpr}), and pays for the
improvement in static-set restrictions and an $O(n)$ build
(\Cref{sec:construction-time}). The $\log_2 e \approx 1.44$ factor the
Bloom filter spends over the floor (\Cref{prop:bloom-overhead})
collapses to a constant additive overhead. The construction draws on a
decades-old perfect-hash literature and the universal-hashing machinery
the Bloom filter also uses, differing only in where the hash is applied;
\Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch14} names the antecedents.

The BHF occupies one end of the perfect-filter line of
\Cref{def:perfect-filter-line}, the FNR-zero membership structures. The
other end holds the FPR-zero ranked-search structures, the perfect hash
filters of chapter~15. The two share a skeleton, a perfect hash and a
fingerprint table, and trade their error modes: the BHF accepts a small
false-positive rate to guarantee no false negatives; the BPHF
(Bernoulli Perfect Hash Filter) accepts a small false-negative rate to
guarantee no false positives. The duality is exact, the BPHF for a set
being the BHF for its complement, modulo the practical detail that the
complement is usually infinite. The applications split accordingly: the
BHF where false negatives are unacceptable (a cache that must not miss a
stored element, a routing layer that must not drop a known address), the
BPHF where false positives are unacceptable (a precision-oriented index
that must not return non-matching results, an authorization filter that
must not admit an unauthorized identifier).

The BPHF construction mirrors the BHF closely: the same two ingredients,
a nearly identical build and query, the space bound $\lceil -\log_2
\fnrate \rceil$ bits per element with $\fnrate$ in the role of the target
error, and analogous trade-offs against the FPR-zero streaming
structures. \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} reuses the present
derivation with the error-mode swap and turns to the ranked-search
setting on the FPR-zero half of the line.
