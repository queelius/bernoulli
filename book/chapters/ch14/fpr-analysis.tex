\section{False-Positive Rate Analysis}
\label{sec:fpr-analysis}

\Cref{sec:bhf-construction} previewed the false-positive argument: for
$x \notin A$ the query compares two independent uniform $r$-bit
strings, matching with probability $2^{-r}$. This section makes the
argument careful. The setup is small, the argument is two cases, the
bound is $\fprate \le 2^{-r}$, and the only nontrivial input is the
uniformity of the fingerprint hash $g$, which the universal-hashing
literature supplies.

\subsection*{Setup}

Fix $A \subseteq \Universe$ with $|A| = n$, a perfect hash $\phi : A
\to [n]$ extended to all of $\Universe$, and a fingerprint hash $g :
\Universe \to \{0, 1\}^r$ with $r = \lceil -\log_2 \fprate \rceil$. The
table is populated by \eqref{eq:bhf-insert}: $F[\phi(a)] = g(a)$ for
every $a \in A$. For a query point $x \notin A$,
\[
  \Prob{\textsc{Query}(x) = \top \Given x \notin A} \;=\; ?
\]
where the probability is over the choice of $g$ ($\phi$ is fixed by the
build phase). The query reads slot $\phi(x)$, computes $g(x)$, and
returns $\top$ iff $F[\phi(x)] = g(x)$; the question is when that holds
for a non-member.

\subsection*{Two cases for \texorpdfstring{$\phi(x)$}{phi(x)}}

$\phi$ is injective on $A$ but defined on all of $\Universe$ by its
extension rule. For $x \notin A$, two situations arise.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Case (a): $\phi(x) \in \phi(A)$.} The slot $\phi(x) = i$
coincides with $\phi(a^*)$ for the unique $a^* \in A$ mapped there, so
$F[i] = g(a^*)$ and the query returns $\top$ iff $g(a^*) = g(x)$. Since
$x \neq a^*$, uniformity of $g$ makes $g(a^*)$ and $g(x)$ independent
uniform on $\{0, 1\}^r$, colliding with probability $2^{-r}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Case (b): $\phi(x) \notin \phi(A)$.} The extension may
flag $x$ as out-of-range, returning $\bot$ directly (no false
positive), or map $x$ to an unused slot $i \in [n] \setminus \phi(A)$.
An unused slot holds either an explicit sentinel designed to mismatch
every fingerprint (collision probability zero) or a residual value
independent of $g(x)$ (collision probability at most $2^{-r}$). In the
sentinel variant, which the BHF assumes by default, case (b)
contributes nothing.

The worst case is every $x \notin A$ falling into case (a), giving
$\fprate = 2^{-r}$ exactly; the best case flags case (b) out-of-range,
removing the non-image contribution. Both bracket the bound.

\subsection*{The bound}

\begin{proposition}[BHF false-positive rate]
\label{prop:bhf-fpr}
Let the BHF of \Cref{def:bhf} be constructed for $A$ with parameters
$\phi$, $g$, and $r = \lceil -\log_2 \fprate \rceil$, and assume $g$ is
uniformly distributed on $\{0, 1\}^r$ over the query point. For every
$x \notin A$,
\[
  \fprate_{\text{BHF}} \;=\; \Prob{\textsc{Query}(x) = \top \Given
  x \notin A} \;\le\; 2^{-r} \;\le\; \fprate.
\]
The BHF meets the target false-positive rate by construction.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is the second half of the optimality story: with
\Cref{thm:bhf-optimality} it certifies the BHF as both space-optimal
(minimum bits per element up to rounding) and accuracy-correct
(delivers the target FPR). The inequality is tight in the sentinel-free
variant (case (b) at arbitrary residual contents achieves $2^{-r}$ for
every non-member) and strict in the sentinel variant (case (b)
contributes zero, so the FPR is $2^{-r}$ weighted by the probability
$\phi(x) \in \phi(A)$, strictly below $1$ when $\Universe$ extends
beyond $A$). The chained $2^{-r} \le \fprate$ is a direct consequence
of $r = \lceil -\log_2 \fprate \rceil$: ceiling rounding forces $r \ge
-\log_2 \fprate$, hence $2^{-r} \le \fprate$, with the over-achievement
the same rounding slack \Cref{sec:space-analysis} measured on the space
side. One integer bit of width buys one integer bit of FPR precision.

\subsection*{The uniformity hypothesis}

The hypothesis that $g$ is uniform over the query point deserves
comment, because it can fail adversarially: an attacker who knows $g$
can choose $x$ with $g(x) = g(a^*)$ for a target $a^*$, driving the
per-query FPR to $1$. Uniformity protects the average case, where query
points are drawn independently of the implementation's choice of $g$,
which covers most data-structure use.

The requirement is exactly pairwise independence of $g$. The collision
probability $\Prob{g(a^*) = g(x)} = 2^{-r}$ holds whenever $g(a^*)$ and
$g(x)$ are pairwise independent, and \emph{universal hashing} in the
sense of \textcite{carter-wegman-1979} delivers a family in which, for
every $a^* \neq x$, $\Prob{g(a^*) = g(x)} \le 1/|\text{codomain}|$ over
the random choice of $g$. The BHF selects $g$ from such a family at
build time and fixes it; the bound then applies pointwise over
non-adversarial queries. In practice $g$ is a wide cryptographic hash
(SHA-256, BLAKE3) truncated to $r$ bits or a high-quality
non-cryptographic hash (MurmurHash3, xxHash), and the adversarial case
is handled by salting $g$ with a build-time secret, denying the
attacker advance knowledge of the hash. \Cref{ch:not-bernoulli} returns
to the adversarial setting; here the non-adversarial hypothesis is the
operating assumption.

\subsection*{Worked check at \texorpdfstring{$r = 7$}{r = 7}}

The worked example of \Cref{sec:bhf-construction} took $\fprate = 0.01$,
$r = 7$. The proposition predicts $\fprate_{\text{BHF}} \le 2^{-7} =
1/128 \approx 0.0078$, below the target by a factor $0.0078/0.01 \approx
0.78$, so every non-member query has at most a $0.78\%$ chance of a
false positive against the $1\%$ target. The $22\%$ over-achievement is
the rounding slack, and it matches on both sides of the trade-off:
$1 - 2^{-r}/\fprate = 1 - 2^{6.644 - 7} = 1 - 2^{-0.356} \approx 0.219$,
the same rounding that costs $0.356$ bits per element on the space side.
At a power-of-two target the slack vanishes: $\fprate = 2^{-7}$ is met
exactly with $r = 7$. Awkward targets pay at most one bit per element
and at most a factor of $2$ in FPR, small against the Bloom filter's
multiplicative $\log_2 e \approx 1.44$.

The check closes the loop. The construction satisfies the FNR-zero
guarantee unconditionally and the FPR target under the uniformity
hypothesis, at a budget of $\lceil -\log_2 \fprate \rceil$ plus the
sublinear perfect-hash term. \Cref{sec:construction-time} turns to
build time, query latency, and the static-versus-streaming trade-off
that distinguishes the BHF from the Bloom filter in deployment.
