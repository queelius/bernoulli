\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch14}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
The floor that frames the chapter, $n \log_2(1/\fprate)$ bits for an
FNR-zero approximate set, is due to \textcite{carter-et-al-1978}; its
two-sided extension, with a bounded fraction of false negatives, is
settled to leading order by \textcite[Prop.~1]{pagh-rodler-2001}. The
BHF of \Cref{def:bhf} follows the program's manuscript
\parencite{towell-bernoulli-hash-function}, but its mechanism, one seed
required to satisfy $n$ independent Bernoulli constraints, with a seed
index of $\log_2(1/s) + O(1)$ bits and $1/s$ expected trials, is the
standard single-seed baseline of seed search. It is analyzed as such by
\textcite[Sec.~1.1]{lehmann-et-al-2025} and used on small buckets by
RecSplit and ShockHash
\parencite{esposito-graf-vigna-2020,lehmann-sanders-walzer-2024}. The
perfect hash filter, a minimal perfect hash with
$\lceil \log_2(1/\fprate) \rceil$-bit fingerprints and its $\log_2 e$
overhead, is described by \textcite[Sec.~1]{dietzfelbinger-pagh-2008};
the $n \log_2 e$ lower bound for minimal perfect hashing is
\textcite{fredman-komlos-1984}, and the constructions cited in
\Cref{sec:construction-time} run from \textcite{fredman-komlos-szemeredi-1984}
through \textcite{botelho-pagh-ziviani-2007}, \textcite{belazzougui-2009},
and \textcite{limasset-et-al-2017}. Retrieval-based filters that approach
the floor at linear build time are the Bloomier filter
\parencite{chazelle-et-al-2004}, the retrieval structures of
\textcite{dietzfelbinger-pagh-2008}, xor and binary fuse filters
\parencite{graf-lemire-2020,graf-lemire-2022}, and ribbon
\parencite{dietzfelbinger-et-al-2026-ribbon}; dynamic alternatives are
the cuckoo filter \parencite{fan-cuckoo-2014}, the quotient filter
\parencite{bender-quotient-2012}, and the structure of
\textcite{pagh-pagh-rao-2005}. Fingerprint uniformity rests on the
universal hashing of \textcite{carter-wegman-1979}.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
A presentation of the salt-search BHF and the perfect hash filter side
by side as the two ends of a space-versus-build trade on the FNR-zero
arm, with the BHF's expected size bounded on both sides
(\Cref{thm:bhf-optimality}) and the perfect hash filter's break-even with
the Bloom filter stated with real addressing costs. It also records two
corrections to the source manuscript and to this book's earlier drafts:
the adaptive threshold trades recall rather than saving space, and its
exact discrete law is a binomial tail (\Cref{rem:adaptive-threshold});
and the perfect-hash-addressed table, which an earlier draft of this
chapter presented as the BHF, does not meet the floor, because no minimal
perfect hash is sublinear.

\paragraph{Left out.}
Bucketed seed-search encodings and their analysis, dynamic and
expandable filters, cache-efficient layouts, and measured throughput.
The map extension appears only through its success probability; the
source manuscript develops it.
