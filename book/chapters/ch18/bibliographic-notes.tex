\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch18}

\paragraph{The encrypted-search thesis.}
The chapter's confidentiality analysis and the Bloom-filter
construction it builds on are developed at length in
\textcite{towell-encrypted-search-thesis}, ``On the
Confidentiality of Bloom-Filter-Based Encrypted Search,'' the
master's thesis that first laid out the framework's
encrypted-search reading. The thesis treats the construction in
operational detail (key schedule, indexing structure, query
protocol) the present chapter has abbreviated, and develops the
adversarial-estimation problem of
\Cref{sec:adversarial-estimation} as the central concern. The
present chapter has reorganized the thesis material around the
Bernoulli framework that the rest of the book builds on; the
thesis predates the framework's current formulation and reads as
a self-contained application of approximate-membership ideas to
the encrypted-search setting.

\paragraph{Estimating confidentiality via multi-armed bandits.}
The MAB-based estimation framework of
\Cref{sec:adversarial-estimation} draws on
\textcite{towell-es-conf-mab}, ``Estimating encrypted-search
confidentiality via multi-armed bandits,'' which developed the
moving-average bootstrap CI as a tighter alternative to the
union-bound construction the section described. The paper
presented at IEEE CloudCom 2016 frames the encrypted-search
estimation problem as a bandit problem and develops the
adversarial-sampling CI in its full generality; the chapter's
treatment summarizes the framing and the worked example,
leaving the bootstrap construction and the empirical
calibration data to the paper. Readers interested in the
estimator's finite-sample behavior should consult the paper's
simulation studies; the chapter's worked example reproduces
the qualitative gap between iid Chernoff and MAB UCB intervals
but does not exhibit the bootstrap construction's tighter
behavior under concentrated workloads.

\paragraph{The searchable-symmetric-encryption literature.}
The chapter's Bloom-filter constructions sit in a literature on
searchable symmetric encryption (SSE) that traces back to
\textcite{song-wagner-perrig-2000}, ``Practical Techniques for
Searches on Encrypted Data,'' the foundational paper that
introduced the encrypted-search problem in the form
\Cref{sec:encrypted-search-problem} restated. Song-Wagner-Perrig
developed a stream-cipher-based construction that supports
keyword search on encrypted documents without revealing the
queried keyword to the server; the paper's threat model and
operational assumptions are the ones the present chapter has
inherited. \textcite{curtmola-et-al-2006}, ``Searchable
Symmetric Encryption: Improved Definitions and Efficient
Constructions,'' tightened the security definitions to the
adaptive-adversary setting and gave the first sub-linear-time
SSE construction; the paper is the standard reference for SSE
security notions and the entry point to the modern SSE
literature, which has grown into a substantial subfield in the
two decades since. The chapter has used the SSE literature for
the threat model and the construction style without entering
the formal security-definition machinery, which is
necessary for a cryptographic-engineering treatment and which
the SSE literature surveys cover in depth.

\paragraph{Maximizing confidentiality.}
The PPV-as-confidentiality identification of
\Cref{sec:confidentiality-measures} extends a line of analysis
the cipher-maps manuscript started in
\textcite{towell-maximizing-confidentiality} (already cited
from chapter~17), ``Maximizing confidentiality in encrypted
search,'' which developed the framework's confidentiality
measure in the encrypted-search setting before the broader
trapdoor-computing framing of chapter~17 was in place. The
manuscript treats the trade-off curve quantitatively, gives a
closed-form solution for the optimal $\fprate^*$ at a
target confidentiality, and addresses the multi-query
accumulation behavior that the chapter's
\Cref{prop:multi-query-leakage} stated without proof. The
manuscript and the present chapter cover overlapping ground;
where they diverge, the chapter has preferred the framework's
algebraic reading and left the manuscript's
information-theoretic supplements for the reader who wants the
quantitative refinement.
