\section{Closing the Book}
\label{sec:closing-the-book}

The book has built one framework in five parts and eighteen
chapters. Each part lifted the preceding part's algebra to a
larger domain, and each chapter inside a part filled in one
component of the lift. The chapter that ends here is the final
application of the framework to a working construction; this
section closes the book by tracing the arc that the eighteen
chapters built.

\subsection*{The atom}

The book opened (\Cref{ch:noisy-bit}) with a single object: the
noisy bit, a Boolean value transmitted across a binary channel
that flips it with a known probability. The opening chapter named
the framework's two atomic parameters, the false-positive rate
$\fprate$ (the probability a $0$ comes through as $1$) and the
false-negative rate $\fnrate$ (the probability a $1$ comes through
as $0$), and introduced the Bernoulli square as the parameter
space they jointly inhabit. \Cref{ch:axioms-kronecker} formalized
the atom: the type $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ of \Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}
collects all $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ Bernoulli channels; the
chapter~4 axioms (element independence in \Cref{ax:element-indep},
block-rate independence in \Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}) prescribe
how multiple atoms combine; the chapter~4 Kronecker theorem
(\Cref{thm:kronecker}) gives the operational composition law in
matrix form. Part~I closed with the framework's whole machinery
distilled into one $2 \times 2$ matrix per atom and a Kronecker
product for composition.

\subsection*{The lift to sets}

Part~II lifted the atom from a single bit to a whole set of bits,
one per element of an underlying universe. \Cref{ch:approximate-sets}
framed the lift: a random approximate set is a function from a
plaintext set to a sequence of Bernoulli atoms, one per element,
and the chapter~5 axioms inherit unchanged from chapter~4.
\Cref{ch:set-composition} worked out the composition theorem for
set-algebraic operations (union, intersection, complement,
difference); the framework's two rates aggregate predictably
across set algebra, and the perfect-filter line of chapter~6
\S6.4 marks the structures (Bloom filters chief among them) that
sit on the $\fnrate = 0$ axis. \Cref{ch:classification-measures}
lifted the chapter~4 atomic rates to the chapter~7 derived measures
(precision, recall, PPV, NPV, $\Fone$, Youden's $J$), and
\Cref{ch:information-bounds} priced the space cost of approximate
membership at the entropy floor $-\log_2 \fprate$ bits per element.
Part~II closed with the algebra of approximate sets fully priced:
construction is bounded by Shannon, composition is bounded by
Kronecker, classification is bounded by the chapter~7 posterior.

\subsection*{The lift to maps and types}

Part~III lifted the algebra from sets to functions.
\Cref{ch:approximate-functions} took the chapter~4 binary channel
to its $n$-ary generalization, the random approximate map from
plaintext keys to approximate values. \Cref{ch:sketches-estimators}
treated the streaming sketches that compress the map into bounded
working memory; \Cref{ch:randomized-algorithms} extended the
framework to the randomized algorithm setting, where the algorithm
itself rolls dice that the framework prices; and
\Cref{ch:approximate-relations} took the relational-algebra lift,
where the map's domain and codomain both carry approximate
structure. \Cref{ch:algebra-types} closed the part by unifying the
preceding chapters under a single algebraic-types framework: every
Bernoulli-lifted structure is an instance of the parametric type
$\Bernoulli{T}$ for some type $T$, and the part's compositions
inherit from the algebra of types.

\subsection*{The constructive optimum}

Part~IV asked whether the algebra's space-accuracy lower bound is
achievable in practice. \Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} answered
yes for the Bloom-filter-style random approximate set: the
Bernoulli hash function (BHF) achieves the chapter~8 entropy floor
within a vanishing additive overhead, and the construction is
explicit. \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} gave the FPR-zero
counterpart: a perfect hash filter (BPHF) for the
ranked-retrieval setting where the map from queries to documents
is deterministic and the only error budget is the false-negative
side. Part~IV exhibited the algebra's theoretical bounds as
operationally achievable, not merely necessary.

\subsection*{The boundary and applications}

Part~V identified where the framework holds and where it does not.
\Cref{ch:not-bernoulli} catalogued the boundary: when the per-query
rates are not iid, when the channel matrix is non-stationary, when
the inputs are chosen adversarially, the framework's predictions
fail in predictable ways and have to be either patched (with
distinct estimators and per-input bounds) or abandoned in favor of
a different framework. \Cref{ch:cipher-maps-trapdoor} took the
algebra to the cryptographic setting: cipher maps generalize the
Bernoulli atoms to the trapdoor-computing paradigm, and the
chapter~7 PPV becomes the cryptographic confidentiality measure
in a re-reading that the chapter made explicit. \Cref{ch:encrypted-search},
the present chapter, instantiated cipher maps for a working
application: encrypted search with Bloom-filter-based indexing,
where the framework's rate-prevalence triangle prices the
trade-off between recovery and leakage in operational terms.

\subsection*{What comes next}

The framework is not exhausted by the eighteen chapters. Three
research threads point past the book's current scope: approximate
query languages that lift the chapter~12 relational algebra to a
declarative query interface with framework-priced execution costs;
federated computation, where the cipher-map machinery generalizes
to multiple parties each holding partial trapdoor keys; and the
relation to differential privacy, which uses a structurally
similar noise-budget abstraction (with the noise being explicitly
controlled rather than emergent) and which the framework should
ultimately subsume as a constrained sibling. Each thread is a
working extension, not a planned chapter; the book closes with
the framework in a state that admits the extensions but does not
require them.
