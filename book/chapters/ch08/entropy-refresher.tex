\section{Shannon Entropy Refresher}
\label{sec:entropy-refresher}

The chapter's two results, the BSC capacity (\Cref{sec:bsc-capacity}) and the
entropy floor for approximate sets (\Cref{sec:entropy-floor}), rest on a small
slice of Shannon's information theory: the entropy of a random variable, its
binary special case, and mutual information. We collect them and fix notation;
\textcite{cover-thomas} is the depth reference.

\paragraph{Entropy.}
For a discrete $X$ on a finite alphabet $\mathcal{X}$, the \emph{Shannon
entropy} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:entropy}
  H(X) \;=\; -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \Prob{X = x}\,\log_2 \Prob{X = x},
\end{equation}
with $0 \log_2 0 = 0$, measured in bits. Operationally $H(X)$ is the average
number of yes/no questions an optimal binary search needs to identify a draw:
$0$ for a constant, $\log_2|\mathcal{X}|$ for the uniform distribution. By
Shannon's source-coding theorem it is also the tight lower bound on the
expected length of any prefix-free code for $X$, the reading
\Cref{sec:entropy-floor} uses.

\paragraph{Binary entropy.}
For $X \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, \eqref{eq:entropy} collapses to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:binary-entropy}
  H(p) \;=\; -p \log_2 p - (1 - p)\log_2(1 - p), \qquad p \in [0, 1],
\end{equation}
overloading $H$ (a random variable in $H(X)$, a scalar in $H(p)$; context
disambiguates). Three properties recur in this chapter and chapter~14: $H$ is
strictly concave ($H''(p) = -1/[p(1-p)\ln 2] < 0$); it peaks at $H(1/2) = 1$
(a fair coin is the most uncertain bit); and $H(0) = H(1) = 0$. It is
symmetric, $H(p) = H(1-p)$, with $H(0.01) \approx 0.081$, $H(0.1) \approx
0.469$. Distinct from $H(\fprate)$ is the \emph{self-information} (surprisal)
$-\log_2 \fprate$ of a single event of probability $\fprate$: the floor of
\Cref{sec:entropy-floor} is $-\log_2 \fprate$ bits per element, the cost of one
such event, not the indicator entropy $H(\fprate) \approx -\fprate \log_2
\fprate$ that weights it by $\fprate$.

\paragraph{Mutual information and capacity.}
For jointly distributed $X, Y$, the \emph{mutual information}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mutual-info}
  I(X; Y) \;=\; H(X) - H(X \mid Y) \;=\; H(Y) - H(Y \mid X)
\end{equation}
measures how much observing one reduces uncertainty about the other, with
$H(X \mid Y) = \sum_y \Prob{Y = y}\,H(X \mid Y = y)$ the conditional entropy.
Equivalently
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mutual-info-joint}
  I(X; Y) \;=\; \sum_{x, y} \Prob{X = x, Y = y}
    \log_2 \frac{\Prob{X = x, Y = y}}{\Prob{X = x}\Prob{Y = y}},
\end{equation}
a Kullback--Leibler divergence between the joint and the product of marginals,
hence nonnegative and zero iff $X, Y$ are independent. For a channel with fixed
$\Prob{Y \mid X}$, $I(X; Y)$ depends on the input distribution, and the
\emph{capacity} is the maximum,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:capacity-def}
  C \;=\; \max_{\Prob{X}} I(X; Y),
\end{equation}
the most information one channel use can convey. \Cref{sec:bsc-capacity}
evaluates it for the BSC of \Cref{sec:bsc} and gets $C = 1 - H(\fprate)$;
\Cref{sec:entropy-floor} applies the source-coding reading to approximate-set
representations and gets the $-\log_2 \fprate$ floor;
\Cref{sec:bloom-gap} measures the standard Bloom filter's $\log_2 e \approx
1.443$ overhead above it.
