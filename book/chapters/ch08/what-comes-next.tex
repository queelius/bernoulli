\section{What Comes Next}
\label{sec:ch8-what-comes-next}

Part~II is complete. From the approximate set as a noisy bit per universe
element (\Cref{ch:approximate-sets}) it developed the algebra of unions,
intersections, and complements (\Cref{ch:set-composition}), the estimation and
diagnostic discipline for the two rates (\Cref{ch:classification-measures}),
and the information-theoretic floor that pins the per-element space cost of any
FNR-zero representation (this chapter). The atom is now fully described: its
rates are estimable, its compositions predictable, its space cost bounded below
by $-\log_2 \fprate$ (\Cref{thm:entropy-floor}), and the standard Bloom filter
sits a known $\log_2 e$ above that floor (\Cref{sec:bloom-gap}).

Part~III generalizes from sets to maps: \Cref{ch:approximate-functions} lifts
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ to $\Bernoulli{T}$ for arbitrary algebraic types,
where an approximate map into a $k$-element output is a $k \times k$ channel
rather than a single $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ pair, and chapters~10 through~13
develop sketches, randomized algorithms, approximate relations, and the formal
type framework. Part~IV builds the constructive payoff:
\Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} constructs the Bernoulli Hash Function that
meets the entropy floor asymptotically by abandoning the flat bit array, and
\Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} builds the FPR-zero counterpart on the other
one-sided axis. Part~V draws the boundary: \Cref{ch:not-bernoulli} catalogues
the systems that are \emph{not} Bernoulli, where errors correlate across
inputs, rates drift, or adversarial queries defeat uniform sampling, and the
closing chapters turn the model's controlled error into a cryptographic
resource. The thread is one throughout: an atom of controlled error, its
algebra, its floor, its optimal constructions, and the boundary that fences
off what the atom cannot describe.
