\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch8}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
The floor of \Cref{thm:entropy-floor} is the counting bound of
\textcite{carter-et-al-1978}, who introduced approximate membership testers
and proved that about $n\log_2(1/\fprate)$ bits are necessary. Its two-sided
form, \Cref{prop:two-sided-floor}, is Proposition~1 of
\textcite{pagh-rodler-2001}; the false-negative case is therefore not open.
That the floor is attainable up to lower-order terms is due to
\textcite{pagh-pagh-rao-2005} and, through the reduction to retrieval, to
\textcite{dietzfelbinger-pagh-2008}; \textcite{lovett-porat-2010} show that
structures supporting insertions cannot reach it at constant $\fprate$. The
Bloom filter's factor of $\log_2 e$ is the classical benchmark
\parencite{broder-mitzenmacher-2004}, and the compressed filter of
\Cref{rem:compressed-bloom} is due to \textcite{mitzenmacher-2002}. Entropy,
mutual information, and the BSC capacity are from \textcite{shannon-1948};
\textcite{cover-thomas} is the reference treatment.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
Presentation. The pigeonhole count is written in the exact form $L \ge
n\log_2(1/(\fprate + n/N))$; the floor is read as a self-information
$-\log_2\fprate$ per element rather than an entropy $H(\fprate)$; and the
two-sided bound is interpreted in the book's per-element model, where it says
that false negatives buy space only linearly. The computations in the
program's notation, including the joint entropy of the error counts, are in
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-entropy}; its earlier statement that the two-sided
case is open is superseded by \Cref{prop:two-sided-floor}.

\paragraph{Left out.}
Lower-order terms and the precise constants of near-optimal constructions;
dynamic constructions and their time-space tradeoffs; the joint entropy of
the false-positive and false-negative counts; and adaptive query sequences,
for which the guarantees change \parencite{bender-et-al-2018} and which
chapter~16 takes up.
