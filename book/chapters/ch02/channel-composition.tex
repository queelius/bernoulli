\section{Composition of Channels}
\label{sec:channel-composition}

Many systems chain noisy steps: an amplifier feeds another, a filter feeds a
ranker. If an element passes through $Q_1$ and its output enters $Q_2$, the
end-to-end channel is the matrix product.

\begin{definition}[Serial composition of binary channels]
\label{def:channel-composition}
For binary channel matrices $Q_1$ and $Q_2$, the \emph{serial
composition}\index{channel!serial composition} applying $Q_1$ then $Q_2$ has
channel matrix $Q_{\mathrm{total}} = Q_1 Q_2$, with $(Q_{\mathrm{total}})_{ij} =
\sum_k (Q_1)_{ik}(Q_2)_{kj}$.
\end{definition}

The sum is the law of total probability over the intermediate value $k$:
$(Q_1)_{ik}$ carries block $B_i$ to intermediate output $k$, and $(Q_2)_{kj}$
carries $k$ to final output $j$. Because the matrices are row-stochastic and act
on row vectors of input probabilities, the stage applied first is the left
factor: an input distribution $p$ leaves the chain as $p\,Q_1 Q_2$. This is the
two-step Chapman-Kolmogorov relation of a Markov chain, and the cascade of
channels of information theory \parencite{cover-thomas}.

\subsection*{Composing two BSCs}

Two BSCs in series produce a third. Multiplying circulant matrices preserves
the circulant form, and the off-diagonal entry of $Q_1 Q_2$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bsc-composition}
  \fprate_{\mathrm{total}}
  \;=\; \fprate_1(1 - \fprate_2) + (1 - \fprate_1)\fprate_2
  \;=\; \fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - 2\fprate_1\fprate_2.
\end{equation}
The composed error is the exclusive-or\index{parity!noisy} of the two individual flips: the input is
corrupted exactly when one channel flips and the other does not (if both flip,
the errors cancel). For $0 < \fprate_1, \fprate_2 < 1/2$ this exceeds both
operands, so two imperfect symmetric channels in series are noisier than either
alone; at $\fprate_1 = 0.05$, $\fprate_2 = 0.10$ the composed crossover is
$0.14$. Chaining copies of one BSC drives the crossover toward the stationary
$1/2$, where the channel carries no information.

\subsection*{Composing asymmetric channels}

The general rule is the same matrix product. For $Q_1, Q_2$ with rates
$(\fprate_i, \fnrate_i)$, the $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ entries of $Q_1 Q_2$ give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asym-composition}
  \fnrate_{\mathrm{total}} = (1 - \fnrate_1)\fnrate_2 + \fnrate_1 (1 - \fprate_2),
  \qquad
  \fprate_{\mathrm{total}} = \fprate_1 (1 - \fnrate_2) + (1 - \fprate_1)\fprate_2 .
\end{equation}
Each term follows one path through the intermediate value: a true $\top$ ends
as $\bot$ either by surviving stage~1 and being dropped by stage~2, or by being
dropped at stage~1 and then not being restored to $\top$ by stage~2 (which
treats the intermediate $\bot$ as a negative). At $(\fprate_1, \fnrate_1) =
(0.05, 0.10)$ and $(\fprate_2, \fnrate_2) = (0.02, 0.20)$ the chain has
$\fnrate_{\mathrm{total}} = 0.278$ and $\fprate_{\mathrm{total}} = 0.059$.
Composition of asymmetric channels is not generally symmetric: the BSC is the
special case in which symmetry is preserved because both factors are
symmetric, forcing $\fprate_{\mathrm{total}} = \fnrate_{\mathrm{total}}$.

\subsection*{Serial versus parallel}

Serial composition must not be confused with the parallel query structure of
\Cref{sec:kronecker} (the Kronecker product\index{Kronecker product}). Serial composition feeds one element's output into the
next channel and yields a $2 \times 2$ matrix, the ordinary product $Q_1 Q_2$.
Parallel querying applies $m$ independent channels to $m$ distinct elements and
yields a $2^m \times 2^m$ joint matrix, the Kronecker product $Q_1 \otimes
\cdots \otimes Q_m$, which \Cref{thm:kronecker} shows is forced by the
independence axioms. The two answer different questions, end-to-end error of a
pipeline versus joint distribution of simultaneous queries, and combine when
$k$ multi-stage pipelines run in parallel: the Kronecker product across
pipelines, the ordinary product within each. The serial product needs only the
law of total probability; the Kronecker product needs the axioms
(\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional}).

\subsection*{Algebraic properties}

Serial composition is associative, since matrix multiplication is, so a chain of
channels groups unambiguously. It is not commutative in general: $Q_1 Q_2 \neq
Q_2 Q_1$ unless the factors share a symmetry. Two BSCs do commute, because
\eqref{eq:bsc-composition} is symmetric in $\fprate_1$ and $\fprate_2$; a BSC
and an asymmetric channel generally do not, as a direct computation of $Q_1 Q_2$
and $Q_2 Q_1$ shows, so the order of stages in a mixed pipeline matters.

For one-sided stages of the same orientation the product simplifies. With
$\fnrate_1 = \fnrate_2 = 0$, \eqref{eq:asym-composition} gives
$\fnrate_{\mathrm{total}} = 0$ and $\fprate_{\mathrm{total}} = 1 - (1 -
\fprate_1)(1 - \fprate_2)$, the form $\eta_{\mathrm{total}} = 1 -
\prod_{i}(1 - \eta_i)$ that \Cref{sec:composition-theorem} states for chains in
which any single stage error is fatal.
