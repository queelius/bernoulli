\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch2}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
The channel matrix, the binary symmetric channel, and its capacity $1 -
H_2(\fprate)$ are Shannon's \parencite{shannon-1948}; the asymmetric binary
channel and the Z-channel are standard textbook objects
\parencite[Ch.~7]{cover-thomas}. Serial composition as a product of
row-stochastic matrices is the law of total probability, the cascade of
channels and the two-step Chapman-Kolmogorov relation. The same $2 \times 2$
object arose independently in biostatistics, where \textcite{bross-1954}
analyzed misclassification in $2 \times 2$ tables, and in survey design, where
\textcite{warner-1965} passed each respondent's true indicator through a known
asymmetric channel and recovered the population rate by inverting it. Model
order, the number of blocks in the coarsest partition of the input domain whose
elements share an error probability, is the organizing vocabulary of the
source manuscripts \parencite{towell-bernoulli-sets,towell-bernoulli-data-type};
the Boolean ceiling (\Cref{thm:boolean-ceiling}) is the elementary fact that a
two-element set has at most two blocks.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
A single row convention for $Q$ used throughout the book, the explicit
asymmetric composition formulas \eqref{eq:asym-composition} with the order of
factors fixed by that convention, and the separation of model order from
parameter count: the Z-channel has order two and one free parameter, and the
$m$-query count is governed by the axioms of chapter~4, not by the order. The
forward link that matters most is negative: serial composition never leaves the
order-two ceiling, but Boolean gates over distinct latent bits do
(\Cref{sec:boolean-formulas}).

\paragraph{Left out.}
Channel coding, capacity-achieving codes, and channels with memory are not
treated; \textcite{cover-thomas} is the reference. The estimation-theoretic use
of $Q$ (inverting a known channel to recover prevalence, as randomized response
and readout-error mitigation do) returns only in chapters 7 and 19.
