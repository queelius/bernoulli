\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch17-bridge}

The chapter built one bridge in six pieces. The cipher-map abstraction
(\Cref{sec:what-is-cipher-map}) attached an error profile to a computation
on encodings; the error-model section (\Cref{sec:bernoulli-error-model})
identified that profile with the Bernoulli atom and showed that chained
cipher maps compose per query by the serial channel product, with a
caveat for shared intermediate values; the trapdoor-paradigm section
(\Cref{sec:trapdoor-paradigm}) introduced the client-server split, and
the FHE sidebar (\Cref{sec:fhe-sidebar}) marked the zero-error,
stronger-security baseline. \Cref{sec:boolean-trapdoor} and
\Cref{sec:maximizing-confidentiality} pushed the framework in two
directions, Boolean composition over trapdoor sets and the false-positive
cover as a chapter 7 posterior.

\subsection*{The dual role of the error rates}

The recurring thread is that $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ do two jobs. In
Parts I through IV they were operational parameters, kept small subject to
storage and runtime budgets. Here they also set a cover: under the
observation model of \Cref{sec:maximizing-confidentiality} the
false-positive rate fixes the share of an observer's $\top$-observations
that fail to identify members, and with the prevalence it fixes the
posterior $1 - C$. The second job is partial. It concerns one leakage
channel, the result bit of one structure, and it erodes across
independently keyed instances; repetition, access patterns, and result
sizes leak through channels the rates do not touch. The geometric reading
of chapter 6, the predictive reading of chapter 7, and the cover reading
here are the same algebra applied to the same $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ point,
but only the first two are complete descriptions of what they describe.

\subsection*{Forward to chapter 18}

Chapter 18 specializes the machinery to encrypted search, fixing $f$ to a
membership-test family, ``does record $r$ match query $q$?'', and asking
how a server holding an encrypted index answers faithfully without
revealing $r$ or $q$. The Bloom-filter cipher maps of
\Cref{sec:boolean-trapdoor} reappear as the simplest index (the
perfect-hash structures of chapters 14 and 15 would serve as more
compact ones), and the two-party setup of
\Cref{sec:trapdoor-paradigm} stays in force, the $(\fprate, \fnrate)$
point still doing double duty. What chapter 18 adds is the encrypted-search setting itself: the two
standard Bloom-index layouts, the cover read per document, multi-query and
multi-instance leakage, and the estimation of workload prevalence under
adversarial queries, set against the searchable-encryption literature's
leakage notions. Chapter 17 is the
abstract algebra; chapter 18 is the concrete instance, the same move
chapters 14 and 15 made together. \Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch17} attaches the
chapter's prose to its antecedents in the FHE, cipher-map, and
secure-computation literatures.
