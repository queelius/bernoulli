\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch17-bridge}

The chapter built one bridge in six pieces. The cipher-map abstraction
(\Cref{sec:what-is-cipher-map}) reframed encryption as a pair of error
rates rather than a pair of keys; the error-model section
(\Cref{sec:bernoulli-error-model}) identified those rates with the
Bernoulli atom and lifted the Kronecker composition law to chained
cipher-map evaluation; the trapdoor-paradigm section
(\Cref{sec:trapdoor-paradigm}) introduced the client-server split, and
the FHE sidebar (\Cref{sec:fhe-sidebar}) marked the zero-error baseline.
\Cref{sec:boolean-trapdoor} and \Cref{sec:maximizing-confidentiality}
pushed the framework forward in two directions, Boolean composition over
trapdoor sets and confidentiality as a chapter 7 PPV.

\subsection*{The dual role of the error rates}

The recurring thread is the dual role of $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$. In
Parts I through IV they were operational parameters, kept small subject to
storage and runtime budgets. Here they acquire a second job: the
false-positive rate is also the share of an adversary's $\top$-observations
that fail to identify members, the false-negative rate the share of
$\bot$-observations that protect real members, and together they fix the
map's PPV at prevalence $\pi$, the complementary $1 - \PPV$ being the
confidentiality margin. The duality is not a coincidence. The framework
identifies a structure by its $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ point; chapter 6 read
that point geometrically as a position on the perfect-filter line, chapter
7 predictively as PPV and NPV, and chapter 17 cryptographically as the
trade-off between operational fidelity and informational opacity. The
three readings are the same algebra, and a single choice of where to live
in the Bernoulli square now answers three questions at once.

\subsection*{Forward to chapter 18}

Chapter 18 specializes the machinery to encrypted search, fixing $f$ to a
membership-test family, ``does record $r$ match query $q$?'', and asking
how a server holding an encrypted index answers faithfully without
revealing $r$ or $q$. The Bloom-filter cipher maps of
\Cref{sec:boolean-trapdoor} reappear as the simplest index, the chapter 14
BHF as a space-optimal alternative, and the two-party setup of
\Cref{sec:trapdoor-paradigm} stays in force, the $(\fprate, \fnrate)$
point still doing triple duty. What chapter 18 adds is the structure the
encrypted-search literature has built around the membership case:
data-oblivious access patterns, side-channel control, multi-query leakage
bounds, and the relation between the confidentiality $C$ and the
information-theoretic measures that literature prefers. Chapter 17 is the
abstract algebra; chapter 18 is the concrete instance, the same move
chapters 14 and 15 made together. \Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch17} attaches the
chapter's prose to its antecedents in the FHE, cipher-map, and
secure-computation literatures.
