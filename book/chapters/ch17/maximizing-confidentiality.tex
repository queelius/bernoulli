\section{Maximizing Confidentiality}
\label{sec:maximizing-confidentiality}

\Cref{sec:trapdoor-paradigm} separated two things the error rates do: they
set the client's correctness, and they give reported positives a cover of
plausible deniability. This section quantifies the cover. It does so
under one explicit observation model, and the model's limits are part of
the result.

\subsection*{The observation model}

The observer is an honest-but-curious server that sees the
\emph{plaintext result bit} of each membership query, as it does in a
keyed-filter secure index, but not the queried item, and that knows the
workload's prevalence $\pi$, the probability that a queried item is a
member. The question is what one reported $\top$ tells it about whether
the queried item is a member. The model leaves out everything the rates
do not govern: whether two queries were equal, which positions they
touched, how many results came back, and whether the same item can be
tested against several independently keyed structures. Each of those is a
separate leakage channel with its own literature
\parencite{curtmola-et-al-2006,cash-et-al-2015,kamara-moataz-ohrimenko-2018}.
FHE falls outside the model altogether: its server never sees the result
bit, which is how it achieves its stronger guarantee.

\subsection*{The cover is a posterior}

For a single Boolean query the observer's target is
\[
  \Pr\bigl[\,x \in A \;\big\vert\; \tilde f(x) = \top\,\bigr],
\]
the probability that a reported $\top$ identifies a genuine member rather
than a false positive. Close to $1$, the report has pinned membership
down; close to the prior $\pi$, it has revealed nothing. We measure the
cover by
\[
  C \;=\; 1 - \PPV,
\]
the share of $\top$-reports that are false positives. By the positive
predictive value\index{positive predictive value} of \Cref{def:ppv},
\[
  \PPV = \frac{\pi(1 - \fnrate)}{\pi(1 - \fnrate) + (1 - \pi)\fprate},
  \qquad
  C = \frac{(1 - \pi)\fprate}{\pi(1 - \fnrate) + (1 - \pi)\fprate},
\]
the numerator $\pi(1-\fnrate)$ the rate of correct $\top$ on a member,
$(1-\pi)\fprate$ the rate of spurious $\top$ on a non-member. Zero false
positives gives $C = 0$, a report that exposes membership completely; an
unconditional $\top$ gives $C = 1 - \pi$. The negative case mirrors it,
the cover of a $\bot$-report being $1 - \NPV$ (\Cref{def:npv}), though in
typical encrypted search $\pi$ is small and the $\top$ side dominates.
That false positives provide this kind of deniability is a standard
observation about filter-based indexes \parencite{goh-2003}; the formula
is the chapter 7 posterior read with the observer in mind.

\begin{remark}[The cover in bits per query]
\label{rem:confidentiality-bits}
Under the same observation model the leakage of one result bit is the
mutual information\index{mutual information} between membership and the report,
\begin{multline*}
  L \;=\; H(\pi) - \mathbb{E}_{\text{report}}\!\left[H(\text{posterior})\right] \\
  \;=\; H(\pi) - \bigl[\Pr(\top)\,H(\PPV) + \Pr(\bot)\,H(1 - \NPV)\bigr],
\end{multline*}
with $H$ the binary entropy and $\Pr(\top) = \pi(1-\fnrate) +
(1-\pi)\fprate$. An exact result bit ($\fprate = \fnrate = 0$) has
deterministic posteriors and $L = H(\pi)$: the full prior entropy leaks.
A map with positive rates leaks a fraction of $H(\pi)$ per result bit,
the information-theoretic counterpart of $C$.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The cover does not survive independent instances}

The measure is for one structure. If the observer can test the same item
against $t$ independently keyed structures over the same set (rebuilds
under fresh keys, replicas, or published releases), a member reports
$\top$ in all of them while a non-member does so in each independently
with probability $\fprate$, so at $\fnrate = 0$
\[
  C_t \;=\; \frac{(1 - \pi)\fprate^{\,t}}{\pi + (1 - \pi)\fprate^{\,t}}
  \;\le\; \frac{1 - \pi}{\pi}\,\fprate^{\,t},
\]
which decays geometrically in $t$. This is the coincidence attack in its
simplest form\index{coincidence attack}, and it is why key rotation helps
only when old structures are not available to the observer
(\Cref{prop:multi-query-leakage} states the general version). Repeating a
query against one structure, by contrast, returns the same frozen answer
and adds nothing about that item.

\subsection*{Trading rate budget for cover}

On the perfect-filter line of \Cref{def:perfect-filter-line} the
trade-off has a single parameter. An $\fnrate = 0$ map sits on the
$\fprate$-axis, and raising $\fprate$ raises $(1-\pi)\fprate$ and so
raises $C$ monotonically, while chapter 5's space formula makes storage a
decreasing function of $\fprate$: under this observation model, smaller
filters give more cover. Storage couples to accuracy through the chapter 8
bound and accuracy to cover through the chapter 7 posterior, so one
parameter sets three coupled quantities. Maximizing $C$ in the abstract is
degenerate (walk to $\fprate = 1$, $C = 1 - \pi$, the map useless), so the
real problem constrains the operational error: maximize $C$ subject to
$\fprate \le \fprate^*$ for an application-imposed cap, the optimum being
the cap itself.

\subsection*{A worked example}

Take prevalence $\pi = 0.01$ and rates $\fprate = 0.05$, $\fnrate = 0$.
The PPV formula gives
\[
  \PPV = \frac{0.01}{0.01 + 0.99 \cdot 0.05} = \frac{0.01}{0.0595}
  \approx 0.168,
  \qquad C \approx 0.832.
\]
When the server sees a $\top$, its posterior assigns about $16.8\%$ to
genuine membership and $83.2\%$ to a false positive; one report leaves
the question far from settled. Two independently keyed instances already
give $C_2 \approx 0.198$ by the formula above, and three give $C_3 \approx
0.012$. The example also shows the workload
dependence: holding $(\fprate, \fnrate) = (0.05, 0)$ but moving to a
balanced workload $\pi = 0.5$ gives $\PPV = 0.5/0.525 \approx 0.952$ and
$C \approx 0.048$. The same map gives good cover on a rare-positive
workload and little on a balanced one. The parameters stay separate: the
map carries $(\fprate, \fnrate)$, the workload carries $\pi$, the
deployment carries the number of instances $t$, and $C$ is a function of
all four.
