\section{Boolean Formulas Under Noise}
\label{sec:boolean-formulas}

The gate formulas of \Cref{sec:and-under-noise} and \Cref{sec:or-not} describe
one gate whose inputs observe distinct latent bits. A formula\index{composition!of Boolean formulas} feeds gate outputs
into further gates, and the natural hope, that each gate's output can be
summarized by a pair $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ and the summary passed upward, fails.
This section states what is exact, shows by example how the two-rate summary
fails, and gives the two summaries that are sound.

\subsection*{Exact propagation, one latent assignment at a time}

\begin{definition}[Latent assignment]
\label{def:latent-assignment}
Let $\tilde{b}_1, \ldots, \tilde{b}_n$ observe latent bits $w_1, \ldots, w_n$
through channels with rates $(\fprate_i, \fnrate_i)$, each bit observed once,
the observations $o_i$ independent given $w$. The vector $w = (w_1, \ldots,
w_n)$ is the \emph{latent assignment}\index{latent assignment}, written as a
word over $\{0, 1\}$ with $1$ for $\top$. For a Boolean formula $f$ its
\emph{conditional error} is $e_f(w) = \Pr[f(o) \neq f(w) \mid w]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact conditional propagation]
\label{prop:latent-propagation}
\index{composition!per latent assignment}
Under \Cref{def:latent-assignment}, for every formula $f$ and every latent
assignment $w$,
\[
  \Pr[f(o) = \top \mid w]
  \;=\; \sum_{o \in \{0,1\}^n} f(o) \prod_{i=1}^{n} q_i(o_i \mid w_i),
\]
where $q_i(1 \mid 0) = \fprate_i$, $q_i(0 \mid 0) = 1 - \fprate_i$,
$q_i(1 \mid 1) = 1 - \fnrate_i$, and $q_i(0 \mid 1) = \fnrate_i$. Hence
$e_f(w)$ is a polynomial in the rates selected by $w$, of degree at most one in
each. If $f$ is read-once (each variable occurs at most once), the same
probabilities are computed bottom-up by the rules
\[
  p(\neg g) = 1 - p(g),
  \quad
  p(g \wedge h) = p(g)\,p(h),
  \quad
  p(g \vee h) = 1 - (1 - p(g))(1 - p(h)),
\]
with leaves $p(\tilde{b}_i) = \fprate_i$ if $w_i = 0$ and $1 - \fnrate_i$ if
$w_i = 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given $w$, the observations are independent with the stated marginals
(\Cref{prop:factored-joint}), so the law of $o$ is the product measure and
$f(o)$ is a function of $o$; summing over $o$ gives the display. Each factor
$q_i(o_i \mid w_i)$ is affine in the one rate that $w_i$ selects, so the sum is
multilinear in the selected rates. For a read-once formula the two operands of
any gate are functions of disjoint sets of observations, hence independent given
$w$, and the three rules are the probabilities of the complement, intersection,
and union of independent events.
\end{proof}

The read-once rules are the signal-probability calculus that Parker and
McCluskey gave for combinational networks\index{signal probability}
\parencite{parker-mccluskey-1975}; probabilistic transfer matrices are its
matrix form for general circuits, including reconvergent fan-out
\parencite{krishnaswamy-et-al-2008}\index{probabilistic transfer matrix}. A
variable that occurs more than once is still one observation feeding several
places, so its occurrences are perfectly correlated; conditioning on the
observed values of the repeated variables (a Shannon expansion) restores the
read-once case at a cost of $2^r$ passes for $r$ repeated variables. The
companion module \texttt{bernoulli.formula} implements the proposition: its
\texttt{error\_probability} and \texttt{error\_profile} evaluate $e_f(w)$ at one
or all latent assignments.

\begin{remark}[Parity, per latent assignment]
\label{rem:parity-latent}
For the parity $f = b_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus b_n$ the proposition gives
\[
  e_f(w) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(1 - 2 q_i(w)\bigr)\Bigr),
  \qquad
  q_i(w) = \begin{cases} \fprate_i & w_i = 0, \\ \fnrate_i & w_i = 1, \end{cases}
\]
since the parity errs exactly when an odd number of observations flip. The
identity $\Pr[\text{odd number of flips}] = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \prod_i(1 -
2q_i))$ for independent flips is Lemma~1 of \textcite{gallager-1962}, and in
cryptanalysis it is Matsui's piling-up lemma
\parencite{matsui-1993}\index{Gallager's lemma}\index{piling-up
lemma}\index{parity!noisy}. For BSCs, $q_i(w) = \fprate_i$ at every $w$ and
$n = 2$ recovers the serial BSC composition \eqref{eq:bsc-composition}; for
asymmetric channels the parity error depends on how many latent bits are
$\top$, and no single rate describes it.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Why a rate pair does not compose}

Viewed as a channel from latent assignments to the reported bit, a formula's
output has one row per latent assignment, and its model order in the sense of
\Cref{def:model-order}, applied to the domain $\{0,1\}^n$, is the number of
distinct values $e_f(w)$ takes. A two-rate description is exact only when that
number is at most one per truth class. The AND of two distinct latent bits
already has three negative rows \eqref{eq:and-negative-rows}: composition
raises the model order\index{model order}. Carrying a single number per gate upward therefore
discards which latent assignment occurred, and the error compounds.

\begin{example}[A two-rate summary halves the error]
\label{ex:two-rate-counterexample}
\index{counterexample!two-rate composition}
Take $f = (b_0 \wedge b_1) \vee b_2$ with every channel at $\fprate = 0.02$ and
$\fnrate = 0.10$, and the latent assignment $w = 010$ ($b_0$ false, $b_1$ true,
$b_2$ false), at which $f(w) = \bot$. The AND observes the pair $(\bot, \top)$
and reports $\top$ with probability $\fprate(1 - \fnrate) = 0.018$; the OR
reports $\bot$ only if both of its inputs do, so
\[
  e_f(010) \;=\; 1 - (1 - 0.018)(1 - 0.02) \;=\; 1 - (0.982)(0.98) \;=\; 0.03764.
\]
A two-rate summary that carries the AND's all-negative rate $\fprate^2 = 0.0004$
upward gives $1 - (1 - 0.0004)(0.98) = 0.020392$, about half the true value; it
is the exact error at $w = 000$ only. Exhaustive enumeration of the eight
latent assignments gives two distinct false-positive values ($0.020392$ at
$000$; $0.03764$ at $010$ and $100$) and four distinct false-negative values
($0.09996$ at $001$; $0.0982$ at $011$ and $101$; $0.1862$ at $110$; $0.019$ at
$111$): six error regimes, model order six on the latent domain, for a formula
built from three identical two-rate atoms (\Cref{fig:latent-tree}).
\end{example}

\subsection*{Two sound summaries}

When a single pair is wanted anyway, two constructions are sound.

\begin{remark}[Prior-averaged rates]
\label{rem:prior-averaged-composition}
If the latent bits are independent with prevalences $\pi_i$, the marginal rates
$\bar{\fprate}_f = \Pr[f(o) = \top \mid f(w) = \bot]$ and $\bar{\fnrate}_f$ are
exact averages of $e_f$ over each truth class\index{prior-averaged rate}, and
\eqref{eq:and-fpr-general} is the two-input case with $\pi_1 = \pi_2 = \pi$.
For a read-once formula they can be computed bottom-up: if $g$ and $h$ read
disjoint variables, with truth probabilities $\pi_g, \pi_h$ and averaged rates
$(\bar{\fprate}_g, \bar{\fnrate}_g)$, $(\bar{\fprate}_h, \bar{\fnrate}_h)$, then
\[
  \bar{\fprate}_{g \wedge h}
  = \frac{\pi_g(1 - \pi_h)(1 - \bar{\fnrate}_g)\bar{\fprate}_h
        + (1 - \pi_g)\pi_h \bar{\fprate}_g (1 - \bar{\fnrate}_h)
        + (1 - \pi_g)(1 - \pi_h)\bar{\fprate}_g \bar{\fprate}_h}
       {1 - \pi_g \pi_h},
\]
because $(g, \text{observed } g)$ and $(h, \text{observed } h)$ are independent
pairs. Averages compose in this one setting and only as averages: they are the
right numbers for a workload drawn from the prior, never the conditional rate at
a given input, and they fail once two subformulas share a variable, since the
operand pairs are then dependent. \texttt{bernoulli.formula.marginal\_rates}
computes them by enumeration.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Sound worst-case rate bounds]
\label{prop:worst-case-rate-bounds}
\index{composition!worst-case rate bounds}
Suppose formulas $g$ and $h$ satisfy $e_g(w) \le \bar{E}_g$ at every $w$ with
$g(w) = \bot$ and $e_g(w) \le \bar{W}_g$ at every $w$ with $g(w) = \top$, and
likewise for $h$. Then at every latent assignment:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item NOT: $\neg g$ satisfies the bounds $(\bar{W}_g, \bar{E}_g)$.
  \item AND: the false-positive error of $g \wedge h$ is at most $\max(\bar{E}_g,
    \bar{E}_h)$, with no independence assumption. Its false-negative error is at
    most $\bar{W}_g + \bar{W}_h$ in general, and at most $1 - (1 - \bar{W}_g)(1 -
    \bar{W}_h)$ when $g$ and $h$ read disjoint variables.
  \item OR: dually, the false-negative error of $g \vee h$ is at most
    $\max(\bar{W}_g, \bar{W}_h)$, and its false-positive error is at most
    $\bar{E}_g + \bar{E}_h$ in general and $1 - (1 - \bar{E}_g)(1 - \bar{E}_h)$
    for disjoint variables.
\end{enumerate}
Applied recursively from the leaves $(\bar{E}, \bar{W}) = (\fprate_i,
\fnrate_i)$, these give a pair $(\bar{E}_f, \bar{W}_f)$ that bounds $e_f(w)$ in
each truth class at every latent assignment.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For AND with $f(w) = \bot$, at least one operand is false at $w$, say $g(w) =
\bot$; the event that the observed conjunction is $\top$ is contained in the
event that the observed $g$ is $\top$, so its probability is at most $e_g(w)
\le \bar{E}_g$. For $f(w) = \top$ both operands are true, and the observed
conjunction is $\bot$ iff at least one observed operand is $\bot$; the union
bound gives $\bar{W}_g + \bar{W}_h$, and when the operands read disjoint
variables they are independent given $w$, so the probability is $1 - (1 -
e_g(w))(1 - e_h(w)) \le 1 - (1 - \bar{W}_g)(1 - \bar{W}_h)$. NOT exchanges the
truth classes, which gives part~1, and part~3 follows from parts~1 and~2 by De
Morgan.
\end{proof}

On \Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample} the recursion gives $(0.02, 0.19)$ for the
AND and $(1 - 0.98^2, \max(0.19, 0.10)) = (0.0396, 0.19)$ for the formula,
which bounds the exact worst values $0.03764$ and $0.1862$. The exact worst pair,
the maximum of $e_f$ over each truth class, is sharper but needs the enumeration
of \Cref{prop:latent-propagation} (\texttt{bernoulli.formula.worst\_case\_rates});
the recursive bound costs one pass. Neither is the exact rate at a given input,
and the gap between the bound and the corner product is real: in the
all-negative regime the AND's false-positive rate is $\fprate_g \fprate_h$
(\Cref{sec:composition-theorem}), far below $\max(\bar{E}_g, \bar{E}_h)$, because
there the latent assignment is known. That regime, and the one-sided chains in
which a single error is fatal, are where corner products are exact; the next
section states them as the composition theorem.
