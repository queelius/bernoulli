\section{AND Under Noise}
\label{sec:and-under-noise}

A $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance is a noisy bit with rates $(\fprate,
\fnrate)$ (\Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}). The first question of the chapter is
what a Boolean operation does to two of them. AND is the natural start: it is
the operation behind a Bloom filter's $k$-hash query and Miller-Rabin's
$k$-round test, and OR and NOT follow from it (\Cref{sec:or-not}).

Let $\tilde{b}_1, \tilde{b}_2$ be independent instances with rates
$(\fprate_i, \fnrate_i)$ observing two \emph{latent} (true) bits $b_1, b_2$,
and apply AND to their observed outputs; the true value is the AND of the
latent bits. Two independence assumptions are in play, and the chapter's
purpose is to invoke them deliberately. Axiom~1
(\Cref{ax:element-indep})\index{axiom!element-wise independence} makes the
error events of the two instances independent given the latent bits, licensing every product below; Axiom~2
(\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep})\index{axiom!block-rate independence} makes each instance's rates stable constants
rather than a drifting process. Chapter~4 proves both and shows they are the
content of the Kronecker factorization (\Cref{sec:axiom-1},
\Cref{sec:axiom-2}).

The AND of the observed outputs is $\top$ exactly when both report $\top$,
which by Axiom~1 happens with the following probabilities across the four
latent pairs:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\hline
$b_1$ & $b_2$ & correct AND & $\Pr[\text{observed AND} = \top]$ \\
\hline
$\top$ & $\top$ & $\top$ & $(1 - \fnrate_1)(1 - \fnrate_2)$ \\
$\top$ & $\bot$ & $\bot$ & $(1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2$ \\
$\bot$ & $\top$ & $\bot$ & $\fprate_1(1 - \fnrate_2)$ \\
$\bot$ & $\bot$ & $\bot$ & $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection*{Output rates}

The true AND is $\top$ only in the first row, so a false negative\index{false-negative rate} (true $\top$,
observed $\bot$) is the complement of that row's success:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:and-fnr}
  \fnrate_\wedge \;=\; 1 - (1 - \fnrate_1)(1 - \fnrate_2)
                 \;=\; \fnrate_1 + \fnrate_2 - \fnrate_1 \fnrate_2.
\end{equation}
This depends only on the input rates, not on the input prior: the true AND is
$\top$ at exactly one latent pair, $(\top, \top)$, so there is exactly one
false-negative rate, and the AND succeeds there only if both instances do.

The false-positive rate\index{false-positive rate} is different. The true AND is $\bot$ at three latent
pairs, and the observed AND is $\top$ there with the three conditional
probabilities
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:and-negative-rows}
  (\top,\bot):\ (1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2,
  \qquad
  (\bot,\top):\ \fprate_1(1 - \fnrate_2),
  \qquad
  (\bot,\bot):\ \fprate_1 \fprate_2 .
\end{equation}
These are generally three different numbers. Viewed as a channel from the
latent pair\index{latent assignment} to the reported bit, the AND output has
one positive row and three negative rows, so in the sense of
\Cref{def:model-order}, applied to the four-element domain of latent pairs, it
can have model order\index{model order} up to four. No single false-positive
rate describes it: the AND of two $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances on distinct
latent bits is \emph{not} again a two-rate noisy bit.
\Cref{sec:boolean-formulas} develops the consequence for formulas, including a
counterexample (\Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample}) in which reusing a single
rate halves the true error.

A single number arises only by averaging. If the two latent bits are
independent with common prevalence\index{prevalence} $\pi = \Pr[b_i = \top]$,
the probability that a randomly drawn negative pair is reported $\top$ is the
prior-weighted mixture of \eqref{eq:and-negative-rows},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:and-fpr-general}
  \fprate_\wedge
  \;=\;
  \frac{\pi(1 - \pi)\bigl[(1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2 + \fprate_1(1 - \fnrate_2)\bigr]
        + (1 - \pi)^2 \fprate_1 \fprate_2}
       {1 - \pi^2}.
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{prior-averaged rate}\index{prior-averaged rate}: an exact
statement about the gate under the stated input distribution, and the right
number for a workload drawn from it, but not the conditional rate at any
particular input, and not a parameter a downstream gate can reuse except in the
narrow case of \Cref{rem:prior-averaged-composition}. The two extremes are
instructive. When both inputs are negative (the all-negative regime, $\pi \to 0$
or both inputs known negative) the boundary rows drop out and
\[
  \fprate_\wedge \;=\; \fprate_1 \fprate_2,
\]
the product, which drives the false-positive rate down: two noisy bits both have
to err for the conjunction to err. When one input is positive, the relevant row
contributes $\approx \fprate$ (only one instance need err), so the mixture lies
between $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ and $\max(\fprate_1, \fprate_2)$. The
all-negative product regime is the one Miller-Rabin operates in: its $k$ rounds
all test the same composite, so every round observes the same latent value
$\bot$ and the conjunction's false-positive rate is the clean product
$\prod_i \fprate_i$ (\Cref{sec:miller-rabin}).

AND\index{AND gate} therefore maps two $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances to a
channel with a single false-negative rate \eqref{eq:and-fnr} and a
false-positive rate that depends on which negative latent pair occurred: the
product at $(\bot, \bot)$, the larger boundary values at $(\top, \bot)$ and
$(\bot, \top)$. For two independent fair coins read through channels with
rates $(0.10, 0.05)$ and $(0.08, 0.12)$, reporting whether both landed heads
gives $\fnrate_\wedge = 0.164$ by \eqref{eq:and-fnr}. The three negative rows
are $0.076$, $0.088$, and $0.008$, and at $\pi = 0.5$ their average
\eqref{eq:and-fpr-general} is $\fprate_\wedge \approx 0.057$, a number that
matches none of them. If instead both channels read the \emph{same} coin, only
the latent pairs $(\top, \top)$ and $(\bot, \bot)$ occur and the
false-positive rate is exactly the product $0.008$: this shared-truth reading is
the Miller-Rabin regime, and it is the reading under which the companion
package's \texttt{BernoulliBool} conjunction returns $(\fprate_1 \fprate_2,
1 - (1 - \fnrate_1)(1 - \fnrate_2))$. AND trades false-negative accuracy for
false-positive suppression, but by an amount that depends on the input.
