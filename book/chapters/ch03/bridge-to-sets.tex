\section{Composition is the Bridge to Sets}
\label{sec:bridge-to-sets}

The Boolean algebra of $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ is the foundation for the
richer types of Parts~II and~III, and three concrete bridges carry it there.

\paragraph{Bloom filters are $m$-fold conjunctions.}
A Bloom filter\index{Bloom filter} reports membership by checking that all
$m$ hash positions are set, an $m$-fold AND of one-sided lookups. Each lookup
has $\fnrate = 0$ (a member sets every position) and per-hash false-positive
rate $\fprate_i$. In the all-negative regime of a non-member, the conjunction
false-positives only if every probe lands on a set bit, so by the dual
\eqref{eq:mr-k-round} of the composition theorem the filter's false-positive
rate is $\prod_{i=1}^{m} \fprate_i$; conditionally on a realized array with a
fraction $p$ of bits set and independent uniform probes it is exactly $p^m$.
Averaged over the random array this conditional rate gives the classical Bloom
formula only approximately, since the fill fraction is itself random
\parencite{bose-et-al-2008,christensen-roginsky-jimeno-2010};
\Cref{ch:approximate-sets} treats the distinction. The structure is identical to Miller-Rabin; only the source of the
per-stage rate differs, a design parameter here, a number-theoretic theorem
there. \Cref{ch:approximate-sets} develops the filter and its parameter
selection in full.

\paragraph{Set operations are Boolean operations on membership.}
For approximate sets $\tilde{A}, \tilde{B}$, membership $x \in \tilde{A}$ is a
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance, and the set operations are pointwise Boolean
operations on these: intersection is AND, union is OR, complement is NOT. The
rate formulas of \Cref{sec:and-under-noise} and \Cref{sec:or-not} therefore
give, per element and per latent pair of memberships,
\[
  \fnrate_\cap = 1 - (1 - \fnrate_A)(1 - \fnrate_B),
  \quad
  \fprate_\cup = \fprate_A + \fprate_B - \fprate_A \fprate_B,
  \quad
  (\fprate_{A^c}, \fnrate_{A^c}) = (\fnrate_A, \fprate_A).
\]
The intersection's false-positive rate is the product $\fprate_A \fprate_B$ for
elements outside both sets (the bulk regime), and is boundary-dependent for
elements in exactly one set, exactly the latent-pair dependence of the AND in
\eqref{eq:and-negative-rows}: an approximate intersection has more than one
false-positive rate, while an approximate union of one-sided sets keeps a single
one (\Cref{prop:boolean-closure}). \Cref{ch:set-composition} carries the general case,
including the rate-uniformity conditions an approximate set must satisfy for
these per-element rates to define a single $\Bernoulli{\Set{S}}$ instance,
conditions that chapter~16 (\Cref{ch:not-bernoulli}) shows can fail.

\paragraph{The Boolean case is a fiber of the map algebra.}
Replacing the Boolean codomain with an arbitrary type $Y$ lifts the same
composition law: an approximate map $\tilde{f} : X \to Y$ has a channel matrix
that grows from $2 \times 2$ to $|Y| \times |Y|$, and for a single input the
end-to-end channel of a composition is still the matrix product. The joint
behavior across inputs is another matter: when the exact inner map sends two
inputs to one intermediate value, a frozen error of the outer map at that value
is shared by both composed outputs, so their errors are dependent even though
each marginal is right. The Boolean algebra of this chapter is the $Y =
\BoolType$ fiber of the map algebra that \Cref{ch:approximate-functions}
develops, and the latent-assignment caveat of \Cref{sec:boolean-formulas}
carries over to it.
