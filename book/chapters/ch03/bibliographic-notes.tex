\paragraph{Miller and Rabin.}
The primality test of \Cref{sec:miller-rabin} has two parents.
\textcite{miller-1976} gave the witness structure, compute $a^d \bmod n$ and
test for a non-trivial square root of unity, and a deterministic polynomial-time
variant conditional on the Extended Riemann Hypothesis.
\textcite{rabin-1980} removed the hypothesis by randomizing the witness and
proving the unconditional bound: for any composite $n$, at most a quarter of
the witnesses are strong liars. That $1/4$ is the one fact the framework
borrows; the chapter's reading of a single round as a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
with $\fnrate = 0$ and $\fprate \le 1/4$ restates Rabin's result in this book's
language. Textbook treatments with the full number-theoretic argument are
\textcite[Ch.~31]{cormen-clrs} and, for randomized algorithms broadly,
\textcite{mitzenmacher-upfal}.

\paragraph{The Bernoulli papers.}
The composition theorem of \Cref{sec:composition-theorem} is the
Boolean-algebra form of the rate-propagation result of
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-sets}, restated for a single $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instance rather than a set, and the AND, OR, and NOT rules of
\Cref{sec:and-under-noise} are the Boolean base case of the algebraic-type
treatment in \textcite{towell-bernoulli-data-type}.

\paragraph{One algebra, three lenses.}
A single composition algebra runs through chapters 2 through 4 at three
abstraction layers: the \emph{matrix product} $Q_2 Q_1$ for serial channels
(\Cref{sec:channel-composition}); the \emph{Boolean-algebra} form
$\eta_{\mathrm{total}} = 1 - \prod_i(1 - \eta_i)$ for chained one-sided gates
(this chapter); and the \emph{Kronecker product} $Q_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes
Q_m$ for parallel queries (\Cref{thm:kronecker}). The Boolean form differs from
the matrix product for the BSC, where cancellation makes the two disagree, and
coincides with it for one-sided channels. Chapters 2 and 4 situate the
matrix-product and Kronecker layers in their own literatures
(\Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch2}, \Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch4}); seeing all three as one
algebra is a contribution of this text.
