\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch3}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
Forward propagation of error probabilities through AND, OR, and NOT is old.
The read-once rules of \Cref{prop:latent-propagation} are the
signal-probability calculus of \textcite{parker-mccluskey-1975}, which already
handled asymmetric inputs; probabilistic transfer matrices
\parencite{krishnaswamy-et-al-2008} give the matrix form for whole circuits,
including reconvergent fan-out. Reliable computation from noisy gates\index{noisy circuits} goes
back to \textcite{von-neumann-1956}, with sharp formula-size results by
\textcite{pippenger-1988} and \textcite{evans-schulman-1999}. The parity
identity of \Cref{rem:parity-latent} is Lemma~1 of \textcite{gallager-1962} and
Matsui's piling-up lemma \parencite{matsui-1993}. The composition theorem is
the product rule for a series system. Miller-Rabin has two parents:
\textcite{miller-1976} gave the witness structure under the Extended Riemann
Hypothesis, and \textcite{rabin-1980} randomized the witness and proved the
unconditional $1/4$ bound; \textcite[Ch.~31]{cormen-clrs} and
\textcite{mitzenmacher-upfal} give textbook treatments.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
A uniform reading of these gates as operations on two-rate atoms, and a
correction. The quantum-readout manuscript of this program
\parencite{towell-bernoulli-quantum-readout} asserted that the pair
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$ is carried exactly through any Boolean tree. It is not: a gate over distinct latent bits has one rate on one side and up to three
on the other (\Cref{prop:boolean-closure}), and
\Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample} shows the summary halving the true error.
What survives is exact per-latent-assignment propagation, prior-averaged rates
that compose only for read-once formulas, and the recursive worst-case bounds
of \Cref{prop:worst-case-rate-bounds}. The composition paper of the program
\parencite{towell-bernoulli-composition} handles the set-level version
correctly, by letting composition raise the model order.

\paragraph{Left out.}
Fault-tolerant constructions, threshold results for noisy formulas, and the
complexity of exact evaluation for formulas with many repeated variables (where
the Shannon expansion is exponential) are not treated; the references above
cover them. Correlated gate errors are deferred to chapter~16.
