\section{OR and NOT}
\label{sec:or-not}

\Cref{sec:and-under-noise} derived what happens when we AND two
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances. We found that AND is a binary
operation on the type: two instances with rates $(\fprate_1, \fnrate_1)$
and $(\fprate_2, \fnrate_2)$ combine to produce a new instance with
closed-form rates $\fprate_\wedge$ and $\fnrate_\wedge$. The computation
invoked both axioms: Axiom~1 to factor joint probabilities, and Axiom~2 to
treat the per-instance rates as stable constants.

OR and NOT call for the same treatment. As we will see, NOT is immediate
from the channel-matrix perspective, and OR follows from De Morgan's law
applied to noisy gates. We then verify the De Morgan derivation by
re-deriving OR from the truth table directly, confirming that both paths
agree. The section closes by identifying what the three gates together
buy us: closure of $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ under all of Boolean algebra.

\subsection*{NOT under noise}

Let $\tilde{b}$ be a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance with channel matrix
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate     & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its NOT, $\neg\tilde{b}$, reports the complement of the observed output.
What are the rates of the complemented instance?

We answer this by tracing the meaning of the four outcomes. If the true
value of $\tilde{b}$ is $b$ and the observed output is $\hat{b}$, then
$\neg\tilde{b}$ has true value $\neg b$ and observed output $\neg\hat{b}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{False-positive rate of $\neg\tilde{b}$.}
A false positive of $\neg\tilde{b}$ is the event that the true value of
$\neg b$ is $\bot$---meaning the true value of $b$ is $\top$---while the
observed output of $\neg\tilde{b}$ is $\top$---meaning the observed output
of $\tilde{b}$ is $\bot$. In other words, a false positive of $\neg\tilde{b}$
is a \emph{false negative} of $\tilde{b}$. The rate is:
\[
  \fprate_{\neg} \;=\; \fnrate.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{False-negative rate of $\neg\tilde{b}$.}
A false negative of $\neg\tilde{b}$ is the event that the true value of
$\neg b$ is $\top$---meaning $b = \bot$---while the observed output of
$\neg\tilde{b}$ is $\bot$---meaning $\hat{b} = \top$. In other words, a
false negative of $\neg\tilde{b}$ is a \emph{false positive} of
$\tilde{b}$. The rate is:
\[
  \fnrate_{\neg} \;=\; \fprate.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Summary.}
The NOT operation acts on the channel matrix by swapping the
roles of false positives and false negatives:
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\neg\tilde{b}}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fprate & \fprate \\
    \fnrate     & 1 - \fnrate
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:not-Q}
\end{equation}
In rate notation: $(\fprate, \fnrate) \xmapsto{\neg} (\fnrate, \fprate)$.

This is the simplest operation in the Boolean algebra of noisy bits. The
channel matrix is not recomputed from a truth table; it is obtained by
reading the rows of $Q$ in the opposite order. Complementing a predicate
swaps the positive and negative blocks; the row that formerly governed
positives now governs negatives, and vice versa.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{NOT is its own inverse.}
Applying NOT twice returns the original:
\[
  (\fprate_{\neg}, \fnrate_{\neg}) \;=\; (\fnrate, \fprate)
  \;\xmapsto{\neg}\;
  (\fprate, \fnrate).
\]
In channel-matrix terms, the row-swap~\eqref{eq:not-Q} applied twice
restores $Q$ exactly. More precisely, NOT is an \emph{involution} on
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$: $(\neg)^2 = \mathrm{id}$.

This is the noisy-gate analog of the classical fact $\neg\neg b = b$. Under
noise, the equality is at the level of the channel matrix, not the
individual observation: applying NOT twice to a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instance returns an instance with the same rates as the original, not the
same realized output on any given query.

\subsection*{OR via De Morgan's law}

De Morgan's law says $b_1 \vee b_2 = \neg(\neg b_1 \wedge \neg b_2)$.
We apply this identity at the gate level, using NOT (derived above) and
AND (from \Cref{sec:and-under-noise}).

Let $\tilde{b}_1$ and $\tilde{b}_2$ be two independent
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances with rates $(\fprate_1, \fnrate_1)$ and
$(\fprate_2, \fnrate_2)$ respectively.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Negate each input.}
By the NOT formula above:
\begin{align*}
  \neg\tilde{b}_1 &\text{ has rates } (\fnrate_1, \fprate_1), \\
  \neg\tilde{b}_2 &\text{ has rates } (\fnrate_2, \fprate_2).
\end{align*}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: AND the negated inputs.}
We apply the AND formula from \Cref{sec:and-under-noise} to
$\neg\tilde{b}_1$ and $\neg\tilde{b}_2$ with their swapped rates.
For two $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances with rates $(a_1, c_1)$ and
$(a_2, c_2)$, the AND gate's false-negative rate is
$c_1 + c_2 - c_1 c_2$ (from \Cref{eq:and-fnr}).

For the AND of $\neg\tilde{b}_1$ and $\neg\tilde{b}_2$, the roles
of $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ are swapped relative to the original instances,
so $a_i = \fnrate_i$ and $c_i = \fprate_i$:
\begin{align}
  \fnrate_{\neg\wedge\neg}
  &\;=\; \fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2.
  \label{eq:and-of-nots-fnr}
\end{align}
The FPR of the AND of the negated inputs is, again from the AND formula,
now with the input FPRs equal to $\fnrate_1, \fnrate_2$ (the swapped values).
It depends on the input prior over the negated values; but we will see in
Step~3 that the outer NOT maps it to the OR's FNR, which we handle directly
from the truth table.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Negate the result.}
The outer NOT swaps the rates of $\neg\tilde{b}_1 \wedge \neg\tilde{b}_2$:
\begin{align}
  \fprate_\vee
  &\;=\; \fnrate_{\neg\wedge\neg}
   \;=\; \fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2,
  \label{eq:or-fpr}
\end{align}
and $\fnrate_\vee = \fprate_{\neg\wedge\neg}$.

The FPR of the OR gate, $\fprate_\vee = \fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2$,
is the inclusion-exclusion formula for the probability that at least one of
two independent events occurs. It is input-distribution-free: it depends
only on the individual false-positive rates, not on the prior over input
patterns. This is the noisy-gate analog of the classical fact that
$\Pr[A \cup B] = \Pr[A] + \Pr[B] - \Pr[A \cap B]$ for independent events.

\subsection*{OR via the direct truth table}

We now re-derive the OR false-positive rate directly from the truth table,
to confirm that both paths yield the same result.

The OR of the observed outputs is $\top$ if \emph{at least one} of
$\hat{b}_1$, $\hat{b}_2$ is $\top$. The true OR output, $b_1 \vee b_2$,
is $\top$ if at least one of $b_1$, $b_2$ is $\top$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\hline
True $b_1$ & True $b_2$ & Correct OR output & $\Pr[\text{OR output} = \bot]$ \\
\hline
$\top$ & $\top$ & $\top$ & $\fnrate_1 \fnrate_2$ \\
$\top$ & $\bot$ & $\top$ & $\fnrate_1 (1 - \fprate_2)$ \\
$\bot$ & $\top$ & $\top$ & $(1 - \fprate_1)\fnrate_2$ \\
$\bot$ & $\bot$ & $\bot$ & $(1 - \fprate_1)(1 - \fprate_2)$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The rightmost column gives $\Pr[\text{observed OR} = \bot]$ for each
true-input combination (the OR is $\bot$ only when both observed outputs
are $\bot$, which by Axiom~1 has probability equal to the product of the
individual $\bot$-reporting probabilities).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{False-positive rate of OR.}
A false positive of the OR gate occurs when the true OR is $\bot$---meaning
both true inputs are $\bot$---but the observed OR is $\top$. This is the
complement of both observed outputs being $\bot$:
\begin{equation}
  \fprate_\vee
  \;=\; \Pr[\hat{b}_1 = \top \text{ or } \hat{b}_2 = \top \mid b_1 = \bot, b_2 = \bot]
  \;=\; 1 - (1 - \fprate_1)(1 - \fprate_2)
  \;=\; \fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2.
  \label{eq:or-fpr-direct}
\end{equation}
This matches~\eqref{eq:or-fpr} from the De Morgan derivation exactly.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{False-negative rate of OR.}
A false negative of the OR gate occurs when the true OR is $\top$---meaning
at least one true input is $\top$---but the observed OR is $\bot$. The
observed OR is $\bot$ when \emph{both} observed outputs are $\bot$.

The three cases where the true OR is $\top$ are $(\top,\top)$, $(\top,\bot)$,
and $(\bot,\top)$:
\begin{align*}
  \Pr[\hat{b}_1 = \bot, \hat{b}_2 = \bot \mid b_1 = \top, b_2 = \top]
  &\;=\; \fnrate_1\fnrate_2, \\
  \Pr[\hat{b}_1 = \bot, \hat{b}_2 = \bot \mid b_1 = \top, b_2 = \bot]
  &\;=\; \fnrate_1(1 - \fprate_2), \\
  \Pr[\hat{b}_1 = \bot, \hat{b}_2 = \bot \mid b_1 = \bot, b_2 = \top]
  &\;=\; (1 - \fprate_1)\fnrate_2.
\end{align*}

As with the AND's false-positive rate, the OR's false-negative rate depends
on the input distribution: different mixtures of the three patterns yield
different marginal FNR values. For the special case $\fprate_1 = \fprate_2 = 0$
(no false positives), the three conditional FNRs simplify to $\fnrate_1\fnrate_2$,
$\fnrate_1$, and $\fnrate_2$ respectively. The dominant pattern is then
whichever positive-input combination is most common: if most queries have
exactly one positive input, the FNR of OR is close to $\fnrate_1$ or $\fnrate_2$
individually (OR requires only one correct positive report, but it still misses
when the positive input's reporter makes a false negative).

In contrast, the FPR of OR~\eqref{eq:or-fpr-direct} is fully determined
by the individual FPRs alone: the only way the true OR is $\bot$ is if
both true inputs are $\bot$, and the only way the observed OR is then $\top$
is if at least one of the reporters makes a false positive. Both the input
pattern and the conditional false-positive probability are distribution-free.
This asymmetry between the OR's FPR and FNR is the dual of the AND's
asymmetry: AND has a distribution-free FNR and a distribution-dependent FPR;
OR has a distribution-free FPR and a distribution-dependent FNR.

\subsection*{Summary of the three gates}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Gate & FPR & FNR \\
\hline
$\tilde{b}_1 \wedge \tilde{b}_2$ (AND)
  & depends on input prior (see \S\ref{sec:and-under-noise})
  & $\fnrate_1 + \fnrate_2 - \fnrate_1\fnrate_2$ \\
$\neg\tilde{b}$ (NOT)
  & $\fnrate$
  & $\fprate$ \\
$\tilde{b}_1 \vee \tilde{b}_2$ (OR)
  & $\fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2$
  & depends on input prior \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The table crystallizes the fundamental duality between AND and OR. Under
noise, AND suppresses false positives at the cost of inflating false negatives
(the FNR of AND grows monotonically with the number of ANDed instances), while
OR suppresses false negatives at the cost of inflating false positives (the FPR
of OR grows monotonically with the number of ORed instances). NOT is the
exchange operator that converts one regime into the other.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Worked check: consistency at $\fprate = \fnrate$.}
For the symmetric case $\fprate_1 = \fprate_2 = \fprate$ and $\fnrate_1 =
\fnrate_2 = \fnrate$:
\begin{align*}
  \fnrate_\wedge &= 2\fnrate - \fnrate^2, \\
  \fprate_\vee   &= 2\fprate - \fprate^2.
\end{align*}
For a BSC with crossover probability $\fprate = \fnrate$, these are symmetric:
the AND gate inflates errors for positives by the same formula as the OR gate
inflates errors for negatives. De Morgan's duality is exact, as expected.

\subsection*{Boolean algebra closure under noise}

AND, OR, and NOT together generate all of two-valued Boolean algebra. Every
Boolean function of $n$ variables can be expressed as a formula built from
AND, OR, and NOT (this is the completeness of the connective basis $\{\wedge,
\vee, \neg\}$). We have now derived the channel matrices for each of the three
generators. The key structural observation is the following.

\begin{proposition}[Closure of $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ under Boolean operations]
\label{prop:boolean-closure}
If $\tilde{b}_1, \ldots, \tilde{b}_n$ are $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances,
then every Boolean formula $\varphi(\tilde{b}_1, \ldots, \tilde{b}_n)$
built from AND, OR, and NOT is also a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance.
Its channel matrix has entries expressible as polynomials in the input
rates $\fprate_1, \fnrate_1, \ldots, \fprate_n, \fnrate_n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on the structure of $\varphi$. The base case is a single
instance $\tilde{b}_i$, which is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ by assumption.
For the inductive step:

\emph{NOT}: if $\tilde{b}$ is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ with rates
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$, then $\neg\tilde{b}$ is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
with rates $(\fnrate, \fprate)$ by the derivation above.

\emph{AND}: if $\tilde{b}_1$ and $\tilde{b}_2$ are $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instances, then $\tilde{b}_1 \wedge \tilde{b}_2$ is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
by the derivation in \Cref{sec:and-under-noise}, with FNR given
by~\eqref{eq:and-fnr} and FPR by~\eqref{eq:and-fpr-general}. Both are
polynomial functions of the input rates.

\emph{OR}: by De Morgan's law, $\tilde{b}_1 \vee \tilde{b}_2 =
\neg(\neg\tilde{b}_1 \wedge \neg\tilde{b}_2)$. The outer and inner NOTs
each preserve $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ membership (by the NOT case); the
AND of the negated instances is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ (by the AND case).
The composition is therefore $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$.

Since every Boolean formula is built by finitely many applications of AND,
OR, and NOT, the induction terminates and the result holds.
\end{proof}

Closure is not merely a formal nicety. It is what makes the Bernoulli
framework composable: we can build complex Boolean circuits from noisy
gates and know that every intermediate and final result is still a
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance. Error propagation through the circuit
is computable in closed form; it does not require re-deriving the framework
from scratch at each stage.

\subsection*{Looking ahead to composition}

The OR gate illustrates a structural point about the composition theorem,
which \Cref{sec:composition-theorem} will state precisely.

The FPR of OR is $\fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2$. For small
$\fprate_1$ and $\fprate_2$, this is approximately $\fprate_1 + \fprate_2$:
error rates \emph{add} under OR. Equivalently, the probability of no error
is $(1 - \fprate_1)(1 - \fprate_2)$: no-error probabilities \emph{multiply}.

The AND gate, dually, has FNR equal to $\fnrate_1 + \fnrate_2 - \fnrate_1\fnrate_2$:
a symmetric formula showing that false-negative error rates add under AND.
The no-false-negative probability $(1 - \fnrate_1)(1 - \fnrate_2)$ again
factors as a product.

Both formulas are instances of the same pattern. When we chain $k$ independent
identity-with-error stages---each of which fails independently with rate
$\eta_i$---the total no-error probability is $\prod_i (1 - \eta_i)$, and the
total error rate is $\eta_\mathrm{total} = 1 - \prod_i (1 - \eta_i)$.
\Cref{sec:composition-theorem} proves this formula in its general form and
shows that the AND and OR formulas above are special cases. Once we have the
composition theorem in hand, Miller-Rabin's $k$-round error bound becomes
immediate: $k$ rounds of witness-ANDing drive the FPR to
$(1/4)^k$ because each round is an independent stage of error suppression,
and the no-error probability multiplies.

The gates derived in this section---NOT, OR, AND, and their interaction via
De Morgan---are the moving parts of that composition argument. They are also
the gates that give the Bernoulli framework its claim to being a complete
algebra, not merely a collection of special-purpose error-rate formulas.
