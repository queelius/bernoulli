\section{OR and NOT}
\label{sec:or-not}

NOT is immediate and OR follows from it by De Morgan, completing the Boolean
basis.

\subsection*{NOT}

Complementing the output of a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance turns each false
negative into a false positive and conversely: a $\bot$ reported on a true
$\top$ becomes a $\top$ reported on the complemented true $\bot$. So
NOT\index{NOT gate} swaps the rate pair, $(\fprate, \fnrate) \mapsto (\fnrate,
\fprate)$, which on the channel matrix is the row exchange
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:not-Q}
  Q_{\neg} \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fprate & \fprate \\
    \fnrate     & 1 - \fnrate
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
NOT is an involution on $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$: applying it twice restores the
rates, the noisy-bit form of $\neg\neg b = b$ (at the level of the channel
matrix, not of any realized output). Unlike AND and OR, NOT acts on a single
latent bit, so it maps a two-rate instance to a two-rate instance exactly.

\subsection*{OR}

By De Morgan\index{De Morgan duality}, $\tilde{b}_1 \vee \tilde{b}_2 =
\neg(\neg \tilde{b}_1 \wedge \neg \tilde{b}_2)$. Negating the inputs swaps
their rates, the AND of the negated instances has false-negative rate $\fprate_1
+ \fprate_2 - \fprate_1 \fprate_2$ by \eqref{eq:and-fnr} with rates swapped, and
the outer NOT swaps that into the OR's false-positive rate. The truth table
confirms it directly: the observed OR\index{OR gate} is $\bot$ only when both
instances report $\bot$, so a false positive (true $\bot\bot$, observed $\top$)
is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:or-fpr-direct}
  \fprate_\vee \;=\; 1 - (1 - \fprate_1)(1 - \fprate_2)
               \;=\; \fprate_1 + \fprate_2 - \fprate_1 \fprate_2,
\end{equation}
the inclusion-exclusion probability that at least one instance false-positives.
There is one negative latent pair, so this rate is single-valued and prior-free.
The OR's false-negative rate, dually, takes three values over the three positive
latent pairs, $\fnrate_1 \fnrate_2$ at $(\top, \top)$, $\fnrate_1(1 -
\fprate_2)$ at $(\top, \bot)$, and $(1 - \fprate_1)\fnrate_2$ at $(\bot,
\top)$; it reduces to the product $\fnrate_1 \fnrate_2$ only in the
all-positive regime.

The duality is exact: AND has a single false-negative rate \eqref{eq:and-fnr}
and a latent-dependent false-positive rate \eqref{eq:and-negative-rows}, while
OR has a single false-positive rate \eqref{eq:or-fpr-direct} and a
latent-dependent false-negative rate. AND suppresses false positives and
inflates false negatives; OR does the reverse; NOT exchanges the two regimes.
In the symmetric case $\fprate_i = \fnrate_i = \fprate$, the single-valued
sides inflate by the same $2\fprate - \fprate^2$, and De Morgan's duality is
manifest.

\begin{proposition}[What the Boolean operations preserve]
\label{prop:boolean-closure}
Let $\tilde{b}_1, \tilde{b}_2$ be $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances with rates
$(\fprate_i, \fnrate_i)$ on distinct latent bits, independent given those bits.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\neg \tilde{b}_1$ is a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance with rates
    $(\fnrate_1, \fprate_1)$.
  \item $\tilde{b}_1 \wedge \tilde{b}_2$ has the single false-negative rate
    \eqref{eq:and-fnr} and the three negative-row rates
    \eqref{eq:and-negative-rows}. If neither operand lies on the
    zero-information line $\fprate_i + \fnrate_i = 1$
    (\Cref{rem:zero-information-line}), the three coincide, and the output is a
    two-rate instance, if and only if $\fprate_1 = \fprate_2 = 0$.
  \item Dually, $\tilde{b}_1 \vee \tilde{b}_2$ has the single false-positive rate
    \eqref{eq:or-fpr-direct}, and under the same proviso its three positive-row
    rates coincide if and only if $\fnrate_1 = \fnrate_2 = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the one-sided class $\{\fnrate = 0\}$ is closed under OR, the class
$\{\fprate = 0\}$ is closed under AND, NOT exchanges the two classes, and the
general two-rate type is closed under NOT alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~1 is \eqref{eq:not-Q}. For part~2, the rates in \eqref{eq:and-negative-rows}
follow from the truth table by Axiom~1. Equality of $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ and
$(1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2$ holds iff $\fprate_2 = 0$ or $\fprate_1 + \fnrate_1 =
1$; equality of $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ and $\fprate_1(1 - \fnrate_2)$ holds iff
$\fprate_1 = 0$ or $\fprate_2 + \fnrate_2 = 1$. Excluding the zero-information
line leaves $\fprate_1 = \fprate_2 = 0$, at which all three rows vanish. Part~3
follows from part~2 by De Morgan, since NOT swaps $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$. The
closure statements are the special cases: OR of two instances with $\fnrate_i =
0$ has false-negative rate $0$ at every positive latent pair and false-positive
rate \eqref{eq:or-fpr-direct}, and dually for AND.
\end{proof}

The one-sided closure under OR is the reason the union of two Bloom filters is
again a Bloom-type structure with no false negatives
(\Cref{ch:set-composition}); the failure of closure under AND is the reason
their intersection is not, its false-positive rate depending on whether an
element lies in one operand or neither. Repeated independent tests of a
\emph{single} latent bit are the other exception: only the latent pairs
$(\top, \top)$ and $(\bot, \bot)$ can occur, and the conjunction is a two-rate
instance with rates $(\fprate_1 \fprate_2, 1 - (1 - \fnrate_1)(1 -
\fnrate_2))$, the structure of Miller-Rabin. For general instances the gate formulas
remain exact, but only per latent pair: what a whole formula inherits is the
subject of the next section, where a two-rate summary is shown to mislead and
two sound replacements are given.
