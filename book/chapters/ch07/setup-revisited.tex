\section{The Setup Revisited}
\label{sec:setup-revisited}

\Cref{sec:sampling-rates} posed the estimation problem and stopped short of
the theory. An approximate set $\ASet{A} : \Universe \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
arrives with two unknown rates: $\fprate$, the chance it reports $\top$ on a
$\bot$-input, and $\fnrate$, the chance it reports $\bot$ on a $\top$-input.
Query it on $n$ labelled inputs, split them into the $n_0$ trials whose truth
is $\bot$ and the $n_1 = n - n_0$ whose truth is $\top$, count false positives
and false negatives, and report $\hat{\fprate} = \mathrm{FP}/n_0$,
$\hat{\fnrate} = \mathrm{FN}/n_1$. Chapter~1 stated the binomial form, the
unbiasedness, and a Wald confidence interval, all without proof, and deferred
four things: the estimator's exact properties (bias, variance, the MLE
identity, efficiency); proper confidence intervals to replace the Wald form;
the cause of the sawtooth under-coverage its notebook displayed; and the
derived measures, precision and the predictive values, that depend on the
workload as the rates do not. This chapter supplies all four, in that order,
and closes with a deployed Bloom filter run through the whole toolkit.

\paragraph{Notation.}
The rates of \Cref{sec:quantifying-noise} carry their usual symbols:
$\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ for the two error rates, $\tprate = 1 - \fnrate$ for
the true-positive rate\index{true-positive rate} (\emph{recall} or
\emph{sensitivity}), and $\tnrate = 1 - \fprate$ for the true-negative
rate\index{true-negative rate} (\emph{specificity}); the
hatted forms are their empirical estimators. Sample sizes are $n_0$ (truth
$\bot$) and $n_1$ (truth $\top$), so $\hat{\fprate} = \mathrm{FP}/n_0$ and
$\hat{\fnrate} = \mathrm{FN}/n_1$, and $z_{\alpha/2}$ is the upper-$\alpha/2$
standard-Normal quantile. The workload prevalence\index{prevalence} $\pi = \Prob{X = \top}$ is
the fraction of $\top$-inputs in the inference sample; PPV, NPV, and $\Fone$
depend on it and $\fprate, \fnrate$ do not.
