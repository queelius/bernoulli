\section{Confidence Intervals Properly}
\label{sec:confidence-intervals}

\Cref{sec:binomial-estimator} fixed $\hat{\fprate} = \mathrm{FP}/n_0$ as the
unbiased, efficient MLE. A point estimate is not a confidence
statement\index{confidence interval}, and
the Wald interval of \Cref{sec:sampling-rates} fails in exactly the regime a
practitioner most needs it. This section diagnoses the failure and derives
three alternatives.

\begin{definition}[Wald confidence interval]
\label{def:wald-ci}
\index{confidence interval!Wald}At level $1 - \alpha$, the Wald interval for $\fprate$ is $\hat{\fprate} \pm
z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat{\fprate}(1 - \hat{\fprate})/n_0}$.
\end{definition}

It reads the central-limit Normal $\hat{\fprate} \sim \mathrm{Normal}(\fprate,
\fprate(1 - \fprate)/n_0)$ off the sampling distribution and plugs
$\hat{\fprate}$ in for $\fprate$ in the variance. Correct as $n_0 \to \infty$,
it fails at finite $n_0$ near a boundary for two reasons. \emph{Boundary:} the
symmetric radius can push the lower endpoint below $0$ or the upper above $1$,
assigning probability to impossible rates and needing ad hoc clipping. At
$\hat{\fprate} = 0$ it collapses to the degenerate point $[0,0]$.
\emph{Skew:} $\mathrm{Binomial}(n_0, \fprate)$ is symmetric only at $\fprate =
1/2$; near a boundary the central limit theorem has not overcome the binomial
skew, and the symmetric interval miscalibrates, dropping below nominal
coverage (near $0.88$ at $n_0 = 100$, $\fprate = 0.05$ rather than $0.95$).
A third, finer effect is the \emph{sawtooth}: coverage as a function of $n_0$
oscillates jaggedly because the interval is continuous while $\mathrm{FP}$ is
integer-valued, so the cover/miss boundary shifts in discrete jumps. The
sawtooth afflicts every interval on a discrete distribution; Wald is unusual
only in how far its dips fall below nominal.

\subsection*{Wilson, Clopper--Pearson, Jeffreys}

Wilson inverts the \emph{score} statistic, which keeps the true $\fprate$ in
the variance rather than its estimate. The confidence set $\{\fprate :
|Z(\fprate)| \le z\}$ with $Z(\fprate) = (\hat{\fprate} - \fprate)/
\sqrt{\fprate(1 - \fprate)/n_0}$ and $z = z_{\alpha/2}$ squares to the
quadratic
\[
  \fprate^2\Bigl(1 + \tfrac{z^2}{n_0}\Bigr)
  - 2\fprate\Bigl(\hat{\fprate} + \tfrac{z^2}{2 n_0}\Bigr)
  + \hat{\fprate}^2 \le 0,
\]
whose two roots are the interval endpoints.

\begin{definition}[Wilson confidence interval]
\label{def:wilson-ci}
\index{confidence interval!Wilson}At level $1 - \alpha$ with $z = z_{\alpha/2}$,
\[
  \frac{\hat{\fprate} + z^2/(2 n_0)}{1 + z^2/n_0}
  \;\pm\;
  \frac{z}{1 + z^2/n_0}
    \sqrt{\frac{\hat{\fprate}(1 - \hat{\fprate})}{n_0} + \frac{z^2}{4 n_0^2}}.
\]
\end{definition}

The center is $\hat{\fprate}$ shrunk toward $1/2$, and the radius carries a
correction $z^2/(4 n_0^2)$ that keeps the interval non-degenerate at
$\hat{\fprate} \in \{0, 1\}$: at $\hat{\fprate} = 0$ it gives $[0, z^2/(n_0 +
z^2)]$, admitting small positive rates. Both endpoints lie in $[0,1]$ by
construction, killing the boundary problem, and the shift toward $1/2$
corrects the direction the symmetric Wald interval got wrong.

Clopper--Pearson abandons the Normal approximation and inverts the exact
binomial test: $\fprate$ is included iff the observed $\mathrm{FP}$ is
consistent with $\mathrm{Binomial}(n_0, \fprate)$ at level $\alpha$. The
endpoints are Beta quantiles, via the binomial--Beta identity
$\Prob{\mathrm{Binomial}(n_0, \fprate) \ge k} = \Prob{\mathrm{Beta}(k, n_0 - k
+ 1) \le \fprate}$.

\begin{definition}[Clopper--Pearson confidence interval]
\label{def:clopper-pearson-ci}
\index{confidence interval!Clopper--Pearson}At level $1 - \alpha$, the endpoints are $L = F^{-1}_{\mathrm{Beta}
(\mathrm{FP},\, n_0 - \mathrm{FP} + 1)}(\alpha/2)$ and $U = F^{-1}_{\mathrm{Beta}
(\mathrm{FP} + 1,\, n_0 - \mathrm{FP})}(1 - \alpha/2)$, with $L = 0$ if
$\mathrm{FP} = 0$ and $U = 1$ if $\mathrm{FP} = n_0$.
\end{definition}

Called \emph{exact} for using the exact binomial CDF, it guarantees coverage
at least $1 - \alpha$ but, because the binomial is discrete, over-covers at
most $\fprate$, sometimes substantially. Conservatism is the price of the
guarantee.

Jeffreys reports a posterior credible interval under the invariant prior of
\textcite{jeffreys-1946}, $\pi_J(\fprate) \propto \sqrt{I(\fprate)} \propto
\fprate^{-1/2}(1 - \fprate)^{-1/2}$, the $\mathrm{Beta}(1/2, 1/2)$ kernel; by conjugacy the posterior is
$\mathrm{Beta}(\mathrm{FP} + 1/2, n_0 - \mathrm{FP} + 1/2)$.

\begin{definition}[Jeffreys credible interval]
\label{def:jeffreys-ci}
\index{confidence interval!Jeffreys}The central $1 - \alpha$ quantile interval of $\mathrm{Beta}(\mathrm{FP} +
1/2,\, n_0 - \mathrm{FP} + 1/2)$.
\end{definition}

It resembles Clopper--Pearson with a half-integer shift but treats the
endpoints symmetrically and is non-degenerate at the boundaries; its
frequentist coverage is close to nominal at small $n_0$ without
Clopper--Pearson's heavy conservatism.

\begin{remark}[The log-odds scale]
\label{rem:log-odds}
A fourth boundary-robust route transforms before forming a symmetric interval.
The logit $g(\fprate) = \log[\fprate/(1 - \fprate)]$ has $g'(\fprate) =
1/[\fprate(1 - \fprate)]$, so by the delta method
$\mathrm{Var}[g(\hat{\fprate})] \approx 1/[n_0\,\fprate(1 - \fprate)]$. The
transform sends the boundaries to $\pm\infty$, where the raw estimator's
variance collapses to zero and the Wald interval degenerates; a symmetric
interval built on the logit scale and mapped back by the inverse logit stays
strictly inside $(0,1)$ and is asymmetric in $\fprate$. This is the
re-parametrization behind logistic-regression intervals.
\end{remark}

The Wilson interval is this book's default and is what \Cref{sec:derived-measures}
feeds into the delta method: it corrects the Wald boundary failure without
Clopper--Pearson's conservatism and needs only a Normal-quantile table.
Jeffreys is the Bayesian alternative with excellent frequentist coverage;
Clopper--Pearson is the conservative choice when an absolute coverage floor is
required; Wald is acceptable only at large $n_0$ away from the boundaries,
where all four agree. \Cref{sec:derived-measures} now takes up the derived
measures and the delta-method translation of these intervals.
