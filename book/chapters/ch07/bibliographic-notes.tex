\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch7}

\paragraph{The classification-measures manuscript.}
The treatment of PPV, NPV, recall, and $\Fone$ as functions of $(\fprate,
\fnrate, \pi)$, with delta-method variances and confidence intervals, follows
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-classification-measures}, the primary source for
\Cref{sec:derived-measures}: the predictive-value expressions, the
prevalence-dependence diagnostic, the bivariate computation of
\Cref{prop:ppv-variance}, and the $\Fone$ derivation are all from it. The
chapter's addition is to connect that machinery to the Part~II approximate-set
framework through the predicate $\ASet{A} : \Universe \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
and to work the deployed Bloom-filter diagnostic of \Cref{sec:bloom-diagnostics}.

\paragraph{The binomial confidence intervals.}
The interval of \Cref{def:wilson-ci} is due to \textcite{wilson-1927}, who
inverted the score statistic, keeping $\fprate$ free in the variance and
solving the resulting quadratic, rather than fixing $\fprate = \hat{\fprate}$
as the Wald form does. It is the standard modern recommendation for the
binomial proportion at small $n_0$ or near a boundary.
\Cref{def:clopper-pearson-ci} is due to \textcite{clopper-pearson-1934}, who
introduced test inversion in its cleanest form, taking the interval to be the
$\fprate$ values a level-$\alpha$ binomial test fails to reject; the exact
binomial CDF forces coverage strictly above $1 - \alpha$ at most rates, the
over-coverage that suits safety-critical use. The Jeffreys interval of
\Cref{def:jeffreys-ci} predates the Bayesian terminology under which it is now
derived, and its higher-order coverage-matching property is a result in
asymptotic inference.

\paragraph{The delta method.}
The asymptotic-Normality result for smooth functions of an asymptotically
Normal estimator (\Cref{thm:delta-method}) follows from the first-order Taylor
expansion and Slutsky's lemma; a proof in the form used for the PPV and
$\Fone$ derivations is in \textcite[Ch.~5]{casella-berger}, with the
multivariate version and regularity conditions.

\paragraph{The prevalence question.}
The prevalence-dependence of PPV and NPV at fixed $(\fprate, \fnrate)$, the
central caveat of \Cref{sec:derived-measures}, drives the medical
diagnostic-testing literature noted in \Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch1}: a test with
high sensitivity and specificity can still have low PPV when the condition is
rare, because PPV is the classifier-and-workload combination, not the
classifier alone. This chapter's contribution is to make the dependence
explicit and to give a proper interval for any derived quantity at any
specified prevalence.
