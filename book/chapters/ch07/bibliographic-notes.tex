\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch7}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
Every tool in the chapter is standard. The predictive values and their
dependence on prevalence are the core of diagnostic-test epidemiology, where
sensitivity and specificity play the roles of $\tprate$ and $\tnrate$, and the
prevalence-free
index $J = 1 - \fprate - \fnrate$ is due to \textcite{youden-1950};
\textcite{goutte-gaussier-2005} give a probabilistic treatment of precision,
recall, and $\Fone$ with posterior distributions. The score interval is due
to \textcite{wilson-1927}, the exact test-inversion interval to
\textcite{clopper-pearson-1934}, and the invariant prior behind the Jeffreys
interval to \textcite{jeffreys-1946}. The failure of the Wald interval and
the coverage sawtooth are documented by \textcite{agresti-coull-1998} and
\textcite{brown-cai-dasgupta-2001}, whose recommendations the chapter
follows; the delta method is textbook material \parencite[Ch.~5]{casella-berger}.
The chapter's derivations follow the classification-measures manuscript
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-classification-measures}.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
No new statistics. The chapter places these tools inside the channel model,
separating the two intrinsic rates of an approximate set from the
workload-dependent measures computed from them, and works a deployed Bloom
filter through the full chain from design rate to interval to decision
(\Cref{sec:bloom-diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Left out.}
Receiver-operating-characteristic analysis and threshold selection;
multi-class confusion matrices, which chapter~9 treats as $n$-ary channels;
and a caveat on sampling units. The binomial model treats the queries of one
built structure as independent trials, which they are only conditionally on
that build. An interval computed from one filter therefore estimates that
filter's rate, not the mean rate of the construction; estimating the latter
requires independent builds, a point chapter~16 develops under correlated
noise.
