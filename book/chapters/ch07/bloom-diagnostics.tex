\section{Worked Application: Bloom Filter Diagnostics}
\label{sec:bloom-diagnostics}

One deployment problem exercises the whole toolkit: a spam filter implemented
as a Bloom filter, with a question, is the filter precise enough on this
workload, and if not, what to change.

\subsection*{Scenario and observation}

The filter (\Cref{def:bloom-filter}, \Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch}) has
parameters $(m, k, n) = (50000, 7, 5000)$, so $m/n = 10$, $k = 7$ put it in
the optimal regime of \Cref{thm:bloom-optimal-k}, and \eqref{eq:bloom-fpr} at
$kn/m = 0.7$ predicts $\fprate \approx (1 - e^{-0.7})^7 \approx 0.0082$, with
$\fnrate = 0$ by construction. A workload of $N = 10000$ messages arrives,
$n_\top = 1000$ spam and $n_\bot = 9000$ not; the filter reports $\top$ on all
spam and on $\mathrm{FP} = 92$ of the non-spam, so
\[
  \hat{\fprate} = \frac{92}{9000} \approx 0.01022,
\]
about $25\%$ above the design value. The Wilson $95\%$ interval
(\Cref{def:wilson-ci}) at $z = 1.96$ has center
$(0.01022 + 1.96^2/18000)/(1 + 1.96^2/9000) \approx 0.01043$ and half-width
\[
  \frac{1.96}{1.000427}\sqrt{\frac{0.01022 \cdot 0.98978}{9000}
    + \frac{1.96^2}{4 \cdot 9000^2}} \approx 0.00209,
\]
giving $[0.00834, 0.01252]$. The design $\fprate = 0.0082$ sits just below the
lower endpoint: at $n_\bot = 9000$ the interval is narrow enough to detect the
$25\%$ deviation, plausibly from hash collisions that mildly violate the
uniform-hash assumption or an item count above the design $n$. The deployment
$\fprate$ is an empirical quantity to measure, not a design parameter to
trust; we carry $\hat{\fprate} = 0.0102$ forward.

\subsection*{PPV and its interval}

With $\hat\pi = n_\top/N = 0.1$, \Cref{def:ppv} gives
\[
  \widehat{\PPV} = \frac{0.1 \cdot 1}{0.1 \cdot 1 + 0.9 \cdot 0.01022}
  = \frac{0.1}{0.10920} \approx 0.9158,
\]
so about $92\%$ of flagged messages are spam. The delta-method variance
(\Cref{prop:ppv-variance}) simplifies because $\fnrate = 0$ kills the
$\sigma_t^2$ term: with $\bar t = 1000$, $\bar f = 92$, $\sigma_f^2 = 9000
\cdot 0.01022 \cdot 0.98978 \approx 91.06$,
\[
  \Var[\widehat{\PPV}] \approx \frac{1000^2 \cdot 91.06}{1092^4}
  \approx 6.40 \times 10^{-5},
\]
a standard error of $0.0080$, so the $95\%$ interval is $0.9158 \pm 1.96 \cdot
0.0080 \approx [0.900, 0.932]$. The boundary concern of
\Cref{sec:confidence-intervals} does not bite ($\widehat{\PPV}$ is far from
$0$ and $1$, the conditional samples are large). All uncertainty here comes
from the false-positive count; were $\fnrate > 0$, recall would be random and
both variance terms would contribute.

\subsection*{The diagnostic}

Suppose the requirement is $\PPV > 0.95$. The estimate $0.9158$ falls short and
the interval $[0.900, 0.932]$ excludes $0.95$: a fix is needed, and the
interval, not the point, is what establishes it. A point estimate of $0.9158$
alone would have suggested the target was nearly met; the interval rules out
$0.95$ at $95\%$ confidence. \Cref{def:ppv} has three knobs, and $\fnrate = 0$
already, leaving two routes.

\emph{Route A, re-tune.} Lower $\fprate$. Solving $0.95 = 0.1/(0.1 + 0.9
\fprate)$ gives $\fprate \approx 0.00585$; at $\fprate = 0.005$ the PPV is
$0.1/(0.1 + 0.9 \cdot 0.005) \approx 0.957$, above target. Reaching $0.005$
from $0.0102$ by \eqref{eq:bloom-fpr} costs about $16\%$ more bits at fixed
$k$, a one-time hardware decision that buys an $\fprate$ surviving the
worst-case workload.

\emph{Route B, wait.} Keep the filter and bound the acceptable workload.
Solving $0.95 = \pi/(\pi + (1 - \pi) \cdot 0.01022)$ gives $\pi \approx 0.163$;
at $\pi = 0.2$ the PPV is $\approx 0.961$. Doing nothing structural is
acceptable once the spam rate rises above about one in six, but it trades
capital cost for the assumption that the workload mix will shift, which the
deployment does not control.

The choice is operational, not statistical. Both routes used the same three
tools, \eqref{eq:bloom-fpr} for the design rate, the Wilson interval for its
empirical estimate, and \Cref{def:ppv} for the translation into precision at
prevalence. What the chapter added beyond the chapter-5 design formulas is the
honest accounting of uncertainty that turns ``nearly met'' into ``ruled out.''
