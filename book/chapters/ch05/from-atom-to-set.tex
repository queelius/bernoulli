\section{From Atom to Set}
\label{sec:from-atom-to-set}

Part~I studied a single noisy bit\index{noisy bit}. Part~II takes many of them, arranges
them into a structure, and studies the algebra of that structure. The
first such structure is the approximate set, and it is forced rather than
invented.

A classical set $A \subseteq \Universe$ is characterized entirely by its
membership predicate $\SetIndicator{A} : \Universe \to \{\top, \bot\}$,
which returns $\top$ on members and $\bot$ elsewhere; two sets are equal
exactly when their predicates agree everywhere. The predicate \emph{is}
the set, and set-builder notation $\{x : \SetIndicator{A}(x) = \top\}$ is
the predicate read backwards. Replace the exact predicate with a noisy
one and each query $\ASet{A}(x)$ becomes an instance of the formal type
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$\index{Bernoulli Boolean} of \Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}: a Boolean that is
usually, but not always, the correct verdict. There is nowhere else for
the noise to live, because the membership test is the only behavioral
handle on a set.

\begin{definition}[Approximate set]
\label{def:approximate-set}
Let $\Universe$ be a fixed universe and $A \subseteq \Universe$ an
\emph{objective set}. An \emph{approximate set}\index{approximate set} of $A$ is a function
$\ASet{A} : \Universe \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ whose evaluation
$\ASet{A}(x) \in \{\top, \bot\}$ is the structure's report on whether
$x \in A$.
\end{definition}

The objective set $A$ is the truth; $\ASet{A}$ is its noisy report. The
model never loses $A$: the rates of $\ASet{A}$ are defined against it.
What a query loses is the ability to read $A$ off directly. The structure
may be a Bloom filter, a perfect hash filter, or a thresholded
classifier; what makes it an approximate set in the sense of this book
(a \emph{Bernoulli set}\index{Bernoulli set}) is that, under the axioms of
chapter~4, each query is governed by one $2 \times 2$ channel
matrix\index{channel matrix}, two rates of error and nothing else. Read
element by element, this is the misclassification model of a binary
measurement and, for an indicator, the randomized-response
channel\index{randomized response}; \Cref{sec:bib-notes-ch5} traces that
lineage.

\paragraph{The two rates.}
Every query is one of two kinds. For $x \notin A$ the correct verdict is
$\bot$, and the only error is reporting $\top$; for $x \in A$ the correct
verdict is $\top$, and the only error is reporting $\bot$. These define
the false-positive\index{false-positive rate} and false-negative
rates\index{false-negative rate},
\[
  \fprate = \Prob{\ASet{A}(x) = \top \Given x \notin A},
  \qquad
  \fnrate = \Prob{\ASet{A}(x) = \bot \Given x \in A},
\]
the same two rates a single noisy bit carried in \Cref{sec:three-faces},
now read at the level of a set. The description does not grow with the
set: one $\fprate$ governs every non-member query and one $\fnrate$ every
member query, no matter how large $|A|$ or $|\Universe|$.

\paragraph{The block structure.}
That collapse from $2|\Universe|$ potential per-element rates to two is
not automatic; it is \Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom~2) at work.
Membership partitions the universe into the in-set block $B_1 = A$ and the
out-of-set block $B_2 = \Universe \setminus A$, and the axiom forces one
rate per block. An approximate set is thus a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instance whose input domain is $\Universe$ rather than $\{\top, \bot\}$,
with the membership partition standing in for the Boolean one. The
governing matrix is the chapter-4 channel,
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
row~$1$ for the in-set block, row~$2$ for the out-of-set block. Many
queries inherit the chapter-4 machinery wholesale:
\Cref{thm:kronecker} factors the joint behavior of $n$ distinct queries
as the Kronecker product\index{Kronecker factorization} of their per-query channels, using Axiom~1
(\Cref{ax:element-indep}) for cross-element independence and Axiom~2 for
the shared per-block rate. The full joint description of an $n$-query
workload costs two numbers, not $2^n$. \Cref{sec:predicate-algebra} makes
the transfer precise; \Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch} first builds the
canonical instance, the Bloom filter, in closed form, and
\Cref{sec:one-sided-two-sided} draws the line between approximate sets
with one structural zero rate and those with two positive rates.
