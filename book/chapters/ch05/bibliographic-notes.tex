\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch5}

\paragraph{Bloom's original paper.}
The construction is due to \textcite{bloomfilter}, a four-page 1970 note
framed operationally: hyphenation dictionaries for typesetting, trading a
small rate of mishyphenated words for not storing the dictionary. That paper
already contains the optimal-$k$ derivation \Cref{thm:bloom-optimal-k}
reproduces, including $k = (m/n)\ln 2$ and the per-bit rate $(1/2)^k$ at the
optimum. What this monograph adds is the framing, the Bloom filter as one
construction of a function $\Universe \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ rather than
a bit-array trick; the two readings agree on every numerical prediction.

\paragraph{Survey and textbook context.}
\textcite{mitzenmacher-upfal} treat hashing, Bloom filters, and the
balls-and-bins analysis underlying both, with the same optimal-$k$ calculus
used in \Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch} but without the
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ framing. \textcite{broder-mitzenmacher-2004} survey
applications across networking, distributed systems, and databases, and
document the variants, counting, scalable, and compressed filters, that the
present chapter mentions only in passing.

\paragraph{The set-as-predicate viewpoint.}
Reading an approximate set as a function $\Universe \to
\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$, rather than a data structure that happens to have an
error rate, is the contribution of \textcite{towell-bernoulli-sets}. The
earlier literature, including \textcite{bloomfilter} and
\textcite{broder-mitzenmacher-2004}, characterizes such a structure by a
single false-positive probability; the Bernoulli reading instead makes the
function the object of study, its per-query output a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instance in the sense of \textcite{towell-bernoulli-data-type}, and the data
structure one realization among many. The algebra of
\Cref{sec:predicate-algebra} is the direct lift of the order-2 algebra of
chapter~4 to the per-element setting.

\paragraph{One-sided and perfect-filter lineage.}
The dichotomy of \Cref{def:one-sided} has two ancestors. The FNR-zero shape
is the Bloom filter's structural property, named in \textcite{bloomfilter}
but isolated as a category only once the literature accumulated several
constructions sharing it. The FPR-zero shape, a perfect filter on a
designated set, has constructive roots in the universal hashing of
\textcite{carter-wegman-1979}: without a perfect hash on $A$, a
fingerprint table cannot guarantee $\fprate = 0$ on $x \in A$. The perfect
hash filter of \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} is the modern descendant.
Both \Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets} and \Cref{ch:perfect-hash-filters}
build on \textcite{towell-bernoulli-hash-function}, which derives the
space-optimal Bernoulli hash filter for the unrestricted and FPR-zero
regimes and proves the entropy lower bound that makes ``optimal'' precise.
