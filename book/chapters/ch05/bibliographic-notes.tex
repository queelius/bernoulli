\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch5}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
The Bloom filter is due to \textcite{bloomfilter}. Its approximate
false-positive formula and the optimal $k = (m/n)\ln 2$ are standard in the
survey and textbook literature \parencite{broder-mitzenmacher-2004,mitzenmacher-upfal};
\textcite{bose-et-al-2008} showed that the formula neglects the dependence
between probed bits, \textcite{christensen-roginsky-jimeno-2010} gave an exact
expression, and \textcite{kirsch-mitzenmacher-2008} justified double hashing.
The space question the filter answers was posed and bounded by
\textcite{carter-et-al-1978}. The object the chapter calls an approximate set
is older than the filter. A per-element noisy indicator with one
false-positive and one false-negative rate is the misclassification model of
\textcite{bross-1954} and, applied to a sensitive indicator, the
randomized-response channel of \textcite{warner-1965};
\textcite{hirota-1981} studied probabilistic sets as mathematical objects.
Filters with false negatives also exist as constructions: the retouched
\parencite{donnet-baynat-friedman-2006} and generalized
\parencite{laufer-velloso-duarte-2011} Bloom filters are two-sided by design,
and the counting filter of \textcite{fan-et-al-2000} acquires false negatives
under counter overflow \parencite{guo-et-al-2010}.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
A presentation rather than a new structure. The Bloom filter is read as one
realization of a function $\Universe \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ and placed
under the two axioms, so that the Kronecker factorization of chapter~4 fixes
its joint behavior; the one-sided and two-sided vocabulary then organizes
chapters~6, 14, and~15. The set-as-predicate reading follows
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-sets}, and the $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ type follows
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-data-type}. The chapter is explicit that a perfect
hash with fingerprints is FNR-zero, like the Bloom filter, and that the two
axes of the perfect filter line obey the same composition rules.

\paragraph{Left out.}
Dynamic variants beyond the counting filter (scalable, stable
\parencite{deng-rafiei-2006}, and deletable filters); cuckoo
\parencite{fan-cuckoo-2014} and quotient \parencite{bender-quotient-2012}
filters; blocked, cache-efficient layouts; finite-$m$ exact analysis beyond the
smallest example; and adversarially chosen queries, which chapter~16 takes up.
