\section{The Predicate Algebra}
\label{sec:predicate-algebra}

\Cref{sec:from-atom-to-set} described an approximate set's rates informally
and \Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch} built one instance. This section places
the abstraction inside the chapter-4 machinery at the matrix level: the
per-element channel, the Axiom-2 collapse to two per-block rates, and the
Kronecker factorization over many queries. The payoff is the chapter's
first formal statement of parametric parsimony\index{parametric parsimony}, that an approximate set's
entire joint behavior is fixed by two numbers.

\subsection*{Per-element channels and their collapse}

For each $x \in \Universe$ the query $\ASet{A}(x)$ is a
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance. In the most general setting each $x$
carries its own rate, $\fnrate(x)$ if $x \in A$ and $\fprate(x)$ if
$x \notin A$, assembling into a per-element channel
\[
  Q_x \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate(x) & \fnrate(x) \\
    \fprate(x) & 1 - \fprate(x)
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
row~$1$ for the in-set case, row~$2$ for the out-of-set case, matching the
convention of \Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}. Unconstrained, the family
$\{Q_x\}$ carries up to $2|\Universe|$ parameters. Axiom~2
collapses it\index{axiom!block-rate independence}.

\begin{proposition}[Block-stability of per-element rates]
\label{prop:block-stability}
Let $\ASet{A} : \Universe \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ satisfy
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom~2) over the partition $\{A,\,
\Universe \setminus A\}$. Then there exist scalars $\fprate, \fnrate \in
[0,1]$ with $\fnrate(x) = \fnrate$ for all $x \in A$ and $\fprate(x) =
\fprate$ for all $x \notin A$, so the per-element channel is one fixed
matrix $Q$ for every query, and the rate description collapses from
$2|\Universe|$ parameters to the pair $(\fprate, \fnrate)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Axiom~2 assigns each block $B_i$ a single rate $\alpha_i$.
\Cref{prop:stable-Q} then makes $Q_x$ depend on $x$ only through its
block, with row~$i$ determined by $\alpha_i$. For the two-block partition
this gives $\fnrate$ on row~$1$ (every $x \in A$) and $\fprate$ on row~$2$
(every $x \notin A$); the off-diagonal entries follow from row-stochasticity.
Since the block fixes the row and the two rows are the whole matrix,
$Q_x = Q$ for every $x$.
\end{proof}

Throughout Part~II we work in the deterministic-rates regime of
\Cref{ch:axioms-kronecker}, where $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ are fixed
parameters rather than random variables; the conditional and unconditional
Kronecker forms then coincide and we write a single $Q$ for $\ASet{A}$.
What varies across queries is the row a query reads, not the matrix.

\begin{remark}[Heterogeneous blocks]
\label{rem:heterogeneous-block}
The collapse is exactly as strong as Axiom~2. A construction whose error
rate genuinely varies \emph{within} a block escapes it. A learned
classifier used as a membership oracle might answer far-from-boundary
out-of-set queries at $\fprate_{\text{easy}} = 0.01$ but near-boundary ones
at $\fprate_{\text{hard}} = 0.30$, so $\fprate(x)$ depends on $x$ inside
$B_2$ and no single block rate fits.
\Cref{cor:kronecker-set} below then fails in its two-parameter form: the
joint distribution must carry per-element rates and is no longer
universe-size-independent. The gap is workload-dependent. A workload
sampling uniformly from $B_2$ averages over the heterogeneity and looks
single-rate; an adversarial workload concentrating on near-boundary
elements exposes it. This is the boundary between the clean theory and the
classifiers of \Cref{ch:classification-measures}.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The joint distribution factors via Kronecker}

A workload of $m$ queries on distinct elements $x_1, \ldots, x_m$, with
$x_\ell$ in block $j_\ell$ and report $k_\ell$, has joint behavior governed
by \Cref{thm:kronecker}. Its hypotheses are the two axioms: Axiom~1
(\Cref{ax:element-indep}) for cross-element
independence\index{independence!across elements} and Axiom~2 for
the block-stability just established. Since every per-query matrix is the
same $Q$, the joint confusion matrix is the $m$-fold Kronecker power.

\begin{corollary}[Kronecker factorization for approximate sets]
\label{cor:kronecker-set}
\index{Kronecker factorization}Let $\ASet{A}$ satisfy Axioms~1 and~2 over $\{A,\, \Universe \setminus A\}$
in the deterministic-rates regime. The joint confusion matrix of $m$
queries on $m$ distinct elements is
\[
  Q^{(m)} \;=\; Q^{\otimes m},
  \qquad
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
so the full joint description of $\ASet{A}$ requires only
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$, independent of $|\Universe|$ and $|A|$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{prop:block-stability} makes every per-element matrix the same $Q$.
\Cref{thm:kronecker} factors $Q^{(m)} = Q_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes Q_m$,
and in the deterministic-rates regime the conditional and unconditional
forms agree with every factor equal to $Q$, so $Q^{(m)} = Q^{\otimes m}$.
The two scalars fixing $Q$ fix the whole power.
\end{proof}

This earns at the joint level the informal claim of
\Cref{sec:from-atom-to-set}: two scalars fix $Q$, $Q$ fixes the Kronecker
power, and the power fixes the behavior of arbitrarily many queries. To see
it concretely, take $\Universe = \{1, \ldots, 10\}$, $A = \{1, 2, 3\}$,
$\fprate = 0.1$, $\fnrate = 0.05$, so $Q = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0.95 &
0.05 \\ 0.10 & 0.90 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The probability that two
in-set queries and two out-of-set queries all report $\top$ (two true
positives, two false positives) is the product of the corresponding cells,
\[
  Q_{1,1}^2\, Q_{2,1}^2 \;=\; (1 - \fnrate)^2 \fprate^2
  \;=\; 0.95^2 \cdot 0.10^2 \;=\; 0.009025,
\]
while the all-correct outcome has probability $(1-\fnrate)^2(1-\fprate)^2 =
0.731$. Doubling the universe leaves these unchanged; doubling the workload
grows the Kronecker power but keeps each entry a product of two-rate
factors, one per query. The Bloom filter is the special case $\fnrate = 0$;
any other construction supplies a different $Q$ but the same algebra. The
distinction \Cref{sec:one-sided-two-sided} draws next is what happens when
one of the two parameters is structurally zero.
