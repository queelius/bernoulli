\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch19}

\paragraph{Kronecker products and finite-dimensional linear algebra.}
The Kronecker product that carries the tensored-channel construction of
\Cref{prop:set-operator-is-axiom-1}, together with its mixed-product rule and
its action on spectra, is treated in \textcite{horn-johnson-matrix},
\emph{Topics in Matrix Analysis}, the standard reference for the operator
identities this chapter uses. The eigenvalues-multiply-along-a-product reading of
serial composition is the elementary spectral theory of commuting operators in
the same source.

\paragraph{Stochastic matrices and Markov chains.}
A Bernoulli map is a column-stochastic matrix, and the algebra of such matrices,
products, stationary vectors, and spectra, is the algebra of finite Markov
chains. \textcite{levin-peres-wilmer-2017}, \emph{Markov Chains and Mixing
Times}, is the modern textbook treatment; \textcite{mitzenmacher-upfal},
\emph{Probability and Computing}, gives the same material in the algorithmic
setting closest to this book's use of it. The transpose convention, whether the
stochastic matrix acts on the left of column vectors or the right of row vectors,
differs across these sources and from \Cref{sec:n-ary-channels}; the underlying
matrix is the same and only its layout changes.

\paragraph{Fourier analysis on the Boolean cube.}
The $\pm 1$-bias and Walsh-coefficient readings of the flip eigenvalue in
\Cref{prop:parity-spectral} belong to the Fourier-analysis-of-Boolean-functions
tradition, for which \textcite{odonnell-2014}, \emph{Analysis of Boolean
Functions}, is the comprehensive reference. The multiplicativity of the
order-one coefficient under XOR, the identity behind the noisy-parity rate, is
the cube-Fourier face of the tensor factorization of
\Cref{prop:set-operator-is-axiom-1}.

\paragraph{Reliable computation from noisy components.}
The accounting of error through XOR gates by the product of $\pm 1$ biases is the
classical tradition of computing reliably with unreliable parts.
\textcite{von-neumann-1956}, ``Probabilistic Logics and the Synthesis of
Reliable Organisms from Unreliable Components,'' began it, and
\textcite{pippenger-1988}, ``Reliable Computation by Formulas in the Presence of
Noise,'' gave the formula-depth analysis in which the bias-product bookkeeping is
standard. The syndrome-bit version of the same identity, a measured parity
inheriting $\prod_i (1 - 2 q_i)$ from its constituent single-bit readout errors,
is the quantum-readout instance of \Cref{sec:n-ary-channels}.

\paragraph{Density matrices and the diagonal sector.}
The locating statement of \Cref{sec:operator-classical-sector}, that the
framework is the classical stochastic algebra on the diagonal of the density
operator in the computational basis, rests on standard density-matrix and
decoherence facts. \textcite{nielsen-chuang-2010}, \emph{Quantum Computation and
Quantum Information}, is the textbook reference for the density operator, the
distinction between its diagonal probabilities and its off-diagonal coherences,
and the readout-error channel that this chapter reads as a Kronecker product of
single-qubit stochastic matrices.
