\section{N-ary Channels}
\label{sec:n-ary-channels}

\Cref{sec:atom-to-function} established the type-lifting picture: an
approximate function $\tilde{f} : \Universe \to T$ produces a noisy
$T$-valued report at every input, and the per-block behavior is a
distribution on $T$ rather than the single Bernoulli parameter that
sufficed for the Boolean case. This section converts that picture into
a formal definition. The object is a row-stochastic matrix; the
parameter count and the axioms transfer directly from chapter~4, with
the binary $2$ replaced by the model-order $K$ on the input side and
the output cardinality $L$ on the output side. The rest of Part~III
builds on the matrix defined here.

\subsection*{The formal definition}

The setup mirrors \Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}: an input domain, an
output domain, and a channel matrix that records the per-block
output distribution. What changes is the size of the matrix.

\begin{definition}[n-ary channel]
\label{def:n-ary-channel}
An \emph{n-ary channel} is a triple $(X, Y, Q)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $X$ is the \emph{input domain}, partitioned by the finest
    compatible partition (\Cref{def:model-order}) into $K$ blocks
    $B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_K$. The integer $K$ is the \emph{model
    order} of the channel.
  \item $Y$ is the \emph{output domain}, with $|Y| = L$. The
    elements of $Y$ are indexed $1, 2, \ldots, L$.
  \item $Q$ is a $K \times L$ \emph{row-stochastic matrix}: its
    entries satisfy $Q_{ij} \ge 0$ for every $i \in \{1, \ldots, K\}$
    and $j \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$, and every row sums to one,
    \[
      \sum_{j = 1}^{L} Q_{ij} \;=\; 1
      \qquad
      \text{for each } i \in \{1, \ldots, K\}.
    \]
    The entry $Q_{ij}$ is the probability that the channel reports
    output $j$ given that the input lies in block $B_i$:
    \[
      Q_{ij} \;=\; \Prob{\text{output} = j \Given \text{input} \in B_i}.
    \]
  The triple satisfies \Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom~1: error events
  at distinct inputs are mutually independent) and
  \Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom~2: the per-block output
  distributions are conditionally independent of one another given
  the objective function).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The triple $(X, Y, Q)$ is the n-ary analogue of the Boolean triple of
\Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}. Setting $K = L = 2$ recovers that
definition exactly, with the two rows of $Q$ governing the two
blocks $B_1 = \{\top\}$ and $B_2 = \{\bot\}$. Allowing $K$ or $L$ to
exceed $2$ produces a strictly larger family of channels, of which
the Boolean case is the smallest non-trivial member.

Three components of the definition deserve highlighting. First, the
input partition is the finest compatible partition in the sense of
\Cref{def:model-order}: two inputs go in the same block precisely
when their output distributions agree. This is the same convention as
chapter~2; the model order $K$ is a property of the channel itself,
not a description of the input domain's set-theoretic structure.
Second, the rows are indexed by input blocks and the columns by
output values, with row $i$ giving the distribution conditional on
input block $B_i$. The convention matches the $2 \times 2$ orientation
of chapter~4 (row 1 for $\{\top\}$, row 2 for $\{\bot\}$) and
generalizes without surprise to the $K \times L$ case. Third, the
row-stochastic constraint $\sum_j Q_{ij} = 1$ is the only constraint
the entries satisfy beyond non-negativity. Every row is a probability
distribution on the output alphabet, and that distribution is
arbitrary up to summing to one. The shape of the matrix is wider when
$L$ is larger and taller when $K$ is larger; non-square shapes ($K
\ne L$) are the rule rather than the exception, and a $K = 5, L = 2$
channel (say, a five-block classifier whose output is binary
accept/reject) is just as much an n-ary channel as the square cases.

\subsection*{Notation: $K$ uppercase, $k$ lowercase}

Throughout Part~III, the symbol $K$ (uppercase) denotes the
model-order of the input partition, as fixed by
\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}. The symbol $k$ (lowercase) is reserved for
the two unrelated counts that have run through Parts~I and~II: the
number of hash functions in a Bloom filter (\Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch})
and the number of independent witness draws in a round of
Miller-Rabin primality testing (\Cref{sec:miller-rabin}). The two
roles are unrelated, and the case distinction (uppercase versus
lowercase) is the one device that keeps them apart.

The collision is real, not contrived. A Bloom filter has model order
$K = 2$ (the input domain partitions into ``in the set'' and ``not in
the set'') and hash count $k$ (the per-element bookkeeping parameter
that controls space and the false-positive rate). Miller-Rabin has
model order $K = 2$ (the input partitions into ``prime'' and
``composite'') and round count $k$ (the independent witnesses
combined per query). In both cases the model-order $K$ is fixed by
the problem (it is binary), and the lowercase $k$ is a design knob
that the implementer chooses. Treating them as a single symbol would
collapse two genuinely different roles and obscure the fact that
$K = 2$ is structural while $k$ is tunable. Beyond Part~II, where
$K = 2$ is implicit throughout and the lowercase $k$ shoulders the
narrative weight, Part~III makes $K$ explicit and lets it vary, while
the lowercase $k$ continues to denote per-construction tuning
parameters in the chapters that introduce them.

The same case discipline applies to $L$. The output cardinality is
the uppercase $L$; any lowercase $l$ that appears in derivations is
a per-stage index or summation variable and is local in scope. The
convention is the same one used in chapter~4 for $Q$ and in
chapter~8 for capacity: structural parameters of the channel are
uppercase, indices and tuning knobs are lowercase.

\subsection*{Examples}

The definition specializes to every channel that has appeared so
far in the book, plus the new n-ary cases that motivate Part~III.
We work through four examples: two binary, one three-way, and one
with a countably infinite input and output domain.

\paragraph{Example 1: $K = 2$, $L = 2$, symmetric.}
The simplest n-ary channel is the binary symmetric channel of
\Cref{def:bsc}. The input partitions into $B_1 = \{\top\}$ and $B_2
= \{\bot\}$, both with the same flip probability $\fprate$, and the
matrix is
\[
  Q_{\mathrm{BSC}} \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fprate & \fprate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The model order is $K = 2$ by partition count under the discrete
partition, with the symmetry constraint $\fprate$ shared between the
rows; the output cardinality is $L = 2$; and the matrix is square.
The definition specializes exactly: $Q_{\mathrm{BSC}}$ is a $2 \times
2$ row-stochastic matrix and the axioms transfer directly from
\Cref{def:bernoulli-bool}.

\paragraph{Example 2: $K = 2$, $L = 2$, asymmetric.}
Dropping the symmetry constraint recovers the asymmetric binary
channel of \Cref{def:asymmetric-channel}:
\[
  Q_{\mathrm{asym}} \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate     & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Row 1 governs $B_1 = \{\top\}$ with false-negative rate $\fnrate$;
row 2 governs $B_2 = \{\bot\}$ with false-positive rate $\fprate$.
The matrix is again $2 \times 2$, but the two rows now have
independently chosen probabilities, and the channel is fully
specified by the pair $(\fprate, \fnrate)$. The Z-channel is the
specialization $\fnrate = 0$ (or $\fprate = 0$): a one-sided
asymmetric channel still living in the $K = L = 2$ family.

\paragraph{Example 3: $K = 3$, $L = 3$, a 3-class classifier.}
A confusion matrix is exactly an n-ary channel. Suppose
$\tilde{f}$ classifies an input image as one of three labels: cat,
dog, or bird. The input domain partitions into three blocks by true
class, $B_1 = \text{cats}$, $B_2 = \text{dogs}$, $B_3 = \text{birds}$,
and the output alphabet is also $\{\text{cat}, \text{dog},
\text{bird}\}$. A representative channel with 90\% per-class accuracy
and the off-diagonal mass split evenly between the two competing
labels is
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0.90 & 0.05 & 0.05 \\
    0.05 & 0.90 & 0.05 \\
    0.05 & 0.05 & 0.90
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each row sums to $1$; the diagonal entries are the per-class
accuracies; the off-diagonal entries are the per-class confusion
rates. The matrix has six free parameters once row sums are imposed
($3 \times (3 - 1) = 6$). The example above is constrained beyond
the row-sum requirement (each row has only one free parameter, the
diagonal $0.90$), but a generic 3-class classifier uses all six
degrees of freedom and produces a non-symmetric confusion matrix in
which different true classes have different error profiles.

\paragraph{Example 4: $K = \infty$, $L = \infty$, a counter.}
Sketches preview. Let $\tilde{f}$ be a frequency estimator whose
input is a stream item and whose output is an integer estimate
$\hat{n}$ of that item's true count $n$. The input domain partitions
into the blocks $B_n = \{x : \text{true count of } x = n\}$ for
$n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, giving $K = \infty$. The output alphabet is
$\mathbb{N}$, so $L = \infty$. The channel matrix is infinite in both
dimensions, but row $n$ is still a probability distribution on
$\mathbb{N}$ (typically concentrated near $n$ with a tractable tail).
Writing the matrix out is impractical, but the structure transfers:
$Q_{nm}$ is the probability that the estimator reports $m$ when the
true count is $n$, and every row sums to one. Concrete sketches
realize this object with specific tail shapes (CountMin gives a
one-sided tail, HyperLogLog gives a relative-error tail), and
chapter~10 builds out the constructions in detail.

\subsection*{Axiom transfer from chapter 4}

The two chapter~4 axioms transfer to the n-ary setting without
modification. Both axioms are stated in chapter~4 for the Boolean
case, but neither statement is binary in any essential way: they are
claims about independence, and independence is indifferent to
alphabet size.

\paragraph{Axiom 1 (\Cref{ax:element-indep}) lifts unchanged.}
The Boolean form of the axiom asserts that error events at distinct
inputs are mutually independent: the probability that the channel
errs on input $x_1$ is independent of the probability that it errs
on $x_2$, for any pair of distinct inputs. The same statement
applies verbatim in the n-ary case, with ``errs'' replaced by
``produces output $j$'': the events $\{\text{output on } x_1 =
j_1\}$ and $\{\text{output on } x_2 = j_2\}$ are independent for
all $x_1 \ne x_2$ and all $j_1, j_2 \in Y$. The Boolean case is the
$L = 2$ instance of the same condition. Nothing in the proof of
\Cref{ax:element-indep} (or in any of its uses) depended on $L = 2$,
and the proof template carries forward.

\paragraph{Axiom 2 (\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}) lifts unchanged.}
The Boolean form asserts that the per-block rates $\fprate$ and
$\fnrate$ are conditionally independent of each other given the
objective set. In the n-ary case, each block $B_i$ has a per-block
output distribution rather than a single rate, but the independence
claim is the same: the per-block distributions
$Q_{1\,\cdot}, Q_{2\,\cdot}, \ldots, Q_{K\,\cdot}$ (the $K$ rows
of $Q$ viewed as probability distributions on $Y$) are mutually
conditionally independent given the objective function. The Boolean
case is the $K = 2$ instance, with the two row distributions
parametrized by $(\fprate)$ and $(\fnrate)$ respectively. The
generalization to $K$ rows is dimensional, not conceptual.

The conjunction of Axiom~1 and Axiom~2 is what makes the channel
matrix $Q$ a sufficient summary of the n-ary channel's behavior, in
the same sense that the pair $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ was sufficient for
the Boolean case. \Cref{sec:n-ary-kronecker} converts the
independence claim into a factorization theorem for joint queries.

\subsection*{Parameter count}

A $K \times L$ row-stochastic matrix has $K \cdot L$ entries, but
the $K$ row-sum constraints (one per row) eliminate one degree of
freedom per row. The free-parameter count is therefore
\[
  K \cdot L - K \;=\; K(L - 1).
\]
Each row of $L$ probabilities is a probability distribution on a
finite set, and a distribution on an $L$-element set has $L - 1$ free
parameters. Multiplying by the $K$ rows gives the total. The formula
is the n-ary generalization of the Boolean parameter count of
\Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}: at $K = L = 2$, it reduces to
$2 \cdot (2 - 1) = 2$, the familiar $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ pair.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{The growth rate.}
The parameter count grows linearly in both $K$ and $L$ (a $K = 5,
L = 3$ channel has $5 \cdot 2 = 10$ parameters; a $K = 10, L = 4$
channel has $10 \cdot 3 = 30$). For practical specification, the
growth is gentle: a 10-class classifier with model order $K = 10$ is
specified by 90 numbers (90\% of the 100 entries of $Q$). The
contrast with the unconstrained joint-distribution parameter count
is sharp. A general joint distribution over the same $K \times L$
input-output pairs has $K \cdot L - 1$ parameters in total (just
one normalization constraint over the whole table). The Axiom~1
and Axiom~2 reduction from chapter~4 cuts this to $K(L - 1)$ by
imposing per-row normalization, exactly as in the Boolean case.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Constraints from problem structure.}
Many channels carry additional structural constraints that further
reduce the parameter count below $K(L - 1)$. Example~1's BSC
imposes the symmetry constraint $\fprate$ shared between the two
rows, dropping the count from $2$ to $1$. Example~3's 3-class
classifier with uniform off-diagonal mass (and a common diagonal)
imposes additional constraints that reduce the six row-sum-free
parameters to a single accuracy value $0.90$. Strongly symmetric
n-ary channels (\Cref{sec:n-ary-capacity}) impose that the rows
of $Q$ be permutations of each other and that the columns be
permutations as well, reducing the count to a single error
probability. The $K(L - 1)$ count is the upper bound; specific
channels can be much more compact under their own symmetries.

\subsection*{Where the chapter goes next}

The chapter now has the formal object that \Cref{sec:atom-to-function}
previewed: a $K \times L$ row-stochastic matrix $Q$, satisfying
the two chapter~4 axioms, with $K(L - 1)$ free parameters in general.
The next two sections lift the two structural results of Parts~I and~II
to the n-ary setting. \Cref{sec:n-ary-kronecker} generalizes
\Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}: the joint distribution over $m$
queries on an n-ary channel factors as the $m$-fold Kronecker product
of the per-query channel matrices, with parameter count growing as
$mK(L - 1)$ rather than exponentially in $m$. \Cref{sec:n-ary-capacity}
generalizes the BSC capacity result: capacity is still the maximum
mutual information $I(X; Y)$ over input distributions on the $K$
blocks, with closed forms for the symmetric sub-family and numerical
recipes (Blahut-Arimoto) for the general case. Both results have the
same shape as their Boolean ancestors; the change is dimensional, and
the proofs are mostly bookkeeping once the n-ary Kronecker structure
is in hand.
