\section{The n-ary Kronecker Theorem}
\label{sec:n-ary-kronecker}

\Cref{sec:n-ary-channels} closed with the promise that the joint
distribution over $m$ queries on an n-ary channel would factor in the
same way as for the Boolean case of chapter~4. This section delivers
the promise. The statement, the proof, the corollary, and the worked
example all mirror \Cref{sec:kronecker}; the only change is dimensional,
with the binary output alphabet replaced by an output alphabet of size
$L$ and the binary input partition replaced by one of size $K$.

\subsection*{Recalling the chapter 4 theorem}

At model-order 2, chapter~4 proved that the joint confusion matrix of
$m$ queries on a Bernoulli predicate factors as a Kronecker product of
the $m$ per-query channel matrices. The headline statement is
\Cref{thm:kronecker}: under Axiom~1 (error events at distinct inputs
are mutually independent, \Cref{ax:element-indep}) and Axiom~2
(per-block rates are mutually independent across blocks,
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}), the rate-conditional joint matrix
factors as
\[
  Q^{(m) *}(\alpha) \;=\; Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes Q^*_2(\alpha)
                          \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha).
\]
\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional} promotes this conditional statement
to an unconditional one when the queried blocks are pairwise distinct,
or, more importantly for the worked examples of Parts~I and~II, when
the per-block rates are deterministic constants. In the
deterministic-rates regime, $Q^*_\ell = Q_\ell$ for every query
position $\ell$, and the conditional and unconditional matrices
coincide. Plan~5's §5.3 review of Part~II confirmed that every Bloom
filter and Miller-Rabin computation in the book operates in this
regime, and Part~III continues the convention. From here on we drop
the conditional star and write $Q$ for the (deterministic) per-query
channel matrix.

The structural payoff of the chapter~4 theorem was the parameter-count
collapse of \Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}: the joint matrix
$Q^{(m)}$ requires $nm$ free parameters rather than the
$n(2^m - 1)$ that an unconstrained $m$-query channel would demand. The
exponent $2^m$ became a linear $2m$ because the two axioms force a
Kronecker structure that no general joint distribution shares. The
$n$-ary generalization preserves this collapse, with the binary $2$
replaced by the output cardinality $L$.

\subsection*{The headline theorem}

Fix an n-ary channel $(X, Y, Q)$ in the sense of
\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}: input domain $X$ partitioned into $K$ blocks
$B_1, \ldots, B_K$, output domain $Y$ of size $L$, and channel matrix
$Q$ a $K \times L$ row-stochastic matrix whose entries $Q_{ij}$ record
$\Prob{Y = j \Given X \in B_i}$. The axioms transfer from chapter~4
without modification, as already established in
\Cref{sec:n-ary-channels}. We now query the channel $m$ times.

\begin{theorem}[n-ary Kronecker factorization]
\label{thm:n-ary-kronecker}
Let $(X, Y, Q)$ be an n-ary channel of model-order $K$ and output
cardinality $L$, satisfying \Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom~1) and
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom~2), and working in the
deterministic-rates regime of chapter~4. Let $X_1, \ldots, X_m$ be
$m$ inputs queried in order, with $X_i \in B_{j_i}$ for each
$i = 1, \ldots, m$, and let $Y_1, \ldots, Y_m$ be the corresponding
outputs. Then the joint distribution of outputs given the input
blocks factors as
\[
  \Prob{Y_1, \ldots, Y_m \Given X_1 \in B_{j_1}, \ldots,
                                 X_m \in B_{j_m}}
  \;=\;
  \prod_{i = 1}^{m} Q_{j_i,\, Y_i}.
\]
Equivalently, the $K^m \times L^m$ joint channel matrix
$Q^{(m)}$ whose
$(j_1 \cdots j_m,\, y_1 \cdots y_m)$-entry is the probability above
factors as the $m$-fold Kronecker product
\[
  Q^{(m)} \;=\; Q \otimes Q \otimes \cdots \otimes Q
          \;=\; Q^{\otimes m}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The statement is the n-ary version of
\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional} once the conditional form
\Cref{thm:kronecker} is specialized to deterministic rates. Each
per-query channel matrix is the same $K \times L$ matrix $Q$, so the
$m$-fold Kronecker product is a Kronecker power. Different queries on
inputs from the same block draw from the same row of $Q$, and the
independence forced by Axiom~1 makes the joint probability the
product of $m$ row-column lookups in $Q$. The factor for query $i$ is
the $(j_i, Y_i)$ entry: pick row $j_i$ for the input block, read
column $Y_i$ for the observed output, and multiply across queries.

Two notes on scope. First, the statement is in the
deterministic-rates regime of chapter~4, the regime that every worked
example in Parts~I and~II inhabited and that Part~III continues to
inhabit by default. In the more general random-rates setting, the
same conditional-then-unconditional structure of
\Cref{thm:kronecker} applies, with the same Jensen-gap caveat for
repeated-block queries. The deterministic case is the case we use, and
keeping the statement focused on it keeps the bookkeeping clean.
Second, the queries are on distinct input elements; repeated queries
on the same element produce perfectly correlated outputs in any
deterministic implementation, just as in chapter~4. Repeated queries
on \emph{different} elements that happen to lie in the same block
$B_{j_i} = B_{j_{i'}}$ are still independent under Axiom~1: the
events depend on different elements' draws, and only the row of $Q$
they consult is shared.

\subsection*{Proof sketch}

The proof recycles the chapter~4 argument with one cosmetic change:
each row of $Q$ has $L$ entries instead of two. The four steps below
mirror \Cref{thm:kronecker}'s proof step for step.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: reduce to a joint probability calculation.}
Index the queries by $i = 1, \ldots, m$. Query $i$ inputs element
$X_i \in B_{j_i}$ and observes output $Y_i \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$.
The $(j_1 \cdots j_m,\, y_1 \cdots y_m)$-entry of $Q^{(m)}$ is
\[
  Q^{(m)}_{j_1 \cdots j_m,\, y_1 \cdots y_m}
  \;=\;
  \Prob{Y_1 = y_1, \ldots, Y_m = y_m \Given
        X_i \in B_{j_i} \text{ for each } i}.
\]
We aim to show this equals $\prod_{i = 1}^{m} Q_{j_i,\, y_i}$, which
is the corresponding entry of $Q^{\otimes m}$ by the definition of
the Kronecker product.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Axiom 2 makes each per-query row stable.}
The deterministic-rates regime means each per-block output
distribution is a fixed row of $Q$, not a random draw from a
distribution over rows. Axiom~2 (\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep})
guarantees that the rows of $Q$ are jointly meaningful: the
distribution of outputs given $X_i \in B_{j_i}$ is row $j_i$ of $Q$,
the same row regardless of which other queries are made or what their
input blocks are. In the chapter~4 proof this was the role of the
proposition that rates remain stable across queries. In the n-ary
case the statement is identical, with rows of $Q$ in place of the
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$ pair.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Axiom 1 factors the joint distribution.}
The outputs $Y_1, \ldots, Y_m$ are deterministic functions of the
error events at $X_1, \ldots, X_m$. Axiom~1
(\Cref{ax:element-indep}) asserts that these error events are
mutually independent across distinct inputs, and the $L = 2$
restriction in the original Boolean statement plays no role in the
independence claim itself. Independence of error events implies
independence of outputs, so
\begin{align*}
  &\Prob{Y_1 = y_1, \ldots, Y_m = y_m \Given X_i \in B_{j_i}
          \text{ for each } i} \\
  &\quad =\; \prod_{i = 1}^{m}
              \Prob{Y_i = y_i \Given X_i \in B_{j_i}} \\
  &\quad =\; \prod_{i = 1}^{m} Q_{j_i,\, y_i}.
\end{align*}
The first equality is independence; the second is the definition of
$Q_{j_i,\, y_i}$ as the probability that the channel reports output
$y_i$ given input block $B_{j_i}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: read off the matrix.}
The product $\prod_{i = 1}^{m} Q_{j_i,\, y_i}$ is, by the definition
of the Kronecker product, the
$(j_1 \cdots j_m,\, y_1 \cdots y_m)$-entry of $Q \otimes \cdots
\otimes Q = Q^{\otimes m}$. Since the identification holds for every
index tuple, the joint matrix factors:
\[
  Q^{(m)} \;=\; Q^{\otimes m}. \qed
\]

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{The conditions used.} The deterministic-rates regime
plus Axiom~1 plus Axiom~2 is exactly the n-ary version of what the
chapter~4 proof of \Cref{thm:kronecker} required. Axiom~1 supplies
factorization across queries; Axiom~2 supplies stability of each
per-block row across queries; the deterministic-rates regime collapses
the rate-conditional and unconditional statements into one. The only
binary-specific step in chapter~4 was reading the two entries of each
row as $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ or $(\tprate, \fnrate)$ pairs; the n-ary
proof reads each row as a length-$L$ probability vector and the rest
is the same calculation.

\subsection*{Parameter count}

\begin{corollary}[Parameter count for joint n-ary queries]
\label{cor:n-ary-parameter-count}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker}, the joint channel
matrix $Q^{(m)}$ of $m$ queries on an n-ary channel of model-order
$K$ and output cardinality $L$ is fully specified by the single
$K \times L$ matrix $Q$, which carries $K(L - 1)$ free parameters.
Adding queries does not add free parameters; it adds only the
specification of which input block each query draws from. The general
$m$-query joint channel, by contrast, would have $K (L^m - 1)$ free
parameters per source-block convention, growing exponentially in $m$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Kronecker product $Q^{\otimes m}$ is determined entirely by the
single matrix $Q$. Each query position contributes one row index
$j_i$ (the input block) and one column index $y_i$ (the observed
output), and the joint entry is the product of $m$ entries of $Q$
read off in the order given by these indices. The free parameters in
$Q$ are exactly $K (L - 1)$, as established by the parameter count of
\Cref{sec:n-ary-channels} (each of the $K$ rows is a probability
distribution on $L$ outputs, hence $L - 1$ free parameters per row).
Adding a query position $m + 1$ multiplies the joint matrix shape
from $K^m \times L^m$ to $K^{m+1} \times L^{m+1}$, but the underlying
parameters do not change: the new factor in the Kronecker product is
still $Q$. The exponential parameter cost of the unconstrained
$m$-query channel is
\[
  K (L^m - 1),
\]
one probability distribution on $L^m$ joint outputs per source block,
minus the normalization constraint per block. The ratio
$\frac{K(L^m - 1)}{K(L - 1)} = \frac{L^m - 1}{L - 1}$ grows
exponentially in $m$ for fixed $L \ge 2$.
\end{proof}

This corollary is the direct generalization of
\Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}. At $K = L = 2$, the
parameter count $K(L - 1) = 2$ recovers the familiar
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$ pair of the Boolean case, and the unconstrained
count $K(L^m - 1) = 2(2^m - 1)$ recovers the exponential reference
point of chapter~4. The growth in $m$ is the same: linear under the
axioms, exponential without them. The growth in $L$ is new and
benign: the unconstrained joint scales as $L^m$, but each row of the
per-query channel scales only as $L$, so a 10-output channel queried
50 times is specified by $K \cdot 9$ numbers rather than
$K \cdot (10^{50} - 1)$. The reduction is even more dramatic at
larger $L$ than it was at $L = 2$, which is part of why the n-ary
generalization is worth stating explicitly.

\subsection*{Worked example: the 3-class classifier}

We compute a joint probability with the 3-class classifier of
\Cref{sec:n-ary-channels}, Example~3. The channel has $K = L = 3$
and matrix
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0.9  & 0.05 & 0.05 \\
    0.05 & 0.9  & 0.05 \\
    0.05 & 0.05 & 0.9
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Row $i$ governs inputs in block $B_i$ (the inputs whose true class is
$i$), column $j$ records the probability that the classifier outputs
class $j$, and the diagonal entries are the per-class accuracies of
$90\%$. The matrix has $K(L - 1) = 3 \cdot 2 = 6$ free parameters in
general; this particular $Q$ is constrained beyond that, with a single
diagonal value and uniform off-diagonal mass, but the structure of
$Q^{\otimes m}$ does not care about those extra constraints.

Consider three queries with inputs
\[
  (X_1, X_2, X_3) \in (B_1, B_2, B_1)
\]
and observed outputs
\[
  (Y_1, Y_2, Y_3) = (1, 2, 1).
\]
Each query is correctly classified: $X_1$ is a true class-1 input
that the classifier reports as class $1$; $X_2$ is a true class-2
input reported as class $2$; $X_3$ is another true class-1 input
reported as class $1$. The block sequence $(B_1, B_2, B_1)$ has $B_1$
appearing twice; in the random-rates setting of chapter~4 this would
trigger the Jensen-gap caveat, but in the deterministic-rates regime
both class-1 queries draw from the same fixed row of $Q$ and the
products work out cleanly.

\Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} gives the joint probability as
\begin{align*}
  \Prob{Y_1 = 1, Y_2 = 2, Y_3 = 1 \Given X_1 \in B_1, X_2 \in B_2,
        X_3 \in B_1}
  &\;=\; Q_{1, 1} \cdot Q_{2, 2} \cdot Q_{1, 1} \\
  &\;=\; 0.9 \cdot 0.9 \cdot 0.9 \\
  &\;=\; 0.729.
\end{align*}
Three independent queries each correctly classified with probability
$0.9$ produces a joint correct-classification probability of $0.9^3$,
which is the n-ary analogue of the BSC's $(1 - \fprate)^m$ joint
success probability from chapter~4's worked example.

To exercise an off-diagonal entry, consider the same input blocks
$(B_1, B_2, B_1)$ but the output sequence $(Y_1, Y_2, Y_3) =
(2, 2, 1)$ (first input misclassified as class $2$, second and third
correct). The joint probability is
\begin{align*}
  Q_{1, 2} \cdot Q_{2, 2} \cdot Q_{1, 1}
  &\;=\; 0.05 \cdot 0.9 \cdot 0.9 \\
  &\;=\; 0.0405.
\end{align*}
The first factor is the probability of class-1 input being reported
as class $2$ ($0.05$, off-diagonal); the next two factors are the
correct-classification probabilities for the second and third queries.

The joint matrix $Q^{(3)}$ for this classifier has shape
$27 \times 27$. The unconstrained joint parameter count by the
per-source-block convention is $K(L^m - 1) = 78$ for $m = 3$, but
the Kronecker factorization brings it down to the $K(L - 1) = 6$
that specifies $Q$ itself. For $m = 10$ the gap is starker:
$3 \cdot (59\,049 - 1) \approx 1.77 \times 10^5$ unconstrained
parameters versus the same $6$. The exponential cost of joint
queries without independence is the chapter~4 story told with
bigger matrices.

\subsection*{Looking ahead}

\Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker} closes the structural loop opened in
\Cref{sec:n-ary-channels}. The two chapter~4 axioms transfer to the
n-ary setting, and the Kronecker theorem they force transfers with
them. \Cref{sec:n-ary-capacity} turns from structure to information,
generalizing the BSC capacity formula of chapter~8 to the n-ary case.
