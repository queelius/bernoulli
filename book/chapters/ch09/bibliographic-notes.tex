\section{Notes and Lineage}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch9}

\paragraph{Lineage.}
The n-ary channel is Shannon's discrete channel with its transition matrix
\parencite{shannon-1948}. Capacity as maximal mutual information, the closed
forms for symmetric and weakly symmetric channels, and the Blahut--Arimoto
iteration are standard \parencite[Chs.~7 and~10]{cover-thomas}, and the Kronecker
law for queries on distinct inputs is the product (memoryless) channel. Multi-class
confusion matrices and the assignment matrices of quantum readout mitigation
\parencite{bravyi-et-al-2021,nation-et-al-2021} are instances. On the
data-structure side, the map-valued counterpart of approximate membership is
\emph{retrieval}\index{retrieval}: a static function that returns the stored value
on keys and an arbitrary value elsewhere, as in the Bloomier
filter\index{Bloomier filter} \parencite{chazelle-et-al-2004} and the succinct
retrieval structures of \textcite{dietzfelbinger-pagh-2008}, who also reduce
approximate membership to retrieval.

\paragraph{What this chapter adds.}
The type-general reading of the noisy bit as $\Bernoulli{T}$, and the separation
of matrix row count from model order, follow the approximate-maps manuscript
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-maps}. The chapter's contribution is organizational:
classifiers, counters, sketches, and readout share one channel vocabulary. It also
corrects that manuscript. Serial composition through a stored stage preserves
per-query marginals but not Axiom~1 (\Cref{rem:channel-composition}), so
composition closure of approximate maps holds for marginal rates, and for the
joint law only when distinct inputs reach distinct intermediate values or the
later stage is re-randomized per query.

\paragraph{Left out.}
Retrieval constructions and their space bounds (the membership side returns in
chapters~14 and~15), coding theorems beyond capacity, and the estimation of $Q$
from data. The source manuscript and \Cref{ch:operator-representation} call this
object a \emph{Bernoulli map}\index{Bernoulli map}; the same name denotes the
doubling map $x \mapsto 2x \bmod 1$ of dynamical systems, so the term is best
paired with ``approximate'' when searching the literature.
