\item \exstar
\label{ex:ch9-1}
\textbf{Write a 3x3 channel matrix.}
For a 3-class classifier (cats, dogs, birds) with 90\% per-class
accuracy and the off-diagonal $10\%$ split uniformly across the
two competing labels, write the $3 \times 3$ row-stochastic
matrix $Q$ in the sense of \Cref{def:n-ary-channel}. Label rows
by input block, columns by output value, and verify each row
sums to one.

\item \exstar
\label{ex:ch9-2}
\textbf{Parameter count.}
For a $K \times L$ channel matrix with $K = 5$ and $L = 3$,
count the free parameters using \Cref{cor:n-ary-parameter-count}.
Contrast with the $K \cdot L = 15$ entry count, and confirm the
$K = L = 2$ specialization recovers the $(\fprate, \fnrate)$
pair of \Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}.

\item \exstar
\label{ex:ch9-3}
\textbf{BSC limit of the n-ary capacity formula.}
The strongly-symmetric formula \eqref{eq:strongly-symmetric-capacity}
reads $C = \log_2 L - H(1 - p_e, p_e / (L - 1), \ldots,
p_e / (L - 1))$. Substitute $L = 2$ and $p_e = \fprate$ to
recover $C = 1 - H(\fprate)$, matching \Cref{prop:bsc-capacity}.
Show the row-distribution substitution and confirm the
$\log_2 2 = 1$ leading term.

\item \exstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-5}
\textbf{Joint probability via the n-ary Kronecker theorem.}
Take the $K = 3$ classifier matrix $Q$ of \Cref{ex:ch9-1}, with
blocks $(B_1, B_2, B_3) = (\text{cats}, \text{dogs}, \text{birds})$
and output alphabet $\{1, 2, 3\}$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] For input sequence $(B_1, B_2, B_3, B_1)$ and output
    sequence $(1, 2, 1, 3)$, write the joint probability as the
    product $\prod_{t = 1}^{4} Q_{i_t, j_t}$ produced by
    \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker}.
  \item[(b)] Substitute the entries from \Cref{ex:ch9-1} and
    compute the numerical value.
  \item[(c)] In one sentence, identify which two chapter~4 axioms
    make the factorization a plain product over time steps.
\end{enumerate}

\item \exstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-6}
\textbf{Composition of two n-ary channels.}
Let $Q_1$ be a $K_1 \times L_1$ row-stochastic matrix (input
blocks $\to$ intermediate alphabet) and $Q_2$ be an $L_1 \times
L_2$ row-stochastic matrix (intermediate $\to$ output).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Show that the composed channel is the $K_1 \times
    L_2$ matrix product $Q = Q_1 Q_2$, with $Q_{i, k} = \sum_{j}
    (Q_1)_{i, j} (Q_2)_{j, k}$ giving the probability that input
    block $i$ produces output $k$ after both stages.
  \item[(b)] Verify $Q$ is row-stochastic: show $\sum_{k}
    Q_{i, k} = 1$ for every $i$ using row-stochasticity of $Q_1$
    and $Q_2$ separately.
  \item[(c)] In one sentence, state why this rule makes a
    multi-stage approximate pipeline (e.g., coarse classifier
    feeding fine classifier) itself an n-ary channel in the
    sense of \Cref{def:n-ary-channel}.
\end{enumerate}

\item \exstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-7}
\textbf{Sketch as an n-ary channel.}
The CountMin sketch (previewed in \Cref{sec:bridge-to-sketches}
and developed in \Cref{ch:sketches}) estimates the frequency of
each stream item and reports a non-negative integer.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Identify the input partition: two items share a
    block when their true stream frequencies agree. State the
    model order $K$ in terms of the multiset of true frequencies.
  \item[(b)] Identify the output cardinality: the estimator
    returns a non-negative integer, so $L = \infty$.
  \item[(c)] Describe qualitatively what the rows of the channel
    matrix look like: the row indexed by true frequency $n$ is
    supported on $\{n, n + 1, n + 2, \ldots\}$ (CountMin
    over-counts but never under-counts). In one sentence, relate
    this one-sided support to the FNR-zero shape from
    \Cref{thm:entropy-floor}.
\end{enumerate}

\item \exstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-8}
\textbf{Asymmetric 3-class with a rare class.}
Take the channel matrix $Q$ of \Cref{ex:ch9-1} (90\% accuracy,
uniform off-diagonal) on a workload with one rare class: $P_X(B_3)
= 0.01$, $P_X(B_1) = P_X(B_2) = 0.495$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Compute the PPV of \Cref{def:ppv} for the rare class:
    the probability that the true label is $B_3$ given the
    classifier reports $B_3$. Use Bayes' rule with the workload
    prior and the column of $Q$ indexed by output $3$.
  \item[(b)] Compare to the per-class accuracy $0.90$. The PPV
    falls far below the diagonal: false positives from the two
    common classes swamp true positives from the rare class.
  \item[(c)] In one sentence, connect this to
    \Cref{sec:derived-measures}: per-row accuracies of $Q$ are
    workload-independent, PPV depends on $Q$ and the workload
    prior jointly, and Bayes' rule converts between the two.
\end{enumerate}

\item \exstarstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-9}
\textbf{Transfer of Axiom 2 from binary to n-ary.}
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom 2 of \Cref{sec:axiom-2})
states that the per-block rates $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ of a
Boolean Bernoulli predicate are conditionally independent given
the objective function.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Restate the axiom in the n-ary setting: the $K$ row
    distributions $Q_{1, \cdot}, \ldots, Q_{K, \cdot}$ of $Q$
    are mutually conditionally independent given the objective
    function. Show that the Boolean form is the $K = 2$ instance,
    with the two rows parametrized by $(\fnrate)$ and
    $(\fprate)$ respectively.
  \item[(b)] Argue informally that the chapter~4 proof transfers
    verbatim: every step appeals to independence at the per-block
    level and not to the binary nature of the alphabet, so the
    same argument carries when $2$ is replaced by $K$ throughout.
  \item[(c)] In one sentence, identify which fact of the n-ary
    Kronecker theorem (\Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker}) depends on
    this lifted Axiom 2 rather than on Axiom 1 alone.
\end{enumerate}

\item \exstarstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-10}
\textbf{Capacity bounded by output entropy.}
For any n-ary channel $(X, Y, Q)$ in the sense of
\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}, show that $C \le \log_2 L$ where $L =
|Y|$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Start from $C = \max_{P_X} [H(Y) - H(Y \mid X)]$.
    Use $H(Y \mid X) \ge 0$ to obtain $C \le \max_{P_X} H(Y)$.
  \item[(b)] Apply the maximum-entropy bound on a finite alphabet:
    $H(Y) \le \log_2 L$, with equality at uniform $P_Y(j) = 1/L$.
    Combine with (a).
  \item[(c)] Characterize when equality $C = \log_2 L$ is
    achieved: simultaneously, $H(Y \mid X) = 0$ (the channel is
    deterministic given input) and some input distribution makes
    $P_Y$ uniform. Identify this regime: the channel is a
    noise-free function from input blocks to output values, with
    at least one input block per output value.
\end{enumerate}

\item \exstarstarstar
\label{ex:ch9-11}
\textbf{$T$-valued Bloom-like construction.}
The standard Bloom filter of \Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch} stores
bits at hashed positions. A $T$-valued generalization stores
small numerals from $T = \{1, \ldots, L\}$ at hashed positions
and reports the modal numeral at the $k$ cells hashed by the
query key.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] Sketch the construction: $m$ cells holding values
    in $T$, $k$ independent hash functions, and a majority-vote
    query. At insertion, $(x, v)$ writes $v$ to the $k$ cells
    hashed by $x$ (collisions resolved by overwrite or per-cell
    rule).
  \item[(b)] Cast the result as a $K \times L$ channel matrix in
    the sense of \Cref{def:n-ary-channel}: input blocks indexed
    by true numeral $v$, output values by reported numerals, and
    $Q_{v, w}$ the probability the query reports $w$ given true
    $v$.
  \item[(c)] Derive $Q_{v, v}$ and the off-diagonal $Q_{v, w}$
    for $w \ne v$ in terms of the load factor $n / m$ and
    alphabet size $L$. In one sentence, identify the FNR-zero
    analog (diagonal is $1$ when no collisions occur at the $k$
    hashed positions) and connect to the FNR-zero Bloom filter
    of \Cref{sec:bloom-from-scratch} as the $L = 2$ specialization.
\end{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}
