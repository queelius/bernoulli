\section{Bridge to Sketches}
\label{sec:bridge-to-sketches}

A \emph{sketch}\index{sketch} maps a stream or multiset to a compact summary,
often sublinear in the input size, from which a query routine reconstructs an
answer with error bounded by an analytic expression in the summary size: the
user trades a known accuracy for a known space
budget\index{space--accuracy tradeoff}. What distinguishes a
sketch from lossy compression is that bounded, calculable error. The three
canonical sketches, CountMin for frequency, HyperLogLog for cardinality, and
MinHash for similarity, can each be read as a $\Bernoulli{T}$ value in the sense
of this chapter.

The reading summarizes each sketch's per-query error law as a channel in the
sense of \Cref{def:n-ary-channel}, one row per true value. CountMin is a counter
($T = \mathbb{N}$) whose one-sided overestimation puts all of $Q$'s mass at or
above the diagonal; HyperLogLog is a counter whose mass concentrates in a
multiplicative band around the diagonal (relative rather than one-sided error);
MinHash is a similarity estimator ($T = [0,1]$) whose $Q$ is a binomial kernel
with output concentrated in a band of width $O(1/\sqrt{k})$ at signature length
$k$. Two limits of the reading should be stated before it is used. First, it is
a vocabulary, not a new analysis: the constructions and their bounds are those
of their authors, cited in \Cref{ch:sketches-estimators}. Second, the rows are
per-query marginals. Answers to several queries against one stored sketch share
its hash functions and counters, so Axiom~1 (\Cref{ax:element-indep}) does not
hold across them and the Kronecker factorization of \Cref{thm:n-ary-kronecker}
does not describe their joint law; the merge rules are sketch-specific monoid
operations, not channel compositions. \Cref{ch:sketches-estimators} builds the
three from scratch, the first non-Boolean instances of $\Bernoulli{T}$ the book
treats in detail.
