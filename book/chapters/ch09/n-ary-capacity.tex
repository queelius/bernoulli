\section{Capacity for n-ary Channels}
\label{sec:n-ary-capacity}

\Cref{sec:n-ary-kronecker} settled the structural question: an n-ary
channel with $K \times L$ matrix $Q$ has the same Kronecker
factorization across queries that the Boolean case enjoyed in
chapter~4. This section settles the information-theoretic question:
how many bits per use does the channel convey? The formula of
chapter~8 §8.2, $C = 1 - H(\fprate)$, is the $K = L = 2$ special case
of a general definition that lifts without surprise to arbitrary $K$
and $L$. The lift is mostly definitional; what changes is that the
optimization underlying the definition no longer has a single closed
form, and a class of structurally symmetric channels has to be
identified to recover one. The two sub-families that do admit closed
forms are stated, the general case is bridged to the numerical
literature, and the worked example computes capacity for a four-state
strongly symmetric channel.

\subsection*{Recall: capacity for the BSC}

Chapter~8 §8.1 defined channel capacity as the maximum mutual
information achievable by choosing the input distribution freely:
\[
  C \;=\; \max_{P_X} I(X; Y),
\]
the expression \eqref{eq:capacity-def} of \Cref{sec:entropy-refresher},
with $P_X$ ranging over distributions on the input alphabet and the
conditional $\Pr[Y \mid X]$ fixed by the channel matrix. For the
binary symmetric channel of chapter~2 \S2.1, the closed form
\eqref{eq:bsc-capacity} is
\[
  C \;=\; 1 - H(\fprate)
  \quad \text{bits per channel use,}
\]
attained at the uniform input $P_X(\top) = P_X(\bot) = 1/2$
(\Cref{prop:bsc-capacity}). The argument leading to that form used two
facts about the BSC. First, the conditional entropy $H(Y \mid X)$
equals $H(\fprate)$ for every input, because the two rows of $Q$ are
permutations of each other; averaging over any input distribution
gives the same $H(\fprate)$. Second, the output marginal entropy $H(Y)$
is maximized when the output is uniform on $\{\top, \bot\}$, which by
column-symmetry of $Q$ happens precisely at uniform input. Both facts
generalize to a class of n-ary channels with the same symmetry
structure; both fail for channels without it, and the general
optimization has to fall back on numerical methods. The two strands
organize the rest of the section.

\subsection*{The general n-ary case}

For an n-ary channel $(X, Y, Q)$ in the sense of
\Cref{def:n-ary-channel}, the capacity definition copies over
verbatim. An input distribution $P_X$ is now a probability vector on
the $K$ blocks $B_1, \ldots, B_K$, with $P_X(B_i) \ge 0$ summing to
one. The output distribution $P_Y$ it induces is
\[
  P_Y(j) \;=\; \sum_{i = 1}^{K} P_X(B_i) \, Q_{ij},
  \qquad j = 1, \ldots, L,
\]
a $K$-fold average of the rows of $Q$ weighted by the input
distribution. The mutual information \eqref{eq:mutual-info} between
input and output is
\[
  I(X; Y) \;=\; H(Y) - H(Y \mid X),
\]
where $H(Y \mid X) = \sum_{i = 1}^{K} P_X(B_i) \, H(Q_{i\,\cdot})$ is
the input-averaged row entropy of $Q$ and $H(Y)$ is the entropy of the
induced output distribution $P_Y$. The capacity is the maximum over
input distributions on the $K$ blocks:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:n-ary-capacity-def}
  C \;=\; \max_{P_X} \;\bigl[ H(Y) - H(Y \mid X) \bigr],
\end{equation}
the same object as \eqref{eq:capacity-def}, now ranging over the
$(K - 1)$-dimensional simplex of distributions on input blocks rather
than the one-dimensional interval $[0, 1]$ that parametrized the BSC
input. The dimension of the optimization grew, but the object did
not.

The general optimization has no closed form. The function $I(X; Y)$
viewed as a function of $P_X$ on the input simplex is concave (a
standard fact from \cite{cover-thomas}), so the maximum exists and is
unique up to ties on the boundary, but the maximizing $P_X$ has to
satisfy a system of stationarity conditions whose solution is
implicit. The standard numerical recipe is the \emph{Blahut-Arimoto
algorithm}, a fixed-point iteration over alternating updates of $P_X$
and a posterior $\Pr[X \mid Y]$. Each iteration tightens a lower
bound on $I(X; Y)$, the iterates converge to the capacity-achieving
distribution under mild conditions, and the convergence rate is
geometric. Cover and Thomas~\cite{cover-thomas} chapter~10 develops
the algorithm in detail and proves its convergence; the chapter~9
notebook implements it for the worked examples that close this section
and for the exercises. The takeaway for the reader is that the general
n-ary capacity is computable to any precision, just not via a single
analytic expression. The two sub-families below recover analytic
expressions by imposing enough structural symmetry on $Q$ to collapse
the maximization to a single calculation.

\subsection*{Symmetric and strongly symmetric channels}

The BSC's two key properties were that its rows are permutations of
each other (which made $H(Y \mid X)$ input-independent) and that its
columns are permutations of each other (which made $P_Y$ uniform at
uniform input). Lifting each property to the n-ary case identifies a
sub-family of channels for which capacity has a closed form, and
combining both properties identifies a still smaller sub-family for
which the closed form is especially clean.

\paragraph{Symmetric n-ary channel.}
An n-ary channel is \emph{symmetric} if every row of $Q$ is a
permutation of every other row: there is a single multiset of
probabilities $\mathbf{r} = (r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_L)$ summing to one,
and each row of $Q$ is some rearrangement of $\mathbf{r}$. Under this
hypothesis, every row of $Q$ has the same entropy
$H(\mathbf{r}) = -\sum_{j} r_j \log_2 r_j$, so the conditional entropy
$H(Y \mid X) = \sum_i P_X(B_i) \, H(Q_{i\,\cdot}) = H(\mathbf{r})$ is
input-independent. The capacity definition then reduces to
\[
  C \;=\; \max_{P_X} H(Y) \;-\; H(\mathbf{r}),
\]
and the remaining maximization is over the output marginal entropy
alone. The output alphabet has $L$ values, so $H(Y) \le \log_2 L$
with equality at the uniform output distribution $P_Y(j) = 1/L$. The
symmetric hypothesis does not by itself guarantee that uniform input
produces uniform output: that requires the column structure of the
next sub-family. The bound $C \le \log_2 L - H(\mathbf{r})$ is
nevertheless tight whenever an input distribution making $P_Y$ uniform
exists, which is generic for symmetric channels (every row permutation
of $\mathbf{r}$ contributes the same total mass to every output, and
the uniform input distribution suffices). The closed form for a
symmetric n-ary channel is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:symmetric-capacity}
  C \;=\; \log_2 L \;-\; H(\mathbf{r}),
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{r}$ any row of $Q$ (the choice does not matter, since
all rows have the same entropy). The result is the n-ary analogue of
$C = 1 - H(\fprate)$: the term $\log_2 L$ is the maximum possible
output entropy of an $L$-valued channel, the term $H(\mathbf{r})$ is
the irreducible per-use uncertainty from the row distribution, and the
difference is the per-use information bound.

\paragraph{Strongly symmetric n-ary channel.}
A symmetric channel is \emph{strongly symmetric} if, in addition, the
columns of $Q$ are also permutations of one another. The two
hypotheses together force a very rigid structure on $Q$. A standard
parametrization writes a single per-input error probability $p_e \in
[0, 1)$ and spreads the off-diagonal mass uniformly across the other
$L - 1$ outputs:
\[
  Q_{ij}
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    1 - p_e & j = i, \\[2pt]
    \dfrac{p_e}{L - 1} & j \ne i.
  \end{cases}
\]
The matrix is square with $K = L$, the diagonal is the common
correct-transmission probability $1 - p_e$, and every off-diagonal
entry is the same $p_e / (L - 1)$. The BSC of chapter~2 is the
$L = 2$ instance with $p_e = \fprate$. Each row of $Q$ has the form
$\mathbf{r} = (1 - p_e, \,p_e/(L-1), \ldots, \,p_e/(L-1))$, with one
``correct'' coordinate and $L - 1$ uniform ``error'' coordinates, and
the row entropy is
\[
  H(\mathbf{r})
  \;=\; H\!\left(1 - p_e,\; \frac{p_e}{L - 1},\; \ldots,\;
                \frac{p_e}{L - 1}\right).
\]
Substituting into \eqref{eq:symmetric-capacity} gives the closed form
that the rest of the section uses.

\begin{proposition}[Strongly symmetric n-ary capacity]
\label{prop:n-ary-capacity}
The capacity of a strongly symmetric n-ary channel with output
alphabet size $L$ and total error probability $p_e \in [0, 1)$ per
input is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:strongly-symmetric-capacity}
  C \;=\; \log_2 L \;-\; H\!\left(1 - p_e,\;
              \tfrac{p_e}{L - 1},\;\ldots,\; \tfrac{p_e}{L - 1}\right)
  \quad \text{bits per channel use,}
\end{equation}
attained by the uniform input distribution $P_X(B_i) = 1/L$ for every
input block.
\end{proposition}

At $L = 2$ and $p_e = \fprate$, the row distribution
$\mathbf{r} = (1 - \fprate, \fprate)$ has entropy $H(\fprate)$,
giving $C = \log_2 2 - H(\fprate) = 1 - H(\fprate)$. The
strongly-symmetric formula recovers \eqref{eq:bsc-capacity} exactly,
confirming that the n-ary capacity result subsumes the BSC capacity
of chapter~8 §8.2 as the $L = 2$ specialization. The endpoints carry
across too: $p_e = 0$ gives $H(\mathbf{r}) = 0$ and $C = \log_2 L$
(noiseless $L$-ary channel transmits $\log_2 L$ bits per use); $p_e =
(L - 1)/L$ gives the uniform row $\mathbf{r} = (1/L, \ldots, 1/L)$,
$H(\mathbf{r}) = \log_2 L$, and $C = 0$ (maximally noisy $L$-ary
channel transmits nothing). The strongly-symmetric family is the
cleanest generalization of the BSC: a single error parameter
parametrizes the whole family, and the closed form mirrors the
chapter~8 result with $\log_2 L$ replacing the ceiling of one bit.

\subsection*{Worked example: a 4-state strongly symmetric channel}

Take $L = 4$ and $p_e = 0.1$. The channel matrix is
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0.9       & 0.0333 & 0.0333 & 0.0333 \\
    0.0333    & 0.9    & 0.0333 & 0.0333 \\
    0.0333    & 0.0333 & 0.9    & 0.0333 \\
    0.0333    & 0.0333 & 0.0333 & 0.9
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
writing the off-diagonal entry $p_e / (L - 1) = 0.1 / 3 \approx
0.0333$ to four significant figures. Each row sums to $0.9 + 3 \cdot
0.0333 \approx 1$; the diagonal is the common $90\%$ correct
transmission probability; every off-diagonal entry is the same
$3.33\%$. The channel is strongly symmetric by construction (rows and
columns are both permutations of each other), so
\Cref{prop:n-ary-capacity} applies.

The output-alphabet ceiling is
\[
  \log_2 L \;=\; \log_2 4 \;=\; 2 \text{ bits per use.}
\]
The row entropy is
\[
  H(\mathbf{r})
  \;=\; -0.9 \log_2 0.9
       \;-\; 3 \cdot \tfrac{0.1}{3} \log_2 \tfrac{0.1}{3}.
\]
The two terms compute as follows. The diagonal contribution is
\[
  -0.9 \log_2 0.9
  \;\approx\; -0.9 \cdot (-0.152)
  \;\approx\; 0.137 \text{ bits.}
\]
The off-diagonal contribution is
\[
  -3 \cdot 0.0333 \cdot \log_2 0.0333
  \;\approx\; -3 \cdot 0.0333 \cdot (-4.908)
  \;\approx\; 0.490 \text{ bits.}
\]
Adding gives
\[
  H(\mathbf{r})
  \;\approx\; 0.137 + 0.490
  \;=\; 0.627 \text{ bits.}
\]
The capacity is the difference:
\[
  C
  \;=\; \log_2 4 - H(\mathbf{r})
  \;\approx\; 2 - 0.627
  \;\approx\; 1.373 \text{ bits per channel use.}
\]
A four-symbol channel with a $90\%$ per-symbol correct-transmission
probability conveys about $1.37$ bits per use, well below the
noiseless ceiling of $2$ bits and well above the BSC's $0.531$ bits
per use at the same per-symbol error rate of $0.1$. The gap to the
ceiling shrinks as $L$ grows for a fixed $p_e$, because the off-diagonal
mass spreads more thinly across more outputs and the row entropy
grows sublinearly in $L$ at fixed $p_e$. The chapter~9 notebook
verifies the numerical value $C \approx 1.373$ against a
Blahut-Arimoto run on the same $Q$, confirming both the
strongly-symmetric closed form and the general numerical recipe.

\subsection*{Where capacity goes next}

The capacity expression \eqref{eq:n-ary-capacity-def} closes the
information-theoretic loop for chapter~9. Every n-ary channel of the
form fixed by \Cref{def:n-ary-channel} has a well-defined per-use
information bound; the strongly symmetric sub-family has the closed
form \eqref{eq:strongly-symmetric-capacity} that
\Cref{prop:n-ary-capacity} records; and the general case yields to
Blahut-Arimoto when a closed form is not available.
\Cref{sec:bridge-to-sketches} previews how concrete sketches
instantiate the chapter~9 framework: a CountMin sketch is an n-ary
channel with $L = \infty$ and a one-sided error profile; a
HyperLogLog is an n-ary channel with $L = \infty$ and a relative-error
profile; a MinHash is an n-ary channel with $L = [0, 1]$ output. Each
sketch carries its own $Q$, its own capacity, and its own
specialization of the chapter~9 machinery. Chapter~10 instantiates the
framework in detail, building three sketches from scratch with
explicit error bounds and composition rules.
