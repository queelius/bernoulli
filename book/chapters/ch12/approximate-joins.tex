\section{Approximate Joins in Databases}
\label{sec:approximate-joins}

\Cref{sec:relation-composition} treated the join algebraically. This section reads
the database \emph{similarity join} in the same vocabulary: the standard
MinHash-LSH analysis supplies the rates of a sketch-based approximate relation,
and the chapter's main caution, that tests reading the same randomness are not
independent, applies inside the pipeline itself.

\subsection*{The similarity join}

For relations $R_1, R_2$ whose rows are token sets, the similarity join\index{similarity join} at
threshold $\theta$ is $R_1 \bowtie_\theta R_2 = \{(r_1, r_2) : J(r_1, r_2) \ge
\theta\}$, the cross-relation pairs whose Jaccard overlap is at least $\theta$.
Record linkage, near-duplicate detection, and entity resolution reduce to it. The
naive evaluation computes $|R_1| \cdot |R_2|$ Jaccard values; the sketch evaluation
is an approximate relation $\tilde S_\theta(r_1, r_2) = [\hat J(r_1, r_2) \ge
\theta]$.

The MinHash\index{MinHash} sketch (\Cref{def:minhash}) supplies $\hat J$: the
fraction of agreeing signature coordinates is an unbiased estimate of $J$
(\Cref{prop:minhash-collision}). With length-$k$ signatures, a false positive needs
$\hat J$ to overshoot a sub-threshold truth and a false negative needs it to
undershoot a super-threshold one, and the Hoeffding bound\index{Hoeffding's inequality} of
\Cref{prop:minhash-error} gives $\Prob{|\hat J - J| \ge t} \le 2 \exp(-2kt^2)$ with
$t$ the gap to $\theta$, so both rates fall exponentially in $k$ away from the
threshold. These are pointwise rates for a fixed pair; pairs sharing a row share
that row's signature and are not independent, exactly as join outputs sharing an
operand tuple are not (\Cref{thm:join-correlation}).

\subsection*{LSH bands}

The remaining quadratic factor is candidate enumeration, which
locality-sensitive hashing\index{locality-sensitive hashing}
(\Cref{sec:minhash-lsh}) prunes. Partition the signature into $b$ bands of $r$
rows ($k = br$), hash each band, and call $(r_1, r_2)$ a candidate if any band
collides. A band collides with probability $J^r$, so a pair survives with
probability $1 - (1 - J^r)^b$, an S-curve with transition near $J^* \approx
(1/b)^{1/r}$. The pipeline reports a pair when it is a candidate \emph{and} passes
the threshold test. Both tests read the same signature, so they are dependent and
the product rules of \Cref{thm:join-rates} and chapter~3 do not apply. Sound
statements remain: the pipeline's false-positive rate is at most that of either
stage, and its false-negative rate is at most the sum of the two stages'
false-negative rates, by the union bound\index{union bound}, whatever the dependence.

\begin{remark}[The Bloom equijoin]
\label{rem:bloom-equijoin}
For an equijoin on a shared key, build a Bloom filter over $R_2$'s keys, scan
$R_1$, and look up in $R_2$ only those rows whose key hits the filter: the Bloom
semijoin of distributed query processing \parencite{mullin-1990}\index{semijoin}.
The candidate relation ``$r_1$'s key hits the filter'' is an FNR-zero approximate
relation (\Cref{def:bloom-filter}); exact verification of each candidate removes
the residual false positives, so the final join is exact and the filter serves
only as a pruner. This is the certain-reject structure of
\Cref{ch:approximate-sets} deployed as a join accelerator.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Worked example}

Take $|R_1| = |R_2| = 1000$ ($10^6$ candidate pairs), $\theta = 0.5$, and $k = 200$
as $b = 20$ bands of $r = 10$. The threshold test is sharp: a pair at $J = 0.3$ is
emitted with probability at most $\exp(-2 \cdot 200 \cdot 0.2^2) = e^{-16} \approx
1.1 \times 10^{-7}$, a pair at $J = 0.7$ is missed with the same bound, and at $J =
\theta$ the test is a coin flip.

The banding dominates, and its tuning needs care. The transition sits at $J^* =
(1/20)^{1/10} \approx 0.74$, above the threshold, an aggressive precision-favoring
choice: the survival probability $1 - (1 - J^{10})^{20}$ is about $0.436$ at $J =
0.7$, $0.0194$ at $J = 0.5$, and $1.18 \times 10^{-4}$ at $J = 0.3$. Suppose $0.1\%$
of pairs have $J \ge 0.5$, $1\%$ lie in $[0.3, 0.5)$, and the rest lie below $0.3$,
and take $J = 0.7$, $0.5$, and $0.3$ as representative of the three bands (the last
two are upper ends, so their counts are upper estimates). The candidate set is then
about
\[
  1000 \cdot 0.436 + 10000 \cdot 0.0194 + 989000 \cdot 1.18 \times 10^{-4}
  \approx 436 + 194 + 117 \approx 747
\]
pairs, three orders of magnitude below the all-pairs scan, verified with about
$1.5 \times 10^5$ signature comparisons against $2 \times 10^8$ for the naive scan.
The cost is recall: fewer than half of the strongly similar pairs and about $2\%$ of
threshold pairs survive. Centering $J^*$ on $\theta$ (the standard tuning
$(1/b)^{1/r} = \theta$, here with more bands) admits more candidates in exchange for
higher recall. This precision and recall trade through $(b, r)$ is the standard
operating regime of MinHash-LSH similarity joins. \Cref{sec:ch12-bridge} closes the
chapter.
