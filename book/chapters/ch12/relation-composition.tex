\section{Composition of Approximate Relations}
\label{sec:relation-composition}

The universe $T \times U$ carries structure beyond ``set of pairs'': a relation
admits projection, selection, and join. Each operation lifts to approximate
relations, and the propagation of $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ follows from chapter~3's
AND/OR analysis (\Cref{sec:and-under-noise}) and chapter~6's intersection
theorem (\Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr}) with pairs in place of elements.

\subsection*{Natural join}

The natural join of $R_1 \subseteq T \times U$ and $R_2 \subseteq U \times V$ on
the shared attribute is $R_1 \bowtie R_2 = \{(t, u, v) : (t, u) \in R_1,\, (u, v)
\in R_2\}$, with membership predicate $\SetIndicator{R_1}(t, u) \land
\SetIndicator{R_2}(u, v)$. The approximate join reads as the AND of two noisy
Booleans, $(\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2)(t, u, v) = \tilde R_1(t, u) \land
\tilde R_2(u, v)$, the object whose rates chapter~3 derived, with one added
hypothesis: cross-relation independence, that the per-query randomness of
$\tilde R_1$ at $(t, u)$ is independent of that of $\tilde R_2$ at $(u, v)$. This
is Axiom~1 applied across the two structures, holding when they are built and
queried independently.

\begin{theorem}[Join error rates]
\label{thm:join-rates}
Let $\tilde R_1, \tilde R_2$ be FNR-zero approximate relations with
false-positive rates $\fprate_1, \fprate_2$, under cross-relation independence.
Then $\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2$ has $\fnrate_{\bowtie} = 0$ and, over the
three regions of out-of-join triples (the pair matched in $R_1$ but not $R_2$,
in $R_2$ but not $R_1$, in neither) with weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$,
\[
  \fprate_{\bowtie} \;=\; w_1 \fprate_2 + w_2 \fprate_1 + w_3 \fprate_1 \fprate_2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A true join triple has both component pairs in their relations, each reported
$\top$ with probability $1 - \fnrate_i = 1$, so $\fnrate_{\bowtie} = 0$. A false
positive needs both components to report $\top$ at an out-of-join triple, and the
per-region probability depends on which components are genuinely present: on the
region where $(t, u) \in R_1$ but $(u, v) \notin R_2$, the first reports $\top$
correctly while the second false-positives, $(1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2 =
\fprate_2$; on the mirror region, $\fprate_1$; on the both-absent region,
$\fprate_1 \fprate_2$. Total probability over the three regions gives the stated
rate. This is \Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr} of chapter~6 on the triple
universe, with the in-join block as $A \cap B$.
\end{proof}

The bare product $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ is only the both-absent term: it is exact
when out-of-join triples are dominated by the region where \emph{neither}
component pair is present, which holds when both relations are sparse in a large
triple universe, the typical database regime. Otherwise the linear terms $w_1
\fprate_2 + w_2 \fprate_1$ dominate, because a triple with one component genuinely
present false-positives at the surviving single rate, not at the product. If
either relation has $\fnrate_i > 0$, the join inherits a positive
$\fnrate_{\bowtie}$ by the full intersection formula; the FNR-zero case is the
one-sided special case.

\subsection*{Projection and selection}

The projection $\pi_T(R) = \{t : \exists u,\, (t, u) \in R\}$ collapses the $U$
axis by an existential quantifier, so $t \in \pi_T(\tilde R)$ iff some $(t, u)$
reports $\top$, the OR of $|U|$ membership tests. An FNR-zero input gives an
FNR-zero projection (a true $t$ has a witness pair reporting $\top$), but the FPR
\emph{amplifies}: a non-projecting $t$ has every pair $(t, u)$ outside the
relation, each false-positive at rate $\fprate$ independently, so $\fprate_\pi =
1 - (1 - \fprate)^{|U|} \to 1$ as $|U|$ grows. Existential quantification over a
large co-domain inflates any false-positive bias, the one structural penalty the
lift to relations adds, and the motivation for the sketch-based projection of
\Cref{sec:approximate-joins}.

The selection $\sigma_P(R) = \{(t, u) \in R : P(t, u) = \top\}$ passes the rates
through unchanged when $P$ is exact (a deterministic restriction of the support).
When $P$ is itself approximate with rate $\fprate_P$, the selection predicate is
the AND $\tilde R(t, u) \land \tilde P(t, u)$, evaluated at the same pair, so its
FPR is the three-region rate of \Cref{thm:join-rates} again, $\approx \fprate
\fprate_P$ only in the both-absent regime.

\subsection*{Worked example}

Let $\tilde F$ be a Bloom-style friend-of relation with $\fnrate_F = 0$,
$\fprate_F = 0.05$, and $\tilde C$ a coworker relation with $\fnrate_C = 0$,
$\fprate_C = 0.02$, built and queried independently. The join $\tilde F \bowtie
\tilde C$ returns triples $(p_1, p_2, p_3)$ where $\tilde F$ reports $p_1$ a
friend of $p_2$ and $\tilde C$ reports $p_2$ a coworker of $p_3$. By
\Cref{thm:join-rates}, $\fnrate_{\bowtie} = 0$ and $\fprate_{\bowtie} = w_1
\fprate_C + w_2 \fprate_F + w_3 \fprate_F \fprate_C$. In a sparse population,
where almost every out-of-join triple has $p_1$ unrelated to $p_2$ \emph{and}
$p_2$ unrelated to $p_3$, the both-absent term dominates and $\fprate_{\bowtie}
\approx \fprate_F \fprate_C = 0.001$, three orders below either operand, the
relational face of the chapter-6 intersection gain. But the gain is conditional:
a triple where $p_1$ truly is a friend of $p_2$ while $p_2$ is not a coworker of
$p_3$ false-positives at $\fprate_C = 0.02$, the linear rate, so where such
half-matched triples are common the join rate sits closer to $\max(\fprate_F,
\fprate_C)$ than to the product. The cost is in the assumptions, not the algebra:
cross-relation independence must hold (shared hash families break the product),
and the relations must be sparse enough for the both-absent region to dominate.

\subsection*{Composing the operators}

The three operators compose in any order, with rate propagation by induction on
the expression tree. A query $\pi_T(\sigma_P(\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2))$
evaluates bottom-up: the join gives \Cref{thm:join-rates}'s rate, the exact
selection passes it through, the projection inflates the false-positive side by
the existential factor and preserves the structural zero. Each operator has a
closed-form rate-update rule, and chaining them gives the rule for the whole
expression, the same closure-under-composition chapter~6 told for sets. The one
addition is the projection inflation: a union is a single Boolean OR per query
with no existential quantifier over a domain of unknown size, while a projection
quantifies over the full second attribute, and \Cref{sec:approximate-joins}
replaces it with a sketch that estimates the quantifier without paying the $|U|$
amplification in full.
