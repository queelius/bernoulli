\section{Composition of Approximate Relations}
\label{sec:relation-composition}

The universe $T \times U$ carries structure beyond ``set of pairs'': a relation
admits projection, selection, and join. Each operation lifts to approximate
relations, and the propagation of $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ follows from chapter~3's
AND/OR analysis (\Cref{sec:and-under-noise}) and chapter~6's intersection
theorem (\Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr}) with pairs in place of elements.

\subsection*{Natural join}

The natural join of $R_1 \subseteq T \times U$ and $R_2 \subseteq U \times V$ on
the shared attribute is $R_1 \bowtie R_2 = \{(t, u, v) : (t, u) \in R_1,\, (u, v)
\in R_2\}$, with membership predicate $\SetIndicator{R_1}(t, u) \land
\SetIndicator{R_2}(u, v)$. The approximate join reads as the AND of two noisy
Booleans, $(\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2)(t, u, v) = \tilde R_1(t, u) \land
\tilde R_2(u, v)$, the object whose rates chapter~3 derived, with one added
hypothesis: cross-relation independence, that the per-query randomness of
$\tilde R_1$ at $(t, u)$ is independent of that of $\tilde R_2$ at $(u, v)$. This
is Axiom~1 applied across the two structures, holding when they are built and
queried independently.

\begin{theorem}[Join error rates]
\label{thm:join-rates}
Let $\tilde R_1, \tilde R_2$ be FNR-zero approximate relations with
false-positive rates $\fprate_1, \fprate_2$, under cross-relation independence.
Then $\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2$ has $\fnrate_{\bowtie} = 0$ and, over the
three regions of out-of-join triples (the pair matched in $R_1$ but not $R_2$,
in $R_2$ but not $R_1$, in neither) with weights $w_1, w_2, w_3$,
\[
  \fprate_{\bowtie} \;=\; w_1 \fprate_2 + w_2 \fprate_1 + w_3 \fprate_1 \fprate_2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A true join triple has both component pairs in their relations, each reported
$\top$ with probability $1 - \fnrate_i = 1$, so $\fnrate_{\bowtie} = 0$. A false
positive needs both components to report $\top$ at an out-of-join triple, and the
per-region probability depends on which components are genuinely present: on the
region where $(t, u) \in R_1$ but $(u, v) \notin R_2$, the first reports $\top$
correctly while the second false-positives, $(1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2 =
\fprate_2$; on the mirror region, $\fprate_1$; on the both-absent region,
$\fprate_1 \fprate_2$. Total probability over the three regions gives the stated
rate. This is \Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr} of chapter~6 on the triple
universe, with the in-join block as $A \cap B$.
\end{proof}

The bare product $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ is only the both-absent term: it is exact
when out-of-join triples are dominated by the region where \emph{neither}
component pair is present, which holds when both relations are sparse in a large
triple universe, the typical database regime. Otherwise the linear terms $w_1
\fprate_2 + w_2 \fprate_1$ dominate, because a triple with one component genuinely
present false-positives at the surviving single rate, not at the product. If
either relation has $\fnrate_i > 0$, the join inherits a positive
$\fnrate_{\bowtie}$ by the full intersection formula; the FNR-zero case is the
one-sided special case.

\subsection*{A join breaks element-wise independence}

\Cref{thm:join-rates} gives the rate of \emph{one} output triple. It is silent
on a sharper question: are the output triples independent of one another? They
are not, and the failure is the one place the lift to relations breaks Axiom~1
(\Cref{ax:element-indep}) rather than merely re-deriving its consequences.

\begin{theorem}[A join does not preserve element-wise independence]
\label{thm:join-correlation}
The approximate join $\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2$ is a Bernoulli relation only
\emph{marginally}. Each output triple carries the rates of
\Cref{thm:join-rates}, but the joint law over output triples does not factor:
two output triples that share a component pair are positively correlated, so
element-wise independence (\Cref{ax:element-indep}) fails for the join.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take two output triples $(t, u, v_1)$ and $(t, u, v_2)$ with $v_1 \neq v_2$ that
share the same $R_1$-pair $(t, u)$. Both acceptance events contain the
\emph{same} trial $A = \{\tilde R_1(t, u) = \top\}$ as a conjunct:
\[
  \{(t, u, v_i) \in \tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2\}
  = A \cap B_i,
  \qquad
  B_i = \{\tilde R_2(u, v_i) = \top\},
\]
where $A$, $B_1$, $B_2$ are independent under cross-relation independence. Write
$a = \Prob{A}$. The two triples are accepted together with probability
\[
  \Prob{A \cap B_1 \cap B_2} = a\, \Prob{B_1} \Prob{B_2},
\]
whereas the product of their marginals is
$(a\Prob{B_1})(a\Prob{B_2}) = a^2 \Prob{B_1}\Prob{B_2}$. For $0 < a < 1$ the two
differ by the factor $1/a > 1$, so the joint acceptance exceeds the
independent-model value and the triples are positively correlated. The shared
trial $\tilde R_1(t, u)$ is reused by every output triple built from $(t, u)$,
welding those triples together; the same holds for triples sharing an
$R_2$-pair.
\end{proof}

A concrete reading. Suppose the shared $R_1$-pair fires with probability $a =
0.9$ and each of $\tilde R_2(u, v_1)$, $\tilde R_2(u, v_2)$ fires with
probability $0.9$. The two triples are accepted together with probability $0.9
\cdot 0.9 \cdot 0.9 = 0.729$, whereas an independence assumption would predict
$0.81 \cdot 0.81 = 0.6561$; the truth exceeds it by the factor $1/0.9 \approx
1.11$. A query planner that estimated the chance both rows survive as a product
of per-row probabilities would understate it.

The marginal rates of \Cref{thm:join-rates} remain exactly right for any
\emph{single} triple, and an expected false-positive count over a join still adds
tuple by tuple, since linearity of expectation needs no independence. It is the
higher moments, and any all-correct probability computed as a product over output
tuples, that feel the correlation. The next two operators do preserve
independence, which is exactly what makes the join the exception.

\subsection*{Projection and selection}

The projection $\pi_T(R) = \{t : \exists u,\, (t, u) \in R\}$ collapses the $U$
axis by an existential quantifier, so $t \in \pi_T(\tilde R)$ iff some pair in
the \emph{fiber} of $t$, its pre-image $\{(t, u) : u \in U\}$, reports $\top$.
Projection is a logical OR over the fiber, and its \emph{fan-in} is the fiber
size $k(t)$. An FNR-zero input gives an FNR-zero projection (a true $t$ has a
witness pair reporting $\top$), but the FPR \emph{amplifies}: a non-projecting
$t$ has all $k(t)$ fiber pairs outside the relation, each false-positive at rate
$\fprate$ independently, so
\[
  \fprate_\pi(t) = 1 - (1 - \fprate)^{k(t)} \;\ge\; \fprate,
\]
climbing toward $1$ with the fan-in. The true-positive side is boosted in step: a
non-member fiber pair can itself false-positive and so rescue the projection of a
genuine $t$, giving $\tprate_\pi(t) = 1 - (1 - \tprate)^{j}(1 -
\fprate)^{k(t) - j}$ when $j \ge 1$ of the fiber pairs are genuine. For a binary
relation projected onto $T$ the fiber is the whole $U$-axis, so $k(t) = |U|$ is
uniform and the rate is $1 - (1 - \fprate)^{|U|}$; in a relation of higher arity
the fan-in varies from one projected tuple to the next.

That variation is structural. Distinct projected elements have \emph{disjoint}
fibers, so they read disjoint sets of trials and stay mutually independent:
projection preserves Axiom~1 (\Cref{ax:element-indep}), unlike the join. But the
per-element rate $1 - (1 - \fprate)^{k(t)}$ depends on the fan-in $k(t)$, which
need not be constant, so $\pi_T(\tilde R)$ is in general a higher-order Bernoulli
relation, one rate block per distinct fan-in value, collapsing to a single
first-order rate only when the fan-in is uniform. Existential quantification over
a large co-domain inflates any false-positive bias, the one structural penalty
the lift to relations adds, and the motivation for the sketch-based projection of
\Cref{sec:approximate-joins}.

The selection $\sigma_P(R) = \{(t, u) \in R : P(t, u) = \top\}$ passes the rates
through unchanged when $P$ is exact (a deterministic restriction of the support).
When $P$ is itself approximate with rate $\fprate_P$, the selection predicate is
the AND $\tilde R(t, u) \land \tilde P(t, u)$, evaluated at the same pair, so its
FPR is the three-region rate of \Cref{thm:join-rates} again, $\approx \fprate
\fprate_P$ only in the both-absent regime.

\subsection*{Worked example}

Let $\tilde F$ be a Bloom-style friend-of relation with $\fnrate_F = 0$,
$\fprate_F = 0.05$, and $\tilde C$ a coworker relation with $\fnrate_C = 0$,
$\fprate_C = 0.02$, built and queried independently. The join $\tilde F \bowtie
\tilde C$ returns triples $(p_1, p_2, p_3)$ where $\tilde F$ reports $p_1$ a
friend of $p_2$ and $\tilde C$ reports $p_2$ a coworker of $p_3$. By
\Cref{thm:join-rates}, $\fnrate_{\bowtie} = 0$ and $\fprate_{\bowtie} = w_1
\fprate_C + w_2 \fprate_F + w_3 \fprate_F \fprate_C$. In a sparse population,
where almost every out-of-join triple has $p_1$ unrelated to $p_2$ \emph{and}
$p_2$ unrelated to $p_3$, the both-absent term dominates and $\fprate_{\bowtie}
\approx \fprate_F \fprate_C = 0.001$, three orders below either operand, the
relational face of the chapter-6 intersection gain. But the gain is conditional:
a triple where $p_1$ truly is a friend of $p_2$ while $p_2$ is not a coworker of
$p_3$ false-positives at $\fprate_C = 0.02$, the linear rate, so where such
half-matched triples are common the join rate sits closer to $\max(\fprate_F,
\fprate_C)$ than to the product. The cost is in the assumptions, not the algebra:
cross-relation independence must hold (shared hash families break the product),
and the relations must be sparse enough for the both-absent region to dominate.

\subsection*{Composing the operators}

The three operators compose in any order, with the \emph{marginal} rate of any
single output tuple propagating by induction on the expression tree. A query
$\pi_T(\sigma_P(\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2))$ evaluates bottom-up: the join
gives \Cref{thm:join-rates}'s rate, the exact selection passes it through, the
projection inflates the false-positive side by the fan-in factor and preserves
the structural zero. Each operator has a closed-form rate-update rule for a
single tuple, and chaining them gives the per-tuple rule for the whole
expression, the same closure-under-composition chapter~6 told for sets.

Two cautions separate the relational case from the set case. The projection
inflation is one: a union is a single Boolean OR per query with no existential
quantifier over a domain of unknown size, while a projection quantifies over the
full second attribute, and \Cref{sec:approximate-joins} replaces it with a sketch
that estimates the quantifier without paying the fan-in amplification in full.
The join correlation is the other and the deeper one. The bottom-up rates above
are marginal, exact for any single output tuple, but \Cref{thm:join-correlation}
showed that a join correlates the tuples it produces, so the probability that the
\emph{whole} result is error-free does not factor as a product of per-tuple
rates. Composing marginal rates is sound; composing them as though the output
tuples were independent is not, once a join sits anywhere in the tree.
