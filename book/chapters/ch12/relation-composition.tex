\section{Composition of Approximate Relations}
\label{sec:relation-composition}

Selection, projection, and join build new relations from old ones. Applied to
approximate operands, each operator induces a derived approximate relation whose
membership test is a Boolean function, its \emph{lineage}\index{lineage
(databases)}, of the operand tests. Every operator raises two questions: what
are the derived marginal rates, and are the derived tuples still independent? The
answers depend on which sources the outputs read
(\Cref{def:source-model}). Unless stated otherwise, the two operands of a binary
operator are built independently of each other (cross-relation independence),
each satisfies the independent-tuple model, and every predicate in an operator
(the selection condition, the join condition on attribute values) is exact: the
only randomness is inherited from the operand tests.

\subsection*{Selection}

Selection\index{selection} $\sigma_P(R) = \{t \in R : P(t)\}$ with an exact
predicate $P$ is the operand read on the smaller universe $\{t : P(t)\}$. It
keeps the rates within each existing truth class and keeps independence under the
independent-tuple model; for a source-sharing input it keeps whatever dependence
the selected tuples already had. Selection preserves the rate partition: it does
not turn a two-rate model into a one-rate model. Viewed over the original
universe, the tuples outside $\{t : P(t)\}$ are deterministic negatives. When $P$
is itself approximate, the selection is the AND of two noisy tests at the same
tuple and falls under the join analysis below.

\subsection*{Projection}

The projection\index{projection} $\pi_Y$ onto an attribute subset $Y$ collapses
the remaining attributes by an existential quantifier. The \emph{fiber}\index{fiber (projection)}
$\phi(s)$ of a projected tuple $s$ is the set of candidate tuples projecting to
it, its size $k(s)$ is the \emph{fan-in}\index{fan-in}, and $j(s) = \Card{\phi(s) \cap R}$
counts its true members. The target universe is the image of the candidate
universe, so every fiber is nonempty. Projection is a logical OR\index{OR gate} over the fiber.

\begin{theorem}[Projection]
\label{thm:projection-rates}
Under the independent-tuple model with rates $(\fprate, \tprate)$, the
approximate projection accepts $s$ with probability
\[
  1 - (1 - \tprate)^{j(s)} (1 - \fprate)^{k(s) - j(s)}.
\]
A negative projected tuple ($j(s) = 0$) therefore has false-positive rate $1 -
(1 - \fprate)^{k(s)} \ge \fprate$, and a positive one has true-positive rate at
least $\tprate$. Distinct projected tuples have mutually independent indicators,
and the attained pairs $(k(s), j(s))$ give a sufficient partition into
homogeneous rate blocks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The projection rejects $s$ exactly when every fiber trial rejects. The fiber
holds $j(s)$ members, each rejecting with probability $1 - \tprate$, and $k(s) -
j(s)$ nonmembers, each rejecting with probability $1 - \fprate$; independence of
distinct tuples multiplies these. Distinct projected tuples have disjoint fibers,
so their indicators are functions of disjoint independent trial sets.
\end{proof}

The false-positive side amplifies with the fan-in, and the true-positive side is
boosted, because a nonmember fiber tuple can false-positive and so rescue a
genuine projected tuple. On a full Cartesian candidate universe a coordinate
projection has constant fan-in, the product of the discarded domain sizes, but
the true-member count still varies: at $(\fprate, \tprate) = (0.05, 0.90)$,
fibers with $(k, j) = (3, 1)$ and $(3, 2)$ have true-positive rates $0.90975$ and
$0.9905$. Uniform fan-in alone does not give a common true-positive rate. In the
book's vocabulary the projection raises the model order\index{model order}, one
rate block per attained profile (several profiles may share a rate), while
independence is a separate question.

That question has a different answer once sources are shared. Let $A_s$ be the
set of distinct sources read by the fiber of $s$, so that $Y_s =
\bigvee_{i \in A_s} X_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Projection with shared sources]
\label{thm:projection-shared-sources}
Under the independent-source model, let $\Prob{X_i = 1} = p_i$ and put $q_C =
\prod_{i \in C}(1 - p_i)$, with $q_\emptyset = 1$. Then $\Prob{Y_s = 1} = 1 -
q_{A_s}$ and
\[
  \operatorname{Cov}(Y_s, Y_t)
  = q_{A_s \setminus A_t}\, q_{A_t \setminus A_s}\, q_{A_s \cap A_t}
    \bigl(1 - q_{A_s \cap A_t}\bigr).
\]
If every participating source has $0 < p_i < 1$, the projected indicators are
mutually independent exactly when their nonempty source supports are pairwise
disjoint.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$Y_s = 0$ exactly when all of its sources are $0$, which gives the marginal.
Complementing both indicators preserves covariance, so
\[
  \operatorname{Cov}(Y_s, Y_t) = q_{A_s \cup A_t} - q_{A_s} q_{A_t},
\]
and factoring over the disjoint sets $A_s \setminus A_t$, $A_t \setminus A_s$,
and $A_s \cap A_t$ gives the formula. At interior rates a shared source makes it
strictly positive; disjoint supports give functions of disjoint independent
sources.
\end{proof}

At boundary rates an overlapping support can still give zero covariance; the
formula holds, but the disjointness criterion needs interior rates. The smallest
case shows that distinct tuples need not be distinct trials: on the candidate
universe $\{(a, b), (b, a)\}$ with $a \ne b$, a canonical symmetric test reads one
source $Z$ for both tuples, projection onto the first coordinate has disjoint
tuple fibers, and yet both outputs equal $Z$, with covariance $p(1 - p)$.

\subsection*{Natural join}

The natural join\index{join} of $R_1 \subseteq T \times U$ and $R_2 \subseteq U
\times V$ is $R_1 \bowtie R_2 = \{(t, u, v) : (t, u) \in R_1,\, (u, v) \in
R_2\}$, and its approximate version is the AND\index{AND gate} of two noisy tests,
$(\tilde R_1 \bowtie \tilde R_2)(t, u, v) = \tilde R_1(t, u) \land \tilde R_2(u,
v)$, the object of \Cref{sec:and-under-noise}.

\begin{theorem}[Join error rates]
\label{thm:join-rates}
Let $\tilde R_1, \tilde R_2$ have rates $(\fprate_i, \tprate_i)$ and be
cross-relation independent. A candidate triple in the join has false-negative
rate $1 - \tprate_1 \tprate_2$. A candidate triple outside the join is accepted
with probability $\fprate_1 \tprate_2$ if only its $R_1$-pair is absent,
$\tprate_1 \fprate_2$ if only its $R_2$-pair is absent, and $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$
if both are absent. Under a specified distribution on out-of-join triples with
region weights $(w_1, w_2, w_3)$ for ($R_1$-pair present, $R_2$-pair present,
neither), the average false-positive rate is
\[
  w_1 \tprate_1 \fprate_2 + w_2 \fprate_1 \tprate_2 + w_3 \fprate_1 \fprate_2,
\]
which for FNR-zero operands is $w_1 \fprate_2 + w_2 \fprate_1 + w_3 \fprate_1
\fprate_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The triple is reported exactly when both component tests fire, and the two tests
are independent. A true triple has both pairs present, giving acceptance
$\tprate_1 \tprate_2$. An out-of-join triple has at least one pair absent; an
absent pair fires with probability $\fprate_i$ and a present one with probability
$\tprate_i$, which gives the three pointwise rates and, by total probability, the
weighted average. This is the intersection rule of \Cref{prop:intersection-fpr-fnr}
on the triple universe.
\end{proof}

Without a distribution on queries, or counts of the three regions, the three
pointwise regimes remain distinct: a join output is not a two-rate tuple\index{prior-averaged rate}. The bare
product $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ is only the both-absent corner. It is a good summary
when out-of-join triples are dominated by that corner (both relations sparse in a
large triple universe), and it understates the error wherever half-matched triples
carry weight, since there the surviving single rate applies.

\subsection*{A join breaks element-wise independence}

\Cref{thm:join-rates} describes one output triple. Output triples that share an
operand tuple read the same trial.

\begin{theorem}[Shared-component dependence]
\label{thm:join-correlation}
Let two output triples share their $R_1$-pair, $(t, u, v_1)$ and $(t, u, v_2)$
with $v_1 \ne v_2$, and put
\[
  a = \Prob{\tilde R_1(t, u) = \top},
  \qquad
  b_i = \Prob{\tilde R_2(u, v_i) = \top}.
\]
Under cross-relation independence their indicators have covariance $a(1 - a)\,
b_1 b_2$, strictly positive when $0 < a < 1$ and $b_1 b_2 > 0$. A join whose output tuples
read pairwise disjoint input trials preserves independence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $A$ and $B_i$ be the independent events that $\tilde R_1(t, u)$ and $\tilde
R_2(u, v_i)$ report $\top$. The two outputs are accepted together with probability
\[
  \Prob{A \cap B_1 \cap B_2} = a b_1 b_2,
\]
while the product of their marginals is $a^2 b_1 b_2$; the difference is the
stated covariance. Outputs reading disjoint
trial sets are functions of independent variables.
\end{proof}

At $a \in \{0, 1\}$, or when a partner never fires, the covariance vanishes: the
theorem identifies a source-reuse mechanism, not a claim that every join produces
dependence. As a concrete reading, take two true triples sharing their $R_1$-pair,
with every operand true-positive rate $0.9$. They are accepted together with
probability $0.9^3 = 0.729$, while an independence assumption predicts $0.9^4 =
0.6561$; the covariance is $0.0729$ and the joint probability is $1/0.9 \approx
1.11$ times the product of marginals. A planner that multiplied per-row survival
probabilities would understate the chance that both rows survive. The marginal
rate of any single output is still exact, and an expected false-positive count
over a join still adds tuple by tuple, since linearity of expectation needs no
independence; the higher moments and any all-correct probability computed as a
product over output tuples are what the correlation affects.

\subsection*{Worked example}

Let $\tilde F$ be a friend-of relation with $\fnrate_F = 0$, $\fprate_F = 0.05$,
and $\tilde C$ a coworker relation with $\fnrate_C = 0$, $\fprate_C = 0.02$, built
and queried independently. The join returns triples $(p_1, p_2, p_3)$ with $p_1$
reported a friend of $p_2$ and $p_2$ reported a coworker of $p_3$. A triple with
both pairs absent is accepted with probability $\fprate_F \fprate_C = 0.001$, three
orders below either operand, the relational face of the intersection gain of
chapter~6. A triple in which $p_1$ truly is a friend of $p_2$ but $p_2$ is not a
coworker of $p_3$ is accepted with probability $\fprate_C = 0.02$, so where such
half-matched triples are common the average sits nearer to $\max(\fprate_F,
\fprate_C)$ than to the product. The gain rests on cross-relation independence
(a shared hash family breaks the product) and on sparsity.

\subsection*{Composing the operators: lineage, not rate chaining}

A query built from selection, projection, join, and union assigns each candidate
answer a monotone Boolean lineage over source bits, conjunction combining the
facts one witness needs and disjunction combining alternative witnesses. This is
established provenance\index{provenance semiring} machinery \parencite{green-karvounarakis-tannen-2007,
suciu-2011}. With the exact truth profile fixed and aliases identified, the
answer's marginal probability under the independent-source model is a polynomial
in the source rates read off that lineage, the relational form of the
per-latent-assignment propagation\index{composition!per latent assignment} of \Cref{prop:latent-propagation}.

What does not work is chaining a two-rate update per operator up the expression
tree\index{composition!of relations}. Three failures recur. First, a join output
is not a two-rate tuple (\Cref{thm:join-rates}): feeding one summary rate into a
downstream operator reproduces the error of \Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample}.
Second, in join-then-project the fiber of a projected tuple consists of join
outputs that share operand tuples, so its OR is not over independent trials and
$1 - (1 - \fprate)^k$ does not apply; the source supports of
\Cref{thm:projection-shared-sources} must be carried instead. Third, repeated
sources are not fresh trials: a conjunction reading one absent source eight times
at rate $0.05$ fires with probability $0.05$, whereas treating the occurrences as
independent gives $0.05^8 \approx 3.9 \times 10^{-11}$. What survives is exact
lineage evaluation for a fixed profile, linearity of expectation for counts, the
union bound\index{union bound} over a fixed finite workload without any independence, and the
truth-uniform contracts of \Cref{sec:query-contracts}. The probability that a
whole result is error-free does not factor over output tuples once they share
sources. The projection penalty of a large fan-in is also what motivates the
sketch-based similarity join of \Cref{sec:approximate-joins}.
