\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch12-bridge}

The chapter read one object, the approximate relation, through its operators and
its invariants. \Cref{sec:sets-to-relations} cast it as the chapter-5 framework on
the pair universe $T \times U$ (\Cref{ch:approximate-sets}), with the chapter-9
binary-function view (\Cref{ch:approximate-functions}) an equivalent reading.
\Cref{sec:relation-composition} added the relational operators, and they behave
sharply differently under approximation: selection is rate-transparent, projection
amplifies the false-positive rate by its fan-in and raises the model order while
keeping element-wise independence, and a join breaks that independence outright
(\Cref{thm:join-correlation}), correlating every output tuple built from a shared
component. The join's marginal rate (\Cref{thm:join-rates}) is the chapter-6
intersection rate on the triple universe (\Cref{ch:set-composition}), the bare
product $\fprate_1 \fprate_2$ only in the sparse regime where out-of-join triples
are dominated by the both-unrelated region. \Cref{sec:relation-invariants} then
asked which structural invariants survive: reflexivity, symmetry,
single-valuedness, and transitivity are each preserved only with a probability
that vanishes as the universe grows, the symmetric closure the one cheap repair.
\Cref{sec:approximate-joins} instantiated the machinery on the MinHash-LSH
similarity join (\Cref{sec:minhash-lsh}), reading its FPR/FNR as the chapter-12
join rates specialized to a sketch-based predicate.

\Cref{ch:algebra-types} closes Part~III with the type algebra that unifies
approximate sets, functions, relations, and sketches into one algebra of
$\Bernoulli{T}$ constructors. An approximate relation is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
on a product universe, equivalently $\Bernoulli{T \times U}$ in the
type-constructor view, and the composition rules each prior chapter derived by
direct calculation become functorial properties of the product type. Chapter~12
is one of the first instances of that unification: an approximate relation reread
as $\Bernoulli{T \times U}$ inherits its composition rules from the algebraic
structure of the product type rather than from a separate derivation.
