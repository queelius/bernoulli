\section{Bridge}
\label{sec:ch12-bridge}

The chapter read the approximate relation through its operators, its invariants,
and its queries, and one distinction carried all three: a tuple's rate is a
property of one probe, while a query's error depends on which stored outcomes its
probes share. \Cref{sec:sets-to-relations} separated exact truth, construction
randomness, and source identity. \Cref{sec:relation-composition} found selection
rate-transparent, projection amplifying false positives with rates indexed by the
fiber profile $(k, j)$, and the join correct per tuple but correlating outputs that
share an operand tuple; composing operators requires the lineage over sources, not
a chain of per-operator rate updates. \Cref{sec:relation-invariants} showed every
invariant fragile under independent tuples, and showed that the constructions which
enforce one (canonical sources, OR-closure, deletion) move error into the stored-key
count and the dependence structure rather than removing it.
\Cref{sec:query-contracts} turned the source view into truth-uniform contracts,
$1 - (1 - \fprate)^{s_0}$ and $1 - (1 - \fnrate)^{s_1}$ under independent one-sided
sources, and showed in a stored-filter experiment that sizing sources at the tuple
target can miss a query target by a factor of more than six, while calibrated sizing
meets it. \Cref{sec:approximate-joins} read the MinHash-LSH similarity join in the
same terms.

\begin{remark}[Scope of an encrypted-search reading]
\label{rem:ch12-encrypted-scope}
Approximate relations can model the \emph{functionality} of a private search
index\index{encrypted search}\index{secure index}, with tuples encoding document-term incidence or candidate matches and a
positive query combining approximate probes; subject to a justified construction
law, the contracts above describe its false matches and missed answers. They do not
describe its confidentiality. Two structures with identical false-positive and
false-negative rates can expose different sizes, access patterns, query-equality
patterns, or stored representations, and a secret salt alone does not establish a
security theorem. A security claim needs a construction, an adversary, an
experiment, and a declared leakage, including what repeated and adaptive queries
reveal. \Cref{ch:cipher-maps-trapdoor} and \Cref{ch:encrypted-search} take up
that analysis separately.
\end{remark}

\Cref{ch:algebra-types} closes Part~III with the type algebra\index{approximate type} that organizes
approximate sets, functions, relations, and sketches as $\Bernoulli{T}$
constructors. An approximate relation is $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ on a product
universe, and the type view supplies its marginal rates. The lesson of this chapter
travels with it: marginals compose cleanly, but joint behavior is fixed by shared
sources, which a type signature alone does not record.
