\subsection*{Two axioms, one payoff}

We have named two axioms and shown what each rules out. \Cref{sec:axiom-1}
gave Axiom 1: error events at distinct inputs are mutually independent.
\Cref{sec:axiom-2} gave Axiom 2: per-block error rates are conditionally
independent of each other, which implies that each block's channel row
is stable across queries. Both sections foreshadowed a theorem that their
conjunction enables. We now prove it.

The question is this. Given $m$ queries on a Bernoulli predicate, each on
a distinct element of the universal set---what does the joint
confusion matrix look like? The joint matrix $Q^{(m)}$ is an $n^m \times 2^m$
object: rows are indexed by source-block tuples $(j_1,\ldots,j_m) \in
\{1,\ldots,n\}^m$ (one row per ordered selection of source blocks across
the $m$ queries), and columns are indexed by binary output patterns
$(k_1,\ldots,k_m) \in \{0,1\}^m$. Without any structural assumption,
specifying this matrix requires $n^m(2^m - 1)$ real parameters (one
distribution over $2^m$ output patterns per source-block tuple). For
$m = 50$ and $n = 2$, that is over $10^{30}$ free parameters.

A different, natural parameterization treats only the $n$ distinct source
blocks, not all $n^m$ block-tuples, as the basic unit. Under this
per-source-block convention the joint conditional distribution over $2^m$
output patterns is the same for every query from block $B_j$, giving $n$
distributions of size $2^m$ and hence $n(2^m - 1)$ free parameters. The
parameter counts in this section's corollary and in \Cref{sec:axiom-2}
are stated under this convention---and it is exactly the convention the
axioms enforce, as the corollary explains.

Under Axioms 1 and 2, the per-source-block parameter count drops to
$nm$: $n$ free parameters per query (one per block row), with no
cross-query interaction, summed over $m$ queries. In the order-2 Boolean
case this is $2m$. This is not an approximation; it is the exact
structure forced by independence.

\begin{notation}
The \emph{Kronecker product} of two matrices $P$ and $Q$, written
$P \otimes Q$, is the block matrix formed by replacing each entry
$P_{ij}$ with the scaled submatrix $P_{ij} Q$. If $P$ is $r \times s$
and $Q$ is $p \times q$, then $P \otimes Q$ is $(rp) \times (sq)$.
The Kronecker product is associative, so $P \otimes Q \otimes R$ is
unambiguous without parentheses. We use $\otimes$ for the Kronecker
product throughout this section and the rest of the book.
\end{notation}

\begin{theorem}[Kronecker Factorization, conditional form]
\label{thm:kronecker}
Let $\tilde{A}$ be an $n$-th order Bernoulli predicate satisfying
\Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom~1) and \Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}
(Axiom~2). For any realization of the per-block rates
$\alpha = (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n)$, let $Q^*_1(\alpha), \ldots, Q^*_m(\alpha)$
be the resulting per-query channel matrices, where $Q^*_i(\alpha)$ is the
$n \times 2$ stochastic matrix whose $(j, k)$-entry is the probability,
under the realized rate $\alpha_j$, that a query of an element in block
$B_j$ produces output label $k$ on the $i$-th query. Then the
\emph{rate-conditional} joint confusion matrix
$Q^{(m) *}(\alpha)$ of $m$ queries on $m$ distinct input elements,
whose $(j_1 \cdots j_m,\, k_1 \cdots k_m)$-entry is
\[
  \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = k_1, \ldots, \tilde{A}(x_m) = k_m
        \Given \alpha,\; x_\ell \in B_{j_\ell} \text{ for each } \ell},
\]
factors as the Kronecker product
\[
  Q^{(m) *}(\alpha) \;=\; Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes Q^*_2(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional factorization, distinct-block queries]
\label{cor:kronecker-unconditional}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:kronecker}, if the queried blocks
$j_1, \ldots, j_m$ are pairwise distinct (each query hits a different
block), the unconditional joint confusion matrix factors as
\[
  Q^{(m)} \;=\; Q_1 \otimes Q_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes Q_m,
\]
where $Q_\ell \coloneqq \Expect{Q^*_\ell(\alpha)}$ is the rate-marginalized
per-query channel.
\end{corollary}

Before the proof, two notes on scope. \textbf{First}, the theorem applies
to $m$ queries on $m$ \emph{distinct} input elements. Repeated queries of
the same element introduce correlations that Axioms 1 and 2 do not address:
the two error events share a single realization of the element's
underlying error draw, making them perfectly correlated (in a deterministic
implementation) rather than independent. For repeated-query behavior,
including the effects of memoization and caching, see Chapter~16
(negative space).

\textbf{Second}, the theorem's primary claim is at the rate-conditional
level: given any realization of the per-block rates $\alpha$, the joint
confusion matrix factors. The unconditional matrix factors only when the
queried blocks are pairwise distinct (\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional}).
When two queries hit the same block $B_j$, both factors involve the same
random rate $\alpha_j$, and the unconditional joint
$\Expect{\alpha_j^2}$ exceeds the Kronecker prediction $(\Expect{\alpha_j})^2$
whenever $\alpha_j$ is non-degenerate (a Jensen gap). The conditional
factorization holds always; the unconditional factorization holds when
either the rates are realized as constants (the deterministic-implementation
case used throughout chapters~1--3) or when no block is queried twice. In
the running BSC thread below and in subsequent chapters, we work in the
deterministic-rates regime, where $Q^*_\ell = Q_\ell$ and the conditional
and unconditional forms coincide.

\begin{proof}
We build up the joint distribution by hand and read off the matrix
structure.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: reduce to a joint probability calculation.}
Index the $m$ query positions as $\ell = 1, \ldots, m$. Position $\ell$
queries element $x_\ell \in B_{j_\ell}$ and observes output $k_\ell$.
Fix a realization of the per-block rates $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \ldots,
\alpha_n)$. The $( j_1 \cdots j_m, k_1 \cdots k_m )$-entry of the
rate-conditional matrix $Q^{(m) *}(\alpha)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:joint-entry}
  Q^{(m) *}(\alpha)_{j_1 \cdots j_m,\, k_1 \cdots k_m}
  \;=\;
  \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = k_1, \ldots, \tilde{A}(x_m) = k_m
        \Given \alpha,\; x_\ell \in B_{j_\ell},\; \ell = 1,\ldots,m}.
\end{equation}
We want to evaluate this probability and show it equals
$\prod_{\ell=1}^m (Q^*_\ell(\alpha))_{j_\ell, k_\ell}$, which is precisely
the $( j_1 \cdots j_m, k_1 \cdots k_m )$-entry of
$Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: apply Axiom 2 to fix the per-block rates.}
By Axiom~2 (\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}), the random rate $\alpha_i$ on
block $B_i$ is independent of the random rate $\alpha_{i'}$ on block
$B_{i'}$ for $i \neq i'$. By \Cref{prop:stable-Q}, once the rates are
realized, the rate-conditional channel matrix $Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$ for
query $\ell$ has its row $j$ determined by $\alpha_j$ alone: row $j$
gives the conditional output distribution for an element in block $B_j$
under the realized rate $\alpha_j$. Write
$(Q^*_\ell(\alpha))_{j_\ell,k_\ell}$ for the $(j_\ell, k_\ell)$-entry of
this random matrix, the rate-conditional probability that query $\ell$
from block $B_{j_\ell}$ returns label $k_\ell$ given the rate
$\alpha_{j_\ell}$. Distinct queries on the same block share the same
realization of $\alpha_{j_\ell}$ and hence the same row of
$Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$; this row does not depend on what happened at other
query positions. When all $m$ queries share a single realization of the
channel family (the common case for a stationary data structure),
$Q^*_1(\alpha) = Q^*_2(\alpha) = \cdots = Q^*_m(\alpha) = Q^*(\alpha)$;
the theorem's subscript $\ell$ flags per-query generality, not necessarily
distinct matrices.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: apply Axiom 1 to factor the joint distribution.}
Conditional on the realized rates $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n)$,
the outputs at positions $\ell = 1, \ldots, m$ are mutually independent,
because each output is a deterministic function of the (conditionally
independent) error event at $x_\ell$. By Axiom~1
(\Cref{ax:element-indep}), these error events are mutually independent
given the rates and the objective set $A$. Therefore, conditional on the
rates, the joint probability in \eqref{eq:joint-entry} factors:
\begin{align*}
  &\Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = k_1, \ldots, \tilde{A}(x_m) = k_m
         \Given \alpha, x_\ell \in B_{j_\ell}} \\
  &\quad=\;
  \prod_{\ell=1}^m \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_\ell) = k_\ell
                         \Given \alpha_{j_\ell},\, x_\ell \in B_{j_\ell}} \\
  &\quad=\;
  \prod_{\ell=1}^m (Q^*_\ell(\alpha))_{j_\ell, k_\ell}.
\end{align*}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: read off the conditional matrix.}
The expression $\prod_{\ell=1}^m (Q^*_\ell)_{j_\ell, k_\ell}$ derived in
Step~3 is, by definition, the $(j_1 \cdots j_m,\, k_1 \cdots k_m)$-entry
of the Kronecker product
$Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha)$. Since this holds for
every index tuple, the rate-conditional joint matrix factors:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kronecker-conclusion}
  Q^{(m) *}(\alpha) \;=\; Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha).
\end{equation}
This proves \Cref{thm:kronecker}.

To obtain the unconditional version (\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional})
under the additional hypothesis that the queried blocks are pairwise
distinct, take expectation over $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n)$:
\[
  \Expect{\prod_{\ell=1}^m (Q^*_\ell)_{j_\ell, k_\ell}}
  \;=\;
  \prod_{\ell=1}^m \Expect{(Q^*_\ell)_{j_\ell, k_\ell}}
  \;=\;
  \prod_{\ell=1}^m (Q_\ell)_{j_\ell, k_\ell}.
\]
The first equality uses Axiom~2: when $j_1, \ldots, j_m$ are pairwise
distinct, each factor depends on a distinct rate $\alpha_{j_\ell}$, and
the cross-block independence guaranteed by Axiom~2 makes the factors
mutually independent random variables. (When two queries hit the same
block, this equality fails; see the second scope note above.)
\end{proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Honesty note.} The parametric parsimony observation
underlying this theorem appears as Remark~3.4 in the companion paper
\cite{towell-bernoulli-sets}, which notes that Axiom~1 forces the joint
confusion matrix to factor as a Kronecker product. The companion paper
states this remark in a regime where rates are either deterministic or
implicitly conditioned upon; under that reading the remark is correct,
and the conditional form of \Cref{thm:kronecker} above makes the
conditioning explicit. Axiom~2's role in this textbook is not to extend
the conditional factorization to the unconditional case in general. That
is impossible without further hypotheses, since same-block queries on
random rates produce a Jensen gap $\Expect{\alpha_j^2} > (\Expect{\alpha_j})^2$.
Axiom~2 instead extends the unconditional factorization to the special
case of distinct-block queries (\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional}) and,
more importantly, certifies the rate-stability that lets us speak of
``the'' channel matrix $Q_\ell$ for each query position. The constructive
proof above and the explicit conditional/unconditional decomposition are
new contributions of this textbook.

\subsection*{Corollary: the parameter count drop}

\begin{corollary}[Parameter reduction, conditional form]
\label{cor:parameter-reduction}
Under Axioms~1 and~2, the rate-conditional joint confusion matrix
$Q^{(m) *}(\alpha)$ of an $n$-th order Bernoulli predicate queried $m$
times requires at most $nm$ parameters to specify, rather than the
$n(2^m - 1)$ parameters required by a general (unconstrained) $m$-query
channel. For the order-2 Boolean predicate this reduces to $2m$
parameters (one per query per block) rather than $2(2^m - 1)$. The same
count applies to the unconditional matrix $Q^{(m)}$ when rates are
realized as constants (the deterministic-rates regime of all worked
examples in this book) or when no block is queried twice
(\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
We count the free parameters in
$Q^{(m) *}(\alpha) = Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Constrained case.} Under the axioms, $Q^{(m) *}(\alpha)$
factors as $Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha)$
(\Cref{thm:kronecker}), so each per-query matrix $Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$
contributes its own parameters independently. Each $Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$ is
an $n \times 2$ stochastic matrix: rows are indexed by the $n$ source
blocks, and columns by the two binary output labels. Each row sums to 1,
leaving 1 free parameter per row. With $n$ rows, each $Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$
has $n$ free parameters. By Axiom~2 the per-block rates are independent
across queries, so the $m$ matrices share no parameters. Across $m$
queries: $nm$ free parameters in total.

In the order-2 Boolean case ($n = 2$), the two blocks are positives and
negatives, parametrized by $(\tprate, \fprate)$ per query, or equivalently
by $(\fnrate, \fprate)$. That is $2$ parameters per query, for $2m$
parameters in total across $m$ queries.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{General case.} The full block-tuple matrix has $n^m$
rows and $2^m$ columns; specifying it freely would require $n^m(2^m - 1)$
parameters. The more compact count uses the per-source-block convention
introduced above: without the Kronecker structure but still working
source-block by source-block, each of the $n$ source blocks contributes
one (unconstrained) conditional distribution over $2^m$ joint output
patterns, requiring $2^m - 1$ free parameters after normalization. Total
under this convention: $n(2^m - 1)$. This is the comparison point used
here and in \Cref{sec:axiom-2}, because it reflects the reduction
attributable to the axioms alone rather than conflating it with the
block-tuple explosion.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Transfer to the unconditional matrix.} When per-block
rates are deterministic constants (so $Q^*_\ell(\alpha) = Q_\ell$
identically), the conditional and unconditional matrices coincide and
the $nm$ count applies to $Q^{(m)}$ directly. When rates are random but
the queried blocks are pairwise distinct,
\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional} gives $Q^{(m)} = Q_1 \otimes \cdots
\otimes Q_m$ with each $Q_\ell$ contributing $n$ free parameters, again
totalling $nm$. Both regimes cover every worked example in Part~I.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Ratio.}
\[
  \frac{n(2^m - 1)}{nm} \;=\; \frac{2^m - 1}{m},
\]
which grows exponentially in $m$. At $m = 50$ and $n = 2$:
general case $2(2^{50}-1) \approx 2.25 \times 10^{15}$; constrained
case $2 \times 50 = 100$.
\end{proof}

The corollary matches the closing summary of \Cref{sec:axiom-2}: the
order-2 case requires $2m$ parameters under the axioms versus
$2(2^m - 1)$ in the general case; the $n$-th order case requires $nm$
versus $n(2^m - 1)$. The Kronecker structure, forced by the two axioms,
is what produces this reduction.

\subsection*{Running thread: the BSC composed $m$ times}

The binary symmetric channel (BSC) introduced in \Cref{sec:bsc} is the
simplest nontrivial instance of the Kronecker theorem. A BSC with
crossover probability $\fprate$ has channel matrix
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fprate & \fprate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since the BSC is symmetric, $\fprate$ is both the false-positive rate
(an element outside $A$ is reported as inside) and the false-negative
rate (an element inside $A$ is reported as outside). This is the
symmetric special case of the order-2 Bernoulli model: a single
crossover parameter $\fprate$ suffices because the channel obeys
FPR = FNR = $\fprate$ (the symmetry constraint), and the two rows
$[1-\fprate,\, \fprate]$ and $[\fprate,\, 1-\fprate]$ are reversals of
each other. The model order is 2 because there are two partition blocks
($B_1$ = positives, $B_2$ = negatives); the symmetry constraint reduces
the two free parameters of the general order-2 model to one.

Now suppose we run $m$ independent queries, each through the same BSC.
We work in the deterministic-rates regime announced in the second scope
note above, where $Q^*_\ell = Q$ for every query and the conditional
matrix of \Cref{thm:kronecker} coincides with the unconditional one. The
joint confusion matrix is then
\[
  Q^{(m)} \;=\; \underbrace{Q \otimes Q \otimes \cdots \otimes Q}_{m \text{ copies}}
  \;=\; Q^{\otimes m}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Explicit computation for $m = 2$.} Two queries, each
through a BSC with crossover $\fprate$. The joint input lives in
$\{B_1, B_1\} \cup \{B_1, B_2\} \cup \{B_2, B_1\} \cup \{B_2, B_2\}$,
where we write $B_1$ for ``in $A$'' and $B_2$ for ``not in $A$''.
The joint output lives in $\{(+,+), (+,-), (-,+), (-,-)\}$, where
$+$ means ``reported in $A$'' and $-$ means ``reported not in $A$''.

By the Kronecker product definition, $Q \otimes Q$ is the $4 \times 4$
matrix formed by scaling each entry of $Q$ by the full matrix $Q$:
\[
  Q^{\otimes 2}
  \;=\;
  Q \otimes Q
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    (1-\fprate)Q & \fprate Q \\
    \fprate Q & (1-\fprate)Q
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Expanding each block gives
\[
  Q^{\otimes 2}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    (1-\fprate)^2 & (1-\fprate)\fprate & \fprate(1-\fprate) & \fprate^2 \\
    (1-\fprate)\fprate & (1-\fprate)^2 & \fprate^2 & \fprate(1-\fprate) \\
    \fprate(1-\fprate) & \fprate^2 & (1-\fprate)^2 & (1-\fprate)\fprate \\
    \fprate^2 & \fprate(1-\fprate) & (1-\fprate)\fprate & (1-\fprate)^2
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Reading the matrix: the $(1,1)$-entry is the probability that two elements
from block $B_1$ (both positives) are both reported positive, namely
$(1-\fprate)^2$. The $(1,4)$-entry is the probability that two positives
are both reported negative, namely $\fprate^2$. The $(2,2)$-entry---one
positive, one negative; both reported positive---is $(1-\fprate)\fprate$:
the positive is correctly reported positive ($1-\fprate$) and the
negative is falsely reported positive ($\fprate$).

For a numerical example, take $\fprate = 0.05$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\hline
Joint input blocks & \multicolumn{4}{c}{Joint output probabilities} \\
 & $(+,+)$ & $(+,-)$ & $(-,+)$ & $(-,-)$ \\
\hline
$(B_1, B_1)$ & $0.9025$ & $0.0475$ & $0.0475$ & $0.0025$ \\
$(B_1, B_2)$ & $0.0475$ & $0.9025$ & $0.0025$ & $0.0475$ \\
$(B_2, B_1)$ & $0.0475$ & $0.0025$ & $0.9025$ & $0.0475$ \\
$(B_2, B_2)$ & $0.0025$ & $0.0475$ & $0.0475$ & $0.9025$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The BSC thread that opened in \Cref{sec:bsc} reaches its formal
resolution here: $m$ independent queries through the same BSC produce
a joint channel whose confusion matrix is the $m$-fold Kronecker power
$Q^{\otimes m}$. Each entry of $Q^{\otimes m}$ is a product of $m$
individual BSC transition probabilities, each equal to $\fprate$ or
$1 - \fprate$ according to whether that query position produced an
error. The Kronecker structure is not a coincidence: it is exactly
what Axioms 1 and 2 force.

\subsection*{A 2-query worked verification}

We now verify \Cref{thm:kronecker} with a general order-2 channel
to show that the BSC example is not special.

Let the predicate $\tilde{A}$ have false-positive rate $\fprate$ and
false-negative rate $\fnrate$ (so true-positive rate $\tprate = 1 - \fnrate$
and true-negative rate $\tnrate = 1 - \fprate$). The per-query channel
matrix is
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \tprate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & \tnrate
  \end{pmatrix}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
where row 1 corresponds to positives ($x \in A$, block $B_1$) and
row 2 corresponds to negatives ($x \notin A$, block $B_2$).

Suppose we query two elements $x_1 \in B_{j_1}$ and $x_2 \in B_{j_2}$
and observe outputs $k_1$ and $k_2$ respectively. For concreteness,
take both queries to the same channel (both $Q_1 = Q_2 = Q$, since
the rates are stationary by \Cref{prop:stable-Q}, which relies on
both Axioms~1 and~2). As in the BSC thread, we work in the
deterministic-rates regime, where the conditional matrix of
\Cref{thm:kronecker} coincides with the unconditional one.

\Cref{thm:kronecker} predicts:
\[
  Q^{(2)} \;=\; Q \otimes Q.
\]

Let us compute $Q \otimes Q$ directly and then verify each entry
against its direct probability calculation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The Kronecker product $Q \otimes Q$.}
\begin{align*}
  Q \otimes Q
  &=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \tprate \cdot Q & \fnrate \cdot Q \\
    \fprate \cdot Q & \tnrate \cdot Q
  \end{pmatrix} \\[6pt]
  &=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \tprate^2   & \tprate\fnrate & \fnrate\tprate & \fnrate^2 \\
    \tprate\fprate & \tprate\tnrate & \fnrate\fprate & \fnrate\tnrate \\
    \fprate\tprate & \fprate\fnrate & \tnrate\tprate & \tnrate\fnrate \\
    \fprate^2   & \fprate\tnrate & \tnrate\fprate & \tnrate^2
  \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align*}
Row order is $(B_1B_1, B_1B_2, B_2B_1, B_2B_2)$ and column order is
$(++, +-, -+, --)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Direct probability calculation.}
We verify two entries by direct calculation.

\emph{Entry $(B_1 B_1, ++)$: two positives, both reported positive.}
By Axiom~1, the events are independent:
\[
  \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = +, \tilde{A}(x_2) = + \Given x_1 \in B_1, x_2 \in B_1}
  \;=\; \tprate \cdot \tprate \;=\; \tprate^2.
\]
This matches $(Q \otimes Q)_{11} = \tprate^2$. Axiom~1 licenses the
product; Axiom~2 ensures each $\tprate$ factor is the same stable value.

\emph{Entry $(B_2 B_1, -+)$: negative then positive, first reported
negative (correct), second reported positive (correct).}
\[
  \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = -, \tilde{A}(x_2) = + \Given x_1 \in B_2, x_2 \in B_1}
  \;=\; \tnrate \cdot \tprate.
\]
This matches $(Q \otimes Q)_{33} = \tnrate \tprate$ (row 3, column 3; note
column 3 is the $(-,+)$ output pattern).

\emph{Entry $(B_1 B_2, +-)$: positive then negative, both correctly
reported. Block $B_1 = \{\top\}$ (positive), output $+$ is a true
positive; block $B_2 = \{\bot\}$ (negative), output $-$ is a true
negative.}
\[
  \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = +, \tilde{A}(x_2) = - \Given x_1 \in B_1, x_2 \in B_2}
  \;=\; \tprate \cdot \tnrate.
\]
This matches $(Q \otimes Q)_{22} = \tprate\tnrate$. For the mixed
true-positive / false-positive entry---output $(+,+)$ when blocks are
$(B_1, B_2)$, meaning the positive is correctly reported positive and
the negative is falsely reported positive---we need column 1:
$(Q \otimes Q)_{21} = \tprate \fprate$, which equals
$\Prob{+,+ \Given B_1, B_2} = \tprate \cdot \fprate$. Confirmed.

All entries are products of individual channel probabilities. This is
exactly what \Cref{thm:kronecker} predicts: each row of $Q^{(2)}$ is
the tensor product of the corresponding per-query rows, and the
resulting matrix has $4$ parameters ($\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ per query,
with both queries sharing the same rates by Axiom~2), not the $2(2^2 - 1) = 6$
that a general 2-query channel would need.

\subsection*{What the Kronecker structure buys for \S\ref{sec:and-under-noise}}

The AND derivation in \Cref{sec:and-under-noise} computed the joint
error rate of two noisy bits by treating the two error events as
independent and multiplying probabilities. We can now see exactly which
theorem that derivation was tacitly invoking.

The two-query joint channel for an AND gate is a special case of
$Q^{(2)} = Q_1 \otimes Q_2$ where $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are the per-input
channel matrices for the two operands. The independence assumed in
\Cref{sec:and-under-noise} was Axiom~1 (the two error events are
independent) plus the stability of each operand's rates (Axiom~2).
The Kronecker factorization theorem makes this precise: the joint
distribution of the two AND operands' channel outputs factors, and
the AND gate's output distribution is a deterministic function of
that joint distribution. Nothing more was needed than what the two
axioms provide.

\subsection*{The formal type is next}

The Kronecker theorem is the formal payoff for chapters 1--4's
accumulated independence assumptions. We now have a complete
characterization of the joint confusion matrix under the Bernoulli
framework's two axioms: it is a Kronecker product of per-query
channel matrices, one factor per query, with $nm$ parameters total
for an $n$-th order predicate on $m$ queries.

In \Cref{sec:bernoulli-bool-formal}, we turn this machinery into
a formal definition of the $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ type---the object
that chapters 1--3 have been pointing at. The Kronecker theorem is
what makes that definition tractable: it tells us that the formal type
is parametrized by finitely many numbers (two, for the Boolean case),
not by an exponentially large family of joint distributions.
