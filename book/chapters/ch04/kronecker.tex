\section{The Kronecker Factorization Theorem}
\label{sec:kronecker}

The two axioms together force a precise structure on the joint behavior of many
queries. Index the $m$ queries by position $\ell$, with query $\ell$ on an
element of block $B_{j_\ell}$ producing output $k_\ell$. The
\emph{Kronecker product}\index{Kronecker product} $P \otimes Q$ replaces each entry $P_{ij}$ by the
scaled block $P_{ij} Q$; it is associative. The theorem below is the familiar
fact that independent components form a product channel, a memoryless channel in
information theory and a tensor-product noise model in quantum readout; it is
stated here with the conditioning on random rates made explicit, because the
structures of later chapters have random rates.

\begin{theorem}[Kronecker factorization, conditional form]
\label{thm:kronecker}
\index{Kronecker factorization}
Let $\tilde{A}$ be an $n$-th order Bernoulli predicate satisfying
\Cref{ax:element-indep} and \Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}. For any realization
$\alpha$ of the per-block rates, let $Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$ be the per-query channel
matrix at position $\ell$. Then the rate-conditional joint confusion matrix\index{confusion matrix} of
$m$ queries on $m$ distinct elements factors as
\[
  Q^{(m)*}(\alpha) \;=\; Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots \otimes Q^*_m(\alpha).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the rates $\alpha$. By \Cref{prop:stable-Q} the row of $Q^*_\ell(\alpha)$
for block $B_{j_\ell}$ is determined by $\alpha_{j_\ell}$ alone, independent of
other positions. Conditional on $\alpha$, the outputs are deterministic
functions of the per-element error events, which are mutually independent by
Axiom~1, so the joint entry factors,
\[
  \Prob{\tilde{A}(x_1) = k_1, \ldots \Given \alpha}
  = \prod_{\ell=1}^{m} (Q^*_\ell(\alpha))_{j_\ell, k_\ell},
\]
which is by definition the corresponding entry of $Q^*_1(\alpha) \otimes \cdots
\otimes Q^*_m(\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional factorization, distinct blocks]
\label{cor:kronecker-unconditional}
If the queried blocks $j_1, \ldots, j_m$ are pairwise distinct, the
unconditional matrix factors as $Q^{(m)} = Q_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes Q_m$ with
$Q_\ell = \Expect{Q^*_\ell(\alpha)}$.
\end{corollary}

The conditional factorization holds always; the unconditional one needs the
distinct-block hypothesis because taking the expectation through the product
requires independent factors. When two queries hit the same block $B_j$, both
involve the same random $\alpha_j$, and $\Expect{\alpha_j^2} > (\Expect
\alpha_j)^2$ opens a Jensen gap\index{correlated noise}, so the unconditional joint exceeds the
Kronecker prediction: a mixture of product laws is not a product law. Two regimes avoid this: deterministic rates (where
$Q^*_\ell = Q_\ell$ and the conditional and unconditional forms coincide, the
case of every worked example in this book) and pairwise-distinct queried blocks.

\begin{corollary}[Parameter reduction]
\label{cor:parameter-reduction}
\index{parametric parsimony}
Under the two axioms, the rate-conditional $m$-query matrix needs $nm$
parameters, against $n(2^m - 1)$ for an unconstrained $m$-query channel in the
per-source-block convention; for the order-2 Boolean predicate, $2m$ against
$2(2^m - 1)$. The same count holds for the unconditional matrix in the two
regimes above.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each per-query matrix $Q^*_\ell$ is $n \times 2$ row-stochastic, contributing
one free parameter per row, so $n$ per query and $nm$ across $m$ queries with no
shared parameters (Axiom~2). The unconstrained per-source-block matrix assigns
each of the $n$ blocks a free distribution over $2^m$ output patterns, $2^m - 1$
parameters each. At $m = 50$, $n = 2$: $100$ against $\approx 2.25 \times
10^{15}$.
\end{proof}

The bound is tight: $m$ queries with independently chosen rates $(\fprate_\ell,
\fnrate_\ell)$ satisfy both axioms, and distinct parameter tuples give distinct
joint matrices, so no reparameterization beats $2m$. None of this is deep: the
conditional form is the definition of conditional independence, the
unconditional corollary is the expectation of a product of independent factors,
and the Jensen gap is the positive correlation of any mixture of independent
trials. The source manuscript \parencite{towell-bernoulli-sets} records the
factorization as a remark for deterministic or conditioned rates; the book
separates the conditional and unconditional statements because the
distinction decides whether a Bloom filter's false positives on distinct
non-members can be multiplied (\Cref{sec:axiom-1}).

\subsection*{The BSC, queried m times}

The binary symmetric channel\index{channel!binary symmetric} of \Cref{sec:bsc} is the cleanest instance. It has
one crossover parameter $\fprate = \fnrate$, so its two inputs share a rate and
merge into a single block: the BSC is model order 1 (\Cref{sec:model-order}),
studied as the symmetric constrained member of the order-2 family. In the
deterministic-rates regime, $m$ independent queries through it have joint matrix
$Q^{\otimes m}$. For $m = 2$,
\[
  Q^{\otimes 2} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    (1-\fprate)^2 & (1-\fprate)\fprate & \fprate(1-\fprate) & \fprate^2 \\
    (1-\fprate)\fprate & (1-\fprate)^2 & \fprate^2 & \fprate(1-\fprate) \\
    \fprate(1-\fprate) & \fprate^2 & (1-\fprate)^2 & (1-\fprate)\fprate \\
    \fprate^2 & \fprate(1-\fprate) & (1-\fprate)\fprate & (1-\fprate)^2
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
each entry a product of two transition probabilities. The general order-2
channel $Q = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1-\fnrate & \fnrate \\ \fprate & 1-\fprate
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ verifies the same way: the $(B_1 B_1, {++})$ entry of
$Q \otimes Q$ is $\tprate^2$, which is $\Prob{\text{both } \top \mid \text{both
positive}} = \tprate \cdot \tprate$ by Axiom~1, with each $\tprate$ the same
stable value by Axiom~2. The two-query joint thus carries $4$ parameters, not
the $2(2^2 - 1) = 6$ a general channel would.

This is the theorem the AND derivation of \Cref{sec:and-under-noise} tacitly
used: the two operands' joint channel is $Q_1 \otimes Q_2$, and the AND output
is a deterministic function of that joint, with Axiom~1 supplying the
factorization and Axiom~2 the stable rates. It also explains the caveat of
\Cref{sec:boolean-formulas}: the joint channel has four rows, one per latent
pair, and a deterministic function of it inherits four rows, not two. Serial
composition (matrix product) and this Kronecker factorization (tensor product)
answer different questions in different geometries: serial composition threads
one element through a chain and needs only the law of total probability, while
the Kronecker product queries $m$ elements in parallel and needs the axioms. With the theorem in hand, the formal definition of
\Cref{sec:bernoulli-bool-formal} is parametrized by finitely many numbers, two
in the Boolean case, rather than an exponential family of joint distributions.
