\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$}{Bernoulli[bool]} Type, Formally}
\label{sec:bernoulli-bool-formal}

The two axioms and the Kronecker theorem earn the formal definition the first
three chapters circled.

\begin{definition}[$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$]
\label{def:bernoulli-bool}
\index{Bernoulli Boolean}\index{Bernoulli type}
A \emph{$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance} is a triple $(X, Y, Q)$ with input and
output domains $X = Y = \{\top, \bot\}$, blocks $B_1 = \{\top\}$ and
$B_2 = \{\bot\}$, and a $2 \times 2$ row-stochastic channel matrix
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \tprate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & \tnrate
  \end{pmatrix}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
row $i$ governing block $B_i$, with $\fprate$ the false-positive rate, $\fnrate$
the false-negative rate, and $\tprate = 1 - \fnrate$, $\tnrate = 1 - \fprate$.
The triple satisfies \Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom~1) and
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom~2).
\end{definition}

We write $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ for the type, the collection of all such
triples; a predicate or data structure is an \emph{instance} when its behavior
matches the definition. The pair $(\fprate, \fnrate)$ identifies $Q$ within the
type. Axiom~1 is what lets $Q$ be read as a per-element description (without it,
cross-input correlation needs a richer object), and Axiom~2 is what makes $Q$
time-invariant; together they are the content of
\Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}. The definition is for the Boolean case; the
extension to $\Bernoulli{T}$ for general $T$ is Part~III.

\begin{example}[Biased coin]
\label{ex:biased-coin-formal}
A coin lands heads ($\top$) with prior $p$, observed through a channel with
rates $(\fprate, \fnrate)$. The joint distribution over (truth, report) pairs is
$p\,\tprate$, $p\,\fnrate$, $(1-p)\,\fprate$, $(1-p)\,\tnrate$ for TP, FN, FP,
TN, summing to one. The type characterizes the channel, not the source: the
prior $p$ is not part of $(X, Y, Q)$, though it enters when computing outcome
probabilities. At $p = 0.6$, $\fprate = 0.05$, $\fnrate = 0.10$ the four
outcomes are $0.540, 0.060, 0.020, 0.380$, and $Q = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}
0.90 & 0.10 \\ 0.05 & 0.95 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is a well-formed
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance. Axiom~1 is vacuous for a single query;
Axiom~2 is consistent with choosing $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ without coupling.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Chapters 1 through 3 as instances}

The definition names what the first three chapters were doing. The three faces
of \Cref{sec:three-faces} are all $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$: the biased coin
above, the binary symmetric channel of \Cref{sec:bsc} with $\fprate = \fnrate$,
and the classifier verdict with rates set by its decision boundary. The
asymmetric channel of \Cref{sec:asymmetric-channel} is the general $Q$; the
Z-channel and the Bloom filter are the one-sided instance $\fnrate = 0$.

Only NOT\index{NOT gate} among the gates of chapter~3 is an operation on the type: it is the
unary $\Bernoulli{\BoolType} \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ that swaps
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$. AND\index{AND gate} and OR\index{OR gate} over distinct latent bits do not return an
instance\index{latent assignment}. The AND output has the single false-negative rate $1 - (1 -
\fnrate_1)(1 - \fnrate_2)$ but three negative rows, $\fprate_1\fprate_2$,
$(1 - \fnrate_1)\fprate_2$, and $\fprate_1(1 - \fnrate_2)$, so as a channel from
the latent pair it has more than two blocks and is not a triple $(X, Y, Q)$ of
the definition above (\Cref{prop:boolean-closure}); OR is dual. A two-rate
description of a gate or formula output exists in three restricted senses only:
when all operands read one latent bit (the Miller-Rabin regime), when the
operands are one-sided in the direction the gate preserves (OR of $\fnrate =
0$ instances, AND of $\fprate = 0$ instances), or as a summary that is either
a prior average or a worst-case bound (\Cref{rem:prior-averaged-composition},
\Cref{prop:worst-case-rate-bounds}). The exact object is the error at each
latent assignment (\Cref{prop:latent-propagation}). Serial composition of
channels (matrix product) and the Kronecker factorization (parallel, tensor
product) are different constructions: the first needs only the law of total
probability, the second needs the axioms.

The atom-outward order was deliberate: examples first, axioms next, the formal
definition last, so that it arrives already supported by intuition. The reader
who followed chapters 1 through 3 has watched it emerge. \Cref{sec:what-comes-next}
looks ahead to Part~II.
