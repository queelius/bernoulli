\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$}{Bernoulli[bool]} Type,
  Formally}
\label{sec:bernoulli-bool-formal}

We have arrived at the formal definition. Four sections of scaffolding
have built up to this moment: \Cref{sec:why-axioms} explained why axioms
matter, \Cref{sec:axiom-1} stated that error events at distinct inputs are
mutually independent, \Cref{sec:axiom-2} stated that per-block error rates
are mutually independent across blocks, and \Cref{sec:kronecker} proved
that those two axioms together force the joint confusion matrix to factor
as a Kronecker product. The formal definition is not an assertion; it is
a synthesis of what the preceding chapters have earned.

\subsection*{The definition}

\begin{definition}[$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$]
\label{def:bernoulli-bool}
A \emph{$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance} is a triple $(X, Y, Q)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $X = \{\top, \bot\}$ is the \emph{input domain} (the Boolean universe),
    with $B_1 = \{\top\}$ and $B_2 = \{\bot\}$ as the two partition blocks.
  \item $Y = \{\top, \bot\}$ is the \emph{output domain} (the observed report).
  \item $Q$ is a $2 \times 2$ row-stochastic matrix
    \[
      Q \;=\;
      \begin{pmatrix}
        \tprate & \fnrate \\
        \fprate & \tnrate
      \end{pmatrix}
      \;=\;
      \begin{pmatrix}
        1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
        \fprate & 1 - \fprate
      \end{pmatrix},
    \]
    where row 1 governs elements in $B_1 = \{\top\}$ (positives) and
    row 2 governs elements in $B_2 = \{\bot\}$ (negatives), with
    \begin{itemize}
      \item $\tprate \in [0,1]$: the true-positive rate
        ($\Prob{\text{reported}\;\top \Given \text{true value}\;\top}$),
      \item $\fnrate = 1 - \tprate$: the false-negative rate
        ($\Prob{\text{reported}\;\bot \Given \text{true value}\;\top}$),
      \item $\fprate \in [0,1]$: the false-positive rate
        ($\Prob{\text{reported}\;\top \Given \text{true value}\;\bot}$),
      \item $\tnrate = 1 - \fprate$: the true-negative rate
        ($\Prob{\text{reported}\;\bot \Given \text{true value}\;\bot}$).
    \end{itemize}
  The triple satisfies \Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom~1: error events at
  distinct inputs are mutually independent) and \Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}
  (Axiom~2: per-block error rates $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ are conditionally
  independent of each other given the objective set).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{notation}
We write $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ for the \emph{type}: the collection of all
triples $(X, Y, Q)$ satisfying the definition above. An individual predicate
or data structure is an \emph{instance} of $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ when
its behavior matches Definition~\ref{def:bernoulli-bool}. The two
parameters $(\fprate, \fnrate)$---or equivalently $(\fprate, \tprate)$---
uniquely identify the channel matrix $Q$ within the type.
\end{notation}

Several remarks on what the definition does and does not say.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Scope.} This definition is for the Boolean special case:
the input and output domains are both $\{\top, \bot\}$, giving a $2 \times 2$
channel matrix with two free parameters. The Bernoulli framework extends
to arbitrary algebraic types---$\Bernoulli{T}$ for general $T$---but that
extension is Part~III material and does not appear here. For now, all
instances are Boolean.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The role of the two axioms.} The definition invokes both
axioms explicitly because both are load-bearing.
\Cref{ax:element-indep} (Axiom~1) is what licenses the factored-product
form of joint distributions: queries on distinct Boolean inputs produce
independent error events. Without it, the channel matrix $Q$ could not
be read as a per-element description; correlation structure across inputs
would require a far richer representation.
\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep} (Axiom~2) is what makes $Q$ time-invariant:
the false-positive rate $\fprate$ and false-negative rate $\fnrate$ are
jointly independent, so knowing one tells you nothing about the other, and
neither varies with query history.
Both axioms together are what makes $Q$ a compact, two-parameter object
sufficient to characterize an entire predicate's behavior---the content
of \Cref{cor:parameter-reduction}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The $Q$ convention.} Row 1 of $Q$ corresponds to
$B_1 = \{\top\}$ (positives); row 2 corresponds to $B_2 = \{\bot\}$
(negatives). Column 1 is the ``report $\top$'' output; column 2 is the
``report $\bot$'' output. This convention was introduced in
\Cref{sec:axiom-2} and used throughout \Cref{sec:kronecker}; it is
consistent across the entire book.

\subsection*{Biased-coin worked example}

The biased-coin example has threaded through this book since
\Cref{sec:three-faces}. We introduced it there as one of the three
faces of a noisy bit---a coin with bias $p$ for heads, where the
observation itself may be noisy. In \Cref{sec:boolean-universe-first-look}
we looked at the Boolean universe qualitatively. We deferred the full
joint-probability treatment to this section. Here it lands.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Setup.} Let the coin have bias $p$: heads ($\top$)
with probability $p$, tails ($\bot$) with probability $1 - p$.
The observation is noisy: it reports correctly for positives with
probability $\tprate = 1 - \fnrate$ and correctly for negatives with
probability $\tnrate = 1 - \fprate$.

\begin{example}[Biased coin as a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance]
\label{ex:biased-coin-formal}
Let $U = \{\top, \bot\}$, with $A = \{\top\}$ the objective set (the event
``coin lands heads'') and $A^c = \{\bot\}$ the complement (the event ``coin
lands tails''). Define:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Coin bias: $p = \Prob{\text{true value} = \top}$.
  \item Channel: false-positive rate $\fprate$ (probability of reporting
    heads when the coin actually landed tails), false-negative rate $\fnrate$
    (probability of reporting tails when the coin actually landed heads).
\end{itemize}

The joint PMF over all four (true value, observed report) pairs is:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\hline
Outcome & True value & Observed report & Probability \\
\hline
True positive (TP) & $\top$ & $\top$ & $p\,(1 - \fnrate) = p\,\tprate$ \\
False negative (FN) & $\top$ & $\bot$ & $p\,\fnrate$ \\
False positive (FP) & $\bot$ & $\top$ & $(1-p)\,\fprate$ \\
True negative (TN) & $\bot$ & $\bot$ & $(1-p)\,(1-\fprate) = (1-p)\,\tnrate$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Sanity check:
\begin{align*}
  p\,\tprate + p\,\fnrate + (1-p)\,\fprate + (1-p)\,\tnrate
  &\;=\; p(\tprate + \fnrate) + (1-p)(\fprate + \tnrate) \\
  &\;=\; p \cdot 1 + (1-p) \cdot 1 \;=\; 1. \quad \checkmark
\end{align*}

The channel matrix is:
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \tprate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & \tnrate
  \end{pmatrix}
  \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1 - \fnrate & \fnrate \\
    \fprate & 1 - \fprate
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The triple $(\{\top,\bot\},\, \{\top,\bot\},\, Q)$ is a
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance whenever $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ are
conditionally independent given the coin's objective set $A = \{\top\}$,
which holds when the observation mechanism operates independently on
each side of the partition. The prior $p$ over the input is not part of
the $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ type itself---the type characterizes the
\emph{channel}, not the \emph{source}---but $p$ enters when we compute
the joint or marginal distribution over outcomes.

For a concrete instance, take $p = 0.6$, $\fprate = 0.05$, $\fnrate = 0.10$
(so $\tprate = 0.90$, $\tnrate = 0.95$). The four outcome probabilities are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Outcome & Formula & Value \\
\hline
TP & $0.6 \times 0.90$ & $0.540$ \\
FN & $0.6 \times 0.10$ & $0.060$ \\
FP & $0.4 \times 0.05$ & $0.020$ \\
TN & $0.4 \times 0.95$ & $0.380$ \\
\hline
Total & & $1.000$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The channel matrix for this instance is:
\[
  Q \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0.90 & 0.10 \\
    0.05 & 0.95
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both axioms are satisfied by construction: error events at distinct
inputs are independent (Axiom~1---the coin tosses and observation decisions
are independent of each other), and the false-positive rate $\fprate = 0.05$
is independent of the false-negative rate $\fnrate = 0.10$ (Axiom~2---knowing
one does not change the other). This triple is a well-formed
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance.
\end{example}

The biased-coin example is the simplest nontrivial instance of the type.
It has one free parameter in the source ($p$) and two in the channel
($\fprate$, $\fnrate$). The source prior $p$ controls how often each block
is queried; the channel parameters control how reliably each query is
reported. The formal $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ type isolates the channel:
it says exactly what can go wrong (and how often) for each kind of input,
independently of how often that input arises.

\subsection*{Chapters 1 through 3 as instances}

The formal definition gives us a vocabulary for what we have been doing
all along. We can now look back at every example in chapters 1--3 and
say, precisely, what type each object was.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Chapter 1: the noisy bit (\Cref{sec:three-faces}).}
The three faces introduced in \Cref{sec:three-faces}---biased coin, binary
symmetric channel, classifier verdict---are all $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instances. The biased coin appears above as Example~\ref{ex:biased-coin-formal}.
The binary symmetric channel has $\fprate = \fnrate$ (symmetric noise), so its
channel matrix is $Q = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1-\fprate & \fprate \\
\fprate & 1-\fprate \end{smallmatrix}\right]$, a special case of the general
$Q$ above. The classifier verdict has $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ determined by
the classifier's decision boundary and the data distribution---arbitrary
in general, but still a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance whenever
Axioms~1 and~2 hold.

In \Cref{sec:boolean-universe-first-look} we walked through the Boolean
universe qualitatively, deferring the full PMF treatment to here. That
deferral has now paid off: Example~\ref{ex:biased-coin-formal} gives the
PMF table, and Definition~\ref{def:bernoulli-bool} gives the formal type.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Chapter 2: binary channels (\Cref{sec:bsc},
\Cref{sec:asymmetric-channel}).}
The binary symmetric channel of \Cref{sec:bsc} is exactly
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ with $Q$ symmetric: $\fprate = \fnrate$ reduces the
two free parameters of the general $Q$ to one. The formal $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
type is strictly more general; the BSC lives as a one-parameter subfamily.

The asymmetric binary channel of \Cref{sec:asymmetric-channel} is
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ with $Q$ general: $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$ may differ
freely. The Z-channel (one-sided error) is the extreme case $\fnrate = 0$
or $\fprate = 0$. The Bloom filter, which reports false positives but never
false negatives, is the $\fnrate = 0$ instance: a row-1 channel that always
reports $\top$ correctly and a row-2 channel that reports $\top$ with
probability $\fprate > 0$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Chapter 3: AND, OR, NOT under noise (\Cref{sec:and-under-noise},
\Cref{sec:or-not}, \Cref{sec:composition-theorem}).}
The gate-level operations of chapter 3 are operations on
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances that produce new $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$
instances.

AND: if $\tilde{b}_1$ has rates $(\fprate_1, \fnrate_1)$ and $\tilde{b}_2$
has rates $(\fprate_2, \fnrate_2)$, the AND of two noisy bits derived in
\Cref{sec:and-under-noise} produces a new $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance
with closed-form $\fprate_{\mathrm{AND}}$ and $\fnrate_{\mathrm{AND}}$. The
derivation in \Cref{sec:and-under-noise} implicitly invoked Axiom~1 (the
two error events are independent) and Axiom~2 (the rates are stable across
the joint computation). Now that both axioms are named, we can say it
precisely: AND is a binary operation $\Bernoulli{\BoolType} \times
\Bernoulli{\BoolType} \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$.

NOT: complement swaps the block labels, exchanging $\fprate$ and $\fnrate$.
The result is a $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instance with $Q_{\mathrm{NOT}} =
\left[\begin{smallmatrix} \tnrate & \fprate \\ \fnrate & \tprate
\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ (rows and columns permuted). NOT is a unary
operation $\Bernoulli{\BoolType} \to \Bernoulli{\BoolType}$.

The composition theorem of \Cref{sec:composition-theorem}---that error
rates compose multiplicatively in series---is the special case of
\Cref{thm:kronecker} (the Kronecker factorization) applied to chained
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances. Each stage of the chain is one factor
in the Kronecker product; the total joint distribution is the
$m$-fold tensor $Q_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes Q_m$. This is not a
coincidence: the composition theorem and the Kronecker theorem describe the
same independence structure from two different vantage points.

\subsection*{What the formal definition earned}

A pedagogy that opens with a formal definition and then works out examples
is a different kind of book. This book chose the other order: examples
first, patterns second, formal definition last. The benefit is that the
definition arrives already supported by intuition---the reader knows what
$\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ means before reading the words.

The cost of that ordering is the distance the reader must carry intuition
before it is formalized. In \Cref{sec:boolean-universe-first-look}, we
described the Boolean universe qualitatively and said the full treatment
would come later. That deferred a cognitive debt. This section retires it.

Chapters 1--3 built the intuition. \Cref{sec:axiom-1} and \Cref{sec:axiom-2}
named the independence assumptions those chapters were silently invoking.
\Cref{sec:kronecker} showed what those assumptions buy in terms of
factorization and parameter reduction. Definition~\ref{def:bernoulli-bool}
collects all of that into a single, precise statement.

This is the atom-outward trajectory: intuition-first, formalism at
the end of Part~I rather than the beginning. The formal definition was not
asserted; it was discovered, step by step, over four chapters. The reader
who has followed those chapters does not need to be persuaded that the
definition is the right one. They have seen it emerge.

In \Cref{sec:what-comes-next}, we look ahead at where this definition
leads.
