\section*{Bibliographic Notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Bibliographic Notes}
\label{sec:bib-notes-ch4}

The material in this chapter draws on three streams of literature.

\paragraph{The Kronecker product.}
The product that carries Leopold Kronecker's name appears in his 1866 work on
bilinear forms, though its use in matrix algebra spread gradually through the
following century. Kronecker himself was interested in the algebraic structure
of determinants and quadratic forms; the representation-theoretic and
probabilistic uses of the product came later. For a modern treatment of the
Kronecker product's algebraic properties---including the mixed-product
property, vectorization identities, and spectral structure---see
\textcite{horn-johnson-matrix}, whose Chapter~4 is the standard graduate
reference. The formal theorem statement in \Cref{sec:kronecker} of this
textbook---that under Axioms~1 and~2 the joint confusion matrix factors as a
Kronecker product of per-query matrices---is a new contribution; see the
honesty note at the end of \Cref{sec:kronecker} for a precise account of what
is new and what descends from the companion papers.

\paragraph{Information-theoretic channels.}
Chapter~4's proof of the Kronecker factorization uses the language of channels
and conditional distributions but does not develop information theory from
scratch. Readers who want the theoretical background---binary symmetric
channels, channel capacity, and the source-channel coding theorem---should
consult \textcite{cover-thomas}. Chapters~7 and~8 of that work cover binary
channels at the depth Part~I uses; Chapter~10 covers channel capacity. We
borrow the channel-matrix vocabulary and the notion of a memoryless channel
(which is, in the Bernoulli setting, precisely what Axioms~1 and~2 enforce),
but we do not reproduce Cover and Thomas's development here.

\paragraph{The Bernoulli papers.}
The two-axiom framework that organizes this chapter comes from
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-sets}. Axioms~1 and~2, as formally stated in
\Cref{sec:axiom-1,sec:axiom-2}, are reformulations of the axioms in that
paper; the textbook's contribution is to separate them into named sections,
give each its own worked examples, and prove the Kronecker factorization
theorem as an explicit consequence rather than treating it as a remark. The
companion paper presents the factorization in a regime where rates are
deterministic or implicitly conditioned upon; the textbook makes the
conditional and unconditional forms explicit (\Cref{thm:kronecker} and
\Cref{cor:kronecker-unconditional}), noting that the unconditional form
requires either deterministic rates or pairwise-distinct queried blocks.
The model-order analysis---the idea that the partition of the input domain
into blocks is what governs the parameter count---appears in
\textcite{towell-bernoulli-data-type}, which extends the Bernoulli framework
from Boolean sets to approximate values over arbitrary algebraic types. The
order-2 Boolean case treated in this chapter is the simplest instance of that
general theory.

This textbook reformulates and extends both papers. The formal theorem
statement in \Cref{sec:kronecker}, the full constructive proof, and the
explicit connection between model order and the Kronecker product's parameter
count are contributions of this text.
