\chapter{Principles and Their Scope}
\label{app:principles}

The source manuscripts organized the framework around five principles. This
appendix restates each one in the form the book actually proves, says where it
is established and who established it first, and records the limits found while
writing and auditing the book. The second half is a table of statements that
were made in earlier versions of the source manuscripts and are corrected here.

\section*{The principles}

\paragraph{1. The two axioms.}
Axiom~1\index{axiom!element-wise independence} (\Cref{ax:element-indep}) makes the error events of distinct queries
independent; Axiom~2\index{axiom!block-rate independence} (\Cref{ax:block-rates-indep}) makes each query's rates
stable constants. Together they say that a collection of queries is a product
of per-query binary channels, which is the content of the Kronecker
factorization\index{Kronecker factorization} (\Cref{thm:kronecker}). Product (memoryless) channels are
standard in information theory \parencite{cover-thomas}, and an independent
per-respondent channel on an indicator is randomized response
\parencite{warner-1965}. \emph{Scope.} The axioms are assumptions about a
structure and a query workload, not theorems about hashing. Queries that share
construction randomness are dependent: the false-positive events of two
non-members of one Bloom filter are correlated through the bit array's
occupancy \parencite{bose-et-al-2008}, adaptive queries can be chosen to
exploit a structure's state \parencite{naor-yogev-2015}, and repeated queries of
one key return the same answer. \Cref{ch:not-bernoulli} catalogues these cases.

\paragraph{2. Parametric parsimony.}\index{parametric parsimony}
A general channel on $m$ joint Boolean tests with $n$ blocks has $n(2^m - 1)$
free parameters; under Axiom~1 the Kronecker factorization reduces this to at
most $2m$. This is a counting consequence of independence, presented here as an
organizing device rather than a result. \emph{Scope.} Product types preserve
the factorization; sum types do not (\Cref{ch:algebra-types}), and any shared
input or shared intermediate value removes it.

\paragraph{3. The space--accuracy relation.}\index{space--accuracy tradeoff}
An approximate set with false-positive rate $\fprate$ and no false negatives
needs at least $\log_2(1/\fprate)$ bits per stored key in a large universe\index{space lower bound!approximate membership}
\parencite{carter-et-al-1978}; with at most a fraction $\gamma$ of false
negatives the bound becomes $(1 - \gamma)\log_2(1/\fprate)$ per key to leading
order\index{space lower bound!with false negatives} \parencite{pagh-rodler-2001}. Static retrieval adds the value bits, which
gives the $\log_2(1/\fprate) + \mu$ scale for approximate maps
\parencite{dietzfelbinger-pagh-2008}. An optimally tuned Bloom filter uses
$\log_2 e$ times the lower bound \parencite{broder-mitzenmacher-2004}.
\Cref{ch:information-bounds} presents the bounds and
\Cref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets,ch:perfect-hash-filters} the constructions that
approach them. \emph{Scope.} The bounds are for static structures over large
universes; dynamic structures need more space \parencite{lovett-porat-2010}, and
the bounds price membership information only, not construction time.

\paragraph{4. Composition.}
What composes exactly is the error \emph{conditional on each latent input
assignment}\index{composition!per latent assignment}: for any Boolean formula over independent noisy bits, the
probability that the observed output differs from the latent output at a given
latent word is a polynomial in the input rates
(\Cref{prop:latent-propagation}). Serial channels compose by matrix product,
independent parallel queries by Kronecker product, and one-sided chains by the
product of their correctness probabilities (\Cref{thm:composition}). Forward
error propagation through gates of this kind goes back to
\textcite{parker-mccluskey-1975} and is systematized by probabilistic transfer
matrices \parencite{krishnaswamy-et-al-2008}. \emph{Scope.} A rate pair
$(\fprate, \fnrate)$ is \emph{not} closed under Boolean operations: the AND of
two noisy bits has a false-positive rate that depends on which negative latent
assignment occurred, so composition raises the model order\index{model order}, and reusing a
summary pair can understate the error by about half on a three-input formula
(\Cref{ex:two-rate-counterexample})\index{counterexample!two-rate composition}. The honest summaries are prior-averaged
rates\index{prior-averaged rate}, which compose only for read-once formulas under product priors and only
as averages, and sound worst-case bounds\index{composition!worst-case rate bounds} (\Cref{prop:worst-case-rate-bounds}).
Shared inputs break operand independence. Composed approximate maps lose the
independent-input law when two inputs share an intermediate value
(\Cref{ch:approximate-functions}). Queries over approximate relations need
source-level sensitivities and calibrated per-source rates, not per-tuple rates
alone (\Cref{ch:approximate-relations}).

\paragraph{5. The Boolean thread.}
A membership query returns a noisy bit\index{noisy bit}, so every approximate structure in the
book reduces, query by query, to $\Bernoulli{\BoolType}$ instances combined by
Boolean operations. \emph{Scope.} The reduction is exact per latent assignment
and subject to the composition limits of principle~4. It describes outcome
statistics; it says nothing by itself about confidentiality, which needs a
separate adversary model (\Cref{ch:cipher-maps-trapdoor,ch:encrypted-search}).
For quantum readout it is exact for the outcome statistics of any state
whenever the measurement is diagonal in the computational basis, and it is not
a description of measurements outside that basis
(\Cref{ch:not-bernoulli,ch:operator-representation}).

An adaptive acceptance threshold saves space.
  & At equal guarantees it does not; it trades away recall, and at a fixed

A perfect-hash-addressed fingerprint table meets the space floor up to
  $o(1)$ bits per element (earlier draft of the Bernoulli hash function
  chapter).
  & Every minimal perfect hash needs at least $\log_2 e$ bits per key
    \parencite{fredman-komlos-1984}, so the perfect hash filter costs
    $\log_2(1/\fprate) + c$ bits with $c \ge \log_2 e$ and beats an optimal
    Bloom filter only when $(\log_2 e - 1)\log_2(1/\fprate) > c$; the
    seed-search construction meets the floor in space at an exponential build
    cost.
  & Chs.~\ref{ch:optimal-bernoulli-sets}, \ref{ch:perfect-hash-filters} \\
Approximate maps are closed under composition with independent per-input
  errors.
  & If two inputs share an intermediate value, the frozen error at that value

  & Ch.~\ref{ch:approximate-functions} \\

  & Scoped constructions and bounds; projected tuples that share sources are

  & It is an upper bound under positive association and a lower bound under

The classical readout model is faithful precisely when the density matrix is
  diagonal.
  & It is exact for any state whenever the detector POVM is diagonal:

  & Chs.~\ref{ch:not-bernoulli}, \ref{ch:operator-representation} \\

  (an earlier experiment write-up and the cipher-maps manuscript).
  & The direction is reversed: concentrated codecs defend best and the dense

  & Ch.~\ref{ch:encrypted-search} \\
