## Major Issues

### MAJOR-1. `thm:local_closure`'s general-`m` true-positive rate `1-(1-tau)^m` tacitly assumes orbit homogeneity (source: logic-checker; brief priorities 1 + 2)
- **Location**: `sections/properties.tex:226-253` (`thm:local_closure` and proof), in conjunction with the orbit/local/monotone definitions at `sections/properties.tex:212-224`.
- **Quoted text** (theorem): "as a random approximate relation of false-positive rate $1-(1-\fprate)^{m}$ and true-positive rate $1-(1-\tprate)^{m}$, a cost independent of $\Card{\Set{A}}$." Quoted text (proof): "for an orbit of true members each trial is a true positive with probability $\tprate$, so it is admitted with probability $1-(1-\tprate)^{m}$."
- **Problem**: The clean rate `1-(1-tau)^m` is correct only when a positive orbit is **homogeneous**: every one of its `m` ordered pairs is a genuine member of the exact `R`, so every trial fires with probability `tau`. The paper's definition of a *local* invariant (properties.tex:212-216) requires only that the constrained pairs "partition into disjoint orbits of at most `m` ordered pairs" admissible "on their own"; it does **not** require an orbit to be all-member or all-non-member. For the two invariants actually instantiated this homogeneity holds for free: reflexivity (m=1, the single diagonal pair is in R) and symmetry (m=2, a positive pair has both ordered tuples in R because R is symmetric), so **no stated, instantiated result is wrong**. But the theorem is stated in full generality for arbitrary monotone-local invariants with arbitrary `m`, and for a general/mixed orbit (some pairs members, some not) the fill probability is the *heterogeneous* product `1-(1-tau)^j(1-eps)^{m-j}` with `j` the member count: exactly the form `thm:project` part (ii) (rel_algebra.tex:109-116) already carries for projection. As written, the general-`m` TPR is therefore an over-clean special case presented as the general rule. The brief asked to scrutinize this just-corrected theorem hardest; this is the gap the m>=2 / m=1 correction did not address.
- **Why prior passes missed it**: the prior re-review checked the m=1 and m=2 *instances* (both homogeneous) and the within-orbit non-interference, and found them sound, which they are. It did not test the general statement against a *heterogeneous* orbit, because no such orbit is instantiated.
- **Suggestion**: Either (a) add a homogeneity hypothesis to the theorem ("each orbit is either wholly in `R` or wholly outside `R`", which holds for reflexivity and symmetry and makes the clean rate exactly correct), or (b) state the general rate heterogeneously as `1-(1-tau)^j(1-eps)^{m-j}` for an orbit with `j` member pairs, recovering `1-(1-tau)^m` when `j=m` and `1-(1-eps)^m` when `j=0`. Option (a) is the lighter fix and matches how the two concrete invariants actually behave. Either way, state explicitly that for the instantiated invariants orbits are homogeneous.
- **Cross-verified**: Routed (logic to methodology). The harness (`validate.py:201-207`) only ever builds **homogeneous** orbits: `np.zeros(...)` (all-negative) for the FPR rows and `np.ones(...)` (all-positive) for the TPR rows, so it silently encodes the homogeneity assumption and would *not* catch a heterogeneous-orbit discrepancy; this corroborates that the clean rate is a homogeneous-orbit fact. Verdict: real precision gap in the general statement; not a defect in any instantiated result; fix before a theory-venue submission.
