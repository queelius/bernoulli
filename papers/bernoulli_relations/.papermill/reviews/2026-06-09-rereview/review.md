## Minor Issues

### MINOR-1. `cor:invariant_space_cost` wording reads as a lower bound; it is an achievability result (source: logic-checker; GOAL 2)
- **Location**: `sections/properties.tex:253-264` (`cor:invariant_space_cost`).
- **Quoted text**: "To hold the OR-closure of a monotone local invariant of orbit size $m$ at a target false-positive rate $\fprate'$, the underlying structure **must** run at base rate $\fprate = 1-(1-\fprate')^{1/m} \approx \fprate'/m$ ... an additive surcharge of $\log_2 m$ bits **to buy the invariant**. Symmetry **costs** one extra bit per element; reflexivity is free."
- **Problem**: The corollary correctly derives the cost **of the specific OR-closure construction** via the entropy companion's space-accuracy duality (a positive set spends `-log2(eps)` bits/element). That is an **upper bound / achievability** statement: "this closure preserves the invariant at this cost." The words "must," "to buy the invariant," and "costs" read as a **necessity** claim, that *any* method of preserving the invariant pays `+log2 m` bits. No matching lower bound is proved. A referee (especially at a theory venue) will read "must run at base rate eps" as an unproven lower bound and call it an overclaim.
- **Verification**: I checked the algebra. `eps = 1-(1-eps')^(1/m) ~ eps'/m` and `-log2 eps = -log2 eps' + log2 m + o(1)` are correct; the `o(1)` gap is small, negative, and vanishes as `eps' -> 0` (e.g. under 0.001 bit at eps'=0.001). The math is right; only the *modality* (necessity vs. sufficiency) is mis-worded.
- **Suggestion**: Reword to make achievability explicit, e.g. "the OR-closure **can be held** at target `eps'` by running the base structure at `eps ~ eps'/m`, which by the duality **costs at most** `+log2 m` bits per element; we do not claim this surcharge is necessary." If a necessity claim is wanted, either prove a lower bound or state it as a conjecture. Trivial one-sentence fix; does not affect any other result.
- **Cross-verified**: This is GOAL 2's exact question. Verdict: the corollary **does** verbally overclaim a lower bound, but the underlying result is a sound achievability bound. Flag, do not block.

### MINOR-2. `thm:local_closure` proof should state the within-orbit-closedness it relies on (source: logic-checker; GOAL 1)
- **Location**: `sections/properties.tex:223-242` (`thm:local_closure` and proof).
- **Quoted text** (proof): "The orbits are disjoint and admissibility is upward closed, so forcing each orbit admissible forces $P$ on the whole relation and **no orbit's repair disturbs another**; one pass suffices."
- **Problem**: "No orbit's repair disturbs another" is asserted from disjointness alone. The reason it is actually true is that the OR-closure is *defined* to "admit every pair **of an orbit** as soon as any pair **of that orbit** is reported" (properties.tex:225-227), i.e. the closure only ever adds within-orbit pairs, so a repair of orbit O touches only O, which is disjoint from every other orbit. That within-orbit-closedness is the load-bearing fact, and it is presently implicit in the closure's definition rather than invoked in the proof. The argument **is** sound (I verified it: for reflexivity the orbit is a singleton, for symmetry the closure adds exactly the missing direction, both in-orbit; in general the closure definition confines additions to the orbit), but a referee re-deriving will want the one explicit sentence.
- **Suggestion**: Add to the proof: "Because the OR-closure only admits pairs of the orbit that triggered it, every manufactured pair lies in that orbit; since orbits are disjoint, repairing one orbit cannot place a pair in, or alter the admissibility of, another." Also consider noting that one-pass restoration uses monotonicity: the all-present orbit configuration dominates the exact relation's (admissible) configuration on that orbit, and upward-closed admissibility makes the dominating configuration admissible.
- **Cross-verified**: GOAL 1(a)/(b). The decomposition hypothesis is correctly packaged into the *definition* of "local" (P holds iff every orbit admissible), so the theorem is conditionally correct given locality, which is appropriate. The antitone single-valuedness remark (deletion: keep at most one reported value per key; one-pass, |A|-independent; the selection counterpart of `thm:functional`) is **accurate**.

## Detailed Notes by Domain

### Logic and Proofs (first-hand; GOALS 1, 2)
The three new results are correct and the proofs are rigorous, with two small exposition gaps.

- **`thm:local_closure`**: correctly stated. The "local" definition packages the right decomposition hypothesis (constrained pairs partition into disjoint orbits of size at most m; P holds iff every orbit admissible), and "monotone = admissibility upward-closed." The one-pass claim is sound: the OR-closure is *defined* to add only within-orbit pairs, so non-interference follows by construction from orbit-disjointness, so there is **no hidden cross-orbit interaction gap** (this was the natural worry; it is closed by the closure's own definition). The only improvement is to make that within-orbit-closedness explicit in the proof (MINOR-2). Reflexivity (m=1) and symmetry (m=2, recovering `thm:symmetric_closure`) are correct instances.
- **Antitone single-valuedness remark**: accurate. Deletion ("keep at most one reported value per key") is a genuine one-pass, |A|-independent repair, correctly characterized as "the selection counterpart of `thm:functional`," and "antitone-local (admissible when at most one pair present)" is coherent within the orbit framework (the orbit is the key's row; admissibility downward-closed). Only missing piece is a quantitative rate (Suggestion 2).
- **`cor:invariant_space_cost`**: the algebra is correct and the `o(1)` is honest (verified numerically: gap small, negative, tends to 0 as eps' tends to 0). The result is an **achievability** bound for the OR-closure; the wording overclaims necessity (MINOR-1).
- **`thm:transitivity_cascade`**: the separation is **airtight as an impossibility for closures of the `thm:local_closure` form**. The path witness shows the constraints cannot be partitioned into bounded independent orbits (triples chain / share pairs), which is exactly the hypothesis `thm:local_closure` requires, so no fixed-cost |A|-independent OR-closure of that form exists. This is a valid impossibility argument, not merely "one strategy is slow": it denies the *structural precondition* (decomposability), which rules out the whole class of bounded local closures, not just the iterated one. I verified `passes = ceil(log2 k)` and `pairs = C(k+1,2)` exactly for k=1..64. The one caveat is the implicit parallel-pass semantics (Suggestion 1). Note the theorem proves "no bounded closure **of the form of thm:local_closure**"; it does not, and does not claim to, rule out some entirely different bounded repair; that scoping is honest.
