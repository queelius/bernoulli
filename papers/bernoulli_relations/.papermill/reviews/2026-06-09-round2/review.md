## Minor Issues

### MINOR-1. `thm:local_closure` states `m>=2` as a hypothesis yet treats `m=1` in its conclusion (source: logic-checker / prose-auditor; priority 1+2)
- **Location**: `sections/properties.tex:239` (hypothesis) vs. `sections/properties.tex:246-248` and `sections/properties.tex:261-264` (the `m=1` discussion in statement and proof).
- **Quoted text** (hypothesis, line 239): "Let $P$ be a class-induced invariant with orbits of size at most $m$, $m \ge 2$ (so that, $\Relation{R}$ satisfying $P$, every orbit is homogeneous) ...". **Quoted text** (conclusion, lines 246-248): "The degenerate case $m=1$ (reflexivity) carries no second trial to combine: its orbit is a single known required pair, restored exactly by inserting the fixed diagonal at true-positive rate $1$ and no false positives, which is free."
- **Problem**: The theorem restricts to `m >= 2` in its hypothesis (the parenthetical correctly notes this is what guarantees homogeneity for the *OR-closure rate* to be clean), but then the same theorem's statement and proof both discuss the `m=1` reflexivity case. Read strictly, the theorem asserts a hypothesis (`m>=2`) and then reasons about a case its hypothesis excludes (`m=1`). The `m=1` content is correct on its own (a singleton diagonal orbit is trivially homogeneous, being in `R`, and diagonal insertion is free), so this is a *framing* defect, not a math error. The round-1 MAJOR-1 fix (forcing homogeneity via class-induced) is what motivated the `m>=2` gate, but homogeneity actually holds for `m=1` too (the singleton orbit cannot straddle `R`); the only thing special about `m=1` is that the *OR-closure* has nothing to combine, which is a statement about the repair, not about homogeneity.
- **Suggestion**: Either (a) drop the `m>=2` restriction from the hypothesis and instead say "homogeneity holds for every class-induced invariant; for `m>=2` the OR-closure combines the `m` trials at rates `1-(1-eps)^m`/`1-(1-tau)^m`, while the degenerate `m=1` orbit (reflexivity) has nothing to combine and is restored by free diagonal insertion", which keeps the `m=1` paragraph inside a hypothesis it satisfies; or (b) keep `m>=2` as the hypothesis and move the `m=1` reflexivity sentence out of the theorem into the surrounding prose (it is already restated at `properties.tex:221-222` and in the synthesis). Option (a) is cleaner and needs only the one hypothesis clause reworded.
- **Cross-verified**: Routed logic to methodology. The harness encodes exactly this split: it runs Monte Carlo OR-closure only for `m in {2,3,4}` and asserts the `m=1` reflexive repair *structurally* (`validate.py:235-255`, the `noisy_diag | True` insertion assert), corroborating that `m=1` is a deterministic, non-OR case that sits awkwardly under an OR-closure theorem gated at `m>=2`. No correctness consequence; phrasing only.

### MINOR-2. The abstract's "decompose into bounded orbits is free" drops the *class-induced* qualifier and so over-generalizes to single-valuedness (source: prose-auditor + novelty-assessor; priority 2)
- **Location**: `main.tex:97` (abstract), the clause "an invariant whose constraints decompose into bounded orbits is free".
- **Quoted text**: "A closure dichotomy sharpens the picture: an invariant whose constraints decompose into bounded orbits is free, one canonical trial per orbit preserves it deterministically at the base rates, with an optional $\log_2 m$-bit surcharge buying an $m$-trial recall boost, while transitivity, which no equivalence induces, cascades and admits no bounded closure."
- **Problem**: "Decompose into bounded orbits" is the definition of a *local* invariant; "free via the canonical test" holds only for the narrower *class-induced* invariants. Single-valuedness is local (its constraints decompose into bounded per-key rows, `properties.tex:233-235`) but is explicitly *not* class-induced and is *not* free: it is repaired by deletion at the closed-form rate `tau(1-(1-eps)^|Y|)/(eps|Y|)` (`properties.tex:310-323`). Read literally, the abstract clause therefore implies single-valuedness is free, which contradicts the body. The other three slots are careful: the intro says "A class-induced invariant (reflexivity, symmetry) is *free*" (`intro.tex:51`), the §5.5 synthesis says "for free by the canonical test where the invariant is class-induced" (`properties.tex:396-397`), and the conclusion says "A class-induced invariant is free" (`conclusion.tex:21-22`). Only the abstract omits the qualifier.
- **Suggestion**: Insert the qualifier, e.g. "an invariant induced by an equivalence (reflexivity, symmetry) is free, one canonical trial per orbit preserving it deterministically at the base rates ...", and, if room allows, note that the antitone single-valued case is repaired by deletion at a closed-form rate (the abstract already names single-valuedness as fragile and as the dictionary, so a half-clause closes the loop). One-sentence edit; no math.
- **Cross-verified**: Routed prose to novelty. Novelty view: the distinction *class-induced (free) vs. local-but-antitone (deletion)* is one of the paper's genuine refinements this round, so blurring it specifically in the abstract undersells the precision; confirming this is a framing fix, not a content gap.

### NOTE-1. `lem:no_quotient`'s `|A|>=3` is sufficient but not the minimal hypothesis (source: logic-checker; priority 1)
- **Location**: `sections/properties.tex:357` (statement) and `properties.tex:363-367` (proof).
- **Quoted text** (statement): "For $\Card{\Set{A}} \ge 3$, no equivalence on $\Set{A} \times \Set{A}$ has the transitive relations as its unions of classes." **Quoted text** (proof): "Unions of classes of a fixed equivalence are closed under arbitrary union. The transitive relations are not: $\{\Pair{a}{b}\}$ and $\{\Pair{b}{c}\}$ are each transitive, and their union is not."
- **Assessment**: The lemma is **correct as written** and the union-closure argument is **complete**: unions of classes are closed under arbitrary union, transitive relations are not, contradiction. The brief asked specifically "does it need `|A|>=3` stated as it is?" The honest answer: `|A|>=3` is *sufficient but not minimal*. The obstruction already holds at `|A|=2`: take `{(0,1)}` and `{(1,0)}` (each a transitive singleton, since singletons are always transitive); their union `{(0,1),(1,0)}` is non-transitive (the chain `(0,1),(1,0)` forces `(0,0)`, which is absent). I verified this by exhaustive check. The paper's stated witness `{(a,b)} ∪ {(b,c)}` reads most naturally with `a,b,c` *distinct* (so that `(a,c)` is genuinely a new, absent pair), and distinctness needs `|A|>=3`; hence the `|A|>=3` bound *matches the witness as written*. There is no error.
- **Suggestion** (optional): If a tight statement is wanted, either (a) keep `|A|>=3` (it matches the clean distinct witness and is perfectly correct), or (b) state `|A|>=2` and use the witness `{(a,b)},{(b,a)}` whose union omits `(a,a)`. Since `|A|>=3` is the natural setting for transitivity anyway and the proof is clean, leaving it is defensible; this NOTE only records that the bound is not minimal, in case a referee asks.

### Logic and Proofs (first-hand; priority 1)
Every new closed form and proof checks out; I verified each independently.
- **Deletion closed form (`properties.tex:314-323`).** Re-derived from scratch. With uniform tie-breaking the kept-true rate is `tau * E[1/(1+K)]`, `K~Bin(|Y|-1,eps)` the spurious-report count. Using the identity `C(n,k)/(1+k) = C(n+1,k+1)/(n+1)`, `E[1/(1+K)] = (1-(1-eps)^{n+1})/((n+1)eps)` with `n=|Y|-1`, i.e. `(1-(1-eps)^{|Y|})/(eps|Y|)`. So the rate is `tau(1-(1-eps)^{|Y|})/(eps|Y|)`, **exactly** the paper's equation. Verified symbolically/numerically for `n in {1,3,7,10,20}`, `eps in {0.05,0.1,0.3,0.5}` (all match to 1e-12), and the harness fits it (`|Y|=4`: 0.834722; `|Y|=8`: 0.757304). Both stated asymptotics are correct: to `tau` as `eps -> 0` (checked: `eps=1e-5` gives kept/tau = 0.99997), and `~tau/(eps|Y|)` once `eps|Y| >> 1` (checked: `eps=0.5,|Y|=8` gives 0.2241 vs. `tau/(eps|Y|)=0.225`).
- **`thm:canonical_test` (`properties.tex:275-300`).** Statement and proof correct. (i) *Determinism*: membership depends only on `orbit(t)`, so the output is a union of orbits and satisfies `P` in every realization (correct, since `P`-satisfying relations are exactly the unions of orbits). (ii) *Base rates*: the homogeneity step "`rep(orbit(t)) in R iff t in R`" is **airtight**: `rep(orbit(t))` and `t` share an orbit, which homogeneity makes wholly in or wholly out of `R`, so the canonical trial is a true-positive trial exactly on members and a false-positive trial exactly on non-members, preserving `(eps,tau)`. (iii) *Storage*: "one element per member orbit, `|R|/2` for a symmetric relation with empty diagonal" is correct (each member orbit `{(a,b),(b,a)}` has size 2, so `|R|/2` orbits) and is confirmed by `validate_quotient.py` (128 stored vs. 256). (iv) *Post hoc by query rewriting*: rewriting each query `t -> rep(orbit(t))` makes the answer a function of the orbit alone, restoring the invariant with no rebuild and no rate movement (correct).
- **`lem:no_quotient` (`properties.tex:355-367`).** Correct; argument complete (see NOTE-1 for the non-minimal `|A|>=3`).
- **Class-induced definitions (`properties.tex:212-235`).** No circularity, no gap. The definition layers cleanly: *local* (constraints partition into bounded orbits, invariant holds iff every orbit admissible), then *class-induced* (orbits = equivalence classes, satisfying relations = unions of orbits), then two derived consequences (homogeneity; orbit-by-orbit decomposition). Reflexivity as the "forced" `m=1` case fits: each diagonal pair is its own singleton orbit whose value the invariant forces to true, a legitimate degenerate class-induced case (the singleton orbit is trivially homogeneous, being in `R`). The `m>=3` motivation (`properties.tex:222-226`) is mathematically correct: a `k`-ary relation invariant under cyclic coordinate shifts has orbits of size *dividing* `k` (orbit-stabilizer; e.g. `(a,b,c)` under `C_3` has a genuine size-3 orbit), and one invariant under all coordinate permutations has orbits of size *up to* `k!`. Both are class-induced group-orbit equivalences with automatic homogeneity, so `thm:canonical_test` legitimately covers them. This resolves round-1 MINOR-2 (the `m>=3` machinery is now motivated by named invariants).
- **`thm:local_closure` (`properties.tex:237-269`).** The round-1 MAJOR-1 fix holds: homogeneity is now *derived* from the class-induced hypothesis, not assumed, so the clean rates `1-(1-eps)^m`/`1-(1-tau)^m` are exactly justified, and the heterogeneous product `1-(1-tau)^j(1-eps)^{m-j}` is correctly shown vacuous for class-induced invariants (`properties.tex:264-268`).
