# Methodology Auditor Report, bernoulli_relations

**Agent**: methodology-auditor (experimental design, statistical rigor, reproducibility); also serving as cross-verifier for logic-checker findings CL-1, CL-2, ML-5
**Date**: 2026-05-29
**Verdict**: This is a theory paper with no experiments and, currently, no completed derivations to audit for rigor. The modeling assumptions are reasonable but stated without the validation the foundation paper provides. Cross-verification confirms two of the three routed logic findings and partially confirms the third.

## Scope note
The paper presents no empirical study, no Monte Carlo validation, no datasets, and no reproducibility artifacts (`state.md` lists `experiments: []`, and there is no `code/` or `data/` directory, unlike `bernoulli_sets/`). For a foundational-model extension this is acceptable *if* the analytic results are complete and validated by reference to the foundation paper. They are not yet complete (see logic-checker). The "methodology" under audit is therefore the **modeling methodology**: the assumptions, the parameterization, and whether the claimed propagation results are derived rigorously.

## Modeling-methodology findings

### MA-1 (MAJOR). The independence assumption is asserted but not examined for the product/relational setting
Assumption `asm:ber_trial` states that membership tests on tuples are i.i.d. Bernoulli, justified by "it is common for implementations of approximate sets to exhibit this assumed behavior, like Bloom filters and Perfect hash filters." For *flat* sets this is the standard Bernoulli-set assumption. But the paper's whole point is **relational operators**, and joins/projections create tuples that **share components**. Two result tuples of a join that share their join-key sub-tuple are tested through the *same* underlying approximate-set cells, so their membership errors are **not independent**. The paper never addresses this. The most important methodological question the paper raises (does element-wise independence survive a join?) is exactly the one left unexamined. This connects to the CLAUDE.md "model order vs. parameter count" remark, which the paper gestures at but does not develop. **Recommendation**: add an explicit treatment of when join/project preserve vs. break the independence axiom, ideally with the Kronecker-factorization argument the family uses elsewhere; this is also the paper's clearest novelty (see novelty-assessor N-3).

### MA-2 (MAJOR). FPR/FNR over an infinite or astronomically large universe is left undefined operationally
For a relation `R ⊆ X_1 x ... x X_n`, the "negatives" are `|X_1|···|X_n| - |R|`, which for realistic attribute domains is astronomically large (and infinite for countably infinite domains, which the relations section explicitly admits). Theorem `thm:fpr` derives `Var[FPR_n] = \fprate(1-\fprate)/n` and notes "the more negatives there are, the lower the variance," with `n` the negative count. Methodologically this means the *realized* FPR is essentially deterministic at scale, which is fine, but the paper never reconciles this with the subset/equality predicate formulas in predicates.tex that carry explicit `|U|` (universe cardinality) exponents and **go to 0 as |U| -> infinity** (the manuscript itself notes "as `|U| -> infinity` the conditional probability goes to 0"). The methodological tension (predicate-preservation probabilities vanish in exactly the large-universe regime where the model is meant to apply) is noted in passing but not resolved. A reader cannot tell whether the relational predicates are useful in the intended deployment regime.

### MA-3 (MAJOR). No validation strategy for the propagation formulas
The foundation paper backs its binomial/CLT claims with simulation (`bernoulli_sets/code/`). Here, the new propagation results (symmetric-relation FPR `1-(1-\fprate)^2`, map composition FPR interval, subset rate `\tsr`, equality rate) are precisely the kind of closed-form claims that are easy to get wrong by a stray independence assumption (see MA-1) and that a small Monte Carlo would catch. Given that several of these proofs are incomplete or disputed (logic-checker ML-1..ML-5), a validation harness is not optional polish; it is the cheapest way to discover which formulas are actually correct. **Recommendation**: add a Monte Carlo experiment per propagation theorem (sample base relation, apply random approximation at known rates, apply operator, compare empirical error rate to the formula). This mirrors the foundation paper's methodology and the experiments now present in `src/bernoulli/experiments/`.

### MA-4 (MINOR). Oblivious-database application has no threat model
The oblivious_rel_model.tex application invokes "oblivious" relations for Encrypted Search but states no adversary model, no security definition, and no leakage analysis. For an application section that promises "rank-ordered Encrypted Search," the methodology of *what is hidden from whom* must be specified, or the claim should be downgraded to "sketch of an application." (Cross-reference: the author's trapdoor-computing line has the proper definitions; cite them.)

## Cross-verification of routed logic-checker findings

### Re CL-1 (self-conditioning probability in def:approx_pfun (ii)): CONFIRMED
I reproduced the reasoning independently. The display reads `P(X ∈ Dom(approx f) | X ∉ Dom(approx f))`. Conditioning an event on its own complement yields probability 0 by the axioms (the intersection `{X ∈ Dom(approx f)} ∩ {X ∉ Dom(approx f)}` is empty). The prose intends `X ∉ Dom(f)` (objective). This is an unambiguous error, not a matter of interpretation. Agree with logic-checker; severity **critical** (it makes a definitional axiom of the paper vacuously false as typeset).

### Re CL-2 (rate-argument-order ambiguity): CONFIRMED, and it propagates into the math
Independently checking: def:approx_rel labels both conditional-probability displays with the pair `[\fnrate][\fprate]`, yet `bernoulli_maps` and properties.tex use `(\tprate, \fprate)`. Because the `\ARelation` macro gives the optional args no intrinsic meaning (subscript/superscript only), the reader must infer per-section. I confirm this is not merely cosmetic: in any downstream formula that plugs a rate into the first slot (for example a future composition over relations), the (FNR vs TPR) ambiguity changes the numeric result by the `1-x` complement. Agree with logic-checker; severity **critical** for a paper whose subject is rate propagation.

### Re ML-5 (equality-rate formula offered "without proof"): PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, formula is suspect
I recomputed the per-element agreement probability for two independent approximate observations of a *member* of `A`. "Equality of the two approximate sets on this element" means the two observations agree (both say in, or both say out). For a true member, observation `i` says "in" with prob `\tprate_i`. Agreement probability `= \tprate_1\tprate_2 + (1-\tprate_1)(1-\tprate_2)`. The manuscript writes `\tprate_1\tprate_2 + \fnrate_1\fnrate_2 - \tprate_1\tprate_2\fnrate_1\fnrate_2`. Since `\fnrate_i = 1-\tprate_i`, the correct `(1-\tprate_1)(1-\tprate_2)` equals `\fnrate_1\fnrate_2`, so the first two terms match. **But the manuscript subtracts an extra `\tprate_1\tprate_2\fnrate_1\fnrate_2` term that should not be there** for a plain "both agree" event. The subtracted term is the structure of `P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B)-P(A)P(B)` for *independent* events, i.e., the author computed `P(both-in OR both-out)` treating "both-in" and "both-out" as independent and overlapping, which they are not (they are mutually exclusive, so no inclusion-exclusion correction is needed). For mutually exclusive events the probability is just the sum `\tprate_1\tprate_2 + \fnrate_1\fnrate_2`. **The `-\tprate_1\tprate_2\fnrate_1\fnrate_2` term is an error introduced by an inapplicable inclusion-exclusion.** The off-`A` factor has the same defect (`\fprate_1\fprate_2 + \tnrate_1\tnrate_2 - \fprate_1\fprate_2\tnrate_1\tnrate_2`). I therefore **disagree with letting the formula stand** and recommend it be corrected to the mutually-exclusive sum (then raised to the cardinalities). This strengthens logic-checker ML-5 from "disputed/unproven" to "incorrect as written." Note this material also overlaps `bernoulli_composition` and may simply need to be cited from there if it is correct *there*.
