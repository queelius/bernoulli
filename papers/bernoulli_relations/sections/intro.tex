\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Approximate membership structures trade space for errors~\cite{bf}.
Using them to represent relations introduces a further question: what
error guarantee applies to a query that combines their answers? A tuple
false-positive rate of $\alpha$ becomes $\alpha^2$ for a conjunction of two
independent absent sources, but remains $\alpha$ if both operands read the
same source. Canonicalizing a symmetric pair deliberately creates such
reuse. It can preserve the relation's symmetry and its tuple marginals,
while changing both query error and the difficulty of exact inference.

This paper develops the relational part of the Bernoulli-set model
\cite{bernoulliSets} around that distinction. Exact relations are fixed;
randomness belongs to a constructed approximate representation. Repeated
probes read frozen outcomes. We treat selection, projection, and join, then
examine which invariant-preserving constructions require shared sources.
The main query question is deliberately limited: for positive monotone
lineage, what per-source error budget guarantees a desired query error for
\emph{every} exact truth profile with the relevant answer? We assume no
prior distribution over exact databases.

Our contribution is a scoped synthesis and calibration study:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We give explicit source semantics and operator formulas, distinguishing
independence of tuples from independence of sources. This identifies the
assumptions needed by familiar product formulas and clarifies when canonical
constraint preservation changes them.
\item We derive sharp one-sided truth-uniform contracts from monotone boundary
certificates, together with marginal-only and shared-rate bounds. For binary
witnesses of source occurrence at most two, elementary graph structure makes
the homogeneous contract inexpensive to compute.
\item We connect the contract to a concrete partitioned Bloom representation
of a symmetric relation, including a construction check based only on occupied
bits, and compare it with ordered-tuple storage followed by symmetric repair.
\end{enumerate}
The certificate identities, graph techniques, and probability machinery
are not claimed as new general theory. The study tests whether combining
them gives a useful specification for approximate relational representations.

\paragraph{Position relative to probabilistic databases.}
Boolean lineage and provenance semirings already record how tuples contribute
to an answer~\cite{green2007provenance}. Weighted evaluation, tractability
boundaries, materialized views, and correlated lineage are well developed
\cite{dalvi2012dichotomy,re2007views,kanagal2010lineage,suciu2011}.
Once a truth profile and a construction law are fixed, our query probability
is an instance of probabilistic inference over that lineage. The origin of
uncertainty in a representation does not evade this prior art. Integrity
constraints and conditioning also have substantial prior treatment
\cite{flesca2013constraints,koch2008conditioning}; constraint preservation
is not a territory unique to approximate data structures.

\paragraph{Probability bounds and program logics.}
Dissociation provides sound bounds by replacing shared occurrences with
appropriately parameterized copies~\cite{gatterbauer2014bounds}; subsequent
work develops relational evaluation, weighted model counting, and anytime
refinement~\cite{gatterbauer2017propagation,chou2018dissociation,vandenheuvel2019scaled}.
Union-bound program logics account for failure probabilities without asserting
independence~\cite{barthe2016union}, while separation logics reason explicitly
about independence and conditional probability~\cite{li2023lilac}.
Recent imprecise probabilistic programming also supports structured uncertain
inference~\cite{liellcock2026imprecise}. Our marginal-only bound is a certificate
application of the union bound, not a replacement for these calculi.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and approximate membership.}
For monotone Boolean functions, certificate complexity and sensitivity
coincide~\cite{blanc2022counterfactuals}; pointwise noise bounds in terms of
sensitivity are established~\cite{gopalan2016smooth}. The exact one-sided
extremal formulas below follow directly from maximal false and minimal true
inputs. A targeted literature search did not locate the identical
query-calibration formulation, but this does not establish originality.
Bloom filters supply the implemented membership mechanism~\cite{bf}.
Adaptivity is a separate concern~\cite{bender2017adaptivity}: our guarantees
do not allow a query to be selected from observed errors and then treated as
an independent fixed probe.

The companion manuscripts develop set-level composition, maps, and
information-theoretic storage analysis~\cite{bernoulliComposition,bernoulliMaps,bernoulliEntropy}.
Here storage claims are limited to explicit constructions and measured
payloads. A tuple-rate identity is not by itself a total-memory comparison.
\Cref{sec:relations,sec:approx_rel_model} specify the semantics;
\cref{sec:rel_algebra,sec:invariants} audit operators and structural constraints;
\cref{sec:query_contracts} develops the contracts; and
\cref{sec:calibration} reports their empirical use and limitations.
