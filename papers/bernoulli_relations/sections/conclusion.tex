\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
An approximate relation needs both an error law and a source map. Relational
operators can reuse sources, and canonical constraint preservation can create
such reuse intentionally. The independent-tuple model remains useful for
specific calculations, but its assumptions do not automatically survive
projection, joins, canonicalization, or conditioning.

For monotone queries, boundary certificates give sharp one-sided contracts
under independent sources and sound bounds under weaker assumptions. Binary
witnesses of source occurrence at most two admit inexpensive contract
calculation. A conditional shared-rate model covers a useful kind of
construction-induced dependence. The stored-filter experiment illustrates
the consequence: sizing at a tuple FPR of 0.05 produced query FPR about 0.33
on a valid false profile, while query calibration was compatible with the
0.05 ideal-model target. Canonical storage used fewer bytes than ordered
storage with symmetric repair at that target in the measured instance.

The established lineage, certificate, counting, and membership-structure
results used here are essential prior art. The current evidence supports a
carefully scoped account of their interaction, not a claim of a new universal
calculus. A stronger publication contribution would require a useful contract
compiler with broader measured benefit, a nontrivial tractable class under
additional semantic constraints, or a construction-specific guarantee beyond
the ideal hash model. The present artifacts make those questions testable
without confusing marginal accuracy, query accuracy, and structural validity.
