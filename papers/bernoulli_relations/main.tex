\begin{abstract}
An \emph{approximate relation} is a relation that approximates another relation of objective interest, admitting \emph{false correspondences} and \emph{false complementary correspondences} at specified rates. We model it as a Bernoulli random approximate set over the tuple universe $\Set{X}[1] \times \cdots \times \Set{X}[n]$, so the whole apparatus of the set model (the per-tuple false-positive rate $\fprate$ and true-positive rate $\tprate$, the binomial error counts) transfers to relations at once. On this footing we track how error propagates through the relational operators. Selection is rate-transparent. Projection amplifies the false-positive rate to $1 - (1-\fprate)^{k}$ in the fan-in $k$ and raises the model order while preserving element-wise independence. A join, by contrast, does \emph{not} preserve element-wise independence: output tuples that share an input component are correlated, so a query result that assumes independent rows understates the joint error. We then ask when structural invariants survive approximation: reflexivity, symmetry, single-valuedness, and transitivity are all fragile, their preservation probability tending to zero as the universe grows, with the symmetric closure as the cheap repair. The single-valued special case is the approximate partial function (the dictionary). We close with a trapdoor-database application to encrypted search. Companion papers supply the information-theoretic space bound~\cite{bernoulliEntropy}, the optimal perfect-hash-filter construction, partial-function composition~\cite{bernoulliMaps}, and the set-level subset and equality predicate rates~\cite{bernoulliComposition}.
\end{abstract}
