\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The \gls{gls-phf} sits within a long line of approximate-membership-query (AMQ) data structures. We review that line here, then state precisely what is new in this paper (\cref{sec:related_contribution}): the construction itself is established, and our contribution is its framing, analysis, and application within Bernoulli type theory, not the base structure.

\paragraph{Bloom filters and the classical space bound.}
The Bloom filter~\cite{bloom1970} is the canonical positive approximate set: a bit array shared across $k$ hash functions, with one-sided error (zero false negatives, tunable false positive rate $\fprate$). It is space-efficient but not space-optimal: at its optimal load it spends $-\log_2 \fprate / \ln 2 \approx 1.44 \log_2(1/\fprate)$ bits per element, a factor of $1/\ln 2 \approx 1.44$ above the information-theoretic minimum. Broder and Mitzenmacher~\cite{bf} survey the filter's many network and systems applications. The $1.44$ overhead is the gap that succinct constructions, including the \gls{gls-phf}, aim to close.

\paragraph{The information-theoretic lower bound.}
Carter, Floyd, Gill, Markowsky, and Wegman~\cite{carter1978} established that any data structure answering approximate membership queries for an $m$-element set over a countably infinite universe with false positive rate $\fprate$ and zero false negatives requires at least $-\log_2 \fprate$ bits per element in expectation. This is the bound the \gls{gls-phf} approaches asymptotically (as $\fprate \to 0$); it is a published theorem, not a postulate, and we treat it as such throughout (\cref{thm:approx_l_b}). The companion entropy paper~\cite{emaps} restates and proves it within the Bernoulli framework and discusses the general two-rate case.

\paragraph{The space-optimal Bernoulli construction.}
Within the Bernoulli framework, the construction that attains this floor up to lower-order terms, for sets ($\mu = 0$) and maps alike, is the Bernoulli hash function~\cite{shs}: it searches for a salt under which an acceptance predicate (equality or threshold) holds on the key set, and the salt length concentrates at $-\log_2 \fprate + \mu$ bits per element. This is the single-seed search mechanism of the minimal-perfect-hashing literature, whose storage is near the information floor and whose expected construction work is exponential in the number of keys~\cite{lehmann2025seeds}. The \gls{gls-phf} is a \emph{different} construction, not an instance of it: it uses a minimal perfect hash and a per-slot fingerprint, with no salt and no acceptance predicate, and pays a constant $\log_2 e \approx 1.44$ bits per element above the floor. The two are complementary, trading space against construction cost. The Bernoulli hash function owns the absolute optimum but builds by salt search with exponential expected work; the \gls{gls-phf} is suboptimal by $\log_2 e$ but builds by mature off-the-shelf perfect hashing and gets a false negative rate that is cheap to construct via $k$-perfect hashing (\cref{sec:k_perfect}). We return to this relationship in \cref{sec:related_contribution}.

\paragraph{The minimal-perfect-hash-plus-fingerprint construction.}
The structure underlying the \gls{gls-phf}, place each member at a unique slot via a (minimal) perfect hash function and store a short fingerprint of the member at that slot, is the well-known static AMQ construction. Several modern filters realize this idea or close variants of it. The Bloomier filter~\cite{bloomier} stores arbitrary values for a fixed key set and is exactly the key-value (map) form of the construction. Cuckoo filters~\cite{fan2014} store fingerprints in a cuckoo-hash table, supporting deletion at the cost of dynamic structure. Xor filters~\cite{graf2020} and ribbon filters~\cite{dillinger2021} solve a linear system over the key set so that the exclusive-or (respectively, a ribbon-banded combination) of the slots a key indexes recovers its fingerprint, reaching close to the $-\log_2 \fprate$ asymptote with low constant overhead. The optimal-Bloom-replacement line of Pagh, Pagh, and Rao~\cite{pagh2005} and the retrieval and approximate-membership structures of Dietzfelbinger and Pagh~\cite{dietzfelbinger2008} give succinct constructions and matching analyses for the same query. In particular, Section~1 of Dietzfelbinger and Pagh~\cite{dietzfelbinger2008} states the \gls{gls-phf} itself: a minimal perfect hash function combined with $\lceil\log_2(1/\fprate)\rceil$-bit fingerprints gives an approximate membership structure within about $\log_2 e$ bits per element of the bound, an idea whose origin they report is unknown to them. The \gls{gls-phf} is the perfect-hash instance of this AMQ family (the minimal-perfect-hash-plus-fingerprint structures above); it is not a new member of it, and it is distinct from the salt-search Bernoulli hash function of the previous paragraph.

\paragraph{Perfect hashing.}
The space achieved by the \gls{gls-phf} is governed by the cost of the underlying perfect hash function. Fredman, Koml\'os, and Szemer\'edi~\cite{fks} gave the first $O(1)$-access perfect hash with linear space; Mehlhorn~\cite{mehlhorn1982} established lower bounds on the program size of perfect and universal hash functions, including the $\log_2 e \approx 1.44$ bits-per-key floor for minimal perfect hashing that our space analysis uses. Practical minimal perfect hash constructions, hash, displace, and compress (CHD)~\cite{chd} and the space-efficient method of Botelho, Pagh, and Ziviani~\cite{simple}, approach a few bits per key. Our space comparison (\cref{sec:phf}) uses the idealized $\log_2 e$ floor for the theoretical break-even and the CHD overhead for the practical one.

\paragraph{Fuzzy sets and ranked retrieval.}
The application in \cref{sec:apps} casts Boolean and ranked document retrieval over the \gls{gls-pmf}. Zadeh's fuzzy sets~\cite{zadeh1965} supply the graded membership and the hedges (such as \emph{very}) used there; inverted-index and ranked-retrieval background follows Zobel and Moffat~\cite{zobel2006} and Manning et al.~\cite{manning}. These are the tools the application uses, not objects this paper introduces.

\subsection{What this paper contributes}
\label{sec:related_contribution}

Given the prior art above, the bare minimal-perfect-hash-plus-fingerprint structure is not a contribution of this paper. The space-optimal Bernoulli construction is the Bernoulli hash function~\cite{shs}, not the \gls{gls-phf}; what the \gls{gls-phf} offers is the \emph{practical} construction of the same ADT, and our contribution is its treatment within Bernoulli type theory:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{An AMQ structure realized as a Bernoulli map ADT.} We exhibit the perfect hash filter as a faithful realization of the static \emph{Bernoulli map}~\cite{bmaps} (the \gls{gls-pmf}), with the \emph{positive Bernoulli set}~\cite{bsets} (zero false negatives, tunable false positive rate $\fprate = 2^{-M}$) as the special case where the stored value is the membership indicator (zero-length value, $\mu = 0$), the same set-as-map unification the Bernoulli hash function uses; the structure thereby inherits the family's error model, composition theory, and space bounds automatically rather than by separate analysis.
    \item \textbf{A two-rate ($k$-perfect) variant.} Replacing the perfect hash with a $k$-perfect hash, a standard object that hash, displace, and compress~\cite{chd} constructs directly, admits a nonzero false \emph{negative} rate $\fnrate$ whose marginal channel is binary asymmetric (\cref{sec:k_perfect}). The false negatives are dependent within shared slots, so the variant is not an element-wise independent second-order Bernoulli set. Trading false negatives for space is the lossy dictionary of Pagh and Rodler~\cite{pagh2001lossy}, whose Proposition~1 bounds the space needed when up to a fraction of the members may be rejected; storing a lossy subset in a minimal \gls{gls-phf} uses fewer bits per retained member than the single-fingerprint $k$-perfect design, whose advantage is construction cost only.
    \item \textbf{General values and an information-retrieval application.} We develop the \gls{gls-pmf} for general values, deriving its space lower bound and a matching maximum-entropy coder, and apply it to fuzzy and rank-ordered retrieval (\cref{sec:pmf,sec:apps}).
    \item \textbf{Construction tied to the framework's space-accuracy bound.} We relate the construction's space to the Carter et al.~\cite{carter1978} bound it approaches as $\fprate \to 0$. The $\log_2 e$ overhead is a vanishing fraction of the optimum as $\fprate \to 0$, so the \gls{gls-phf} is succinct in the relative sense, against the Bloom filter's $1.44$ multiplicative overhead; the absolute optimum belongs to the Bernoulli hash function~\cite{shs}.
\end{enumerate}
In short, this paper offers a framing, analysis, and application of a known structure as the practical Bernoulli construction, not a new data structure and not the space-optimal one. The analyses themselves (the fingerprint false positive rate, the perfect-hash space bound, and the occupancy calculation for $k$-perfect hashing) are standard, and no novelty is claimed for them.
