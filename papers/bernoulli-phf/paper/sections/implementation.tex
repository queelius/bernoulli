\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
The \gls{gls-phf} construction (a minimal perfect hash followed by a per-slot
fingerprint, \cref{sec:phf}) is realized directly by mature minimal-perfect-hash
libraries: the perfect hash is built once over the static key set, and the
fingerprint matrix $\matrx{V}$ is a flat array of $M$-bit words indexed by the
hash. The reference implementation is the \texttt{maph} library~\cite{maph}.
Practical deployments use established minimal-perfect-hash constructions such as
PHOBIC~\cite{phobic} and hash, displace, and compress (CHD)~\cite{chd}, which
build a perfect hash over $m$ keys in linear time and a few bits per key, so the
dominant cost of the \gls{gls-phf} (the perfect hash) is supplied off the shelf.

\paragraph{Practical space overhead and the break-even with the Bloom filter.}
The space analysis of \cref{sec:phf} uses the idealized random-oracle floor
$\log_2 e \approx \num{1.4427}$ bits per element for the minimal perfect hash
(\cref{cor:mPHF_size}). An off-the-shelf construction pays a larger constant: CHD
costs $\alpha(r)$ bits per element, where $\alpha$ depends on the load factor $r$
and exceeds the idealized $\log_2 e$, with $\alpha(1) \approx \num{2.07}$ for the
minimal case. The practical minimal \gls{gls-phf} therefore stores about
$-\log_2 \fprate + \alpha(1)$ bits per element, against the $-\log_2 \fprate +
\log_2 e$ of the idealized bound. This shifts the break-even with the Bloom
filter (which costs $-\tfrac{1}{\ln 2}\log_2\fprate = \num{1.4427}\,(-\log_2
\fprate)$ bits per element). Solving $-\log_2\fprate + c = \num{1.4427}\,(-\log_2
\fprate)$ for the constant $c$ gives the break-even fingerprint width
$M^* = c/(\num{1.4427} - 1)$: with the idealized $c = \log_2 e$ the two are
equally efficient near $\fprate \approx 2^{-3}$, while with the CHD overhead
$c = \alpha(1) \approx \num{2.07}$ the break-even moves to a smaller
$\fprate \approx 2^{-4}$. In both cases the \gls{gls-phf} is the more
space-efficient structure once $\fprate$ is small enough, and the margin widens
as $\fprate \to 0$, where its relative efficiency over the Bloom filter
approaches $\num{1.44}$; the practical break-even simply sits at a smaller
$\fprate$ than the idealized one because the practical perfect hash costs more
bits per element than the random-oracle ideal.

Because the \gls{gls-phf} stores each approximate set as a perfect hash paired
with a fingerprint array, set-theoretic compositions over non-enumerable
operands are computed at query time rather than by rebuilding. Given two
approximate sets $\ASet{S}[1]$ and $\ASet{S}[2]$, the union is the disjunction of
the two membership tests,
\begin{equation}
\SetContains[x][\left(\SetUnion[\ASet{S}[1]][\ASet{S}[2]]\right)] \equiv \left(\SetContains[x][\ASet{S}[1]]\right) \lor \left(\SetContains[x][\ASet{S}[2]]\right)\,,
\end{equation}
and any set-theoretic composition reduces to unions and complements over the
stored operands. The space and induced-rate consequences of these
stored-and-composed operations are analyzed in \cref{sec:adt}.
